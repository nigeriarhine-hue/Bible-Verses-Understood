# 2 Corinthians 3
theme: The Surpassing Glory of the New Covenant
main: Paul says he needs no letters of recommendation, because the Corinthian believers themselves are a letter from Christ, written not with ink but with the Spirit of the living God on human hearts. His adequacy for ministry comes not from himself but from God, who has made him a minister of a new covenant of the Spirit, which gives life. Comparing the old covenant given through Moses with the new, Paul shows that if the ministry that brought condemnation came with glory, so that Moses' face shone, the ministry of the Spirit and righteousness has far greater and lasting glory. A veil still lies over hearts when the old covenant is read, but whenever anyone turns to the Lord, the veil is removed. Where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom, and believers, beholding the Lord's glory, are being transformed into His image.

## Chapter Background and Story

In 2 Corinthians 2:16, Paul asked, [[2 Corinthians 2:16|Who is sufficient for these things?]] In chapter 3, he answers. His sufficiency for ministry comes from God, who has made him a servant of the new covenant.

Paul was writing in a context where rival teachers had come to Corinth with letters of recommendation, perhaps from churches in Judea. They may have emphasized the Law of Moses and questioned Paul's credentials. Paul responds by comparing the old covenant given through Moses with the new covenant in Christ. He does not despise the old covenant; he acknowledges that it came with glory. But the new covenant has a greater and more lasting glory.

Paul draws on the story of Exodus 34:29–35. When Moses came down from Mount Sinai after speaking with God, his face shone with God's glory. The Israelites were afraid to come near him. After speaking God's words to them, Moses put a veil over his face. He removed the veil when he went in to speak with the Lord again. Paul uses this story to illustrate the difference between the two covenants.

### A Letter Written on Hearts (3:1–3)

[[v1|Are we beginning again to commend ourselves? Or do we need, as do some, letters of commendation to you or from you?]] In the ancient world, travelers often carried letters of recommendation to introduce them to new communities (Acts 18:27; Romans 16:1). The rival teachers in Corinth apparently boasted of theirs. Paul's answer is studied below.

### Sufficiency from God (3:4–6)

[[v4|Such confidence we have through Christ toward God]]. Paul is confident, but his confidence is not in himself. His explanation is studied below.

### The Greater Glory (3:7–11)

Paul compares the two covenants. [[v7|But if the service of death, written engraved on stones, came with glory, so that the children of Israel could not look steadfastly on the face of Moses for the glory of his face, which was passing away]], [[v8|won’t service of the Spirit be with much more glory?]] The old covenant, written on stone tablets, came with glory. God's glory was so real that Moses' face shone. But it was a "service of death" because the Law, though holy, exposed sin and brought condemnation to those who could not keep it (Romans 7:10). The glory on Moses' face was fading.

[[v9|For if the service of condemnation has glory, the service of righteousness exceeds much more in glory]]. The old covenant revealed condemnation; the new covenant brings righteousness. [[v10|For most certainly that which has been made glorious has not been made glorious in this respect, by reason of the glory that surpasses]]. The new covenant's glory is so great that the old covenant's glory seems faint by comparison, like a candle in the light of the sun. [[v11|For if that which passes away was with glory, much more that which remains is in glory]]. The old covenant was temporary; the new covenant is permanent.

Paul is not saying that the Law was bad. He has called it [[Romans 7:12|holy, righteous, and good]]. But the Law could not give life. The new covenant, through Christ and the Spirit, does what the Law could not.

### Unveiled Faces (3:12–18)

[[v12|Having therefore such a hope, we use great boldness of speech]], [[v13|and not as Moses, who put a veil on his face so that the children of Israel wouldn’t look steadfastly on the end of that which was passing away]]. Paul interprets Moses' veil as hiding the fading of the glory. Paul, by contrast, speaks openly and boldly about the gospel.

[[v14|But their minds were hardened, for until this very day at the reading of the old covenant the same veil remains, because in Christ it passes away]]. Paul uses the veil as an image. When the Law of Moses is read in synagogues, many do not understand that it points to Christ. It is as if a veil covers their understanding. [[v15|But to this day, when Moses is read, a veil lies on their heart]]. Paul writes this with grief, as he did in Romans 9–11, not with contempt. He himself once had this veil. The removal of the veil, and the transformation that follows, are studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### 2 Corinthians 3:2–3 — A Letter of Christ Written on Hearts

verse: 2 Corinthians 3:2-3

#### Simple Explanation

Rival teachers had come to Corinth with letters of recommendation. They may have suggested that Paul needed such letters to prove his authority. Paul responds with a beautiful image.

[[v2|You are our letter, written in our hearts, known and read by all men]]. The Corinthian church itself was Paul's letter of recommendation. Their transformed lives proved that his ministry was genuine. This letter was "written in our hearts," meaning Paul carried them in his heart with love. And it was "known and read by all," since everyone could see what God had done in Corinth.

[[v3|being revealed that you are a letter of Christ, served by us]]. Ultimately, the letter was from Christ. Christ was the author; Paul was simply the one who delivered it, the servant who "wrote it down."

[[v3|written not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God]]. This letter was not written with ink on paper, but by the Holy Spirit. The Spirit had changed the Corinthians' lives.

[[v3|not in tablets of stone, but in tablets that are hearts of flesh]]. This alludes to the Ten Commandments, written on stone tablets at Sinai. God's new covenant is written on human hearts. The prophets had promised this. Ezekiel wrote that God would remove the heart of stone and give a heart of flesh (Ezekiel 36:26). Jeremiah wrote that God would write His law on their hearts (Jeremiah 31:33).

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that transformed lives are the best evidence of genuine ministry. Credentials, titles, and recommendations have their place, but what truly matters is whether God is changing people's lives.

They also show the fulfillment of the new covenant. God promised through Jeremiah, [[Jeremiah 31:33|I will put my law in their inward parts, and I will write it in their heart]]. Through the Holy Spirit, this promise is fulfilled in believers.

Finally, these verses show that every believer is a "letter" that others read. The way believers live communicates something about Christ to those around them.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses remind believers that their lives are a message to the world. People "read" believers' lives, often before they read the Bible. Believers can ask what message their lives communicate about Christ.

They also encourage those in ministry. The fruit of ministry is not measured by credentials or recognition but by changed lives. Pastors, teachers, and servants can find joy in seeing God at work in people.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to depend on the Spirit for transformation. Change is not merely a matter of following rules written on stone, but of the Spirit writing God's ways on the heart.

### 2 Corinthians 3:5–6 — Our Sufficiency Is from God

verse: 2 Corinthians 3:5-6

#### Simple Explanation

In the previous chapter, Paul had asked, "Who is sufficient for these things?" The task of spreading the fragrance of Christ, bringing life to some and death to others, was enormous. Now he answers.

[[v5|not that we are sufficient of ourselves to account anything as from ourselves]]. Paul admits that he is not adequate in his own strength. He cannot claim any credit for his ministry.

[[v5|but our sufficiency is from God]]. Paul's adequacy came from God. God equipped him for the task.

[[v6|who also made us sufficient as servants of a new covenant]]. God made Paul a "servant" (minister) of a "new covenant." The new covenant is God's promise to forgive sins and write His law on hearts, sealed by the blood of Jesus (Luke 22:20).

[[v6|not of the letter but of the Spirit]]. The old covenant was a covenant of "the letter," written laws. The new covenant is a covenant of "the Spirit," in which God's Spirit transforms hearts.

[[v6|For the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life]]. "The letter," the written law by itself, "kills." This does not mean the Law is evil, but that the Law alone cannot give life. It commands but does not empower; it reveals sin and pronounces judgment. "The Spirit gives life." The Holy Spirit gives new life, empowering believers to live in a way that pleases God.

This verse is sometimes misused to mean that believers should ignore the plain meaning of Scripture in favor of their own "spiritual" interpretations. That is not Paul's point. He is contrasting the old covenant of law with the new covenant of the Spirit.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that all ministry depends on God. No one is adequate on their own for the work of the gospel. Jesus said, [[John 15:5|apart from me you can do nothing]]. God supplies what His servants need.

They also show the difference between law and Spirit. Law alone can show what is right but cannot change the heart. The Spirit changes the heart and gives power to obey. Paul writes, [[Romans 8:2|For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus made me free from the law of sin and of death]].

Finally, these verses show the greatness of the new covenant. Believers live under a covenant of life, empowered by the Spirit. This is a privilege that even the greatest Old Testament saints looked forward to.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers who feel inadequate. Many believers feel unqualified to share their faith, serve in the church, or lead others. Their sufficiency comes from God. He equips those He calls.

They also warn against relying on rules alone for spiritual growth. Rules have their place, but lasting change comes through the Spirit. Believers can depend on the Spirit to transform them from within.

Finally, these verses encourage those in ministry to stay humble. Any fruit in their ministry is God's work, not theirs. They can serve with confidence in God and humility about themselves.

### 2 Corinthians 3:16–17 — The Veil Removed; Freedom in the Spirit

verse: 2 Corinthians 3:16-17

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been explaining that when the Law of Moses is read, a "veil" covers the hearts of many, keeping them from understanding that it points to Christ. Now he describes how this veil is removed.

[[v16|But whenever someone turns to the Lord, the veil is taken away]]. This echoes Exodus 34:34, where Moses removed his veil whenever he went in before the Lord. Paul applies it to anyone who turns to Christ. When a person turns to the Lord in faith, the veil is lifted. They can now see the truth, understand the Scriptures, and behold God's glory in Christ.

Notice "whenever someone." This is available to anyone, Jew or Gentile. Paul himself had once been veiled, persecuting the church, until he met the risen Christ and [[Acts 9:18|something like scales fell from his eyes]].

[[v17|Now the Lord is the Spirit]]. This phrase has been understood in different ways. Many interpreters understand Paul to mean that "the Lord" in the Exodus passage, to whom Moses turned, is now experienced through the Spirit. The Spirit makes the Lord present to believers. Paul is not confusing the Persons of the Trinity; he speaks elsewhere of "the Spirit of the Lord," showing that he distinguishes them while emphasizing their unity.

[[v17|and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty]]. Where the Spirit is present, there is freedom: freedom from the veil, from condemnation, from the power of sin, from fear, and from the burden of trying to earn God's favor. Believers are free to approach God, to see His glory, and to live for Him.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that understanding God's truth requires turning to Christ. Without Christ, the Scriptures remain veiled. With Christ, they open up. Jesus opened His disciples' minds to understand the Scriptures (Luke 24:45).

They also show that the Spirit brings freedom. Jesus said, [[John 8:36|If therefore the Son makes you free, you will be free indeed]]. This freedom is not license to sin but liberty to live as God's children.

Finally, these verses offer hope for all who are veiled. Paul wrote with grief about those whose hearts were veiled, but he also wrote with hope: whenever anyone turns to the Lord, the veil is removed.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to pray for those who do not yet understand the gospel. Only the Lord can remove the veil. Believers can pray for friends and family members that their eyes would be opened.

They also invite believers to enjoy their freedom in Christ. Believers can approach God without fear, confident in His grace. They are free from condemnation and free to live for Him.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to read Scripture with dependence on the Spirit. When believers turn to the Lord as they read, He removes the veil and helps them understand His word.

### 2 Corinthians 3:18 — Transformed from Glory to Glory

verse: 2 Corinthians 3:18

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had explained that when people turn to the Lord, the veil is removed, and where the Spirit is, there is freedom. Now he describes what happens to believers whose faces are unveiled.

[[v18|But we all, with unveiled face]]. "We all" includes every believer, not just Moses or apostles. In the old covenant, only Moses saw God's glory with an unveiled face. In the new covenant, every believer can.

[[v18|seeing the glory of the Lord as in a mirror]]. The phrase can mean "beholding" or "reflecting" the glory of the Lord. Believers see the glory of the Lord, revealed in Jesus Christ, through the gospel. Paul writes in the next chapter of [[2 Corinthians 4:6|the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ]]. As in a mirror, the view is real but not yet complete (compare 1 Corinthians 13:12).

[[v18|are transformed into the same image]]. As believers behold the Lord's glory, they are changed into His likeness. The word "transformed" is metamorphoo, the same word used for Jesus' transfiguration (Matthew 17:2) and in Romans 12:2. Believers are gradually being made like Jesus.

[[v18|from glory to glory]]. This transformation is progressive, increasing step by step. Unlike Moses' fading glory, believers' glory increases. It continues throughout life and will be completed when Christ returns.

[[v18|even as from the Lord, the Spirit]]. This transformation is the work of the Spirit. Believers do not transform themselves by willpower. As they behold Christ, the Spirit changes them.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows God's purpose for believers: to make them like Christ. Paul writes, [[Romans 8:29|For whom he foreknew, he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son]]. Transformation into Christ's likeness is the goal of the Christian life.

It also shows how transformation happens: by beholding Christ. People become like what they gaze upon. As believers spend time looking at Jesus through Scripture, prayer, and worship, the Spirit makes them more like Him.

Finally, the verse shows that transformation is gradual. Believers are being changed "from glory to glory." Growth takes time. Believers should not be discouraged by slow progress; God is at work.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to spend time beholding Christ. Reading the Gospels, meditating on Scripture, worshiping, and praying all help believers see the glory of the Lord. As they do, the Spirit transforms them.

It also encourages patience in spiritual growth. Transformation is a lifelong process. Believers can trust that God is changing them, even when progress seems slow.

Finally, the verse gives believers hope. One day, the transformation will be complete. John writes, [[1 John 3:2|we will be like him; for we will see him just as he is]]. Until then, believers can keep looking to Jesus, being changed from glory to glory.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

2 Corinthians 3 shows that transformed lives are the best evidence of genuine ministry. The Corinthian believers were a letter from Christ, written by the Spirit on their hearts. Believers today are "letters" that others read. They can ask what their lives communicate about Christ.

The chapter also teaches that believers' sufficiency comes from God. No one is adequate in themselves for the work of the gospel. God makes His servants sufficient as ministers of the new covenant. Believers who feel unqualified can trust God to equip them.

2 Corinthians 3 celebrates the surpassing glory of the new covenant. The old covenant came with glory, but it was a ministry of condemnation and its glory faded. The new covenant is a ministry of the Spirit and righteousness, with a glory that surpasses and remains. The letter kills, but the Spirit gives life. Believers can rejoice in the privilege of living under the new covenant.

Finally, the chapter shows that when anyone turns to the Lord, the veil is removed, and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom. Believers, beholding the Lord's glory with unveiled faces, are being transformed into His image from glory to glory. Believers can spend time beholding Christ, trusting the Spirit to change them, and praying for others to turn to the Lord and have the veil removed.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord God, thank You for writing Your word on my heart by Your Spirit. Make my life a letter of Christ that others can read. I confess that I am not sufficient in myself for anything You call me to do. My sufficiency is from You. Thank You for making me a servant of the new covenant, which brings life and righteousness through Your Spirit. Thank You that the veil is removed when I turn to You, and that where Your Spirit is, there is freedom. Remove the veil from the hearts of those I love who do not yet know You. Help me to behold Your glory in the face of Jesus Christ, and by Your Spirit, transform me into His image from glory to glory. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
