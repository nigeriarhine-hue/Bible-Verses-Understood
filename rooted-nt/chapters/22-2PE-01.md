# 2 Peter 1
theme: Everything Needed for Life and Godliness
main: Peter greets those who have received a faith as precious as the apostles' through the righteousness of our God and Savior Jesus Christ. God's divine power has given believers everything they need for life and godliness through knowing Him, and through His great and precious promises they share in the divine nature and escape the corruption of the world caused by sinful desire. For this reason, they are to make every effort to add to their faith goodness, knowledge, self-control, endurance, godliness, brotherly affection, and love; these qualities keep them from being ineffective and unfruitful in knowing Christ, while those who lack them are shortsighted and have forgotten their cleansing. They are to confirm their calling and election, and so receive a rich welcome into Christ's eternal kingdom. Knowing that his death is near, Peter wants to keep reminding them of these truths. The apostles did not follow cleverly invented stories when they spoke of Christ's power and coming; they were eyewitnesses of His majesty, hearing the Father's voice on the holy mountain. Believers also have the prophetic word made more certain, a lamp shining in a dark place until the day dawns, for no prophecy of Scripture came by human will, but people spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit.

## Chapter Background and Story

Peter writes this letter knowing that his death is near (v14). Jesus had told him, years earlier, that he would one day be bound and led where he did not want to go, indicating the kind of death by which he would glorify God (John 21:18–19). Early Christian tradition says Peter was crucified in Rome during Nero's persecution.

Like Paul's final letter, 2 Timothy, this is a farewell message. Peter's main concern is that after his death, believers would remember the truth and continue growing in their faith. False teachers were already at work, as chapter 2 will describe, and Peter wants to strengthen believers against them.

In chapter 1, Peter lays a positive foundation before he addresses the false teaching. He reminds believers of the resources God has given them, calls them to grow in Christlike character, and assures them that the faith rests on solid ground: the eyewitness testimony of the apostles and the God-given Scriptures.

### Greeting (1:1–2)

[[v1|Simon Peter, a servant and apostle of Jesus Christ, to those who have obtained a like precious faith with us in the righteousness of our God and Savior, Jesus Christ]]: Peter uses his full name, Simon Peter, the name Jesus gave him (Matthew 16:17–18). He writes to believers who have received a faith "as precious" as the apostles'. Those who never saw Jesus in the flesh have the same faith and the same standing before God as those who walked with Him. Notice the phrase "our God and Savior, Jesus Christ," a clear statement that Jesus is God (compare Titus 2:13).

[[v2|Grace to you and peace be multiplied in the knowledge of God and of Jesus our Lord]]. "Knowledge" is a key word in 2 Peter. It means not merely information, but personal, growing knowledge of God through Christ. This true knowledge contrasts with the false "knowledge" of the false teachers.

### Growing in Godly Character (1:5–11)

The call to add virtues to faith is studied below. [[v9|For he who lacks these things is blind, seeing only what is near, having forgotten the cleansing from his old sins]]. Those who fail to grow are spiritually shortsighted, seeing only what is immediately in front of them. They have forgotten that they were cleansed from their past sins, and so they live as though that cleansing did not matter.

[[v10|Therefore, brothers, be more diligent to make your calling and election sure. For if you do these things, you will never stumble]]. God has called and chosen believers. They are to "make it sure," confirming it, showing its reality by a growing godly life. This does not mean believers secure their own salvation by effort. God's call and choice are His work. But growing godliness is the evidence and confirmation of that call. Those who grow in these qualities will not fall away.

[[v11|For thus you will be richly supplied with the entrance into the eternal Kingdom of our Lord and Savior, Jesus Christ]]. The result is a rich welcome into Christ's eternal kingdom, like a victorious athlete or a returning hero welcomed home with celebration.

### Peter's Reminder (1:12–15)

[[v12|Therefore I will not be negligent to remind you of these things, though you know them and are established in the present truth]]. Peter's readers already knew these truths, but they needed reminders. Believers often need to hear familiar truths again, not because they are new but because they are easily forgotten.

[[v13|I think it right, as long as I am in this tent, to stir you up by reminding you]], [[v14|knowing that the putting off of my tent comes swiftly, even as our Lord Jesus Christ made clear to me]]. Peter compares his body to a tent, a temporary dwelling (see 2 Corinthians 5:1). He knows he will soon "put it off," a gentle image for death.

[[v15|Yes, I will make every effort that you may always be able to remember these things even after my departure]]. Peter wanted his teaching to outlive him. This letter is part of that effort. Some early Christian tradition also connects Peter's desire here with the Gospel of Mark, which was said to preserve Peter's preaching.

## Important Verses Explained

### 2 Peter 1:3–4 — Partakers of the Divine Nature

verse: 2 Peter 1:3-4

#### Simple Explanation

"Seeing that his divine power has granted to us all things that pertain to life and godliness." God's power has given believers everything they need for spiritual life and for living in a way that pleases Him. Nothing essential is missing.

"Through the knowledge of him who called us by his own glory and virtue." These resources come through knowing God personally. He called believers by displaying His own glory and goodness, especially in Christ.

Verse 4: "by which he has granted to us his precious and exceedingly great promises." Through His glory and goodness, God has given believers wonderful promises: forgiveness, His presence, the Spirit, eternal life, and the return of Christ.

"That through these you may become partakers of the divine nature." Through God's promises, believers share in God's own nature. This does not mean believers become God or lose their humanity. It means they share in God's moral character, His holiness, love, and goodness, and in His eternal life. They are being transformed to reflect Him.

"Having escaped from the corruption that is in the world by lust." Believers have escaped the moral decay that sinful desire produces in the world.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses describe the abundant provision God has made for His people. Believers do not need to search for some secret knowledge or special experience to live godly lives, as the false teachers may have suggested. God has already given them everything they need in Christ.

"Partakers of the divine nature" is one of the most remarkable phrases in the New Testament. It describes the goal of salvation: not only forgiveness but transformation, sharing in God's own life and character. Eastern Orthodox Christians have emphasized this as "theosis," or becoming like God by grace. Other traditions speak of sanctification and union with Christ. All agree that believers do not become divine in essence, but they are made new and conformed to God's character. Paul speaks of believers being transformed into Christ's image (2 Corinthians 3:18) and conformed to the image of God's Son (Romans 8:29).

The contrast between "the divine nature" and "the corruption that is in the world" shows two directions a life can take. One leads toward God's holiness and life; the other leads toward decay and death.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers who feel inadequate for living a godly life can take heart. God's power has given them everything they need. They do not need to look elsewhere; they need to draw on what God has already provided in Christ.

Believers can also treasure God's promises. Reading, remembering, and trusting His promises nourishes the new life within them.

Finally, believers can embrace the goal of becoming more like God in character, growing in love, holiness, and goodness, knowing that this is God's purpose for them.

### 2 Peter 1:5–8 — Add to Your Faith

verse: 2 Peter 1:5-8

#### Simple Explanation

"Yes, and for this very cause adding on your part all diligence." Because God has given believers everything they need, they are to respond with effort. God's provision does not make human effort unnecessary; it makes it possible.

Peter then lists qualities to "supply," or add, building one upon another, like a chain or a ladder.

"In your faith supply moral excellence." Faith is the foundation. To it believers add "moral excellence," or virtue, goodness of character.

"And in moral excellence, knowledge." Practical understanding of God and His ways.

Verse 6: "and in knowledge, self-control." The ability to master one's desires. Notice that true knowledge leads to self-control, unlike the false teachers' "knowledge," which led to indulgence.

"And in self-control, perseverance." Endurance, steadfastness under pressure.

"And in perseverance, godliness." Reverence for God expressed in life.

Verse 7: "and in godliness, brotherly affection." Love for fellow believers, the warm affection of family.

"And in brotherly affection, love." The highest virtue: self-giving love for all, the love of God Himself.

Verse 8: "For if these things are yours and abound, they make you to not be idle or unfruitful in the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ." When these qualities are present and growing, believers will be productive and fruitful in their relationship with Christ.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This list resembles the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23). It begins with faith and ends with love, the two great foundations of the Christian life. Paul writes that faith works through love (Galatians 5:6). Peter's list describes the path from one to the other.

The list is not a rigid sequence in which one virtue must be complete before the next begins. Rather, each quality supports and enriches the others, and all grow together. The image is of a choir or a dance, with each part contributing to the whole. The Greek word for "supply" was used for a wealthy patron who generously funded a choir or a festival. Believers are to "fund" their faith generously with these qualities.

This passage holds together God's work and human effort. God has given everything needed (vv3–4); believers make every effort to grow (v5). Paul expresses the same balance: "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling. For it is God who works in you" (Philippians 2:12–13).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can use this list as a guide for spiritual growth. Which qualities are growing in their lives? Which need more attention? They can ask God to help them grow in each one.

Growth requires effort: "all diligence." Believers can cultivate these qualities through Scripture, prayer, practicing self-control, persevering through difficulty, serving others, and choosing love.

Finally, believers can find encouragement in the promise of verse 8. Those who grow in these qualities will be fruitful and effective. Their lives will make a difference.

### 2 Peter 1:16–18 — Eyewitnesses of His Majesty

verse: 2 Peter 1:16-18

#### Simple Explanation

"For we didn’t follow cunningly devised fables when we made known to you the power and coming of our Lord Jesus Christ." The false teachers apparently claimed that the apostles' message about Christ's return was a myth, an invented story. Peter denies this firmly. The apostles did not make up clever stories.

"But we were eyewitnesses of his majesty." Peter had personally seen Christ's glory.

Verse 17: "For he received from God the Father honor and glory when the voice came to him from the Majestic Glory, 'This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.'" Peter refers to the transfiguration, when Jesus' appearance was changed and He shone with glory on a mountain, and the Father's voice declared His identity (Matthew 17:1–8; Mark 9:2–8). "The Majestic Glory" is a reverent way of referring to God.

Verse 18: "We heard this voice come out of heaven when we were with him on the holy mountain." Peter, James, and John were there. They saw and heard. The transfiguration was a preview of Christ's glory at His return.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The Christian faith is rooted in history, not myth. The apostles bore witness to what they had seen and heard (1 John 1:1–3; Acts 4:20). Their testimony was not secondhand speculation but firsthand experience. Many of them, including Peter, died for this testimony. People do not usually die for what they know to be invented.

The transfiguration is significant because it gave the apostles a glimpse of Christ's glory and confirmed His identity as God's beloved Son. It also pointed forward to His coming in glory. The false teachers denied Christ's return; Peter answers that he had already seen a preview of that glory.

This passage also highlights the role of the Father's voice. The same voice spoke at Jesus' baptism (Matthew 3:17). God Himself testified to who Jesus is.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can be confident that their faith rests on reliable testimony. The Gospels record what eyewitnesses saw and heard. Christianity is not built on myths but on historical events.

When believers face doubts or encounter claims that the Christian faith is merely a story, they can remember the apostles' eyewitness testimony, which they were willing to die for.

Believers can also look forward with confidence to Christ's return in glory. The transfiguration was a glimpse of what is to come.

### 2 Peter 1:20–21 — Carried Along by the Holy Spirit

verse: 2 Peter 1:20-21

#### Simple Explanation

In verse 19, Peter says that believers also have "the more sure word of prophecy," the Old Testament prophecies about Christ, which are confirmed by the apostles' testimony. Believers should pay attention to these prophecies, "as to a lamp shining in a dark place, until the day dawns and the morning star arises in your hearts." The prophetic word gives light in a dark world until Christ returns.

Verse 20: "knowing this first, that no prophecy of Scripture is of private interpretation." This may mean that prophecy did not originate from the prophet's own interpretation of events, or that individuals should not interpret Scripture in isolation, apart from its divine source and the community of faith. The context, especially verse 21, favors the first meaning: the prophets did not simply share their own ideas.

Verse 21: "For no prophecy ever came by the will of man, but holy men of God spoke, being moved by the Holy Spirit." Scripture did not originate with human decision. The prophets spoke from God, "moved," literally "carried along," by the Holy Spirit, like a ship carried by the wind.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses, along with 2 Timothy 3:16, are among the most important biblical statements about the inspiration of Scripture. Scripture is both human and divine. Human authors wrote in their own languages, styles, and circumstances. But they were carried along by the Holy Spirit, so that what they wrote was God's word.

This is why believers can trust Scripture. It is not merely human opinion or religious reflection. It is God speaking through human authors. The false teachers apparently twisted or rejected Scripture (2 Peter 3:16). Peter affirms its divine origin and authority.

The image of Scripture as "a lamp shining in a dark place" is beautiful. The world can be spiritually dark, confusing, and full of competing voices. God's word gives light to guide believers until Christ, who calls Himself [[Revelation 22:16|the bright and morning star]], returns and the full light of day arrives.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can approach Scripture with reverence and trust, knowing it comes from God. Reading it regularly, studying it carefully, and obeying it are ways of paying attention to the lamp God has given.

When believers face confusion or darkness, they can turn to Scripture for light and guidance.

Finally, believers can read Scripture humbly, seeking to understand what God intended, and doing so in community with other believers, so that their interpretation is guided by the Spirit and by the wisdom of the church.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Second Peter 1 reminds believers of the rich resources God has given them. His divine power has granted everything needed for life and godliness, and through His great and precious promises they share in His nature, escaping the corruption of the world. They do not need to search for anything beyond what God has provided in Christ.

Because of this provision, believers are called to make every effort to grow. To their faith they add goodness, knowledge, self-control, endurance, godliness, brotherly affection, and love. As these qualities grow, believers become fruitful and effective in their knowledge of Christ. They confirm their calling and election and look forward to a rich welcome into Christ's eternal kingdom.

Peter, facing death, wanted his readers to remember these truths. Believers today also need regular reminders of what they already know, because familiar truths are easily forgotten.

Finally, the chapter shows that the Christian faith rests on solid ground. The apostles were eyewitnesses of Christ's majesty, hearing the Father's voice on the mountain of transfiguration. And believers have the prophetic Scriptures, given not by human will but by people carried along by the Holy Spirit. These Scriptures are a lamp shining in a dark place until the day dawns and the morning star rises in their hearts. Believers can trust them, study them, and live by them.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord, thank You that Your divine power has given me everything I need for life and godliness, and that through Your precious promises I can share in Your nature and escape the corruption of the world. Help me to make every effort to grow: adding to my faith goodness, knowledge, self-control, endurance, godliness, brotherly affection, and love. Keep me from being unfruitful or forgetful of the cleansing You have given me. Thank You for the apostles who saw Your majesty and bore faithful witness. Thank You for Scripture, given by holy men carried along by the Holy Spirit. Let Your word be a lamp shining in the dark places of my life, until the day dawns and the morning star rises in my heart. Remind me again and again of what is true. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
