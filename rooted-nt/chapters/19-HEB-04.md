# Hebrews 4
theme: Entering God's Rest and Drawing Near to the Throne of Grace
main: The promise of entering God's rest still stands, so believers should take care that none of them falls short of it. Israel heard good news in the wilderness, but it did not benefit them because they did not combine it with faith. God's rest goes back to creation, when He rested on the seventh day, and because Psalm 95 still speaks of "today" long after Joshua led Israel into Canaan, it is clear that a deeper Sabbath rest remains for the people of God, in which they rest from their own works as God did from His. Believers must make every effort to enter that rest, for God's word is living and active, sharper than any double-edged sword, judging the thoughts and attitudes of the heart, and nothing is hidden from God's sight. Since believers have a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, they should hold firmly to their faith. He is able to sympathize with their weaknesses, having been tempted in every way as they are, yet without sin, so they can approach the throne of grace with confidence to receive mercy and find grace to help in their time of need.

## Chapter Background and Story

Hebrews 4 continues the reflection on Psalm 95 begun in chapter 3. The wilderness generation failed to enter God's "rest," the promised land, because of unbelief. The author now asks: Does that promise of rest still apply? And what exactly is God's "rest"?

His answer draws on three Old Testament moments. First, at creation, God rested on the seventh day after finishing His work (Genesis 2:2). Second, under Joshua, Israel entered Canaan, the land of rest. Third, centuries later, through David in Psalm 95, God still spoke of entering His rest "today." If Joshua had already given Israel the complete rest God intended, why would God speak of another day of rest so long afterward? The author concludes that the rest of Canaan was a picture of a greater rest still to come: a Sabbath rest for God's people, sharing in God's own rest.

The chapter then turns from warning to encouragement. God's word searches the heart, and nothing is hidden from Him. That might seem frightening. But believers have a great high priest who understands their weakness and invites them to come boldly to God's throne of grace.

### The Promise Still Stands (4:1–2)

[[v1|Let’s fear therefore, lest perhaps anyone of you should seem to have come short of a promise of entering into his rest]]. The promise of rest is still open, but it can be missed. "Fear" here is a healthy concern, a seriousness about not falling short. Why Israel missed the promise is studied below.

### The Sabbath Rest That Remains (4:3–11)

[[v3|For we who have believed do enter into that rest, even as he has said, “As I swore in my wrath, they will not enter into my rest;” although the works were finished from the foundation of the world]]. Believers "do enter" that rest. It is both present and future. God's rest has existed since creation, when His works were completed.

[[v4|For he has said this somewhere about the seventh day, “God rested on the seventh day from all his works;”]] [[v5|and in this place again, “They will not enter into my rest.”]] The author connects Genesis 2:2 with Psalm 95:11. God's [[Genesis 2:2|rest]] is His own Sabbath rest, which He invites His people to share.

[[v6|Seeing therefore it remains that some should enter into it, and they to whom the good news was preached before failed to enter in because of disobedience]], [[v7|he again defines a certain day, “today”, saying through David so long a time afterward (just as has been said), “Today if you will hear his voice, don’t harden your hearts.”]] Since the wilderness generation failed to enter, and since God spoke again through David many centuries later, the opportunity to enter His rest is still open.

[[v8|For if Joshua had given them rest, he would not have spoken afterward of another day]]. In Greek, "Joshua" and "Jesus" are the same name. Joshua led Israel into Canaan, but that was not the final rest. Jesus leads God's people into the true rest. The Sabbath rest that remains, and the call to enter it, are studied below.

### The Living Word (4:12–13)

The searching power of God's word is studied below.

### Our Great High Priest (4:14–16)

[[v14|Having then a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus, the Son of God, let’s hold tightly to our confession]]. Israel's high priest passed through the curtain into the Most Holy Place once a year. Jesus has "passed through the heavens" into the very presence of God. Because He is there for us, believers should hold firmly to their faith. His sympathy and the invitation to the throne of grace are studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### Hebrews 4:2 — The Word Mixed with Faith

verse: Hebrews 4:2

#### Simple Explanation

"For indeed we have had good news preached to us, even as they also did." Both the wilderness generation and present believers received good news from God. For Israel, it was the promise of the land. For believers, it is the gospel of Christ.

"But the word they heard didn’t profit them." The promise did not benefit Israel. They heard it but did not receive its blessing.

"Because it wasn’t mixed with faith by those who heard." The problem was not the message but the response. They heard God's promise but did not combine it with trust. The word "mixed" suggests blending ingredients together, like food that is eaten and digested. The word must be received with faith to do its work.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that hearing God's word is not enough. Many people hear the gospel but are not changed by it. Jesus' parable of the sower makes the same point: the same seed falls on different soils, and only the good soil, the heart that hears and receives the word, bears fruit (Matthew 13:18–23). James urges believers to be doers of the word and not hearers only (James 1:22).

Faith is the essential link between God's promise and its fulfillment in a person's life. Faith does not earn the promise; it receives it. Without faith, even the greatest promises of God remain unclaimed.

This verse also shows that religious privilege does not guarantee spiritual blessing. Israel had seen miracles and received God's word directly. Yet they missed the promise. Believers today who have heard the gospel many times must still respond with ongoing faith.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers who regularly hear sermons, read Scripture, or attend Bible studies can ask whether they are "mixing" what they hear with faith. Are they trusting God's promises and acting on His word, or simply hearing it?

When reading Scripture, believers can pause to respond: thanking God for His promises, confessing where they have fallen short, and asking for faith to trust and obey.

For those who have heard the gospel but have never truly trusted Christ, this verse is an invitation. The good news is for them, and it becomes theirs when they receive it with faith.

### Hebrews 4:9–10 — A Sabbath Rest for the People of God

verse: Hebrews 4:9-10

#### Simple Explanation

"There remains therefore a Sabbath rest for the people of God." This is the conclusion of the author's argument. The rest of Canaan was not the final rest. A deeper, fuller rest still remains. The author uses a special word, "Sabbath rest," which links God's rest at creation with the rest He offers His people.

Verse 10: "For he who has entered into his rest has himself also rested from his works, as God did from his." Entering God's rest means ceasing from one's own works, just as God rested from His creative work on the seventh day.

What does it mean to rest from one's works? In the context of Hebrews, it likely means ceasing from the effort to make oneself right with God through one's own deeds, especially through the sacrifices and rituals of the old covenant. Believers rest in the finished work of Christ, who has "by himself purified us of our sins" and sat down (Hebrews 1:3).

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The "Sabbath rest" has both a present and a future dimension. In the present, believers enter God's rest by trusting Christ (v3). They no longer strive to earn God's favor; they rest in His grace. Jesus said, [[Matthew 11:28|Come to me, all you who labor and are heavily burdened, and I will give you rest]].

In the future, believers will enter God's rest fully, in the new creation, when every struggle and sorrow is over. Revelation 14:13 speaks of the dead who die in the Lord, that they may [[Revelation 14:13|rest from their labors]].

This rest does not mean inactivity. God's "rest" after creation did not mean He stopped sustaining the world (John 5:17). It means completion, satisfaction, and enjoyment. Believers' rest in Christ is not idleness but freedom from anxious striving, a settled peace in God.

Christians hold different views on how the weekly Sabbath relates to this passage. Some see the weekly day of rest and worship as a continuing sign of the rest Christ gives. Others see the Sabbath as fulfilled in Christ, while still valuing regular rest. Either way, this passage points beyond any single day to the rest found in Christ.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many believers live with a constant sense of spiritual pressure, as if they must prove themselves to God. This passage invites them to rest. Christ has done the work. They can stop striving to earn what He has freely given.

Physical rest also matters. Regular rhythms of rest, worship, and refreshment, such as a weekly day set apart, can be a way of practicing trust in God and enjoying His gifts.

Finally, the promise of a future rest gives hope to those who are weary. The struggles of this life will end, and God's people will enter His perfect rest.

### Hebrews 4:12–13 — Living and Active

verse: Hebrews 4:12-13

#### Simple Explanation

"For the word of God is living and active." God's word is not dead words on a page. It is alive, because God Himself speaks through it. It is "active," or effective, accomplishing what God intends (Isaiah 55:10–11).

"And sharper than any two-edged sword." A two-edged sword cuts deeply and precisely. God's word cuts through excuses, pretenses, and self-deception.

"Piercing even to the dividing of soul and spirit, of both joints and marrow." The word penetrates to the deepest parts of a person. These phrases describe its ability to reach the innermost being, not to provide a precise analysis of human makeup.

"And is able to discern the thoughts and intentions of the heart." The word judges not only outward actions but inner thoughts and motives. It reveals what is really in the heart.

Verse 13: "There is no creature that is hidden from his sight, but all things are naked and laid open before the eyes of him to whom we must give an account." Nothing is hidden from God. Everything is exposed before Him. "Laid open" was used for a wrestler gripping an opponent by the neck, or for exposing the throat of a sacrificial animal. It conveys complete exposure. Every person will give an account to God.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses follow the warning about Israel's unbelief. God's word is what Israel heard and failed to believe. And it is the word that confronts every reader. Through Scripture, God searches the heart and exposes hidden unbelief, disobedience, and sin.

This might seem frightening. But the purpose of exposure is healing. A surgeon cuts in order to heal. God's word reveals sin so that it can be confessed and forgiven. The verses that follow (vv14–16) immediately point to the high priest who offers mercy and grace.

The phrase "to whom we must give an account" reminds believers of the final judgment. Everyone will stand before God. This truth should lead to honesty before God now. Since He already sees everything, there is no point in hiding. Believers can bring everything into the light, trusting His mercy.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can approach Scripture expecting God to speak, not only to inform but to search and transform their hearts. Reading the Bible prayerfully, with openness to correction, allows God's word to do its work.

When Scripture convicts, believers can respond with honesty and repentance rather than defensiveness. The word's sharpness is meant for healing, not destruction.

The truth that nothing is hidden from God can bring both sobriety and relief. Sobriety, because believers will give an account. Relief, because they do not need to pretend. God already knows everything, and He still invites them to come to Him.

### Hebrews 4:15–16 — Approach the Throne of Grace with Confidence

verse: Hebrews 4:15-16

#### Simple Explanation

"For we don’t have a high priest who can’t be touched with the feeling of our infirmities." Jesus is not distant or unfeeling. He sympathizes with human weakness. He knows what it feels like to be weary, hungry, sorrowful, rejected, and in pain.

"But one who has been in all points tempted like we are, yet without sin." Jesus faced temptation in every area of human life. He experienced the full force of temptation. But He never sinned. His sinlessness does not make Him less sympathetic; it means He felt the full pressure of temptation without ever giving in.

Verse 16: "Let’s therefore draw near with boldness to the throne of grace." Because Jesus is a sympathetic high priest, believers can come to God with confidence. God's throne, which could be a throne of judgment, has become a "throne of grace" for those who come through Christ.

"That we may receive mercy and may find grace for help in time of need." At the throne of grace, believers receive mercy for their failures and grace for their struggles. "In time of need" means at the moment it is needed, timely help.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses are among the most comforting in the Bible. They hold together two truths that might seem to conflict: God's holiness, which sees everything (v13), and Christ's sympathy, which understands everything (v15). The God who searches the heart is also the God who welcomes the weak through His Son.

In the Old Testament, the people could not approach God directly. Only the high priest could enter the Most Holy Place, and only once a year, with blood (Leviticus 16). Now, through Jesus, every believer can draw near to God with confidence. The curtain has been torn (Mark 15:38). Access is open.

"Boldness" does not mean arrogance. It means freedom and confidence, the assurance that one is welcome. Believers come not because they deserve to, but because their high priest has made the way.

The pairing of mercy and grace is significant. Mercy is not receiving the punishment one deserves. Grace is receiving the help one does not deserve. At the throne, believers receive both.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

When believers feel weak, tempted, or ashamed, they can come to God boldly through Christ. They do not need to clean themselves up first. They can come as they are, trusting that Jesus understands and that God's throne is a throne of grace.

Believers can make this a regular practice: bringing their struggles, temptations, failures, and needs to God in prayer, expecting to receive mercy and grace.

This passage also shapes how believers see Jesus. He is not a stern judge waiting to condemn, but a sympathetic high priest who knows their weakness and stands ready to help. That knowledge can transform their relationship with Him.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Hebrews 4 offers both a sober warning and a glorious invitation. The warning is that God's promise of rest can be missed. Israel heard good news but did not combine it with faith, and so they did not enter. Believers today must also receive God's word with faith, not simply hear it.

The invitation is that a Sabbath rest remains for the people of God. This rest is God's own rest, first glimpsed at creation, pictured in the promised land, and now offered fully in Christ. Believers enter it by trusting Christ and ceasing from their own efforts to earn God's favor. They will enter it fully in the new creation. Those who are weary from striving can rest in what Christ has done.

The chapter also reminds believers that God's word is living and active, searching the heart and exposing what is hidden. Everything is laid open before God. This truth calls for honesty, repentance, and readiness to give an account.

But the chapter ends with deep comfort. Believers have a great high priest, Jesus the Son of God, who has passed through the heavens. He sympathizes with their weaknesses, having been tempted in every way, yet without sin. Because of Him, they can approach the throne of grace with confidence, to receive mercy and find grace to help in their time of need. Whatever they face, they are welcome before God through Christ.
