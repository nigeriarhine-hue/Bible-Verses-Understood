# 1 Corinthians 6
theme: Washed, Sanctified, Justified: Honoring God with the Body
main: Paul rebukes the Corinthians for taking disputes with fellow believers before pagan courts, reminding them that believers will one day judge the world and asking why they would not rather be wronged than harm their own brothers. He warns that those who persist in unrighteousness will not inherit God's kingdom, but rejoices that some of them who once lived that way have been washed, sanctified, and justified in the name of Jesus and by the Spirit. Responding to slogans the Corinthians used to justify immorality, Paul teaches that the body belongs to the Lord, will be raised, and is united to Christ. He calls them to flee sexual immorality, because their bodies are temples of the Holy Spirit and they were bought with a price.

## Chapter Background and Story

1 Corinthians 6 continues Paul's response to problems in the Corinthian church. In chapter 5, he addressed a case of sexual immorality the church had tolerated. Now he addresses two more issues: lawsuits between believers and the misuse of Christian freedom to justify sexual sin.

The Corinthians had absorbed some of their culture's attitudes. In Roman society, lawsuits were common, and wealthier people often used the courts to their advantage over the poor. Some Greek philosophies taught that the body was unimportant, or that what people did with their bodies did not affect their souls. Some Corinthians seem to have reasoned that because they were spiritual, physical actions, including sexual immorality, did not matter. Paul firmly rejects this thinking.

### Lawsuits Among Believers (6:1–8)

[[v1|Dare any of you, having a matter against his neighbor, go to law before the unrighteous, and not before the saints?]] Paul is shocked that believers were suing one another in pagan courts. [[v2|Don’t you know that the saints will judge the world? And if the world is judged by you, are you unworthy to judge the smallest matters?]] Believers will share in Christ's judgment of the world at the end of the age (Daniel 7:22; Matthew 19:28; Revelation 20:4). If they will judge the world, surely they can settle small disputes among themselves. [[v3|Don’t you know that we will judge angels? How much more, things that pertain to this life?]]

[[v5|I say this to move you to shame. Isn’t there even one wise man among you who would be able to decide between his brothers?]] The Corinthians prided themselves on their wisdom, yet they could not find anyone wise enough to settle a dispute. Paul's further rebuke is studied below.

### Who Will Inherit the Kingdom (6:9–11)

Paul's warning and his words of hope are studied below.

### The Body Belongs to the Lord (6:12–20)

The Corinthians apparently had slogans that they used to justify their behavior. Paul quotes them and then corrects them. The first slogan is studied below. The second: [[v13|“Foods for the belly, and the belly for foods,”]] Some Corinthians argued that just as the stomach is made for food, the body is made for sexual satisfaction, so sexual activity is as natural and unimportant as eating. Paul responds: [[v13|but God will bring to nothing both it and them. But the body is not for sexual immorality, but for the Lord, and the Lord for the body]]. The body is not merely a physical machine; it belongs to the Lord.

[[v14|Now God raised up the Lord, and will also raise us up by his power]]. The body matters to God because He will raise it. Christianity teaches the resurrection of the body, not merely the survival of the soul. What believers do with their bodies matters because their bodies have an eternal future.

[[v15|Don’t you know that your bodies are members of Christ? Shall I then take the members of Christ and make them members of a prostitute? May it never be!]] Believers are united to Christ, body and spirit. Corinth was notorious for prostitution, some of it associated with pagan temples. To join one's body to a prostitute would be to involve Christ in that union.

[[v16|Or don’t you know that he who is joined to a prostitute is one body? For, “The two”, he says, “will become one flesh.”]] Paul quotes Genesis 2:24, God's design for marriage. Sexual union creates a deep bond, a "one flesh" union, that God intended only for marriage. [[v17|But he who is joined to the Lord is one spirit]]. Believers have an even deeper union with Christ.

Paul's command to flee immorality, and his teaching that the body is a temple, are studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 Corinthians 6:7–8 — Why Not Rather Be Wronged?

verse: 1 Corinthians 6:7-8

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been rebuking the Corinthians for taking their disputes with fellow believers to pagan courts. Lawsuits were common in Corinth, and they were often used by the powerful to take advantage of the weak. The Corinthian believers were bringing these practices into the church.

[[v7|Therefore it is already altogether a defect in you that you have lawsuits one with another]]. The very existence of lawsuits among believers was a failure. Even before considering who was right or wrong, the fact that believers were suing one another showed that something had gone wrong in their relationships. The word "defect" can also mean "defeat." They had already lost, regardless of the court's verdict.

[[v7|Why not rather be wronged? Why not rather be defrauded?]] Paul asks a radical question. Would it not be better to accept being wronged or cheated than to take a fellow believer to court? This reflects Jesus' teaching about turning the other cheek and giving up one's coat (Matthew 5:39–40). Love is willing to suffer loss rather than harm a brother or sister.

[[v8|No, but you yourselves do wrong and defraud, and that against your brothers]]. Worse still, some of them were the ones doing the wronging and cheating, and they were doing it to their own spiritual family.

It is important to understand what Paul is addressing. He is speaking about civil disputes between believers over "the smallest matters" (6:2), things like property and money, which could be resolved within the church. He is not saying that crimes, such as abuse, violence, or serious fraud, should be hidden from civil authorities. In Romans 13, Paul teaches that governing authorities are God's servants to punish wrongdoing. Victims of crime should be protected, and crimes should be reported. Paul's concern here is believers using courts to pursue their own advantage against one another over everyday disputes.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that the church should be a place of reconciliation. Believers have access to God's wisdom and are called to love one another. They should be able to resolve disputes in ways that reflect Christ's love, rather than through adversarial lawsuits.

They also show the radical nature of Christian love. Love is willing to suffer loss for the sake of others. Jesus suffered the greatest wrong of all, yet He did not retaliate. Peter writes of Christ, [[1 Peter 2:23|When he was cursed, he didn’t curse back. When he suffered, he didn’t threaten, but committed himself to him who judges righteously]].

Finally, these verses show that public conflict between believers damages the church's witness. When believers drag one another into court, unbelievers see a church that is no different from the world.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to resolve conflicts with fellow believers through conversation, reconciliation, and mediation, rather than through lawsuits, whenever possible. Many churches and Christian organizations offer mediation services to help believers settle disputes.

They also challenge believers to be willing to accept loss for the sake of peace. Believers can ask whether insisting on their rights is worth damaging a relationship or the church's witness.

Finally, these verses must not be misused to silence victims. When someone has been abused, defrauded on a large scale, or harmed by criminal behavior, they should seek protection and justice through proper authorities. Paul's teaching addresses believers seeking advantage over one another in ordinary disputes, not the covering up of crimes.

### 1 Corinthians 6:9–11 — Such Were Some of You

verse: 1 Corinthians 6:9-11

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had just said that some Corinthians were wronging and cheating their own brothers. Now he gives a serious warning.

[[v9|Or don’t you know that the unrighteous will not inherit God’s Kingdom? Don’t be deceived]]. Those who live in persistent, unrepentant sin will not inherit God's kingdom. Paul says, "Don't be deceived," because some Corinthians apparently believed that how they lived did not matter.

Paul then lists types of behavior that characterize the unrighteous: [[v9|Neither the sexually immoral, nor idolaters, nor adulterers, nor male prostitutes, nor homosexuals]], [[v10|nor thieves, nor covetous, nor drunkards, nor slanderers, nor extortionists, will inherit God’s Kingdom]]. The list includes sexual sins, idolatry, theft, greed, drunkenness, verbal abuse, and swindling. Notice how broad the list is. Greed and slander are listed alongside sexual sins.

The two Greek words translated "male prostitutes" and "homosexuals" in the World English Bible (malakoi and arsenokoitai) have been the subject of much discussion. Most Christian traditions throughout history have understood them as referring to the passive and active partners in male same-sex sexual relations, in line with Leviticus 18:22 and 20:13, from which the second word appears to be formed. Some modern interpreters have proposed narrower meanings, such as exploitative relationships. Whatever view one holds, the point of Paul's list is that everyone is included in the need for God's grace.

Then comes one of the most hopeful verses in the New Testament: [[v11|Some of you were such, but you were washed. You were sanctified. You were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and in the Spirit of our God]]. Some of the Corinthian believers had once lived in these ways. But they had been changed. Three words describe the change.

"Washed": They had been cleansed from their sins. Their guilt and shame had been washed away.

"Sanctified": They had been set apart as holy, belonging to God.

"Justified": They had been declared righteous before God.

All this happened "in the name of the Lord Jesus, and in the Spirit of our God." Salvation is the work of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that genuine faith leads to a changed life. Those who claim to follow Christ while persisting in unrepentant sin are deceiving themselves. Paul writes elsewhere, [[Galatians 5:21|those who practice such things will not inherit God’s Kingdom]].

They also show the transforming power of the gospel. "Such were some of you." The Corinthian church included former idolaters, thieves, drunkards, swindlers, and people who had been involved in sexual sin of every kind. Christ had washed them clean. No sin is too great for His grace.

Finally, these verses show that past sins do not define believers. They "were" such people, but they are no longer. Their identity is now defined by what Christ has done: washed, sanctified, justified.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses warn believers against thinking that how they live does not matter. Grace is not permission to continue in sin. Believers can examine their lives and turn from any persistent sin.

They also bring hope to anyone burdened by their past. No matter what someone has done, Christ can wash them clean. Believers who struggle with shame over past sins can remember: "Such were some of you, but you were washed."

Finally, these verses call believers to humility and compassion. Every believer was once in need of washing. The church is not a group of people who never sinned; it is a community of sinners who have been forgiven. Believers can treat everyone, whatever their struggles, with dignity and compassion, offering the hope of the gospel rather than contempt.

### 1 Corinthians 6:12 — All Things Are Lawful, but Not All Are Helpful

verse: 1 Corinthians 6:12

#### Simple Explanation

The Corinthians had a slogan: [[v12|“All things are lawful for me,”]]. This may have originally come from Paul's own teaching about freedom in Christ. Paul taught that believers are not saved by keeping the Law and are free from its ceremonial requirements. But some Corinthians twisted this teaching to mean that they could do anything they wanted, including sexual immorality.

Paul responds with two qualifications.

[[v12|but not all things are expedient]]. The word "expedient" means beneficial, helpful, or profitable. Even if something is permitted, it may not be good. Believers should ask not only "Is this allowed?" but "Is this helpful? Does it build me up and help others?"

[[v12|“All things are lawful for me,” but I will not be brought under the power of anything]]. Paul repeats the slogan and adds a second qualification. Even if something is permitted, believers should not let it control them. Freedom that leads to addiction or slavery is not true freedom. There is a wordplay in Greek: "All things are in my power, but I will not be put under the power of anything."

Paul is not denying Christian freedom. He is showing that true freedom is not the right to do whatever one wants. True freedom is the ability to choose what is good and to remain free from slavery to anything.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that Christian freedom has limits. Believers are free from the Law as a way of salvation, but they are not free to sin. Paul writes, [[Galatians 5:13|For you, brothers, were called for freedom. Only don’t use your freedom as an opportunity for the flesh, but through love be servants to one another]].

It also shows that anything can become an idol or an addiction. Even good things, such as food, entertainment, work, or technology, can become masters if believers are not careful. True freedom means not being controlled by anything except Christ.

Finally, the verse shows the importance of wisdom in Christian living. The question is not merely "What is permitted?" but "What is wise, helpful, and edifying?"

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse helps believers make wise decisions. When considering an activity, believers can ask: Is it beneficial? Does it build me up? Does it help others? Could it control me?

It also encourages believers to examine areas where they may be losing their freedom. Habits involving screens, food, substances, shopping, or anything else can become masters. Believers can ask God for help in breaking free from anything that controls them.

Finally, the verse warns against using freedom as an excuse. Some people justify harmful behavior by saying, "I'm free in Christ." True freedom in Christ leads to holiness, not license.

### 1 Corinthians 6:18–20 — Your Body Is a Temple of the Holy Spirit

verse: 1 Corinthians 6:18-20

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had explained that believers' bodies are members of Christ, and that sexual union with a prostitute would join Christ's members to her. Now he gives a clear command.

[[v18|Flee sexual immorality!]] Paul does not say "resist" or "manage" sexual immorality, but "flee" it. Run away from it, as Joseph ran from Potiphar's wife (Genesis 39:12). Some temptations are best handled by getting away from them.

[[v18|“Every sin that a man does is outside the body,” but he who commits sexual immorality sins against his own body]]. This may be another Corinthian slogan that Paul quotes and corrects. Sexual sin is unique in that it involves the body in a deep way, uniting it with another person. It affects the whole person.

[[v19|Or don’t you know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you, whom you have from God?]] In chapter 3, Paul said that the church as a community is God's temple. Here he applies the same image to the individual believer's body. The Holy Spirit lives in each believer. The body is the place where God dwells.

[[v19|You are not your own]], [[v20|for you were bought with a price]]. Believers do not belong to themselves. They were purchased, like slaves bought in a marketplace, but the price was the blood of Christ (1 Peter 1:18–19). They now belong to God.

[[v20|Therefore glorify God in your body and in your spirit, which are God’s]]. Because believers belong to God, they should honor Him with their bodies. (The words "and in your spirit, which are God's" appear in some later manuscripts; the earliest end with "glorify God in your body.") This includes sexual purity, but also every way believers use their bodies: what they eat, how they care for their health, how they work, and how they treat others.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that the body matters to God. Christianity is not a religion that despises the body. God created the body, Christ took on a human body, the Holy Spirit dwells in believers' bodies, and believers will be raised in glorified bodies. The body is holy.

They also show that believers belong to God. They were bought with the price of Christ's blood. This is both a humbling truth and a liberating one. Believers are not their own masters, but they belong to a loving God who paid the highest price for them.

Finally, these verses show God's good design for sexuality. Sexual union is a powerful gift meant to unite husband and wife in a one-flesh relationship (Genesis 2:24). Outside of marriage, it damages the person and dishonors God. Believers who have sinned sexually can find forgiveness and healing in Christ (1 Corinthians 6:11).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses call believers to sexual purity. Believers can flee situations, relationships, media, and thoughts that lead to sexual sin. Fleeing may mean setting boundaries, changing habits, or seeking accountability.

They also remind believers to care for their bodies as temples of the Holy Spirit. This includes physical health, rest, and avoiding substances that harm the body. Believers can honor God in how they treat their bodies.

Finally, these verses offer hope and dignity. Many people have been hurt or ashamed because of sexual sin, whether their own or others' sins against them. The body that has been hurt is still a temple of the Holy Spirit. Christ offers forgiveness, healing, and restoration. Those who have been victims of sexual abuse bear no guilt for what was done to them; God sees their pain, and His people should offer them care and protection.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

1 Corinthians 6 calls believers to resolve disputes with one another in ways that reflect Christ's love. Instead of using courts to gain advantage over fellow believers in ordinary disputes, believers can seek reconciliation and mediation, and can be willing to accept loss for the sake of peace. This never means hiding crimes or abuse, which must be reported to proper authorities.

The chapter also warns that those who persist in unrighteousness will not inherit God's kingdom. Grace is not permission to sin. Yet the chapter offers great hope: "Such were some of you, but you were washed, sanctified, and justified." No one is beyond the reach of Christ's cleansing. Believers can treat others with compassion, remembering that they too have been washed.

1 Corinthians 6 teaches that Christian freedom has limits. Not everything permitted is helpful, and believers should not be mastered by anything. Believers can make wise choices, asking whether something builds them up and keeps them free.

Finally, the chapter teaches that believers' bodies belong to the Lord. They are members of Christ and temples of the Holy Spirit, bought with the price of Christ's blood. Believers are called to flee sexual immorality and to glorify God with their bodies in every area of life, finding forgiveness and healing in Christ when they have failed.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You that though I was once lost in sin, I have been washed, sanctified, and justified in Your name and by Your Spirit. Thank You that my past does not define me. Help me to resolve conflicts with my brothers and sisters in love, and give me the grace to accept loss rather than harm others. Teach me to use my freedom wisely, asking what is helpful and refusing to be mastered by anything but You. Thank You that my body is a temple of the Holy Spirit and that I was bought with a price. Help me to flee sexual immorality and every temptation that would dishonor You. Heal those who carry shame or have been hurt, and let Your church be a place of compassion and restoration. Help me to glorify You with my body in all I do. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
