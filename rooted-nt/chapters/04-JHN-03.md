# John 3
theme: Born Anew: God's Love and the Gift of Eternal Life
main: Nicodemus, a respected Pharisee, comes to Jesus by night and learns that no one can see God's kingdom without being born anew by the Spirit. Jesus explains that, like the bronze serpent Moses lifted up, the Son of Man must be lifted up so that whoever believes may have eternal life, because God so loved the world that He gave His only Son. When John the Baptist's followers worry about Jesus' growing popularity, John rejoices like the bridegroom's friend and declares that Jesus must increase while he decreases.

## Chapter Background and Story

John 3 contains one of the most famous conversations in the Bible and one of its most beloved verses, John 3:16. The chapter follows the end of John 2, where many in Jerusalem believed in Jesus because of His signs, but Jesus did not entrust Himself to them because [[John 2:25|he himself knew what was in man]]. Nicodemus is an example of such a person: impressed by Jesus' signs, but not yet understanding who He is.

### Nicodemus Comes by Night (3:1–2)

Nicodemus was [[v1|a man of the Pharisees]] and [[v1|a ruler of the Jews]], meaning he was likely a member of the Sanhedrin, the ruling council in Jerusalem. Jesus later calls him [[v10|the teacher of Israel]], suggesting he was a well-known teacher of the law. He was respected, educated, and devout.

He came to Jesus [[v2|by night]]. Perhaps he wanted to avoid being seen with a controversial teacher, or perhaps he wanted an uninterrupted conversation. In John's Gospel, night and darkness often symbolize spiritual ignorance (John 1:5; 13:30). Nicodemus came out of the darkness toward the light.

He began respectfully: [[v2|Rabbi, we know that you are a teacher who has come from God, for no one can do these signs that you do, unless God is with him]]. Nicodemus acknowledged Jesus as a God-sent teacher based on His miracles. But this was not yet full understanding of who Jesus is.

### You Must Be Born Anew (3:3–8)

Jesus responded directly: [[v3|Most certainly I tell you, unless one is born anew, he can’t see God’s Kingdom]]. This is studied below. The Greek word translated "anew" can mean "again" or "from above," and Jesus may intend both.

Nicodemus took Jesus' words literally: [[v4|How can a man be born when he is old? Can he enter a second time into his mother’s womb and be born?]] He may have understood that Jesus was speaking figuratively but wondered how an old man could start over completely.

Jesus explained, [[v5|unless one is born of water and Spirit, he can’t enter into God’s Kingdom]]. Christians have understood "water and Spirit" in several ways. Some see "water" as referring to physical birth and "Spirit" to spiritual birth. Others connect "water" with baptism, which was already being practiced by John and Jesus' disciples (John 3:22–23). Many see it as a reference to Ezekiel 36:25–27, where God promised to sprinkle clean water on His people and give them a new heart and a new spirit, describing inner cleansing and renewal by God's Spirit. Since Jesus rebuked Nicodemus as a teacher of Israel for not understanding (v. 10), He likely expected him to recognize this Old Testament promise. In any case, the emphasis is on spiritual cleansing and new life given by the Holy Spirit.

[[v6|That which is born of the flesh is flesh. That which is born of the Spirit is spirit]]. Human birth produces human life, but only the Spirit can produce spiritual life. Jesus then used an image: [[v8|The wind blows where it wants to, and you hear its sound, but don’t know where it comes from and where it is going. So is everyone who is born of the Spirit]]. In both Hebrew and Greek, the same word means "wind," "breath," and "spirit." The Spirit's work is like the wind: invisible and beyond human control, yet its effects are real and observable.

### Heavenly Things (3:9–15)

Nicodemus asked, [[v9|How can these things be?]] Jesus replied, [[v10|Are you the teacher of Israel, and don’t understand these things?]] Nicodemus, as a respected teacher, should have known the prophets' promises of spiritual renewal.

Jesus said that He spoke of what He knew and had seen, yet people did not accept His testimony. If they did not believe when He spoke of earthly things, such as the new birth that happens on earth, how would they believe if He spoke of heavenly things? [[v13|No one has ascended into heaven but he who descended out of heaven, the Son of Man]]. Jesus alone came from heaven and can reveal heavenly truth.

Then He pointed to an Old Testament story: [[v14|As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of Man be lifted up]]. In Numbers 21:4–9, the Israelites complained against God in the wilderness, and venomous snakes bit them, and many died. God told Moses to make a bronze snake and put it on a pole. Anyone who was bitten could look at the bronze snake and live. Jesus said He must be "lifted up" in the same way. In John's Gospel, "lifted up" refers to His crucifixion (John 12:32–33) and also to His exaltation. Just as the Israelites looked at the bronze snake and lived, people who look to Jesus in faith receive eternal life: [[v15|that whoever believes in him should not perish, but have eternal life]].

### God So Loved the World (3:16–21)

Then comes perhaps the most famous verse in the Bible: [[v16|For God so loved the world, that he gave his only born Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish, but have eternal life]]. This verse is studied below. (Scholars debate whether Jesus' words to Nicodemus end at verse 15 or verse 21, with the rest being John's reflection. Either way, these verses explain the meaning of what Jesus taught.)

[[v17|For God didn’t send his Son into the world to judge the world, but that the world should be saved through him]]. God's purpose in sending Jesus was salvation, not condemnation. [[v18|He who believes in him is not judged. He who doesn’t believe has been judged already]]. Those who refuse to believe are already under condemnation, not because Jesus came to condemn them, but because they reject the only way of rescue.

Then John explains the nature of this judgment: people love darkness rather than light. This is studied below. Those who do evil hate the light and avoid it, so their deeds will not be exposed. But those who practice the truth come to the light, so that it may be seen that their deeds have been done in God.

### John the Baptist's Final Testimony (3:22–36)

After this, Jesus and His disciples went into the Judean countryside, where they spent time and baptized. (John 4:2 clarifies that Jesus Himself did not baptize; His disciples did.) John the Baptist was also baptizing at Aenon near Salim, where there was plenty of water, since he had not yet been put in prison.

A dispute arose between some of John's disciples and a certain Jew about purification, perhaps about whose baptism was more effective. John's disciples came to him concerned: the one John had testified about was now baptizing, and [[v26|everyone is coming to him]]. They seemed jealous on John's behalf.

John answered with deep humility. A person can receive only what is given from heaven. He reminded them that he had said he was not the Messiah but was sent ahead of Him. He used the image of a wedding: the bride belongs to the bridegroom, and the friend of the bridegroom, the best man, rejoices when he hears the bridegroom's voice. [[v29|Therefore my joy is made full]]. John was glad that people were going to Jesus. Then he said, [[v30|He must increase, but I must decrease]]. This verse is studied below.

The chapter closes with a reflection on Jesus' heavenly origin and authority (vv. 31–36). The one who comes from above is above all. God has given Him the Spirit without measure. [[v35|The Father loves the Son, and has given all things into his hand]]. [[v36|One who believes in the Son has eternal life, but one who disobeys the Son won’t see life, but the wrath of God remains on him]]. Notice that believing and obeying are linked; true faith shows itself in obedience.

## Important Verses Explained

### John 3:3 — You Must Be Born Anew

verse: John 3:3

#### Simple Explanation

This verse is Jesus' first response to Nicodemus, a Pharisee and respected teacher who came to Jesus at night. Nicodemus had opened with a compliment: he recognized that Jesus was a teacher sent from God, because no one could perform such signs unless God was with him. He seemed ready to engage Jesus in a respectful discussion between teachers.

But Jesus went straight to the heart of the matter: [[v3|Most certainly I tell you, unless one is born anew, he can’t see God’s Kingdom]].

[[v3|Most certainly I tell you]] (literally "Amen, amen") marks a solemn, important statement.

[[v3|unless one is born anew]]. The Greek word "anothen" can mean "again" or "from above." Nicodemus understood it as "again," a second birth. Jesus likely intended both meanings: a person must be born again, and this new birth comes from above, from God. It is not something a person can produce by effort.

[[v3|he can’t see God’s Kingdom]]. "See" here means experience or enter (as verse 5 says, "enter into God's Kingdom"). The kingdom of God is God's reign, His saving rule and the life of the age to come. Without new birth, no one can participate in it.

This statement would have startled Nicodemus. As a devout Pharisee and a Jew, he likely assumed he already belonged to God's people and would share in God's kingdom. He kept God's law carefully. But Jesus told him that even he needed to be born anew. Religious heritage, knowledge, and moral effort were not enough. He needed a completely new beginning from God.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse teaches that salvation requires an inner transformation that only God can accomplish. Human beings cannot reform themselves into God's kingdom. They need new life from God, which the Holy Spirit gives. Paul describes this as becoming a new creation: [[2 Corinthians 5:17|if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation]]. Peter speaks of being born again to a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus (1 Peter 1:3).

It also shows that everyone needs new birth, even the most religious. Nicodemus was respectable, educated, and devout, yet Jesus told him he must be born anew. Religious practice cannot substitute for spiritual rebirth.

Finally, the image of birth emphasizes God's initiative. No one causes their own birth. In the same way, spiritual birth is God's gift. John 1:13 describes believers as born [[John 1:13|not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God]]. This new birth comes through faith in Jesus, as the rest of the chapter explains.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites everyone to consider whether they have been born anew. It is possible to grow up in church, know the Bible, and live morally without having experienced the new life God gives. Each person can come to Jesus in faith, asking for the new birth that only God can give.

It also encourages humility. No one is too good or too religious to need new birth. Believers can approach others, including religious people, with the understanding that everyone needs God's transforming grace.

Finally, this verse offers hope for real change. Many people feel stuck in old patterns, unable to change themselves. The new birth means that God can give a fresh start and a new heart. Believers can trust the Spirit to keep transforming them.

### John 3:16 — For God So Loved the World

verse: John 3:16

#### Simple Explanation

This verse is perhaps the most famous verse in the Bible. It comes in the context of Jesus' conversation with Nicodemus. Jesus had just explained that people must be born anew by the Spirit, and that, as Moses lifted up the bronze serpent in the wilderness so that bitten people could look at it and live, the Son of Man must be lifted up so that whoever believes in Him may have eternal life.

Then comes this summary of the gospel: [[v16|For God so loved the world]]. "So" means "in this way" or "to such a degree." God's love is the source of salvation. "The world" in John's Gospel often refers to humanity in rebellion against God (John 1:10; 7:7). God loved not a perfect world but a sinful one that had turned away from Him. His love is not limited to one nation or group; it extends to the whole world.

[[v16|that he gave his only born Son]]. God's love was shown in action: He gave His Son. "Gave" refers to sending Jesus into the world and, ultimately, giving Him up to death on the cross. "Only born Son" (often translated "only begotten" or "one and only") emphasizes Jesus' unique relationship with the Father. God gave the most precious gift possible. This recalls Abraham being asked to offer his only son Isaac (Genesis 22:2), but where God spared Isaac, He did not spare His own Son (Romans 8:32).

[[v16|that whoever believes in him]]. "Whoever" opens the invitation to everyone. "Believes in him" means trusting Jesus, relying on Him, committing oneself to Him. It is more than agreeing with facts about Jesus; it is personal trust.

[[v16|should not perish, but have eternal life]]. There are two possible destinies. "Perish" means to be lost or destroyed, to experience final separation from God. "Eternal life" is more than endless existence; it is the life of the age to come, a quality of life found in knowing God (John 17:3). It begins now and continues forever.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse summarizes the heart of the Christian faith. God's love is the motivation, the giving of His Son is the means, faith is the response, and eternal life is the result. Every part of the gospel is contained in this one sentence.

It reveals the depth of God's love. God did not love the world because it was lovable; He loved it despite its rebellion. Paul writes, [[Romans 5:8|But God commends his own love toward us, in that while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us]]. God's love is costly, self-giving, and undeserved.

It also shows that salvation is a gift received through faith. Eternal life is not earned by good works but received by believing in Jesus. The word "whoever" means that no one is excluded from the invitation.

Finally, the following verses (vv. 17–18) clarify that God's purpose in sending Jesus was to save, not to condemn. Condemnation is the result of rejecting the gift, not the goal of God's coming.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites every person to believe in Jesus and receive eternal life. God's love extends to everyone, and the offer is open to "whoever believes." Those who have not yet trusted Jesus can do so today.

It also gives believers assurance of God's love. When they feel unworthy or unloved, they can remember that God loved the world so much that He gave His Son. His love for them is proven at the cross.

Finally, this verse calls believers to share God's love with the world. If God loved the world this much, His people should love others too, sharing the good news of Jesus with everyone, regardless of background.

### John 3:19 — Loving Darkness Rather Than Light

verse: John 3:19

#### Simple Explanation

This verse explains why some people reject Jesus. John has just said that God sent His Son not to condemn the world but to save it. Whoever believes in Him is not condemned, but whoever does not believe is condemned already, because they have not believed in the name of God's only Son (v. 18).

Now John explains the nature of that condemnation: [[v19|This is the judgment, that the light has come into the world, and men loved the darkness rather than the light, for their works were evil]].

[[v19|the light has come into the world]]. Jesus is the light. In John 1:4–9, He is described as the light that shines in the darkness and gives light to everyone. Later Jesus says, [[John 8:12|I am the light of the world]]. Light reveals, exposes, guides, and gives life.

[[v19|and men loved the darkness rather than the light]]. Many people preferred darkness. "Darkness" represents sin, ignorance, and separation from God. The problem was not lack of evidence or lack of opportunity. Light had come, but people loved darkness more.

[[v19|for their works were evil]]. The reason is moral, not intellectual. People who are doing evil prefer not to have their deeds exposed. The next verse explains: [[v20|For everyone who does evil hates the light and doesn’t come to the light, lest his works would be exposed]]. Coming to Jesus means having one's sin revealed, and many people would rather hide.

But verse 21 offers contrast: [[v21|But he who does the truth comes to the light, that his works may be revealed, that they have been done in God]]. Those who are willing to live truthfully come to the light without fear.

This passage connects to Nicodemus, who came to Jesus "by night." He was moving from darkness toward light. Later in John's Gospel, he defends Jesus before the council (John 7:50–51) and helps bury Jesus' body (John 19:39), suggesting that he eventually came fully into the light.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse reveals that unbelief is often a matter of the heart and will, not simply a lack of information. People reject Jesus not because the light is unclear but because they love darkness. Sin is attractive and comfortable, and the light threatens to expose it.

It also shows that judgment is a consequence of people's own choices. God sent the light to save, but those who reject the light condemn themselves by preferring darkness. The judgment is not arbitrary; it reflects the choice people make.

Finally, the verse shows that coming to the light requires honesty. To come to Jesus, people must be willing to have their sins exposed and dealt with. This is painful, but it leads to healing and life. John writes elsewhere, [[1 John 1:7|But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus Christ, his Son, cleanses us from all sin]].

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites believers to examine their own hearts. Are there areas of life they are hiding from God, preferring darkness to light? Coming into the light means confessing sin honestly and allowing God to deal with it.

It also helps believers understand why some people resist the gospel. Sometimes the issue is not intellectual objections but unwillingness to give up sin. Believers can pray for such people and share the good news with patience, trusting God to draw them to the light.

Finally, this verse encourages believers to live transparently. Those who have nothing to hide can live in the light with freedom. Honesty, integrity, and confession allow believers to experience the joy of walking in the light with God and others.

### John 3:30 — He Must Increase, But I Must Decrease

verse: John 3:30

#### Simple Explanation

These words were spoken by John the Baptist near the end of his ministry. John had been baptizing at Aenon near Salim, while Jesus and His disciples were baptizing in the Judean countryside. Some of John's disciples became concerned. They came to John and said, [[v26|Rabbi, he who was with you beyond the Jordan, to whom you have testified, behold, he baptizes, and everyone is coming to him]]. They seemed jealous that Jesus was attracting more followers than John.

John's response showed remarkable humility. He said that a person can receive only what is given from heaven. He reminded his disciples that he had told them he was not the Messiah, but one sent ahead of Him.

Then he used an image from a wedding. The bridegroom is the one who has the bride. The friend of the bridegroom (like a best man) does not compete with the groom; he rejoices to hear the bridegroom's voice. John saw himself as the friend, and Jesus as the bridegroom. In the Old Testament, God is pictured as the husband of His people (Isaiah 62:5; Hosea 2:19–20). John was saying that Jesus is the true bridegroom of God's people. John said, [[v29|Therefore my joy is made full]].

Then he concluded: [[v30|He must increase, but I must decrease]]. The word "must" expresses necessity, part of God's plan. Jesus' influence and recognition would grow, and John's would fade. John was not discouraged by this; he embraced it as God's will and the fulfillment of his purpose.

This is the last time John the Baptist speaks in John's Gospel. He was soon imprisoned and later executed by Herod (Mark 6:14–29).

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse expresses the right attitude for every believer and every servant of God. The goal of ministry and life is not self-promotion but making Jesus known. John found joy not in his own success but in Jesus' increase.

It also shows freedom from envy and competition. John's disciples were jealous, but John was content. He knew his role and was glad to fulfill it. Believers often compare themselves with others and feel threatened by others' success. John's example shows a better way: rejoicing whenever Christ is honored, whoever is used.

Finally, the verse describes a lifelong process. As believers grow in faith, Christ increases in their lives and self-centeredness decreases. Paul writes, [[Galatians 2:20|it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me]]. This is the path of spiritual maturity.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites believers to examine their motives. Are they serving to make Jesus known or to gain recognition for themselves? John's example encourages believers to point others to Christ and to find joy in His glory rather than their own.

It also helps believers deal with envy and competition, especially in ministry. When other believers or churches seem more successful, believers can rejoice that Jesus is being honored, rather than feeling threatened.

Finally, this verse can be a daily prayer: "He must increase, but I must decrease." Believers can ask God to help them let go of pride, self-promotion, and selfishness, so that Christ is seen more clearly in their lives.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

John 3 presents the heart of the gospel. Nicodemus, a respected religious teacher, learned that even he needed to be born anew. Spiritual life is not achieved through religious effort, heritage, or knowledge but is given by God through the Holy Spirit. Everyone, no matter how religious, needs this new birth. Believers can examine whether they have experienced it and can trust the Spirit to keep transforming them.

The chapter reveals how new life comes: through faith in Jesus, who was lifted up on the cross like the bronze serpent in the wilderness. God so loved the world that He gave His only Son, so that whoever believes in Him will not perish but have eternal life. Believers can rest in God's love, proven at the cross, and share this good news with everyone, since "whoever believes" includes all.

The chapter also explains why some reject Jesus: they love darkness rather than light because their deeds are evil. Believers can examine their hearts for areas they are hiding, come into the light through honest confession, and live with transparency and integrity.

Finally, John the Baptist's testimony provides a model of humility. "He must increase, but I must decrease." Believers can find joy in Christ's glory rather than their own, rejoice when others are used by God, and let Christ grow more prominent in their lives day by day.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for teaching that I must be born anew. I cannot make myself new; only Your Spirit can give me new life. Give me a new heart and keep transforming me by Your Spirit.

Thank You that God so loved the world that He gave You, His only Son, so that whoever believes in You will not perish but have eternal life. I believe in You. Thank You for being lifted up on the cross for me. Help me to come into Your light, to confess what I have hidden, and to live honestly before You and others. Like John the Baptist, I pray that You would increase and I would decrease. Free me from pride and envy, and help me to find my joy in making You known. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
