# Acts 3
theme: Healing in the Name of Jesus and the Call to Repent
main: At the temple gate called Beautiful, Peter and John meet a man lame from birth. Having no money to give, Peter heals him in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, and the man enters the temple walking, leaping, and praising God. When a crowd gathers, Peter insists that the healing came not from their own power but from the risen Jesus, whom the people had rejected but God had glorified. He calls them to repent and turn to God, so that their sins may be blotted out and times of refreshing may come.

## Chapter Background and Story

Acts 2 ended with a summary of the early church's life, including the note that "many wonders and signs were done through the apostles" (2:43). Acts 3 now tells one of those signs in detail. The healing of the lame man leads to Peter's second public sermon and, in chapter 4, to the first arrest of the apostles. Luke shows how the same pattern that marked Jesus' ministry, mighty works, bold preaching, and opposition from the authorities, now continues through His followers.

### At the Beautiful Gate (3:1–10)

Peter and John were going up to the temple [[v1|at the hour of prayer, the ninth hour]], about three in the afternoon, when the evening sacrifice was offered and crowds gathered to pray. The first believers were Jewish and continued to worship at the temple, seeing their faith in Jesus as the fulfillment of Israel's hope.

A man [[v2|lame from his mother’s womb]] was carried each day and laid at the gate called Beautiful, probably the great Nicanor Gate, made of polished Corinthian bronze, which led into the inner courts. Luke tells us later that he was over forty years old (4:22). Begging near the temple was a good strategy, since worshipers on their way to pray were often in a generous mood, and giving to the poor was considered an act of righteousness. But the man was stuck at the gate. People with his kind of disability were often excluded from the inner temple courts. He spent his life outside, looking in.

When he saw Peter and John, he asked them for money. Peter looked straight at him and said, [[v4|Look at us]]. The man looked up, [[v5|expecting to receive something from them]]. Peter's response is studied below.

Peter took him by the right hand and raised him up. [[v7|Immediately his feet and his ankle bones received strength]]. Luke, the physician, includes these anatomical details. The man did not need weeks of learning to walk; he jumped up, stood, and walked. Then he [[v8|entered with them into the temple, walking, leaping, and praising God]]. The man who had always sat outside the gate now went inside to worship. Isaiah had prophesied that in the age of God's salvation, [[Isaiah 35:6|the lame man will leap like a deer]].

All the people recognized him as the beggar from the Beautiful Gate, and [[v10|They were filled with wonder and amazement at what had happened to him]].

### Not by Our Own Power (3:11–16)

The man clung to Peter and John, and a crowd ran to them in Solomon's Porch, the long covered colonnade where Jesus had once taught (John 10:23). Peter saw an opportunity and spoke. He immediately redirected attention away from himself: [[v12|why do you marvel at this man? Why do you fasten your eyes on us, as though by our own power or godliness we had made him walk?]]

Peter then pointed them to God: [[v13|The God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, the God of our fathers, has glorified his Servant Jesus]]. The title "Servant" recalls the suffering servant of Isaiah 52:13–53:12, of whom God said, "he will be exalted and lifted up." Peter then confronted the people directly. They had handed Jesus over and denied Him before Pilate, even when Pilate wanted to release Him. They had denied [[v14|the Holy and Righteous One and asked for a murderer to be granted to you]], choosing Barabbas instead of Jesus. Peter's words about the source of the healing are studied below.

### Repent and Turn Again (3:17–26)

Peter softened his tone: [[v17|Now, brothers, I know that you did this in ignorance, as did also your rulers]]. Their guilt was real, but Peter recognized that they had not fully understood what they were doing. Jesus had prayed on the cross, [[Luke 23:34|Father, forgive them, for they don't know what they are doing]]. Their ignorance did not excuse them, but it left room for mercy.

Peter added that God had used even their sin to fulfill His plan: [[v18|the things which God announced by the mouth of all his prophets, that Christ should suffer, he thus fulfilled]]. Then he called them to respond, studied below.

Peter supported his appeal with Scripture. Moses had promised, [[v22|The Lord God will raise up a prophet for you from among your brothers, like me. You shall listen to him in all things whatever he says to you]] (Deuteronomy 18:15). Jesus is that Prophet. Anyone who refused to listen to Him would be cut off from God's people (Deuteronomy 18:19). All the prophets from Samuel onward had spoken of these days.

Peter reminded his listeners of their privilege: [[v25|You are the children of the prophets, and of the covenant which God made with our fathers]]. God had promised Abraham, [[v25|All the families of the earth will be blessed through your offspring]] (Genesis 22:18). That promise was being fulfilled in Jesus. Peter concluded, [[v26|God, having raised up his servant Jesus, sent him to you first to bless you, in turning away every one of you from your wickedness]]. The word "first" hints that the blessing would also go beyond Israel to all the families of the earth. And the blessing itself is described as being turned away from sin.

## Important Verses Explained

### Acts 3:6 — What I Have, That I Give You

verse: Acts 3:6

#### Simple Explanation

A man who had been unable to walk since birth sat every day at the temple gate called Beautiful, asking for money from people going in to pray. He had done this for years. When Peter and John came by, he asked them for a gift, as he asked everyone.

Peter looked at him intently and said, "Look at us." The man looked up, expecting money. Then Peter said, [[v6|I have no silver or gold]]. The apostles were not wealthy. The early church had shared its possessions with those in need, and Peter likely had little money of his own.

[[v6|but what I have, that I give you]]. Peter had something far more valuable than money. He had the authority and power of Jesus.

[[v6|In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, get up and walk!]] To act "in the name" of someone meant to act with that person's authority, as their representative. Peter did not claim power of his own. He spoke in the name of Jesus, the One who had healed the lame during His ministry and who now reigned as the risen Lord. The mention of "Nazareth" connected this healing with the man Jesus whom many in Jerusalem had seen and heard.

Peter did not stop at words. [[v7|He took him by the right hand and raised him up]]. The healing was immediate and complete.

The man had asked for money to survive one more day. He received the ability to walk, a new life, and an encounter with the power of Jesus.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that the church's greatest gift to the world is Jesus. Money and material help are important, and the early church gave generously to those in need. But the deepest need of every person is Christ Himself. The church may not always have silver and gold, but it always has the name of Jesus to share.

It also shows that the power of the early church came from the risen Christ. Peter healed in Jesus' name, just as Jesus had promised that His followers would do works in His name (John 14:12–14). The healing demonstrated that Jesus was alive and active.

Finally, the healing is a sign of God's kingdom. Isaiah had foretold that when God came to save His people, the lame would leap. The man walking and leaping in the temple courts was a living sign that the age of salvation had arrived through Jesus.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers who feel they have little to offer. Many believers do not have wealth, influence, or special talents. But every believer has something to give: the love of Christ, the message of the gospel, prayer, encouragement, and their own presence. What believers have in Christ is always worth sharing.

It also challenges believers to look at people the way Peter did. Many people walked past the lame man every day without truly seeing him. Peter stopped, looked at him, and engaged with him. Believers can learn to notice those who are overlooked and to offer them both practical help and the hope of Christ.

Finally, the verse reminds believers that God sometimes gives more than people ask for. The man asked for money and received healing. Believers can bring their needs to God with confidence, knowing that He is able to do far more than they ask or imagine (Ephesians 3:20).

### Acts 3:15–16 — The Prince of Life

verse: Acts 3:15-16

#### Simple Explanation

After the lame man was healed, a crowd gathered in amazement. Peter quickly made it clear that he and John had not healed the man by their own power or holiness. He pointed instead to Jesus.

He reminded the people of what they had done. They had denied the Holy and Righteous One, asked for a murderer to be released, and [[v15|killed the Prince of life]]. The word translated "Prince" can also mean "Author," "Originator," or "Leader." It describes someone who is the source or pioneer of something. Jesus is the source of life itself. The tragic irony is that people put to death the very One who gives life.

[[v15|whom God raised from the dead, to which we are witnesses]]. Human beings killed Him, but God raised Him. The apostles were eyewitnesses of the risen Christ.

Then Peter explained the healing: [[v16|By faith in his name, his name has made this man strong, whom you see and know]]. It was the name of Jesus, His power and authority, that healed the man. And this power was received "by faith." Peter does not say whose faith; it may have been the faith of the apostles, or of the man, or both.

[[v16|Yes, the faith which is through him has given him this perfect soundness in the presence of you all]]. Notice that even faith comes "through him." Faith is not a human achievement that earns healing; it is itself a gift that comes through Jesus. The healing was complete, "perfect soundness," and public, done "in the presence of you all."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The title "Prince of life" reveals Jesus' identity as the source and Lord of life. John's Gospel says of the Word, [[John 1:4|In him was life]]. The One who gives life could not be held by death. His resurrection proves that He is the Author of life, and He gives life to all who trust in Him.

These verses also show the contrast between human rejection and divine vindication. People killed Jesus; God raised Him. This contrast appears again and again in the apostles' preaching (Acts 2:23–24; 4:10; 5:30). God overturned the world's verdict on Jesus.

Finally, the passage shows that faith and the name of Jesus work together. Faith is not a force in itself; its power lies in its object, Jesus. Even faith is received through Him. This keeps all the glory with Christ.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses remind believers that Jesus is the source of life. Whatever believers need, whether forgiveness, healing, strength, or hope, comes from Him. They can turn to Him as the Author of life in every situation.

They also teach believers to give God the credit for what He does through them. Peter refused to accept praise for the healing. When God works through believers, they are called to point others to Jesus rather than drawing attention to themselves.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to place their faith in Jesus Himself rather than in their own faith. Some people worry that their faith is too weak. The power is not in the strength of faith but in the One in whom faith is placed. Even small faith in a great Savior is enough to receive His help.

### Acts 3:19–21 — Times of Refreshing

verse: Acts 3:19-21

#### Simple Explanation

Peter had told the crowd that they had rejected and killed Jesus, the Prince of life, but that God had raised Him from the dead. He acknowledged that they had acted in ignorance, and that God had used their actions to fulfill what the prophets had foretold about the Messiah's suffering. Now he called them to respond.

[[v19|Repent therefore, and turn again]]. "Repent" means to change one's mind and heart. "Turn again" means to turn back to God. Together, these words describe a complete change of direction, turning away from sin and unbelief and turning toward God through faith in Jesus.

Peter then described what would follow. First, [[v19|that your sins may be blotted out]]. The image is of writing being wiped away from a page. In the ancient world, ink on papyrus could be wiped clean. God would erase their record of sin completely.

Second, [[v19|so that there may come times of refreshing from the presence of the Lord]]. Repentance leads to renewal. God's presence brings refreshment, like cool water to a weary traveler.

Third, [[v20|and that he may send Christ Jesus, who was ordained for you before]]. Peter looked forward to Jesus' return. Jesus had been appointed as the Messiah for Israel, and He would come again.

[[v21|whom heaven must receive until the times of restoration of all things, which God spoke long ago by the mouth of his holy prophets]]. Jesus is now in heaven, reigning at the Father's right hand. He will remain there until the time when God restores everything, as the prophets foretold. This restoration includes the renewal of creation, the end of sin and death, and the fulfillment of all God's promises.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show the full scope of salvation. It begins with personal repentance and forgiveness, continues with times of refreshing through God's presence, and reaches its completion in the restoration of all things at Christ's return. Salvation is personal, present, and future.

They also reveal that God's plan includes the renewal of all creation. Paul writes that creation itself [[Romans 8:21|will be delivered from the bondage of decay into the liberty of the glory of the children of God]]. The prophets foresaw a renewed world where righteousness dwells, and Revelation describes a new heaven and a new earth (Revelation 21:1–5).

Finally, these verses show the urgency and hope of the gospel call. Peter's listeners had rejected the Messiah, yet the door of repentance was open to them. God's offer of forgiveness and refreshment is extended even to those who have resisted Him.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses invite everyone to repent and turn to God. Repentance is not only for people who have committed obvious sins; it is for all who need forgiveness and new life. Believers also continue to repent as God reveals areas of their lives that need to change.

They also promise refreshment. Many people are spiritually weary, burdened by guilt or by the pressures of life. Turning to God brings times of refreshing from His presence. Believers can return to Him regularly in confession, prayer, and worship to find renewal.

Finally, these verses give believers hope for the future. Jesus will return, and God will restore all things. The brokenness of the world is not permanent. Believers can live with hope, working for good now while waiting for the day when God makes everything new.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Acts 3 shows that the risen Jesus is still at work. The healing of the lame man demonstrated that Jesus' power had not ended with His ascension; it continued through His followers. Believers today can trust that Christ is alive and active, and that He can work through ordinary people who act in His name.

The chapter also shows what the church has to offer the world. Peter had no silver or gold, but he had the name of Jesus. Believers may not always have material resources to give, but they always have Christ to share. They can offer both practical care and the hope of the gospel, paying attention to people who are often overlooked.

Acts 3 models humility in ministry. Peter refused to take credit for the healing and pointed the crowd to Jesus. When God uses believers, they are called to give Him the glory, and to remember that even faith comes through Christ.

Finally, the chapter calls everyone to repent and turn to God. Peter's listeners had rejected Jesus, yet they were offered forgiveness, refreshment, and a share in God's future restoration. No one is beyond God's mercy. Believers can respond to this call personally and share it with others, holding out the hope of sins blotted out and of a world one day made new.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, You are the Prince of life, whom God raised from the dead. Thank You that Your power did not end when You ascended, but continues through Your people. Help me to see the people around me who are overlooked, as Peter saw the man at the gate. When I feel that I have little to offer, remind me that I always have You to share. Keep me humble, so that I point others to You and never take the glory for myself. I repent of my sins and turn to You again. Thank You for blotting out my sins and for the times of refreshing that come from Your presence. Fill me with hope for the day when You return and restore all things. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
