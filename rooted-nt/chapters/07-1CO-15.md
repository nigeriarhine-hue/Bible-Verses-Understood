# 1 Corinthians 15
theme: Christ Is Risen: The Gospel and the Hope of the Resurrection
main: Paul reminds the Corinthians of the gospel that saves them: Christ died for our sins, was buried, and was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures, appearing to Peter, the Twelve, more than five hundred believers, James, the apostles, and finally Paul himself. Some in Corinth were denying the resurrection of the dead, so Paul shows that if the dead are not raised, Christ is not raised, faith is empty, and believers are still in their sins. But Christ has indeed been raised as the first fruits of all who belong to Him, and He will reign until every enemy, including death, is destroyed. Paul explains that the resurrection body will be imperishable, glorious, powerful, and spiritual, and that at the last trumpet the dead will be raised and the living changed. Death will be swallowed up in victory, so believers can stand firm, knowing their labor in the Lord is not in vain.

## Chapter Background and Story

1 Corinthians 15 is the longest and fullest treatment of the resurrection in the Bible. Paul writes it because some Corinthian believers were saying that [[v12|there is no resurrection of the dead]]. They probably did not deny that Jesus rose. But influenced by Greek thinking, which often viewed the body as a prison for the soul, they may have found the idea of a bodily resurrection unappealing or absurd. Some may have believed that the "resurrection" was a purely spiritual experience that had already happened (compare 2 Timothy 2:18), or that only the soul would survive death.

Paul responds by showing that the resurrection of believers is inseparable from the resurrection of Christ, and that both are essential to the gospel. The chapter moves from the historical fact of Christ's resurrection (15:1–11), to the consequences of denying the resurrection (15:12–19), to the certainty and order of the resurrection (15:20–28), to practical arguments (15:29–34), to the nature of the resurrection body (15:35–49), and finally to the triumphant victory over death (15:50–58).

### The Gospel of the Risen Christ (15:1–11)

Paul's summary of the gospel, with its list of witnesses, is studied below. Paul then describes his own unworthiness: [[v9|For I am the least of the apostles, who is not worthy to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the assembly of God]]. [[v10|But by the grace of God I am what I am. His grace which was given to me was not futile, but I worked more than all of them; yet not I, but the grace of God which was with me]]. Paul's apostleship, like his salvation, was entirely a gift of grace.

### If Christ Is Not Raised (15:12–19)

Paul draws out the consequences of denying the resurrection. [[v13|But if there is no resurrection of the dead, neither has Christ been raised]]. If the dead cannot be raised, then Christ was not raised either. [[v14|If Christ has not been raised, then our preaching is in vain and your faith also is in vain]]. [[v15|Yes, we are also found false witnesses of God]]. If the resurrection did not happen, the apostles were lying about God. The further consequences, and Paul's triumphant answer, are studied below.

### The Order of the Resurrection (15:20–28)

[[v21|For since death came by man, the resurrection of the dead also came by man]]. [[v22|For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will be made alive]]. As in Romans 5, Paul contrasts Adam and Christ. Adam brought death; Christ brings resurrection life to all who are "in Christ."

[[v23|But each in his own order: Christ the first fruits, then those who are Christ’s at his coming]]. The resurrection happens in stages. Christ was raised first. Believers will be raised when He returns. [[v24|Then the end comes, when he will deliver up the Kingdom to God the Father, when he will have abolished all rule and all authority and power]].

[[v25|For he must reign until he has put all his enemies under his feet]]. Christ is reigning now, and He will continue until every enemy is defeated (Psalm 110:1). [[v26|The last enemy that will be abolished is death]]. Death is an enemy, not a friend. It entered the world through sin, and it will be destroyed.

[[v28|When all things have been subjected to him, then the Son will also himself be subjected to him who subjected all things to him, that God may be all in all]]. This verse does not mean the Son is inferior in nature to the Father; the Son is fully God. It describes the Son's role in God's plan: having completed His saving work, He presents the kingdom to the Father, so that God may be all in all. Christians have long understood this as reflecting the loving order within the Trinity, in which the Son, equal with the Father, delights to glorify Him.

### Living in Light of the Resurrection (15:29–34)

[[v29|Or else what will they do who are baptized for the dead? If the dead aren’t raised at all, why then are they baptized for the dead?]] This is one of the most puzzling verses in the New Testament; scholars have proposed dozens of interpretations. Some think certain Corinthians were being baptized on behalf of believers who had died without baptism; Paul mentions the practice without approving it, simply pointing out that it makes no sense if there is no resurrection. Others think it refers to people who were baptized because of the testimony of believers who had died, hoping to be reunited with them. The Christian church has never adopted a practice of baptism for the dead. Paul's point is clear: the resurrection gives meaning to Christian practice.

[[v30|Why do we also stand in jeopardy every hour?]] [[v31|I die daily]]. Paul faced danger and death every day. [[v32|If I fought with animals at Ephesus for human purposes, what does it profit me?]] Paul may be speaking figuratively of fierce human opponents. [[v32|If the dead are not raised, then “let’s eat and drink, for tomorrow we die.”]] (Isaiah 22:13). Without the resurrection, the only logical approach would be to live for pleasure.

[[v33|Don’t be deceived! “Evil companionships corrupt good morals.”]] Paul quotes a well-known saying from the Greek poet Menander. Those who denied the resurrection were having a corrupting influence. [[v34|Wake up righteously and don’t sin, for some have no knowledge of God. I say this to your shame]].

### The Resurrection Body (15:35–49)

Paul answers the question, [[v35|How are the dead raised?]] and, [[v35|With what kind of body do they come?]] He uses the image of a seed: [[v36|You foolish one, that which you yourself sow is not made alive unless it dies]]. [[v37|That which you sow, you don’t sow the body that will be, but a bare grain]]. A seed is buried and dies, but from it comes a plant that is far more glorious than the seed. There is continuity, since the plant comes from the seed, and transformation, since the plant is very different from the seed.

Paul then notes that God has made many kinds of bodies, earthly and heavenly, each with its own glory. The resurrection body is described below.

[[v45|So also it is written, “The first man Adam became a living soul.” The last Adam became a life-giving spirit]]. Adam received life from God (Genesis 2:7); Christ, the "last Adam," gives life. [[v49|As we have borne the image of those made of dust, let’s also bear the image of the heavenly]]. Believers who now bear Adam's earthly image will one day bear Christ's heavenly image.

### Victory over Death (15:50–58)

[[v50|Now I say this, brothers, that flesh and blood can’t inherit God’s Kingdom; neither does the perishable inherit imperishable]]. Human bodies in their present, mortal state cannot enter God's eternal kingdom. They must be transformed.

[[v51|Behold, I tell you a mystery. We will not all sleep, but we will all be changed]], [[v52|in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet. For the trumpet will sound and the dead will be raised incorruptible, and we will be changed]]. Not all believers will die before Christ returns. But all, whether living or dead, will be transformed. It will happen instantly. Paul describes the same event in 1 Thessalonians 4:16–17. [[v53|For this perishable body must become imperishable, and this mortal must put on immortality]]. Paul's song of victory is studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 Corinthians 15:3–8 — Christ Died, Was Buried, Was Raised, and Appeared

verse: 1 Corinthians 15:3-8

#### Simple Explanation

Paul begins by reminding the Corinthians of the gospel he had preached to them, which they had received, in which they stood, and by which they were saved. Then he summarizes it.

[[v3|For I delivered to you first of all that which I also received]]. "First of all" means "of first importance." This is the heart of the Christian message. Paul did not invent it; he "received" it and "delivered" it, the language of passing on sacred tradition. Many scholars believe verses 3–5 are an early Christian creed that Paul learned within a few years of Jesus' resurrection, perhaps when he visited Peter in Jerusalem (Galatians 1:18).

[[v3|that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures]]. Christ's death was not an accident or a tragedy. He died "for our sins," as a sacrifice to take away sin. And this happened "according to the Scriptures," fulfilling prophecies like Isaiah 53.

[[v4|that he was buried]]. His burial confirmed that He truly died.

[[v4|that he was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures]]. The verb "was raised" is in a tense that emphasizes a lasting result: He was raised and remains alive. This too fulfilled Scripture (Psalm 16:10; Hosea 6:2; and the sign of Jonah, Matthew 12:40).

Then Paul lists witnesses. [[v5|and that he appeared to Cephas, then to the twelve]]. Peter (Cephas) and the Twelve saw the risen Jesus (Luke 24:34; John 20:19–29). [[v6|Then he appeared to over five hundred brothers at once, most of whom remain until now, but some have also fallen asleep]]. More than five hundred people saw Him at the same time. Most were still alive when Paul wrote, about twenty-five years later. Paul was saying, in effect, "You can ask them." [[v7|Then he appeared to James, then to all the apostles]]. James, Jesus' brother, who had not believed during Jesus' ministry, saw the risen Lord and became a leader of the church. [[v8|and last of all, as to the child born at the wrong time, he appeared to me also]]. Paul saw the risen Christ on the road to Damascus. He describes himself as "born at the wrong time," perhaps meaning that he came to faith abnormally, after the other apostles, or that he was like a stillborn child, spiritually dead until Christ gave him life.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses summarize the core of the Christian faith: Christ died for our sins and was raised. Everything else in Christianity depends on these facts. This message was taught from the very beginning of the church.

They also show that the resurrection is a historical event, attested by many witnesses. Christianity does not rest on myths or private visions but on public events that people saw. Luke writes that Jesus [[Acts 1:3|showed himself alive after he suffered, by many proofs]].

Finally, these verses show that the gospel fulfills Scripture. The death and resurrection of Christ were the climax of God's plan revealed throughout the Old Testament.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses give believers a clear summary of the gospel to share with others. Believers can explain the good news simply: Christ died for our sins, was buried, and was raised on the third day, as the Scriptures foretold.

They also give believers confidence in their faith. The resurrection was witnessed by many people. Believers can trust that their faith rests on solid historical ground.

Finally, these verses remind believers that the gospel transforms lives. James, who once doubted, and Paul, who once persecuted, became faithful witnesses after seeing the risen Christ. The same risen Christ can transform anyone today.

### 1 Corinthians 15:17–20 — But Now Christ Has Been Raised

verse: 1 Corinthians 15:17-20

#### Simple Explanation

Some Corinthians were saying there is no resurrection of the dead. Paul shows what would follow if that were true.

[[v17|If Christ has not been raised, your faith is vain; you are still in your sins]]. If Christ was not raised, faith in Him is useless. Believers would still be guilty of their sins, because the resurrection is God's proof that Christ's sacrifice was accepted (Romans 4:25). A dead Savior cannot save.

[[v18|Then they also who are fallen asleep in Christ have perished]]. Believers who had died would have perished, lost forever, with no hope of life beyond the grave.

[[v19|If we have only hoped in Christ in this life, we are of all men most pitiable]]. If Christianity offers hope only for this life, believers are to be pitied more than anyone. They have given up worldly pleasures, faced persecution, and sacrificed much, all for a false hope.

Then Paul turns from this dark possibility to glorious reality: [[v20|But now Christ has been raised from the dead]]. These are among the most triumphant words in the Bible. The resurrection is not a theory or a wish; it is a fact.

[[v20|He became the first fruit of those who are asleep]]. "First fruits" refers to the first portion of the harvest, which was offered to God (Leviticus 23:10–11). The first fruits were a promise that the rest of the harvest was coming. Christ's resurrection is the first fruits; the resurrection of believers is the full harvest that will follow. Interestingly, the Feast of First Fruits fell on the day after the Sabbath following Passover, the very day Jesus rose.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that the resurrection is essential to Christianity. Without it, there is no forgiveness, no hope, and no gospel. Christianity stands or falls on this one event.

They also show that believers' future resurrection is guaranteed by Christ's resurrection. Because Christ was raised as the first fruits, all who belong to Him will follow. Jesus said, [[John 14:19|Because I live, you will live also]].

Finally, these verses show that Christian hope reaches beyond this life. Believers are not merely hoping for a better life now; they are looking forward to resurrection and eternal life. This hope gives meaning to sacrifice and suffering.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses give believers confidence in the face of death. Because Christ was raised, believers who die are not lost. They will be raised as Christ was raised.

They also assure believers that their sins are forgiven. Because Christ was raised, His sacrifice was accepted, and believers are no longer in their sins.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to live with eternal hope. The sacrifices believers make for Christ are not wasted. Their hope is not only for this life but for eternity.

### 1 Corinthians 15:42–44 — Raised Imperishable, Glorious, Powerful

verse: 1 Corinthians 15:42-44

#### Simple Explanation

Someone had asked, [[v35|How are the dead raised?]] and [[v35|With what kind of body do they come?]] Paul had answered with the image of a seed that dies and becomes a plant. Now he describes the resurrection body directly, using four contrasts.

[[v42|So also is the resurrection of the dead. The body is sown perishable; it is raised imperishable]]. The present body is like a seed "sown" in the ground at burial. It is "perishable," subject to sickness, aging, decay, and death. The resurrection body will be "imperishable," never again subject to decay or death.

[[v43|It is sown in dishonor; it is raised in glory]]. The present body, especially in death, is "in dishonor," weak and humbled. The resurrection body will be "in glory," radiant and beautiful.

[[v43|It is sown in weakness; it is raised in power]]. The present body is weak, limited, tired, and vulnerable. The resurrection body will be powerful.

[[v44|It is sown a natural body; it is raised a spiritual body. There is a natural body and there is also a spiritual body]]. The present body is "natural," suited to life in this present age. The resurrection body will be "spiritual." This does not mean it will be non-physical or ghostly. Jesus' resurrection body could be touched and could eat (Luke 24:39–43). "Spiritual" means a body fully empowered and directed by the Holy Spirit, suited to the life of the age to come.

There is continuity between the present body and the resurrection body, just as there is continuity between a seed and a plant. The same person will be raised. But there will also be glorious transformation.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that God's salvation includes the body. Christianity does not teach merely that souls escape to heaven. It teaches that God will raise and transform human bodies. Paul writes that Christ [[Philippians 3:21|will change the body of our humiliation to be conformed to the body of his glory]].

They also show the pattern of Jesus' resurrection. Jesus' risen body was real and physical, yet transformed. The disciples recognized Him, touched Him, and ate with Him, yet He could appear and disappear. Believers' resurrection bodies will be like His.

Finally, these verses bring great hope. Every weakness, illness, disability, and limitation of the present body will be overcome. The resurrection body will be glorious and powerful, free from pain and death.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses bring comfort to those who suffer in their bodies. Believers with illness, disability, chronic pain, or the weaknesses of aging can look forward to resurrection bodies free from all suffering.

They also encourage believers to honor their present bodies. The body matters to God. Believers can care for their bodies and use them for His glory, knowing that God will one day transform them.

Finally, these verses bring hope at funerals. When believers bury loved ones who trusted in Christ, they are planting seeds that will rise in glory. Grief is real, but it is grief filled with hope.

### 1 Corinthians 15:55–58 — Death, Where Is Your Sting?

verse: 1 Corinthians 15:55-58

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had described the moment when, at the last trumpet, the dead will be raised and the living changed, when the perishable will put on the imperishable and the mortal will put on immortality. Then the Scripture will be fulfilled: [[v54|Death is swallowed up in victory]] (Isaiah 25:8).

Paul then taunts death, using words from Hosea 13:14: [[v55|“Death, where is your sting? Hades, where is your victory?”]] Death once seemed all-powerful. But now Paul mocks it, because it has been defeated. A "sting" is like the stinger of a scorpion or bee, which causes pain and death. Death's sting has been removed.

[[v56|The sting of death is sin, and the power of sin is the law]]. What makes death so terrible is sin. Sin brings guilt and judgment, making death the gateway to condemnation. And the Law gives sin its power, because the Law reveals sin and pronounces judgment on it.

[[v57|But thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ]]. But Christ has dealt with sin. He died for sins and rose again, removing death's sting. Through Him, believers have victory over sin, the Law's condemnation, and death. This victory is a gift from God.

Then Paul draws a practical conclusion: [[v58|Therefore, my beloved brothers, be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the Lord’s work, because you know that your labor is not in vain in the Lord]]. Because of the resurrection, believers can stand firm, not shaken by doubt or opposition. They can give themselves fully to the Lord's work. And they can know that nothing done for the Lord is wasted. The resurrection guarantees that their work has eternal value.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that death has been defeated. Death is still an enemy, and believers still die. But for those in Christ, death has lost its sting. It is no longer a gateway to judgment but to eternal life. Jesus said, [[John 11:25|I am the resurrection and the life. He who believes in me will still live, even if he dies]].

They also show that victory is a gift. Believers do not defeat death by their own strength. God gives the victory through Jesus Christ. All the glory belongs to Him.

Finally, these verses show that the resurrection gives meaning to life now. Because believers will be raised, their present work for the Lord matters. Nothing done in His name is wasted.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses bring hope to those facing death. Believers can face their own death, and the death of loved ones who trusted Christ, with confidence that death has been defeated.

They also encourage believers to stand firm. When doubts or difficulties come, believers can remember the resurrection and remain steadfast in their faith.

Finally, these verses encourage believers in their service. Believers who serve faithfully, often without seeing results or recognition, can know that their labor in the Lord is never in vain. Every act of faithfulness matters for eternity.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

1 Corinthians 15 reminds believers of the gospel of first importance: Christ died for our sins, was buried, and was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures. This message was witnessed by many and has been passed down from the beginning. Believers can hold firmly to it, share it clearly, and trust that it rests on solid ground.

The chapter shows that the resurrection is essential to the Christian faith. If Christ was not raised, faith is futile and believers are still in their sins. But Christ has been raised, the first fruits of all who belong to Him. Believers can live with confidence that their sins are forgiven and that those who have died in Christ will be raised.

1 Corinthians 15 describes the glorious resurrection body: imperishable, glorious, powerful, and spiritual. Believers who suffer in their bodies can look forward to complete renewal. Those who grieve can do so with hope, knowing that loved ones who died in Christ are like seeds that will rise in glory.

Finally, the chapter declares that death has been swallowed up in victory through Jesus Christ. Death's sting has been removed. Because of this, believers can be steadfast and immovable, always abounding in the Lord's work, knowing that their labor in the Lord is not in vain.
