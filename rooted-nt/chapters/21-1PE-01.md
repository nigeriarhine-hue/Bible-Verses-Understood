# 1 Peter 1
theme: A Living Hope and a Holy Life
main: Peter greets God's chosen people living as foreigners scattered through Asia Minor, chosen by the Father, set apart by the Spirit, for obedience to Jesus Christ. He praises God, who in His great mercy has given believers new birth into a living hope through Jesus' resurrection, and into an inheritance that can never perish, spoil, or fade, kept in heaven for them while they are guarded by God's power through faith. Though they suffer grief in various trials, these trials prove their faith genuine, more precious than gold refined by fire. Though they have not seen Jesus, they love Him and rejoice with inexpressible joy. The prophets searched carefully to understand this salvation, and even angels long to look into it. So believers are to prepare their minds, set their hope fully on the grace coming at Christ's revelation, and be holy in all their conduct, because God is holy. They should live in reverent fear as foreigners, knowing they were redeemed not with silver or gold but with the precious blood of Christ, the spotless Lamb. Having been purified and born again through the living and enduring word of God, they are to love one another deeply from the heart.

## Chapter Background and Story

Peter writes to believers who felt like outsiders. Many of them had been Gentiles who once took part in the idolatry and immorality of their culture. When they turned to Christ, everything changed. They stopped joining in pagan festivals and immoral practices, and their neighbors were surprised and offended (1 Peter 4:3–4). They were slandered, mocked, and treated with suspicion. Some may have lost jobs, friendships, or family relationships.

Peter calls them "foreigners" or "exiles" (v1, 17). This is not mainly a description of their geographical location but of their spiritual identity. They belong to God's kingdom, so they no longer fully belong to the world around them. Like Israel in exile, they live in a land that is not their true home.

Peter begins not with instructions about suffering but with praise. Before he tells them how to live, he reminds them of who they are and what they have: a living hope, an imperishable inheritance, a salvation the prophets longed to see, and a precious redemption. Hope is the foundation for holiness and endurance.

### Greeting (1:1–2)

[[v1|Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ, to the chosen ones who are living as foreigners in the Dispersion in Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia]]. These were Roman provinces in Asia Minor, modern-day Turkey. The believers are "chosen," God's own people, and yet "living as foreigners," not fully at home in the world.

[[v2|according to the foreknowledge of God the Father, in sanctification of the Spirit, that you may obey Jesus Christ and be sprinkled with his blood: Grace to you and peace be multiplied]]. Notice the work of the whole Trinity. The Father chose them according to His foreknowledge. The Spirit set them apart. And they were chosen for obedience to Jesus Christ and for cleansing by His blood. "Sprinkled with his blood" recalls the ceremony at Mount Sinai, where Moses sprinkled the people with blood to seal the covenant (Exodus 24:7–8). Believers belong to the new covenant established by Christ's blood.

### A Living Hope (1:3–5)

Peter's praise for the living hope is studied below. [[v5|who by the power of God are guarded through faith for a salvation ready to be revealed in the last time]]. Not only is the inheritance kept safe for believers; believers are kept safe for the inheritance. God's power guards them, like a military garrison protecting a city. Their faith is the means through which God's protection works.

### Joy in Trials (1:6–9)

Peter's teaching on trials and joy is studied below. [[v8|whom, not having known, you love. In him, though now you don’t see him, yet believing, you rejoice greatly with joy that is unspeakable and full of glory]], [[v9|receiving the result of your faith, the salvation of your souls]]. Peter had seen Jesus with his own eyes; his readers had not. Yet they loved Him and trusted Him, and they experienced a joy too deep for words. Jesus said to Thomas, [[John 20:29|Blessed are those who have not seen, and have believed]].

### The Prophets and the Angels (1:10–12)

[[v10|Concerning this salvation, the prophets sought and searched diligently. They prophesied of the grace that would come to you]], [[v11|searching for who or what kind of time the Spirit of Christ which was in them pointed to when he predicted the sufferings of Christ and the glories that would follow them]]. The Old Testament prophets spoke of the Messiah's suffering and glory (Isaiah 53; Psalm 22), but they did not fully understand when or how it would happen. Notice that "the Spirit of Christ" was in the prophets, showing Christ's existence before His birth.

[[v12|To them it was revealed that they served not themselves, but you, in these things, which now have been announced to you through those who preached the Good News to you by the Holy Spirit sent out from heaven; which things angels desire to look into]]. The prophets were serving future generations, including these believers. And the salvation now revealed is so wonderful that even angels long to look into it, like someone bending down to peer intently at something. Believers have received what prophets longed for and angels marvel at.

### Called to Be Holy (1:13–21)

[[v13|Therefore prepare your minds for action. Be sober, and set your hope fully on the grace that will be brought to you at the revelation of Jesus Christ—]]. "Prepare your minds for action" is literally "gird up the loins of your mind," an image of tucking up a long robe into a belt so one can move quickly. Believers are to be mentally alert, self-controlled, and focused on hope.

[[v14|as children of obedience, not conforming yourselves according to your former lusts as in your ignorance]]. Before they knew Christ, they lived according to their desires. Now, as obedient children of God, they are not to be shaped by those old patterns. The call to holiness is studied below.

[[v17|If you call on him as Father, who without respect of persons judges according to each man’s work, pass the time of your living as foreigners here in reverent fear]]. Believers call God "Father," a term of intimate relationship. But He is also an impartial Judge. Believers live their lives as temporary residents in this world with reverence for Him. The precious redemption that motivates this reverence is studied below.

[[v20|who was foreknown indeed before the foundation of the world, but was revealed in this last age for your sake]], [[v21|who through him are believers in God, who raised him from the dead and gave him glory, so that your faith and hope might be in God]]. Christ's sacrifice was planned before creation and revealed in history. Because God raised Him and glorified Him, believers' faith and hope rest securely in God.

The call to love and the enduring word are studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 Peter 1:3–4 — Born Again to a Living Hope

verse: 1 Peter 1:3-4

#### Simple Explanation

Peter begins with a burst of praise. "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ." To "bless" God means to praise and thank Him.

"Who according to his great mercy." The reason for praise is God's mercy, His compassion toward those who do not deserve it. It is "great" mercy, abundant and overflowing.

"Caused us to be born again." God has given believers a new birth, a new beginning, a new life. This echoes Jesus' words to Nicodemus: [[John 3:7|You must all be born anew]].

"To a living hope." The result of new birth is hope, but not a vague or uncertain hope. It is a "living" hope, active, vibrant, and sure, because it is connected to a living Savior.

"Through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead." The foundation of this hope is the resurrection. Because Jesus rose from the dead, believers have a sure hope of life beyond death.

Verse 4: "to an incorruptible and undefiled inheritance that doesn’t fade away, reserved in Heaven for you." Believers also have an inheritance. It is "incorruptible," it cannot decay or be destroyed. It is "undefiled," pure and untouched by evil. It "doesn't fade away," never losing its beauty. And it is "reserved in Heaven," kept safe by God for His people.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

For Peter, this hope was deeply personal. After Jesus' crucifixion, Peter's hope had died. He had denied Jesus and watched Him die. But on the third day, everything changed. The risen Jesus appeared to Peter (1 Corinthians 15:5) and restored him. Peter knew from experience what it means to be given a living hope through the resurrection.

The inheritance contrasts with earthly inheritances. In the Old Testament, Israel's inheritance was the land of Canaan, which could be lost through war, exile, or disobedience. Earthly wealth can be stolen, lost, or destroyed. Believers' inheritance is in heaven, beyond the reach of decay, corruption, or loss. Jesus told His followers to store up treasures in heaven, where moth and rust do not destroy (Matthew 6:20).

For readers who had lost status, friendships, or possessions because of their faith, this hope was precious. Whatever they lost on earth, their true inheritance was secure.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can begin each day, like Peter, by praising God for His mercy and the hope He has given them. Gratitude for what God has done shapes how they face the challenges ahead.

When believers face loss, disappointment, or uncertainty, they can remember that they have a living hope rooted in Christ's resurrection and an inheritance that can never be taken away.

This hope also gives believers courage to live differently from the world around them. Their security does not depend on the approval of others or the stability of earthly circumstances.

### 1 Peter 1:6–7 — Faith Refined Like Gold

verse: 1 Peter 1:6-7

#### Simple Explanation

"In this you greatly rejoice." "This" refers to the living hope and inheritance just described. Believers have great reason for joy.

"Though now for a little while, if need be, you have been grieved in various trials." At the same time, they are experiencing grief because of trials. Peter does not deny the pain. But he puts it in perspective: it is "for a little while," brief compared with eternity. "If need be" suggests that trials are not random; God allows them for a purpose.

Verse 7: "that the proof of your faith, which is more precious than gold that perishes, even though it is tested by fire." In the ancient world, gold was refined by heating it in fire, which melted away impurities and left pure gold. Trials are like that fire. They test faith and purify it. Genuine faith is more precious than gold, because gold eventually perishes, but faith lasts forever.

"May be found to result in praise, glory, and honor at the revelation of Jesus Christ." When Christ returns, tested and proven faith will result in praise, glory, and honor, both for God and, by His grace, for believers.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage holds together joy and grief. Believers can "greatly rejoice" even while being "grieved." Christian joy does not deny sorrow; it exists alongside it, rooted in hope.

The image of refining fire appears throughout Scripture. Job said, [[Job 23:10|When he has tried me, I will come out like gold]]. Malachi spoke of the Lord as a refiner's fire who purifies His people (Malachi 3:2–3). Trials reveal what is genuine and strengthen it.

This passage is similar to James 1:2–4, which also speaks of joy in trials because they produce endurance. Both apostles teach that God uses hardship to strengthen faith. This does not mean God causes all suffering, or that suffering is good in itself. Much suffering comes from human sin or a broken world. But God can use even painful experiences to refine His people.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

When believers face trials, they can hold onto both grief and hope. They can honestly acknowledge their pain while trusting that God is at work.

Believers can also see their trials as temporary. Compared with eternity, even long hardships are "for a little while." This perspective can bring endurance.

Finally, believers can trust that their tested faith is precious to God. Every time they continue to trust Him through difficulty, their faith is being refined, and it will result in praise, glory, and honor when Christ returns.

### 1 Peter 1:15–16 — Be Holy, for I Am Holy

verse: 1 Peter 1:15-16

#### Simple Explanation

"But just as he who called you is holy, you yourselves also be holy in all of your behavior." God, who called believers to Himself, is holy: completely pure, set apart, without any sin or evil. Believers are to reflect His holiness in "all" of their behavior, every area of life.

Verse 16: "because it is written, 'You shall be holy, for I am holy.'" Peter quotes Leviticus, where God repeatedly commanded Israel to be holy because He is holy (Leviticus 11:44–45; 19:2; 20:7). The standard of holiness is God Himself.

"Holy" means set apart for God. It includes moral purity, but it is more than avoiding sin. It means belonging to God and reflecting His character in every part of life.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

In Leviticus, God's call to holiness was given to Israel, whom He had redeemed from Egypt and brought into covenant with Himself. Peter applies it to believers, whom God has redeemed through Christ. The pattern is the same: God first redeems, then calls His people to live as His own.

Holiness flows from relationship. Believers are not holy in order to become God's people; they are called to be holy because they already are His people. Like children who resemble their father, believers are to reflect the character of their heavenly Father.

The phrase "in all of your behavior" shows that holiness is not limited to religious activities. It touches work, relationships, speech, entertainment, finances, and private thoughts. Every part of life belongs to God.

Holiness is not achieved by human effort alone. The Spirit sanctifies believers (1 Peter 1:2), and God's grace trains them to live godly lives (Titus 2:11–12). Believers cooperate with God's work, but He is the one who makes them holy.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can ask whether there are areas of their lives that they have not surrendered to God. Holiness invites them to bring every area under His lordship.

Holiness may make believers different from the world around them, as it did for Peter's readers. This difference is not about being self-righteous or judgmental, but about reflecting God's beautiful character.

Finally, believers can pursue holiness with hope, not fear. God, who calls them to be holy, is also the one who makes them holy by His Spirit.

### 1 Peter 1:22–23 — Love One Another from the Heart

verse: 1 Peter 1:22-23

#### Simple Explanation

"Seeing you have purified your souls in your obedience to the truth through the Spirit." When believers responded to the gospel in faith and obedience, their souls were cleansed. This cleansing came through the truth of the gospel and the work of the Spirit.

"In sincere brotherly affection." The purpose of this cleansing is genuine love for fellow believers. "Sincere" means without hypocrisy. "Brotherly affection" is the love that family members share.

"Love one another from the heart fervently." "Fervently" means earnestly, intensely, with effort, like stretching out to reach something. Believers are to love one another deeply and wholeheartedly, not superficially.

Verse 23: "having been born again, not of corruptible seed, but of incorruptible, through the word of God, which lives and remains forever." Believers have been born again, given new life, not through human, perishable seed, but through God's imperishable word. This new birth makes them family, brothers and sisters who share the same spiritual life.

Verses 24–25 quote Isaiah 40:6–8: "All flesh is like grass... but the Lord's word endures forever." Human life and glory fade like grass, but God's word lasts forever. The gospel preached to the believers is that eternal word.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses connect new birth with love. Because believers have been born into God's family through His word, they are family. Family members are called to love one another deeply. Jesus said that His disciples would be known by their love for one another (John 13:35).

The contrast between perishable and imperishable seed shows the difference between natural and spiritual life. Natural life comes from human parents and ends in death. Spiritual life comes from God's word and lasts forever. James 1:18 also describes God giving believers new birth through "the word of truth."

The emphasis on God's enduring word is important for believers facing pressure. The world's opinions, values, and powers are like grass that withers. God's word remains. Believers can build their lives on it.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can examine whether their love for fellow believers is sincere and earnest. Is it genuine, or merely polite? Do they make an effort to love those who are difficult, different, or in need?

Loving "from the heart fervently" might mean investing time in relationships, forgiving offenses, supporting those who are struggling, and showing hospitality.

Believers can also anchor their lives in God's enduring word. In a world of shifting opinions and fading glory, Scripture remains a firm foundation. Reading, studying, and obeying it nourishes the new life God has given.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

First Peter 1 begins with praise. God, in His great mercy, has given believers new birth into a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus, and into an inheritance that can never perish, spoil, or fade. They are guarded by God's power through faith. For those who feel like outsiders in their culture or who have lost much for their faith, this hope is a secure anchor.

The chapter honestly acknowledges that believers face trials that bring grief. But these trials are temporary, and God uses them to refine faith, which is more precious than gold. Believers can rejoice even in sorrow, loving and trusting the Jesus they have not yet seen, and they can marvel that they have received a salvation the prophets searched for and angels long to look into.

Because of this hope, believers are called to holiness. They are to prepare their minds, set their hope fully on the grace coming at Christ's return, and be holy in all their conduct, because God is holy. They live as foreigners in this world, with reverence for the Father who judges impartially, remembering that they were redeemed not with silver or gold but with the precious blood of Christ, the spotless Lamb.

Finally, having been born again through God's living and enduring word, believers are to love one another deeply from the heart. Human glory fades like grass, but God's word lasts forever. Believers can build their lives, their hope, and their love on that unshakable foundation.

## Chapter Prayer

Blessed be You, God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ. Thank You that in Your great mercy You caused me to be born again to a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus from the dead. Thank You for an inheritance that can never perish, spoil, or fade, kept in heaven for me, and for guarding me by Your power through faith. When I face trials, help me to trust that You are refining my faith like gold. Though I have not seen Jesus, I love Him, and I rejoice in Him. Help me to set my hope fully on the grace that will come when He is revealed. Make me holy in all my conduct, as You are holy. Thank You that I was redeemed not with silver or gold but with the precious blood of Christ. Help me to love my brothers and sisters deeply from the heart, and to build my life on Your word, which endures forever. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
