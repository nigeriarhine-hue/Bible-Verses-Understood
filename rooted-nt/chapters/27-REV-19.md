# Revelation 19
theme: The Wedding of the Lamb and the Return of the King
main: After the fall of Babylon, John hears what sounds like a great multitude in heaven shouting, Hallelujah! Salvation, glory, and power belong to our God, for His judgments are true and just; He has judged the great prostitute who corrupted the earth and has avenged the blood of His servants. The twenty-four elders and four living creatures fall down and worship, and a voice from the throne calls all God's servants, small and great, to praise Him. Then the great multitude, sounding like rushing waters and loud thunder, cries out that the Lord God Almighty reigns, and that the wedding of the Lamb has come and His bride has made herself ready, clothed in fine linen, bright and clean, which stands for the righteous acts of God's holy people. The angel tells John to write that those invited to the wedding supper of the Lamb are blessed. When John falls at the angel's feet to worship him, the angel stops him, saying that he is a fellow servant and that John must worship God. Then heaven opens, and John sees a white horse whose rider is called Faithful and True, who judges and wages war with justice. His eyes are like blazing fire, He wears many crowns, and He has a name no one knows but He Himself. He is dressed in a robe dipped in blood, and His name is the Word of God. The armies of heaven, dressed in fine white linen, follow Him on white horses. From His mouth comes a sharp sword to strike down the nations; He will rule them with an iron scepter and tread the winepress of God's wrath. On His robe and thigh is written: King of kings and Lord of lords. An angel standing in the sun calls the birds to the great supper of God. The beast, the kings of the earth, and their armies gather to make war against the rider and His army, but the beast and the false prophet are captured and thrown alive into the lake of fire, and the rest are killed by the sword that comes from the rider's mouth.

## Chapter Background and Story

Revelation 19 is a turning point. After the long descriptions of judgment on Babylon and the beast's kingdom, heaven erupts in praise. Then the moment the whole book has been anticipating arrives: Christ returns in glory to defeat His enemies and establish His reign.

The chapter has two main parts. The first is a scene of worship in heaven (vv1–10), celebrating God's judgment on Babylon and announcing the wedding of the Lamb. The second is the vision of Christ's return as the rider on the white horse (vv11–21), defeating the beast, the false prophet, and their armies.

The chapter presents two contrasting feasts. The first is the wedding supper of the Lamb, a feast of joy for those who belong to Him. The second is "the great supper of God," a grim image of judgment on those who opposed Him. Every person will be present at one of these two feasts.

### Hallelujah! (19:1–5)

[[v1|After these things I heard something like a loud voice of a great multitude in heaven, saying, “Hallelujah! Salvation, power, and glory belong to our God;]] [[v2|for his judgments are true and righteous. For he has judged the great prostitute who corrupted the earth with her sexual immorality, and he has avenged the blood of his servants at her hand.”]] "Hallelujah" is a Hebrew word meaning "Praise the LORD." This is the only chapter in the New Testament where it appears, and it appears four times here (vv1, 3, 4, 6). It recalls the "Hallelujah Psalms" (Psalms 146–150) and the psalms that celebrated God's victory over His enemies (Psalms 113–118).

The multitude praises God for two reasons. His judgments are "true and righteous," and He has "avenged the blood of his servants." The call of heaven to rejoice over Babylon (18:20) is now answered. The cry of the martyrs, "How long?" (6:10), has finally been answered in full.

[[v3|A second said, “Hallelujah! Her smoke goes up forever and ever.”]] Babylon's destruction is final. The smoke rising forever echoes Isaiah 34:10, describing the lasting ruin of Edom. Babylon will never rise again.

[[v4|The twenty-four elders and the four living creatures fell down and worshiped God who sits on the throne, saying, “Amen! Hallelujah!”]] This is the last appearance of the elders and the living creatures in Revelation. They began the worship of heaven in chapter 4, and now they affirm God's final victory.

[[v5|A voice came from the throne, saying, “Give praise to our God, all you his servants, you who fear him, the small and the great!”]] All of God's people, regardless of status, are invited to join the praise. "The small and the great" includes everyone, from the humblest believer to the most prominent.

### The Defeat of the Beast (19:17–21)

[[v17|I saw an angel standing in the sun. He cried with a loud voice, saying to all the birds that fly in the sky, “Come! Be gathered together to the great supper of God,]] [[v18|that you may eat the flesh of kings, the flesh of captains, the flesh of mighty men, and the flesh of horses and of those who sit on them, and the flesh of all men, both free and slave, small and great.”]] This grim image comes from Ezekiel 39:17–20, where God invites birds and beasts to a feast on the fallen armies of Gog. In the ancient world, being left unburied and eaten by birds was the ultimate disgrace. The "great supper of God" stands in stark contrast to the "wedding supper of the Lamb." Those who refused the invitation to the Lamb's wedding feast become the feast for the birds. The image is deliberately shocking, underscoring the seriousness of rejecting God.

[[v19|I saw the beast, the kings of the earth, and their armies, gathered together to make war against him who sat on the horse and against his army]]. This is the gathering described in 16:14–16, the battle of Armageddon. The forces of evil assemble for their final stand against Christ.

[[v20|The beast was taken, and with him the false prophet who worked the signs in his sight, with which he deceived those who had received the mark of the beast and those who worshiped his image. These two were thrown alive into the lake of fire that burns with sulfur]]. Remarkably, there is no description of a battle. The beast and the false prophet are simply captured. The great confrontation that the world's armies prepared for is over before it begins. The lake of fire, mentioned here for the first time, is the place of final judgment (20:10, 14–15).

[[v21|The rest were killed with the sword of him who sat on the horse, the sword which came out of his mouth. So all the birds were filled with their flesh]]. Christ defeats His enemies with the sword from His mouth, His word. Interpreters differ on how literally to understand this scene. Some expect a literal battle at Christ's return. Many understand the sword from His mouth as showing that Christ's victory is won not by physical weapons but by the power of His word, the same word that spoke creation into being and will judge the world. In either case, the victory belongs entirely to Christ, and it is complete.

## Important Verses Explained

### Revelation 19:6–8 — The Wedding of the Lamb Has Come

verse: Revelation 19:6-8

#### Simple Explanation

John hears a great sound: "something like the voice of a great multitude, and like the voice of many waters, and like the voice of mighty thunders." The praise of heaven is overwhelming in its volume and joy.

The multitude cries: "Hallelujah! For the Lord our God, the Almighty, reigns!" God's reign is now fully revealed. Every rival power has fallen.

Verse 7 continues: "Let’s rejoice and be exceedingly glad, and let’s give the glory to him. For the wedding of the Lamb has come, and his wife has made herself ready." The long-awaited wedding is at hand. The bride is the people of God, the church.

Verse 8 describes her clothing: "It was given to her that she would array herself in bright, pure, fine linen, for the fine linen is the righteous acts of the saints." Her wedding garment represents the righteous deeds of God's people.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The image of God's people as His bride runs throughout Scripture. In the Old Testament, God called Israel His bride and Himself her husband (Isaiah 54:5; 62:5; Hosea 2:19–20). In the New Testament, Jesus called Himself the bridegroom (Mark 2:19–20), and Paul described the church as the bride of Christ, whom He loved and gave Himself for (Ephesians 5:25–27; 2 Corinthians 11:2). The wedding of the Lamb is the fulfillment of this great love story: the complete and joyful union of Christ and His people.

The bride contrasts sharply with the prostitute of chapters 17–18. The prostitute was dressed in purple, scarlet, gold, and jewels, the gaudy luxury of the world. The bride is dressed in simple, bright, pure linen. The prostitute's beauty was a cover for corruption. The bride's beauty is genuine purity.

Notice the balance in verse 8. The bride "has made herself ready," and yet her clothing "was given to her." Her righteousness is both a gift and a response. Believers are made righteous through Christ, as a gift of grace. And their lives, transformed by grace, produce righteous deeds. These deeds do not earn salvation, but they are the beautiful evidence of God's work in them (Ephesians 2:8–10).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage gives believers a glorious hope. The story of history ends not in tragedy but in a wedding celebration. Believers will be united with Christ in perfect love and joy forever.

This hope can shape how believers live now. Like a bride preparing for her wedding, believers can prepare for Christ's return by growing in holiness and love. Every act of faithfulness, kindness, and righteousness is part of their wedding garment.

This passage also reminds believers that Christ loves His church deeply. Despite the church's failures and weaknesses, Christ will present her as a radiant bride. Believers can love the church as Christ does, working for her purity and unity.

### Revelation 19:9–10 — Invited to the Wedding Supper

verse: Revelation 19:9-10

#### Simple Explanation

The angel tells John: "Write, ‘Blessed are those who are invited to the wedding supper of the Lamb.’" This is the fourth blessing in Revelation. Those invited to the wedding celebration are truly blessed. Then the angel adds: "These are true words of God."

John is so overwhelmed that he falls at the angel's feet to worship him. But the angel immediately stops him: "Look! Don’t do it! I am a fellow bondservant with you and with your brothers who hold the testimony of Jesus. Worship God."

The angel explains: "for the testimony of Jesus is the Spirit of Prophecy." True prophecy points to Jesus.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Jesus often described the kingdom of God as a great feast or wedding banquet (Matthew 8:11; 22:1–14; Luke 14:15–24). At the Last Supper, He told His disciples that He would not drink wine again until He drank it new with them in His Father's kingdom (Matthew 26:29). The wedding supper of the Lamb is that promised celebration.

The invitation is the key. In Jesus' parables, many were invited to the feast, but some refused (Matthew 22:1–10). The blessing belongs to those who accept the invitation. The gospel is God's invitation to the wedding supper, and it is offered freely to all.

John's mistake in worshiping the angel is a powerful lesson. Even an apostle could be tempted to give worship to a glorious messenger rather than to God. The angel firmly refuses. Angels, prophets, and church leaders are all fellow servants. Only God is worthy of worship. This also guards against any temptation to venerate angels in place of God (Colossians 2:18).

The phrase "the testimony of Jesus is the Spirit of Prophecy" has been understood in two ways. It may mean that the testimony about Jesus is the essence of all prophecy; all true prophecy points to Him. Or it may mean that those who bear witness to Jesus are speaking by the Spirit of prophecy. Both are true. Jesus is the center of God's revelation.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can rejoice that they are invited to the wedding supper of the Lamb. This is not an invitation they earned, but a gift of grace. They can respond with gratitude and joy.

This passage also encourages believers to extend the invitation to others. The gospel is an invitation to a feast. Believers can share it with warmth and joy, inviting others to come.

The angel's refusal of worship reminds believers to keep their worship focused on God alone. It is possible to admire spiritual leaders, gifted teachers, or spiritual experiences in a way that takes the place of God. Believers are called to honor others as fellow servants, while giving worship to God alone.

Finally, believers can remember that all true prophecy and spiritual teaching points to Jesus. They can evaluate any message by whether it exalts Christ and bears witness to Him.

### Revelation 19:11–16 — Faithful and True, King of Kings

verse: Revelation 19:11-16

#### Simple Explanation

John sees "heaven opened," and there is "a white horse, and he who sat on it is called Faithful and True." This is Christ returning in glory. "In righteousness he judges and makes war." His judgment and warfare are always just.

Verse 12 describes Him: "His eyes are a flame of fire," seeing all things clearly. "On his head are many crowns," showing His supreme authority. And "He has names written and a name written which no one knows but he himself," pointing to the mystery of His divine nature.

In verse 13, "He is clothed in a garment sprinkled with blood. His name is called ‘The Word of God.’"

Verse 14 describes His army: "The armies which are in heaven, clothed in white, pure, fine linen, followed him on white horses."

Verse 15 describes His power: "Out of his mouth proceeds a sharp, double-edged sword that with it he should strike the nations. He will rule them with an iron rod. He treads the wine press of the fierceness of the wrath of God, the Almighty."

Verse 16 gives His title: "He has on his garment and on his thigh a name written, ‘KING OF KINGS AND LORD OF LORDS.’"

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This is the climactic vision of Christ in Revelation. The Lamb who was slain now comes as the conquering King. Many interpreters see this as the description of Christ's second coming, the return that Jesus promised (Matthew 24:30; Acts 1:11). Some idealist interpreters understand it more broadly as a symbolic portrayal of Christ's victory over evil, but most interpreters across traditions see here the visible, personal return of Christ at the end of the age.

His names reveal His identity. "Faithful and True" contrasts with the deceptive beast and false prophet. "The Word of God" connects with John 1:1, where Jesus is the eternal Word who was with God and was God. "KING OF KINGS AND LORD OF LORDS" declares His supreme authority over every earthly ruler. The hidden name suggests that there is more to Christ than any human can fully understand.

The robe "sprinkled with blood" has been understood in two ways. Some see it as the blood of His enemies, drawing on Isaiah 63:1–3, where the divine warrior's garments are stained from treading the winepress. Others note that the robe is bloodstained before the battle begins, and see it as His own blood, shed on the cross. On this view, the King comes as the Lamb who was slain; His victory is grounded in His sacrifice. Both meanings may be present: the One who shed His blood for His people now comes to judge those who rejected Him.

The armies of heaven are dressed in white linen, the same clothing as the bride (19:8). They may be angels, the redeemed, or both (compare 17:14). Notably, they carry no weapons and do not fight. Christ alone wins the victory with the sword of His mouth, His word.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage gives believers great confidence. The same Jesus who came in humility, who was crucified and rose again, will return in glory. Every injustice will be judged, every enemy defeated, and every promise fulfilled.

The name "Faithful and True" invites believers to trust Christ completely. In a world full of broken promises and lies, He is utterly reliable. Everything He has said will come to pass.

The fact that Christ wins by the sword of His mouth reminds believers that God's word is powerful. They can trust the power of His word to accomplish His purposes and can rely on it in their own lives.

Finally, the title "KING OF KINGS AND LORD OF LORDS" calls believers to give Him their full allegiance. No earthly ruler, nation, or cause deserves the loyalty that belongs to Him.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Revelation 19 is a chapter of celebration and victory. Heaven rejoices over God's just judgment on Babylon and proclaims that the Lord God Almighty reigns. Believers can join this praise now, worshiping God for His justice, His power, and His faithfulness.

The chapter announces the wedding of the Lamb. The story of history ends in a celebration of love, as Christ and His people are united forever. Believers can live with joyful hope, preparing themselves like a bride by growing in holiness and righteous living, knowing that their wedding garment is ultimately a gift of grace.

The blessing on those invited to the wedding supper reminds believers that the gospel is an invitation. They can respond with gratitude and share the invitation with others. At the same time, the angel's refusal of worship calls believers to keep their worship centered on God alone.

The vision of the rider on the white horse gives believers confidence in Christ's return. The Lamb who was slain will return as King of kings and Lord of lords. He is Faithful and True, and He will defeat every enemy by the power of His word. The beast and the false prophet, who seemed so powerful, will be captured without a fight.

The two suppers in this chapter, the wedding supper of the Lamb and the great supper of God, present a solemn choice. Believers are called to accept God's invitation, to live in readiness for Christ's return, and to share His invitation with urgency and love.

Hallelujah! Salvation, glory, and power belong to You, Lord God Almighty, for Your judgments are true and righteous. Thank You that You reign, and that every power that opposes You will fall. Lord Jesus, thank You that You love Your church as a bridegroom loves his bride, and that You are preparing us for the wedding supper of the Lamb. Clothe me in Your righteousness, and help me to live a life of faithful, righteous deeds that honor You. Thank You for the invitation to Your feast; help me to share it joyfully with others. Keep my worship centered on You alone. I look forward to the day when You return, Faithful and True, King of kings and Lord of lords. Come, Lord Jesus. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
