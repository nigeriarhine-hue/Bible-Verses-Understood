# Romans 5
theme: Peace with God, Hope That Does Not Disappoint, and Grace That Reigns
main: Having been justified by faith, believers have peace with God and stand in His grace, rejoicing in the hope of His glory. They can even rejoice in suffering, because God uses it to produce endurance, character, and hope, a hope that will not disappoint because God's love has been poured into their hearts by the Spirit. God proved His love by sending Christ to die for us while we were still sinners and enemies, and if He reconciled us through Christ's death, He will surely save us through His life. Paul then contrasts Adam and Christ: through one man's disobedience sin and death spread to all, but through one man's obedience grace abounds, and where sin increased, grace overflowed even more.

## Chapter Background and Story

Romans 5 marks a transition in Paul's letter. In chapters 1–4, he explained how sinners are justified by faith. Now he begins to describe the results of justification: peace, hope, love, and the reign of grace. Chapters 5–8 explore the new life that believers have in Christ, ending with the triumphant declaration that nothing can separate them from God's love (8:38–39).

The second half of chapter 5 contains one of the most important theological passages in the Bible, comparing Adam, the first man, with Christ. Paul shows that just as Adam's sin affected all humanity, Christ's obedience brings life to all who belong to Him.

### The Results of Justification (5:1–11)

Paul begins with a great "therefore." Because believers have been justified by faith, as he has explained in chapters 3–4, they now experience peace with God, studied below. Their hope, even in suffering, is also studied below.

Paul then describes the love of God displayed in Christ's death. [[v6|For while we were yet weak, at the right time Christ died for the ungodly]]. "Weak" means helpless, unable to save ourselves. "At the right time" means at the moment God had appointed in history (Galatians 4:4). Paul then highlights the astonishing nature of this love, studied below.

[[v9|Much more then, being now justified by his blood, we will be saved from God’s wrath through him]]. If God has already justified believers through Christ's death, He will certainly save them from the coming judgment. [[v10|For if while we were enemies, we were reconciled to God through the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, we will be saved by his life]]. Paul reasons from the greater to the lesser: if God reconciled His enemies through Christ's death, how much more will He save His friends through Christ's risen life. Believers' security rests on the living Christ, who continues to intercede for them (Hebrews 7:25).

[[v11|Not only so, but we also rejoice in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom we have now received the reconciliation]]. The deepest joy is not only in God's gifts but in God Himself.

### Adam and Christ (5:12–21)

Paul now compares Adam and Christ. [[v12|Therefore, as sin entered into the world through one man, and death through sin, so death passed to all men because all sinned]]. Sin entered the world through Adam's disobedience in the Garden of Eden (Genesis 3). Death followed as its consequence. And death spread to all people, because all sinned.

Christians have understood the relationship between Adam's sin and humanity's sin in different ways. Some emphasize that Adam acted as the representative of the whole human race, so that his guilt is counted to all. Others emphasize that Adam's sin introduced a corrupted nature that all people inherit, so that all inevitably sin. Others emphasize that all people follow Adam's pattern by sinning themselves. Many see truth in more than one of these perspectives. All agree that sin and death came through Adam and affect every human being.

Paul notes that sin existed even before the Law of Moses was given, since death reigned from Adam to Moses. Adam is [[v14|a foreshadowing of him who was to come]]. Adam was a "type" or pattern of Christ: just as one man's action affected all humanity, so another man's action would affect all who belong to Him.

But the comparison also involves contrast. [[v15|But the free gift isn’t like the trespass]]. Grace is far greater than sin. [[v15|For if by the trespass of the one the many died, much more did the grace of God and the gift by the grace of the one man, Jesus Christ, abound to the many]]. [[v17|For if by the trespass of the one, death reigned through the one; so much more will those who receive the abundance of grace and of the gift of righteousness reign in life through the one, Jesus Christ]]. Notice that believers will not only escape death; they will "reign in life." Paul's summary of the comparison is studied below.

[[v20|The law came in that the trespass might abound; but where sin abounded, grace abounded more exceedingly]]. When the Law was given, it made sin more visible and showed how serious it was. But no matter how great sin became, God's grace was greater still. The image is of a flood: sin rose high, but grace overflowed far above it.

[[v21|that as sin reigned in death, even so grace might reign through righteousness to eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord]]. Two kings are contrasted: sin, which reigned and brought death, and grace, which reigns and brings eternal life. Through Christ, grace has taken the throne.

## Important Verses Explained

### Romans 5:1–2 — Peace with God

verse: Romans 5:1-2

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had spent the first four chapters of Romans explaining how sinners are justified, declared righteous before God, through faith in Jesus Christ. Now he begins to describe what justification means for believers' lives.

[[v1|Being therefore justified by faith]]. This summarizes everything Paul has taught. Believers have been declared righteous. This is a settled fact, not something that might happen in the future.

[[v1|we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ]]. The first result of justification is peace with God. Before justification, people are at war with God. Paul later says that we were "enemies" (5:10). Sin made people rebels against God and placed them under His judgment. But through Christ, the war is over. God has made peace. This is not merely a feeling of peace, but a real change in relationship.

[[v2|through whom we also have our access by faith into this grace in which we stand]]. Through Christ, believers have "access" to God's grace. The word was used of being brought into the presence of a king. Believers can come into God's presence freely. And they "stand" in grace. Grace is not a place they visit occasionally; it is where they live, their firm and secure position.

[[v2|We rejoice in hope of the glory of God]]. Believers look forward with joy to sharing in God's glory. In Romans 3:23, Paul said that all have fallen short of the glory of God. Now believers can rejoice because they will one day share in that glory. "Hope" in the Bible is not wishful thinking; it is confident expectation based on God's promises.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that justification changes a person's relationship with God. Peace with God is the foundation of every other blessing. Paul writes, [[Ephesians 2:14|For he is our peace]]. Christ Himself is the peace between God and humanity.

They also show the security of believers. They "stand" in grace. Their position does not depend on their feelings or their performance on a given day. They are firmly established in God's favor through Christ.

Finally, these verses show that the Christian life looks forward. Believers have peace now, stand in grace now, and look forward to glory in the future. The best is yet to come.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses bring assurance to believers who doubt whether God accepts them. Because they have been justified by faith, they have peace with God. The war is over. They can approach God with confidence, not fear.

They also invite believers to live in grace. Believers do not need to earn their place before God each day. They stand in grace. This frees them to serve God out of love, not out of anxiety.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to rejoice in hope. Whatever difficulties believers face now, they can look forward to sharing in God's glory. This hope can bring joy even in hard times.

### Romans 5:3–5 — Suffering Produces Hope

verse: Romans 5:3-5

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had just said that believers rejoice in hope of the glory of God. Then he added something surprising: [[v3|Not only this, but we also rejoice in our sufferings]]. Believers can rejoice not only in their future hope but even in their present sufferings.

This does not mean that believers enjoy pain or pretend that suffering is pleasant. It means they can rejoice because of what God does through suffering. Paul explains: [[v3|knowing that suffering produces perseverance]]. Suffering, when faced with faith, produces "perseverance," the ability to endure and keep going.

[[v4|and perseverance, proven character; and proven character, hope]]. Perseverance produces "proven character," a character that has been tested and found genuine, like metal refined by fire. And proven character produces "hope." Each step leads to the next, like a chain: suffering, perseverance, character, hope.

[[v5|and hope doesn’t disappoint us]]. This hope will not let believers down. It will not prove to be empty. The word can also mean "does not put us to shame." Believers will not be embarrassed for having trusted God.

[[v5|because God’s love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit who was given to us]]. The reason believers can be sure of their hope is that they have experienced God's love. The Holy Spirit has "poured" God's love into their hearts, an image of abundance, like water poured out generously. This is the first mention of the Holy Spirit in Romans since chapter 1. Believers know they are loved by God because His Spirit assures them of it.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that God uses suffering for good in the lives of believers. James writes something similar: [[James 1:2|Count it all joy, my brothers, when you fall into various temptations]], [[James 1:3|knowing that the testing of your faith produces endurance]]. Suffering is not meaningless; God uses it to shape believers into the image of Christ.

They also show that Christian hope is grounded in God's love. Hope is not based on positive thinking or favorable circumstances. It is based on the love of God, which the Spirit makes real in believers' hearts. Because God loves them, believers can be sure He will fulfill His promises.

Finally, these verses show the work of the Holy Spirit in the believer's inner life. The Spirit does not only give gifts or power; He fills believers' hearts with the experience of God's love. Paul later says that the Spirit testifies with believers' spirits that they are God's children (Romans 8:16).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses help believers find meaning in suffering. When believers face illness, loss, disappointment, or hardship, they can trust that God is at work, producing endurance, character, and hope. This does not make suffering easy, but it gives it purpose. It also does not mean that believers should stay in situations of abuse or harm; God's purposes in suffering never require accepting mistreatment.

They also encourage believers to persevere. Endurance is built over time. Believers who keep trusting God through difficulties will find their character strengthened and their hope deepened.

Finally, these verses invite believers to rest in God's love. When believers feel discouraged or doubtful, they can remember that God's love has been poured into their hearts by the Holy Spirit. They can ask the Spirit to renew their awareness of God's love.

### Romans 5:8 — While We Were Yet Sinners

verse: Romans 5:8

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been explaining the hope believers have because of God's love. Now he shows how God proved that love.

He first said that Christ died for the ungodly at the right time, while we were weak and helpless (5:6). Then he made a comparison: [[v7|For one will hardly die for a righteous man. Yet perhaps for a good person someone would even dare to die]]. It is rare for anyone to give their life for another person, even for someone who is upright. Perhaps someone might be willing to die for a person who is especially good or kind to them. But this is unusual.

[[v8|But God commends his own love toward us, in that while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us]]. God's love is completely different. Christ did not die for good people. He died for "sinners," people who had rebelled against God and did not deserve His love. "Commends" means proves, demonstrates, or shows clearly. The cross is the clearest proof of God's love.

Notice the timing: "while we were yet sinners." God did not wait for people to improve before sending Christ. He acted while they were still in their sin. His love was not a response to their goodness; it came first.

Notice also that Paul says "God commends his own love." The death of Christ demonstrates God's love. This shows the unity of the Father and the Son. The cross is not the Son persuading an unwilling Father to love; it is the Father's love expressed through the Son's sacrifice.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse reveals the unconditional nature of God's love. Human love is often based on the worth or attractiveness of the one loved. God's love is based on His own character. John writes, [[1 John 4:19|We love him, because he first loved us]].

It also shows the substitutionary nature of Christ's death. Christ died "for us," in our place and for our benefit. He took the penalty we deserved.

Finally, the verse provides the ultimate assurance of God's love. When believers doubt that God loves them, they can look at the cross. God has already proven His love in the most costly way possible.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse brings comfort to anyone who feels unlovable. God's love is not based on how good people are. He loved sinners enough to send His Son to die for them. No one is beyond the reach of His love.

It also challenges believers to love others as God loves. If God loved people while they were still sinners, believers are called to love others even when they do not deserve it, including enemies and those who are difficult to love.

Finally, the verse encourages believers to share the good news of God's love. Many people believe they must earn God's love. The gospel declares that God has already demonstrated His love through Christ's death. This is the message the world needs to hear.

### Romans 5:18–19 — One Act of Righteousness

verse: Romans 5:18-19

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been comparing Adam, the first man, with Jesus Christ. Through Adam's sin, sin and death entered the world and spread to all people. But through Christ, grace and life have come. Now Paul summarizes the comparison.

[[v18|So then as through one trespass, all men were condemned]]. Adam's single act of disobedience, eating the forbidden fruit in the Garden, brought condemnation to all humanity. All people share in the consequences of Adam's sin.

[[v18|even so through one act of righteousness, all men were justified to life]]. Christ's "one act of righteousness," His obedient life culminating in His death on the cross, brings justification and life. Just as Adam's act affected all who are connected to him, Christ's act affects all who are connected to Him.

[[v19|For as through the one man’s disobedience many were made sinners, even so through the obedience of the one, many will be made righteous]]. Adam's disobedience made many sinners. Christ's obedience makes many righteous. Christ succeeded where Adam failed. Adam disobeyed in a garden of plenty; Christ obeyed in the garden of Gethsemane and on the cross.

The word "all" in verse 18 has been understood in different ways. Most Christians understand that Christ's act makes justification available to all, or that it brings life to all who are "in Christ," just as Adam's act brought condemnation to all who are "in Adam." Verse 17 speaks of "those who receive the abundance of grace," showing that the gift must be received. Paul uses "all" to emphasize the universal scope of Christ's work, which includes both Jews and Gentiles.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses teach that humanity is connected to either Adam or Christ. Paul writes elsewhere, [[1 Corinthians 15:22|For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will be made alive]]. Every person is born connected to Adam. Through faith, believers become connected to Christ.

They also show the importance of Christ's obedience. Christ did not only die for sins; He lived a perfectly obedient life. His righteousness is credited to believers. They are "made righteous" through His obedience.

Finally, these verses show the greatness of Christ's work. One act of Adam brought disaster to the whole human race. One act of Christ undid that disaster and brought something even greater: not merely a return to Eden, but eternal life and reigning with Christ.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses help believers understand their identity. Believers are no longer defined by Adam's failure but by Christ's success. They are "in Christ," declared righteous through His obedience.

They also give believers hope in the face of sin and death. Adam brought sin and death, but Christ has overcome both. Believers can live with confidence that sin and death do not have the final word.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to share the gospel. All people are affected by Adam's sin, and Christ's work is offered to all. Believers can invite others to receive the abundance of grace and the gift of righteousness through Christ.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Romans 5 describes the blessings of justification. Believers have peace with God, access to His grace, and confident hope of sharing His glory. They do not need to live in fear of God's judgment or wonder whether He accepts them. The war is over, and they stand firmly in His grace.

The chapter also transforms how believers view suffering. Suffering, faced with faith, produces endurance, character, and hope, a hope that will not disappoint because God's love has been poured into believers' hearts by the Holy Spirit. Believers can find meaning and even joy in hardship, trusting God to use it for their good, while never mistaking God's purposes for permission for anyone to harm them.

Romans 5 proclaims the greatness of God's love. Christ died for us while we were still sinners, helpless and even enemies of God. This love is the foundation of believers' assurance. If God reconciled them while they were enemies, He will certainly save them now that they are His children.

Finally, the chapter shows that grace is greater than sin. Adam's disobedience brought sin and death to all, but Christ's obedience brings righteousness and life. Where sin increased, grace overflowed. Believers can rejoice that grace now reigns, and they can live with confidence that no sin is too great for God's grace to overcome.

## Chapter Prayer

Gracious God, thank You that I have been justified by faith and have peace with You through my Lord Jesus Christ. Thank You for bringing me into Your grace, where I now stand, and for the hope of sharing Your glory. When I face suffering, help me to trust that You are producing endurance, character, and hope in me. Pour Your love into my heart afresh by Your Holy Spirit. Thank You for proving Your love by sending Christ to die for me while I was still a sinner. Lord Jesus, thank You for Your perfect obedience, through which I am made righteous. I rejoice that where sin increased, Your grace overflowed even more. Let grace reign in my life, leading me to eternal life. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
