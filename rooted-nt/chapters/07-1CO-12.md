# 1 Corinthians 12
theme: One Spirit, Many Gifts, One Body
main: Paul begins answering the Corinthians' questions about spiritual gifts by establishing that genuine Spirit-led speech confesses "Jesus is Lord." There are many kinds of gifts, services, and workings, but the same Spirit, Lord, and God gives them all, and each is given for the common good. Using the picture of a human body, Paul shows that the church is one body with many members, all baptized by one Spirit. No member should feel it does not belong because it is different, and no member should look down on another as unnecessary. God gives special honor to the parts that seem weaker, so that members care for one another, suffering and rejoicing together. Not everyone has the same gift, and Paul points them toward a still more excellent way.

## Chapter Background and Story

1 Corinthians 12–14 addresses another question the Corinthians had written about: spiritual gifts. The Corinthian church was richly gifted (1:7), but the gifts had become a source of pride and division. Some believers apparently valued the more spectacular gifts, especially speaking in tongues, above others. Those with these gifts may have considered themselves more spiritual, while those without them may have felt inferior.

Paul's answer has three parts. In chapter 12, he teaches that all gifts come from the same Spirit and serve the one body. In chapter 13, he shows that love is greater than any gift. In chapter 14, he gives practical instructions for using gifts, especially prophecy and tongues, in orderly worship that builds up the church.

Christians today hold different views about some of the gifts Paul lists. Some, often called cessationists, believe that certain gifts, such as prophecy, tongues, and miraculous healing, were given mainly to establish the church in the apostolic age and are no longer given in the same way. Others, often called continuationists, including Pentecostal and charismatic Christians, believe that all the gifts continue today. Many hold views in between. All agree that the Spirit gives gifts to every believer for the good of the church, and that love must govern their use.

### Varieties of Gifts, One Source (12:4–11)

Paul's teaching on the unity behind the variety is studied below. He then lists examples of gifts. [[v8|For to one is given through the Spirit the word of wisdom, and to another the word of knowledge according to the same Spirit]]. A "word of wisdom" may be a Spirit-given insight into how to apply God's truth; a "word of knowledge" may be Spirit-given understanding of God's truth or of a situation. [[v9|to another faith by the same Spirit, and to another gifts of healings by the same Spirit]]. "Faith" here is not saving faith, which all believers have, but a special gift of trust in God for extraordinary things. [[v10|and to another workings of miracles, and to another prophecy, and to another discerning of spirits, to another different kinds of languages, and to another the interpretation of languages]]. "Prophecy" is speaking a message from God for the church's encouragement and building up (14:3). "Discerning of spirits" is the ability to tell whether a message or influence comes from God or from another source. "Different kinds of languages," often called "tongues," is speaking in languages one has not learned, and "interpretation" is the ability to explain the meaning.

[[v11|But the one and the same Spirit produces all of these, distributing to each one separately as he desires]]. The Spirit decides who receives which gift. No one can boast about their gift, because it was given, not earned.

### One Body, Many Members (12:12–26)

[[v12|For as the body is one and has many members, and all the members of the body, being many, are one body; so also is Christ]]. The church is the body of Christ. [[v13|For in one Spirit we were all baptized into one body, whether Jews or Greeks, whether bond or free; and were all given to drink into one Spirit]]. Every believer has been baptized by the Spirit into the one body, regardless of ethnicity or social status. All have been given the Spirit to drink, like water that refreshes and gives life.

Paul then imagines parts of the body talking. [[v15|If the foot would say, “Because I’m not the hand, I’m not part of the body,” it is not therefore not part of the body]]. Some believers felt that because they did not have certain gifts, they did not belong. Paul says they do. [[v17|If the whole body were an eye, where would the hearing be?]] A body needs different parts. [[v18|But now God has set the members, each one of them, in the body, just as he desired]]. God has arranged the body according to His wisdom.

Paul's teaching about the weaker members and mutual care is studied below.

### You Are the Body of Christ (12:27–31)

Paul's declaration is studied below with verse 26. He then lists roles in the church: [[v28|God has set some in the assembly: first apostles, second prophets, third teachers, then miracle workers, then gifts of healings, helps, governments, and various kinds of languages]]. "Helps" may refer to acts of assistance and service; "governments" to administration or guidance. Notice that tongues appears last. The Corinthians may have placed it first, but Paul does not.

[[v29|Are all apostles? Are all prophets? Are all teachers? Are all miracle workers?]] [[v30|Do all have gifts of healings? Do all speak with various languages? Do all interpret?]] The expected answer to each question is no. Not every believer has every gift, and no single gift is required of all.

[[v31|But earnestly desire the best gifts. Moreover, I show a most excellent way to you]]. The "best gifts" are those that most build up the church, such as prophecy (14:1, 5). But even greater than seeking gifts is the "most excellent way," the way of love, which Paul describes in chapter 13.

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 Corinthians 12:3 — "Jesus Is Lord" by the Holy Spirit

verse: 1 Corinthians 12:3

#### Simple Explanation

Paul begins his teaching on spiritual gifts by reminding the Corinthians of their past: [[v2|You know that when you were heathen, you were led away to those mute idols, however you might be led]]. Before they knew Christ, many of the Corinthians had worshiped idols. Pagan worship sometimes included ecstatic experiences, where worshipers felt carried away by spiritual forces. The Corinthians knew what it was like to be "led" by spiritual powers. Not every spiritual experience comes from God.

So how can believers tell whether something comes from the Holy Spirit? Paul gives a basic test: [[v3|Therefore I make known to you that no man speaking by God’s Spirit says, “Jesus is accursed.”]] The Holy Spirit would never lead anyone to curse Jesus. Any message that dishonors Christ is not from God's Spirit. Some interpreters think that someone in Corinth may have actually spoken against Jesus while claiming to be inspired, perhaps distinguishing between the earthly Jesus and a spiritual Christ.

[[v3|No one can say, “Jesus is Lord,” but by the Holy Spirit]]. Anyone can say the words "Jesus is Lord," but no one can truly confess Jesus as Lord, with genuine faith and submission, except by the work of the Holy Spirit. "Jesus is Lord" was the earliest Christian confession (Romans 10:9). It meant acknowledging Jesus as the divine Lord and giving Him one's allegiance, in a world where Caesar demanded to be called lord.

This verse sets the foundation for the whole discussion of gifts. The most important evidence of the Spirit's work is not spectacular gifts, but the confession that Jesus is Lord. Every believer who truly confesses Jesus as Lord has the Holy Spirit.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that the Holy Spirit always glorifies Jesus. Jesus said of the Spirit, [[John 16:14|He will glorify me]]. The Spirit's work is centered on Christ. Any spiritual experience, gift, or teaching that draws attention away from Jesus or contradicts His lordship is not from the Spirit.

It also shows that faith itself is the work of the Spirit. No one can truly confess Jesus as Lord by human effort alone. Every believer's faith is evidence of the Spirit's work in their life.

Finally, the verse shows that all believers are equal in the most important sense. Every believer who confesses Jesus as Lord does so by the Spirit. There are no first-class and second-class Christians. Some may have more visible gifts, but all have the Spirit.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse helps believers evaluate spiritual experiences and teachings. Believers can ask: Does this honor Jesus as Lord? Does it agree with what Scripture teaches about Him? If not, it does not come from the Holy Spirit.

It also encourages believers who feel spiritually inferior. Every believer who truly confesses Jesus as Lord has the Holy Spirit. Spectacular gifts are not the measure of spirituality.

Finally, the verse invites believers to confess Jesus as Lord in every area of their lives. Confessing Jesus as Lord is not only a statement of belief; it is a commitment to follow Him in everything.

### 1 Corinthians 12:4–7 — Different Gifts, the Same Spirit, for the Common Good

verse: 1 Corinthians 12:4-7

#### Simple Explanation

The Corinthian church had many spiritual gifts, but they had become a source of competition and pride. Some believers valued certain gifts above others and looked down on those who did not have them. Paul explains the true nature of gifts.

[[v4|Now there are various kinds of gifts, but the same Spirit]]. The gifts are different, but they all come from the same Holy Spirit. The word "gifts" (charismata) is related to the word "grace" (charis). Gifts are expressions of God's grace.

[[v5|There are various kinds of service, and the same Lord]]. Gifts are also "service." They are not for showing off but for serving others. And all service is done for the same Lord, Jesus Christ.

[[v6|There are various kinds of workings, but the same God who works all things in all]]. Gifts are also "workings," expressions of God's power at work. And it is the same God who works in all believers.

Notice the threefold pattern: the same Spirit, the same Lord, the same God. Paul connects the gifts to the Holy Spirit, the Lord Jesus, and God the Father. The unity of the Triune God is the foundation for the unity of the church. The variety of gifts reflects the richness of God; the unity of the source reflects the oneness of God.

[[v7|But to each one is given the manifestation of the Spirit for the profit of all]]. Two important truths. First, "to each one." Every believer has been given some gift, some way the Spirit shows Himself through them. No believer is left out. Second, "for the profit of all." Gifts are given for the common good, to benefit the whole church, not for personal glory.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that the diversity of gifts reflects the nature of God. Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are distinct Persons yet one God. The church, with its many members and gifts, reflects this unity in diversity.

They also show that every believer has a role. Peter writes, [[1 Peter 4:10|As each has received a gift, employ it in serving one another]]. No believer is meant to be a spectator in the church.

Finally, these verses show that gifts are for others. The purpose of every gift is the common good. Gifts used for self-promotion or to make others feel inferior are being misused.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage every believer to recognize that they have a gift. Believers can ask God to show them how He has gifted them and look for ways to serve others.

They also encourage believers to use their gifts for the good of others. Believers can ask not "How does this gift make me look?" but "How can this gift help others?"

Finally, these verses encourage believers to value different gifts. A church needs many kinds of gifts. Believers can appreciate and honor gifts that are different from their own.

### 1 Corinthians 12:21–22 — The Weaker Members Are Necessary

verse: 1 Corinthians 12:21-22

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been using the image of a human body to describe the church. He had addressed believers who felt they did not belong because they were different. Now he addresses the opposite problem: believers who thought others were unnecessary.

[[v21|The eye can’t tell the hand, “I have no need for you,” or again the head to the feet, “I have no need for you.”]] If an eye said to the hand, "I don't need you," it would forget that without hands, the eye could see things but never pick them up. If the head said to the feet, "I don't need you," it would forget that without feet, the head could never go anywhere. Every part of the body needs the others.

In the church, some believers with prominent gifts may have looked down on others, thinking they did not need them. Paul says this is foolish. Every member is needed.

[[v22|No, much rather, those members of the body which seem to be weaker are necessary]]. Paul goes further. The parts of the body that "seem" weaker are actually essential. Think of the heart, lungs, or brain. They are hidden and delicate, yet absolutely necessary for life.

Paul continues: [[v23|Those parts of the body which we think to be less honorable, on those we bestow more abundant honor]]. People take special care of the parts of the body they consider less presentable, clothing them with modesty. [[v24|But God composed the body together, giving more abundant honor to the inferior part]]. God has arranged the church so that those who seem less important receive special honor.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that every believer is valuable. No one in the church is unnecessary. Paul has already said that God chose the weak and lowly (1:27–28). God gives special honor to those the world overlooks.

They also show the danger of pride in the church. Believers with prominent gifts or positions can be tempted to look down on others. Paul's words humble them: they need those they might consider less important.

Finally, these verses show God's heart for the weak. Throughout Scripture, God cares for the poor, the widow, the orphan, and the stranger. In the church, those who seem weak are given special honor.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses challenge believers to value every member of the church. Believers can ask whether they have overlooked or undervalued certain people, such as the elderly, children, people with disabilities, the poor, or those with less visible gifts.

They also encourage believers who feel weak or insignificant. They are necessary to the body of Christ. Their presence, prayers, and service matter.

Finally, these verses encourage churches to give special care and honor to those who are often overlooked. Churches can make sure that all members are included, valued, and given opportunities to serve.

### 1 Corinthians 12:26–27 — Suffering and Rejoicing Together

verse: 1 Corinthians 12:26-27

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had explained that God arranged the body so that there would be no division, but that members would have the same care for one another. Now he describes what this care looks like.

[[v26|When one member suffers, all the members suffer with it]]. In a human body, when one part is injured, the whole body feels it. A stubbed toe can make the whole body wince. In the church, when one believer suffers, the whole community should share in that suffering. Believers are not isolated individuals; they are connected.

[[v26|When one member is honored, all the members rejoice with it]]. Likewise, when one member is honored or blessed, the whole body rejoices. There is no room for jealousy. One believer's joy is everyone's joy.

[[v27|Now you are the body of Christ, and members individually]]. Paul states the truth plainly. The church is not merely like a body; it is the body of Christ. Christ is present in the world through His church. And each believer is a member, an essential part of this body.

This was a powerful truth for the divided Corinthian church. Instead of competing over gifts, they were called to care for one another, suffering and rejoicing together, because they were one body.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show the deep connection between believers. Paul writes, [[Romans 12:15|Rejoice with those who rejoice. Weep with those who weep]]. Christian community is not a gathering of individuals but a body united by the Spirit.

They also show the dignity of the church. The church is the body of Christ, the visible presence of Christ in the world. Jesus identified so closely with His church that He said to Saul, "Why are you persecuting me?" (Acts 9:4).

Finally, these verses show that each believer has a place. "Members individually" means that every believer is a distinct and valued part of the body. No one is lost in the crowd.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to share one another's sorrows. When a fellow believer is sick, grieving, or struggling, believers can show care through prayer, visits, meals, and practical help.

They also encourage believers to rejoice in one another's blessings. When a fellow believer is honored, succeeds, or experiences joy, believers can celebrate with them, free from jealousy.

Finally, these verses remind believers of the persecuted church. Believers around the world suffer for their faith. Because the church is one body, believers everywhere can share their suffering through prayer and support.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

1 Corinthians 12 teaches that the most important evidence of the Holy Spirit is the confession that Jesus is Lord. Every believer who truly confesses Jesus has the Spirit. Believers can evaluate spiritual experiences and teachings by asking whether they honor Christ, and they can reject any idea that some believers are second-class because they lack certain gifts.

The chapter also teaches that there are many different gifts, but they all come from the same Spirit, Lord, and God, and every believer has been given a gift for the common good. Believers can discover and use their gifts to serve others, honoring the gifts of others rather than competing with them. Though Christians differ on whether certain gifts continue today, all can agree that God equips every believer to serve.

1 Corinthians 12 describes the church as one body with many members. No member should feel that they do not belong because they are different, and no member should look down on others as unnecessary. God gives special honor to those who seem weaker. Churches can make sure that every member, especially those often overlooked, is valued, included, and cared for.

Finally, the chapter calls believers to suffer and rejoice together. When one member suffers, all suffer; when one is honored, all rejoice. Believers are the body of Christ, and each is a member. They can care for one another deeply, and they can seek the most excellent way, the way of love that Paul describes in the next chapter.

## Chapter Prayer

Holy Spirit, thank You that I can confess Jesus as Lord only by Your work in me. Thank You for the gifts You give to each believer for the good of all. Show me how You have gifted me, and help me to use my gifts to serve others, not to draw attention to myself. Thank You for placing me in the body of Christ. When I feel that I do not belong, remind me that You have set me where I am. When I am tempted to look down on others, remind me that I need them. Help me to honor those who seem weaker and to care for every member of Your church. Teach me to suffer with those who suffer and rejoice with those who rejoice. Unite Your church in love, and show us the most excellent way. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
