# John 16
theme: Sorrow Turned to Joy and Peace in a Troubled World
main: Jesus warns His disciples that they will face persecution, but He tells them that His going away is for their good, because He will send the Counselor. The Spirit will convict the world about sin, righteousness, and judgment, and will guide the disciples into all truth, glorifying Jesus. Their grief at His death will be like a woman's labor pains, turning into a joy no one can take away. Jesus closes His farewell teaching with a promise: in the world they will have trouble, but they can take heart, because He has overcome the world.

## Chapter Background and Story

John 16 completes Jesus' farewell teaching before His great prayer in chapter 17. The disciples were filled with sorrow (16:6). Jesus had told them He was leaving, that the world would hate them, and that one of them would betray Him. Now He explains why His departure is necessary, what the Holy Spirit will do, and how their sorrow will become joy. The chapter ends with a frank prediction that the disciples will abandon Him, followed by one of the most comforting promises in the Bible.

### Warning of Persecution (16:1–4)

Jesus said, [[v1|I have said these things to you so that you wouldn’t be caused to stumble]]. He did not want His disciples to be caught off guard and lose their faith when hard times came. He spoke plainly: [[v2|They will put you out of the synagogues. Yes, the time is coming that whoever kills you will think that he offers service to God]].

This prophecy was fulfilled in the early church. Stephen was stoned by religious leaders (Acts 7), and Saul of Tarsus persecuted Christians, convinced he was serving God (Acts 26:9–11; Galatians 1:13–14). Religious zeal without true knowledge of God can become cruel. Jesus explained, [[v3|They will do these things because they have not known the Father nor me]].

Jesus had not told them these things at the beginning because He was with them and protected them. Now that He was leaving, they needed to be prepared.

### The Advantage of Jesus' Departure (16:5–15)

The disciples were so overwhelmed with sorrow that they did not even ask where He was going. Jesus told them a surprising truth: His departure was for their benefit, because then He would send the Counselor. This is studied below, together with the Spirit's work of convicting the world.

Jesus added, [[v12|I still have many things to tell you, but you can’t bear them now]]. The disciples could not yet understand everything. The Spirit would complete their understanding, guiding them into all truth. This is also studied below.

[[v15|All things that the Father has are mine]]. The Father, the Son, and the Spirit work together. The Spirit takes what belongs to the Son, which is also the Father's, and makes it known to believers.

### A Little While (16:16–24)

Jesus said, [[v16|A little while, and you will not see me. Again a little while, and you will see me]]. The disciples were confused and whispered among themselves, [[v18|What is this that he says, ‘A little while’? We don’t know what he is saying]]. Jesus was referring to His death, when they would not see Him, and His resurrection, when they would see Him again. These events would happen within days.

Jesus explained with the image of childbirth, studied below. He also promised a new relationship in prayer: [[v23|whatever you may ask of the Father in my name, he will give it to you]]. [[v24|Until now, you have asked nothing in my name. Ask, and you will receive, that your joy may be made full]]. After the resurrection, the disciples would pray to the Father in Jesus' name, on the basis of His finished work.

### The Father Himself Loves You (16:25–33)

Jesus said that He had been speaking in figures of speech, but the time was coming when He would speak plainly about the Father. Then He said something tender: [[v26|I don’t say to you that I will pray to the Father for you]], [[v27|for the Father himself loves you, because you have loved me, and have believed that I came from God]]. Jesus does intercede for His people (Romans 8:34; Hebrews 7:25), but He wanted His disciples to know that they did not need to persuade a reluctant Father. The Father Himself loves them.

Jesus summarized His mission in one sentence: [[v28|I came from the Father and have come into the world. Again, I leave the world and go to the Father]].

The disciples said that now they understood: [[v30|By this we believe that you came from God]]. Jesus answered with a gentle but sobering question: [[v31|Do you now believe?]] He told them that very soon they would be scattered, each going his own way, and would leave Him alone. [[v32|Yet I am not alone, because the Father is with me]]. Jesus knew His disciples' faith was real but weak. He did not reject them; He prepared them. His final words in this section are studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### John 16:7–8 — It Is to Your Advantage That I Go

verse: John 16:7-8

#### Simple Explanation

The disciples were filled with sorrow. Jesus had been their teacher, friend, and Lord for three years. They could not imagine life without Him. Now He was telling them He was going away.

Jesus said something that must have seemed impossible: [[v7|Nevertheless I tell you the truth: It is to your advantage that I go away]]. How could it be better for them that Jesus would leave? He explained: [[v7|for if I don’t go away, the Counselor won’t come to you. But if I go, I will send him to you]].

While Jesus was on earth in His physical body, He could be in only one place at a time. Through the Holy Spirit, His presence would be with all His followers everywhere, at all times. The Spirit would live within each believer. This was a greater gift than having Jesus physically beside them.

Then Jesus described what the Spirit would do in the world: [[v8|When he has come, he will convict the world about sin, about righteousness, and about judgment]]. The word "convict" means to expose, to prove wrong, or to bring to the point of recognizing guilt. It is the work of a prosecutor who shows the truth of a case.

Jesus explained each part. The Spirit would convict the world [[v9|about sin, because they don’t believe in me]]. The deepest sin is rejecting Jesus. [[v10|about righteousness, because I am going to my Father]]. The world judged Jesus as a criminal, but His resurrection and return to the Father would prove that He was righteous. [[v11|about judgment, because the prince of this world has been judged]]. At the cross, Satan was defeated, and his judgment was sealed.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal the Spirit's essential role in salvation. No one comes to faith simply by human persuasion. The Spirit opens people's eyes to their sin, to Christ's righteousness, and to the reality of judgment. On the day of Pentecost, when Peter preached, the listeners [[Acts 2:37|were cut to the heart]]. That was the Spirit's convicting work.

They also show how the cross and resurrection reverse the world's verdict. The world declared Jesus guilty; God declared Him righteous by raising Him. The world thought it had defeated Him; in reality, the ruler of the world was defeated. The Spirit brings people to see this truth.

Finally, these verses offer comfort to believers. The Spirit's presence is an advantage greater than Jesus' physical presence. Believers are not living in a lesser age, longing for the days when Jesus walked the earth. They have the Spirit of Christ within them.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers in their witness. It is not their job to convict others of sin; that is the Spirit's work. Believers are called to share the truth about Jesus with love, trusting the Spirit to work in people's hearts. This frees them from the pressure of trying to force change in others.

They also help believers understand their own experience. Many believers remember a time when they became aware of their sin and their need for Christ. That awareness was the work of the Spirit. When believers feel the Spirit's conviction today, it is an invitation to turn back to God, not a sign that God has rejected them.

Finally, these verses remind believers of the Spirit's nearness. They can pray for the Spirit's help in their witness, their struggles, and their daily walk, confident that He is present with them.

### John 16:13 — He Will Guide You into All Truth

verse: John 16:13

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus told His disciples, [[v12|I still have many things to tell you, but you can’t bear them now]]. There was much more they needed to understand, but they were not yet ready. They had not yet seen the cross or the resurrection, and they had not yet received the Spirit.

Then He promised, [[v13|However, when he, the Spirit of truth, has come, he will guide you into all truth]]. The Spirit would lead them step by step into a fuller understanding of what Jesus had taught and done. The word "guide" suggests leading someone along a path, like a guide showing travelers the way.

[[v13|for he will not speak from himself; but whatever he hears, he will speak]]. The Spirit would not bring new teaching that contradicted Jesus. He would speak what He heard from the Father and the Son. His teaching would be in perfect harmony with Jesus' words.

[[v13|He will declare to you things that are coming]]. The Spirit would help the disciples understand future events, including the meaning of Jesus' death and resurrection and the hope of His return.

In the next verse, Jesus summarized the Spirit's purpose: [[v14|He will glorify me]]. The Spirit's work always points to Jesus.

This promise was given first to the apostles. The Spirit guided them to understand and record the truth about Jesus. The New Testament is the fruit of this guidance.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse helps explain the authority of the New Testament. The apostles did not simply remember Jesus' words by human memory or develop their own theology. The Spirit guided them into all truth. The Gospels, letters, and Revelation preserve this Spirit-guided teaching. Peter writes that prophecy never came by human will, but [[2 Peter 1:21|holy men of God spoke, being moved by the Holy Spirit]].

It also shows that the Spirit continues to illumine believers today. The Spirit does not give new revelation that adds to Scripture, but He helps believers understand and apply the truth Scripture contains. Paul prays that believers would receive [[Ephesians 1:17|a spirit of wisdom and revelation in the knowledge of him]].

Finally, the verse gives a test for discerning spiritual teaching. The Spirit glorifies Jesus and speaks in harmony with His words. Any teaching that contradicts Jesus or draws attention away from Him does not come from the Spirit of truth.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to read Scripture with confidence. The Bible is not a collection of human opinions but the Spirit-guided record of God's truth. Believers can trust it as their guide for faith and life.

It also invites believers to depend on the Spirit when they read. Understanding Scripture is not only an intellectual task. Believers can ask the Spirit to open their minds and hearts, to show them Jesus, and to help them apply what they read.

Finally, this verse helps believers discern truth in a confusing world. Many voices claim to speak for God. Believers can test them by asking whether they glorify Jesus and agree with His words as revealed in Scripture.

### John 16:20–22 — Sorrow Turned into Joy

verse: John 16:20-22

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus had told His disciples that in a little while they would not see Him, and then in a little while they would see Him again. They were confused. Jesus explained: [[v20|Most certainly I tell you that you will weep and lament, but the world will rejoice]]. Within hours, Jesus would be arrested and crucified. The disciples would grieve deeply, while His enemies would celebrate what they thought was their victory.

[[v20|You will be sorrowful, but your sorrow will be turned into joy]]. Jesus did not say that their sorrow would be replaced by something else. He said that the sorrow itself would be turned into joy. The very thing that caused their grief, His death, would become the source of their joy when they understood it through the resurrection.

Then He gave an illustration: [[v21|A woman, when she gives birth, has sorrow because her time has come. But when she has delivered the child, she doesn’t remember the anguish any more, for the joy that a human being is born into the world]]. Labor pain is real and intense. But it leads to new life, and the joy of holding the child surpasses the memory of the pain.

[[v22|Therefore you now have sorrow, but I will see you again, and your heart will rejoice, and no one will take your joy away from you]]. Jesus would rise from the dead and see them again. Their joy would be permanent. No one, not their enemies, not persecution, not even death, could take it away.

This promise was fulfilled on Easter Sunday. When Jesus appeared to the disciples, [[John 20:20|The disciples therefore were glad when they saw the Lord]].

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal the pattern of the gospel: sorrow turned into joy, death turned into life. The cross, the darkest moment in history, became the source of salvation. What looked like defeat became victory.

The image of childbirth is used in the Old Testament for the painful arrival of God's new age (Isaiah 26:17–19; 66:7–14). Paul also describes creation itself as groaning in labor pains, waiting for the revelation of God's children (Romans 8:22). The sufferings of the present are birth pains leading to the new creation.

Finally, these verses describe the unique nature of Christian joy. It is not dependent on circumstances. It is rooted in the risen Christ, and because He lives forever, the joy He gives cannot be taken away.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses bring hope to believers who are grieving. Sorrow is real, and Jesus does not dismiss it. He said His disciples would weep and lament. But for those who belong to Christ, sorrow is not the final word. God can turn sorrow into joy, sometimes in this life and fully in the life to come.

They also help believers understand suffering as part of a larger story. Like labor pains, some suffering leads to new life. Believers can hold on through painful seasons, trusting that God is bringing something good that is not yet visible.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to anchor their joy in Christ. Joy that depends on circumstances is easily lost. Joy that rests in the risen Lord cannot be taken away. Believers can return again and again to the truth of the resurrection as the foundation of their joy.

### John 16:33 — I Have Overcome the World

verse: John 16:33

#### Simple Explanation

These are the final words of Jesus' farewell teaching to His disciples. He had just told them that very soon they would be scattered and would abandon Him. Even so, He did not end with a rebuke but with a promise.

[[v33|I have told you these things, that in me you may have peace]]. Everything Jesus had taught them that evening, about the Father's house, the way to the Father, the Holy Spirit, the vine and branches, the world's hatred, and sorrow turned to joy, was meant to give them peace. Notice the words "in me." Peace is found in Jesus, in union with Him.

[[v33|In the world you have trouble]]. Jesus did not promise His disciples an easy life. The word "trouble" refers to pressure, affliction, or distress. In the world, believers will face hardship, persecution, sickness, loss, and sorrow. This is a realistic promise.

[[v33|but cheer up! I have overcome the world]]. "Cheer up" can also be translated "take courage" or "take heart." Jesus' disciples can have courage, not because their circumstances are easy, but because Jesus has already won the victory. He spoke in the past tense, "I have overcome," even though the cross was still ahead. His victory was certain.

Within hours, Jesus would be arrested, beaten, and crucified. To the world, it would look like total defeat. But through His death and resurrection, He would overcome sin, death, and Satan.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse holds together two realities that believers live in: "in me" and "in the world." In the world, they face trouble. In Christ, they have peace. Both are true at the same time. Christian peace does not deny the reality of trouble; it rests in the deeper reality of Christ's victory.

It also points to the victory of the cross and resurrection. Jesus overcame the world not by political power or military force but by obedient suffering and death. John later writes, [[1 John 5:4|This is the victory that has overcome the world: your faith]]. Believers share in Christ's victory through faith in Him.

Finally, the verse reveals Jesus' care for weak disciples. He knew they would fail Him that very night. Yet His final words to them were words of peace and courage. His love did not depend on their strength.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse prepares believers for hardship. Some people expect that following Jesus will make life easy, and they are disillusioned when trouble comes. Jesus was honest: in the world, His people will have trouble. Hardship is not a sign that God has abandoned them.

It also gives believers courage. Whatever troubles believers face, Jesus has overcome the world. Their circumstances do not have the final word. Christ's victory is greater than any trouble.

Finally, the verse invites believers to seek peace in Christ. When troubles press in, believers can turn to Jesus in prayer, remember His promises, and rest in His victory. Peace is found not by escaping the world but by abiding in Him.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

John 16 prepares believers for a world that may oppose them. Jesus did not hide the cost of following Him. He spoke plainly about persecution so that His disciples would not be shaken when it came. Believers who face rejection or hostility can remember that Jesus warned them in advance, and that this does not mean God has abandoned them.

The chapter also reveals the gift of the Holy Spirit. Jesus said it was better for His disciples that He go away, so that the Spirit could come. The Spirit convicts the world, guides believers into truth, and glorifies Jesus. Believers can depend on the Spirit in their witness, their understanding of Scripture, and their daily walk, trusting that He is present with them.

John 16 offers hope to those who are grieving. Jesus promised that His disciples' sorrow would turn into joy, like a mother's pain giving way to the joy of new life. Believers who mourn can hold on to the hope of the resurrection, knowing that the joy Christ gives cannot be taken away.

Finally, the chapter ends with a promise for every season: in the world there will be trouble, but Jesus has overcome the world. Believers can face hardship with courage and peace, not because life is easy, but because they are in Christ, who has already won the victory. They can also rest in the truth that the Father Himself loves them.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for telling Your disciples the truth about the trouble they would face, and for not leaving them without hope. Thank You for sending the Holy Spirit, who convicts the world, guides Your people into all truth, and glorifies You. Holy Spirit, open my eyes to the truth of Your Word and help me to see Jesus more clearly. When I grieve, remind me that You can turn sorrow into joy, and that the joy You give cannot be taken away. Thank You, Father, that You Yourself love me. When troubles press in, give me Your peace and help me to take courage, because You have overcome the world. Strengthen believers who suffer for Your name, and keep us all faithful until we see You face to face. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
