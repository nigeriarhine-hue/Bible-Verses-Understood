# John 4
theme: Living Water for the Thirsty and Faith in Jesus' Word
main: Crossing barriers of ethnicity, religion, gender, and reputation, Jesus speaks with a Samaritan woman at a well, offers her living water that satisfies forever, reveals her past without condemning her, teaches that true worship is in spirit and truth, and declares that He is the Messiah. She tells her town, and many Samaritans believe that He is the Savior of the world. Back in Galilee, a royal official trusts Jesus' word that his son will live, and the boy is healed at that very hour.

## Chapter Background and Story

John 4 follows Jesus' conversation with Nicodemus in John 3, and the contrast between the two encounters is striking. Nicodemus was a respected Jewish man, a religious leader and teacher, who came to Jesus at night. The Samaritan woman was an unnamed woman of a despised people, with a troubled personal history, who met Jesus at noon. Nicodemus struggled to understand; the woman came to faith and immediately told others. Jesus offers new life to both the respectable insider and the rejected outsider.

### Why Jesus Went Through Samaria (4:1–6)

The Pharisees heard that Jesus was gaining more disciples than John the Baptist (though Jesus Himself did not baptize; His disciples did). To avoid premature conflict, Jesus left Judea and returned to Galilee. John writes, [[v4|He needed to pass through Samaria]]. Samaria lay between Judea in the south and Galilee in the north. It was the most direct route, though some strict Jews avoided it by crossing the Jordan and traveling on the eastern side. The word "needed" may also suggest divine purpose: Jesus had an appointment in Samaria.

The hostility between Jews and Samaritans went back centuries. When the Assyrians conquered the northern kingdom of Israel in 722 B.C., they deported many Israelites and settled foreigners in the land (2 Kings 17:24–41). The remaining Israelites intermarried with these settlers, and their descendants became the Samaritans. Jews considered them mixed in race and corrupted in religion. The Samaritans accepted only the five books of Moses as Scripture and built their own temple on Mount Gerizim. Jews destroyed that temple around 128 B.C. By Jesus' time, deep resentment existed on both sides.

Jesus came to the town of Sychar, near the land Jacob had given to his son Joseph (Genesis 33:19; 48:22). Jacob's well was there, a deep well that still exists today. Jesus, [[v6|being tired from his journey]], sat down by the well. John, who emphasizes Jesus' divinity, also shows His full humanity: He was tired and thirsty. It was about the sixth hour, around noon.

### The Conversation at the Well (4:7–26)

A Samaritan woman came to draw water. Women usually drew water in the cooler morning or evening and often went together. Coming alone at midday may suggest that she was avoiding others, perhaps because of her reputation.

Jesus said, [[v7|Give me a drink]]. The woman was astonished: [[v9|How is it that you, being a Jew, ask for a drink from me, a Samaritan woman?]] John explains that Jews did not share things with Samaritans; some Jews believed that Samaritan vessels were ritually unclean. Jesus crossed three barriers at once: He spoke to a Samaritan, a woman (Jewish rabbis did not usually speak publicly with women), and someone with a questionable reputation.

Jesus answered, [[v10|If you knew the gift of God, and who it is who says to you, ‘Give me a drink,’ you would have asked him, and he would have given you living water]]. "Living water" was a common phrase for flowing water, such as a spring or stream, as opposed to stagnant water in a cistern. The woman took Him literally. Jesus had no bucket, and the well was deep. Was He greater than their ancestor Jacob, who gave them the well?

Jesus explained the difference between ordinary water and the water He gives. This is studied below. The woman said, [[v15|Sir, give me this water, so that I don’t get thirsty, neither come all the way here to draw]]. She still did not fully understand, but she was interested.

Then Jesus said, [[v16|Go, call your husband, and come here]]. She answered, [[v17|I have no husband]]. Jesus said she was right: she had had five husbands, and the man she was now with was not her husband. In that culture, women could not easily initiate divorce, so the woman may have been divorced by several husbands, widowed, or both. Her situation may have involved great pain as well as sin. Jesus did not condemn her harshly. He simply revealed that He knew her life completely.

The woman said, [[v19|Sir, I perceive that you are a prophet]]. Then she raised a religious question: Samaritans worshiped on Mount Gerizim, while Jews said Jerusalem was the place to worship. Some see this as an attempt to change the subject; others see a sincere question from a woman whose religious and personal life were both unsettled. Jesus took her question seriously. He said that a time was coming when people would worship the Father neither on that mountain nor in Jerusalem. He affirmed that salvation comes from the Jews, through God's covenant with Israel and the Messiah who comes from Israel. But true worshipers would worship the Father in spirit and truth. This is studied below.

The woman said, [[v25|I know that Messiah is coming, he who is called Christ. When he has come, he will declare to us all things]]. The Samaritans expected a coming figure they called the Taheb, a prophet like Moses. Jesus answered, [[v26|I am he, the one who speaks to you]]. This is one of the clearest statements in the Gospels of Jesus revealing that He is the Messiah, and He made it to a Samaritan woman.

### The Woman's Witness and the Harvest (4:27–42)

At that moment, the disciples returned and were surprised that Jesus was talking with a woman, but no one asked Him why. The woman left her water jar, went back to the town, and said, [[v29|Come, see a man who told me everything that I have done. Can this be the Christ?]] The woman who may have avoided people now went to them eagerly. Leaving her water jar showed that something more important had captured her attention.

Meanwhile the disciples urged Jesus to eat. He said, [[v32|I have food to eat that you don’t know about]]. They wondered if someone had brought Him food. Jesus explained, [[v34|My food is to do the will of him who sent me and to accomplish his work]]. Doing the Father's will nourished and satisfied Him more than physical food.

Then He spoke about the harvest, studied below. People from the town were coming out to Jesus across the fields. Many Samaritans believed in Him because of the woman's testimony. They asked Him to stay, and He stayed two days. Many more believed because of His own words. They told the woman, [[v42|Now we believe, not because of your speaking; for we have heard for ourselves, and know that this is indeed the Christ, the Savior of the world]]. The title "Savior of the world" is significant. These were Samaritans, not Jews, and they recognized that Jesus came for everyone.

### Healing the Official's Son (4:43–54)

After two days, Jesus went to Galilee. Although He had said that a prophet has no honor in his own country, the Galileans welcomed Him because they had seen what He did in Jerusalem at the Passover.

Jesus came again to Cana, where He had turned water into wine. A royal official, probably serving Herod Antipas, had a son who was sick in Capernaum, about twenty miles away. The official came to Jesus and begged Him to come and heal his son, who was near death.

Jesus said, [[v48|Unless you people see signs and wonders, you will in no way believe]]. The "you" is plural, addressing the people of Galilee in general who sought miracles. The official persisted: [[v49|Sir, come down before my child dies]]. Jesus said, [[v50|Go your way. Your son lives]]. This verse is studied below.

The official believed and started home. On the way, his servants met him with the news that his son was alive. He asked when the boy began to get better, and they said the fever left him at the seventh hour (about one in the afternoon) the day before, exactly when Jesus had spoken. The official and his whole household believed. John calls this [[v54|the second sign that Jesus did]] after coming from Judea to Galilee, linking it with the first sign at the same village of Cana.

## Important Verses Explained

### John 4:13–14 — Water That Satisfies Forever

verse: John 4:13-14

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus speaks these words to a Samaritan woman at Jacob's well, near the town of Sychar. It was noon, and Jesus was tired and thirsty from His journey. When the woman came to draw water, He asked her for a drink, which surprised her because Jews did not usually associate with Samaritans. Jesus told her that if she knew who He was, she would ask Him, and He would give her "living water." She took this literally, pointing out that He had no bucket and the well was deep.

Jesus then explained: [[v13|Everyone who drinks of this water will thirst again]]. Physical water satisfies only temporarily. The woman had come to this well many times and would have to keep coming back. In a deeper sense, the things people pursue to satisfy their inner needs, such as relationships, possessions, success, and pleasure, leave them thirsty again.

[[v14|but whoever drinks of the water that I will give him will never thirst again]]. The water Jesus gives satisfies the deepest thirst of the human soul. Those who receive it will never again be spiritually thirsty in the same desperate way, because their deepest need has been met.

[[v14|but the water that I will give him will become in him a well of water springing up to eternal life]]. This water does not have to be drawn from outside again and again. It becomes an inner spring, continually flowing up and leading to eternal life. Later in John's Gospel, Jesus explains that the "living water" refers to the Holy Spirit, whom believers would receive (John 7:37–39).

The woman's own life illustrated the truth of Jesus' words. As the conversation revealed, she had had five husbands and was living with a man who was not her husband. She may have been searching for love, security, or worth in relationships, and each one left her thirsty again. Jesus offered her something that would truly satisfy.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal that Jesus satisfies the deepest longings of the human heart. The Old Testament pictures God as the fountain of living waters. Through Jeremiah, God said His people had forsaken Him, [[Jeremiah 2:13|the spring of living waters]], and dug for themselves [[Jeremiah 2:13|broken cisterns that can’t hold water]]. People often look to "broken cisterns" for satisfaction. Jesus offers the true spring.

They also connect with the promise of the Holy Spirit. Isaiah promised that God would pour water on the thirsty land and His Spirit on His people's descendants (Isaiah 44:3). Jesus gives the Spirit, who dwells within believers, bringing life, renewal, and satisfaction from within.

Finally, the verses show that eternal life begins now. The spring "springing up to eternal life" is not only a future hope but a present reality. Those who receive Jesus experience the beginning of eternal life today.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses invite people to consider where they are seeking satisfaction. Many people go from one relationship, achievement, or pleasure to another, always thirsty again. Jesus offers lasting satisfaction. Anyone can come to Him and receive the living water He gives.

They also encourage believers to keep drinking from the spring. Although believers have received the Spirit, they can still drift into seeking satisfaction elsewhere. Through prayer, worship, Scripture, and fellowship, they can return to Jesus and be refreshed.

Finally, these verses offer hope to those who feel empty or ashamed. The Samaritan woman's story shows that Jesus offers living water even to those whose lives are broken. No past is too complicated for His grace.

### John 4:23–24 — Worship in Spirit and Truth

verse: John 4:23-24

#### Simple Explanation

These verses come in the middle of Jesus' conversation with the Samaritan woman. Jesus had revealed that He knew about her five previous husbands and her current relationship. She recognized that He was a prophet and raised a question that had divided Jews and Samaritans for centuries: where should God be worshiped? Samaritans worshiped on Mount Gerizim, near Sychar, where their temple had once stood. Jews insisted that Jerusalem was the only proper place, according to God's command (Deuteronomy 12:5; 2 Chronicles 6:6).

Jesus answered that a time was coming when people would worship the Father neither on that mountain nor in Jerusalem. He affirmed that the Jews knew what they worshiped, [[v22|for salvation is from the Jews]], since God had revealed Himself to Israel and the Messiah came from Israel. But location would no longer be the central issue.

[[v23|But the hour comes, and now is]]. The new era was both coming and already present in Jesus.

[[v23|when the true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth]]. "In spirit" means that worship is not limited to a physical place or external rituals but comes from the heart and is empowered by God's Spirit. "In truth" means worship that is genuine and sincere, and also worship that is based on the truth of who God is, as revealed in Jesus, who is "the truth" (John 14:6).

[[v23|for the Father seeks such to be his worshipers]]. This is a remarkable statement. God is seeking worshipers. He takes the initiative to draw people to Himself.

[[v24|God is spirit]]. God is not limited to a physical body or location. He is present everywhere.

[[v24|and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth]]. Because God is spirit, worship must be spiritual and true, not merely physical, external, or tied to one place.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal that true worship is a matter of the heart and of truth, not of location or ritual. Through Jesus, the true temple (John 2:19–21), people can worship God anywhere. The barrier between Jews and Samaritans, and between all peoples, is broken down. Anyone can worship the Father through Jesus.

They also reveal that God seeks worshipers. Jesus was seeking this woman, and through her, her whole town. God takes the initiative in drawing people to Himself.

Finally, "spirit and truth" belong together. Worship that is sincere but not based on truth can be misguided. Worship that is doctrinally correct but lacks heart is empty. True worship combines a sincere heart, empowered by the Spirit, with accurate knowledge of God revealed in Jesus.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses invite believers to examine their worship. Is it sincere, coming from the heart, or merely external? Is it based on the truth of who God is, as revealed in Scripture and in Jesus? Believers can ask God to help them worship with both spirit and truth.

They also free believers to worship God anywhere: at home, at work, in nature, or in church. While gathering with other believers is important, worship is not confined to a building. Every part of life can be offered to God.

Finally, these verses invite believers to set aside divisions about outward forms. Christians often disagree about worship styles and practices. While these matters have some importance, Jesus' words remind believers that what matters most is worshiping God in spirit and truth.

### John 4:35 — Fields Ready for Harvest

verse: John 4:35

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus speaks these words to His disciples after His conversation with the Samaritan woman. While Jesus spoke with her, the disciples had gone into town to buy food. When they returned, they were surprised to find Him talking with a Samaritan woman. The woman left her water jar and went back to town to tell people about Jesus. The disciples urged Jesus to eat, but He said His food was to do the will of the One who sent Him.

Then He said, [[v35|Don’t you say, ‘There are yet four months until the harvest’?]] This may have been a common saying, meaning "There's plenty of time," or a reference to the actual season; grain was sown in winter and harvested about four months later.

[[v35|Behold, I tell you, lift up your eyes and look at the fields, that they are white for harvest already]]. At that very moment, the Samaritans from the town were coming out across the fields toward Jesus, likely wearing white garments. Jesus pointed to them as a harvest that was ready right now. The disciples did not need to wait four months; people were ready to receive Jesus immediately.

In the following verses, Jesus explained that the one who reaps receives wages and gathers a crop for eternal life, so that the sower and reaper rejoice together. He said, [[v37|One sows, and another reaps]]. Others had done the work of sowing (including the Old Testament prophets, John the Baptist, and the Samaritan woman herself), and the disciples would reap.

The result was remarkable: many Samaritans believed because of the woman's testimony, and many more believed after hearing Jesus Himself.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse teaches that God is preparing people to receive the gospel, often in unexpected places. The disciples would never have expected a Samaritan town to be ready for harvest. Yet there it was. God works in hearts in ways believers cannot see.

It also teaches urgency. The harvest is ready now, not someday. Believers should not assume that people are not interested or that the time is not right. Many are ready to hear about Jesus.

Finally, the verse shows that God's work involves many people. Some sow, some reap, and all rejoice together. No one does the work alone. Paul expresses the same idea: one plants, another waters, but God gives the growth (1 Corinthians 3:6–9).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites believers to "lift up their eyes" and notice the people around them who may be ready to hear about Jesus. Coworkers, neighbors, family members, and strangers may be more open than believers assume.

It also encourages believers not to delay sharing their faith. There is a tendency to think that the right time will come later. Jesus says the fields are ready now.

Finally, this verse encourages believers that their efforts are part of a larger work. Sometimes they sow and others reap; sometimes they reap where others have sown. Believers can rejoice together in what God is doing, whatever their part.

### John 4:50 — Believing Jesus' Word

verse: John 4:50

#### Simple Explanation

This verse describes the second sign Jesus performed in Cana of Galilee. A royal official, likely serving Herod Antipas, had a son who was very sick in Capernaum, about twenty miles away. Hearing that Jesus had returned to Galilee, the official traveled to Cana and begged Jesus to come down and heal his son, who was close to death.

Jesus responded with a rebuke directed at the people in general: "Unless you people see signs and wonders, you will in no way believe." Many Galileans were interested in Jesus mainly because of His miracles. But the official did not give up. He pleaded, "Sir, come down before my child dies."

Then Jesus said, [[v50|Go your way. Your son lives]]. Jesus did not go with him. He did not touch the boy or even see him. He simply spoke.

[[v50|The man believed the word that Jesus spoke to him, and he went his way]]. This is the key. The official had wanted Jesus to come with him, but he accepted Jesus' word without any visible sign. He trusted that what Jesus said was true and started the long journey home.

The next day, his servants met him on the road with the news that his son was alive. The official asked when he began to recover, and they said the fever left him at the seventh hour, about one in the afternoon, the day before. That was exactly when Jesus had said, "Your son lives." The official and his whole household believed.

The official's faith grew in stages: first he believed enough to come to Jesus, then he believed Jesus' word without seeing, and finally he and his household believed fully when they saw the result.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows the power of Jesus' word. He did not need to be physically present to heal. His word alone accomplished His will, just as God's word created the world (Genesis 1:3; John 1:3). This reveals Jesus' divine authority.

It also shows the nature of faith. The official believed Jesus' word before he saw the result. This is the kind of faith Jesus desires: trust in His word, not dependence on signs and wonders. Later, Jesus tells Thomas, [[John 20:29|Blessed are those who have not seen, and have believed]].

Finally, the verse shows that faith grows. The official's faith began with desperation, grew into trust in Jesus' word, and became full belief that spread to his whole household. Faith often develops gradually through experience of God's faithfulness.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites believers to trust Jesus' word. Believers today do not see Jesus physically, but they have His words in Scripture. They can trust His promises even when they do not yet see the results.

It also encourages believers to bring their urgent needs to Jesus, as the official did. They can pray boldly for those they love and trust Jesus to answer in His way.

Finally, this verse shows how faith can spread through a household. The official's encounter with Jesus led his whole family to believe. Believers' faith can influence their families and those around them.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

John 4 shows Jesus crossing barriers to reach people. He spoke with a Samaritan woman despite ethnic hostility, cultural rules about men and women, and her troubled reputation. Believers can follow His example by reaching out to people others avoid, crossing barriers of ethnicity, culture, and social status to share God's love.

Jesus' offer of living water speaks to everyone who is spiritually thirsty. Many people seek satisfaction in relationships, possessions, achievements, or pleasures that leave them thirsty again. Jesus offers lasting satisfaction through the Holy Spirit. Believers can drink deeply from Him and invite others to do the same.

The conversation about worship teaches that true worship is not about location or outward forms but about worshiping the Father in spirit and truth. Believers can worship God sincerely, based on the truth revealed in Jesus, in every part of their lives.

The Samaritan woman's response shows the power of personal testimony. She simply told her town, "Come, see a man who told me everything that I have done." Many believed because of her words and then came to believe through Jesus Himself. Believers can share what Jesus has done for them and invite others to come and see. And Jesus' words about the harvest remind believers that people are often more ready to hear the gospel than they assume.

Finally, the royal official's story teaches believers to trust Jesus' word even before they see the outcome. Jesus' word has power, and faith that trusts His word grows into deeper faith that can influence a whole household.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for crossing every barrier to reach people who were thirsty and broken. Thank You for offering living water that satisfies forever. I come to You with my thirst. Fill me with Your Spirit, and help me to stop seeking satisfaction in things that leave me empty.

Teach me to worship the Father in spirit and truth, with a sincere heart and in the truth You have revealed. Give me courage to tell others what You have done for me, as the Samaritan woman did. Open my eyes to see the fields that are ready for harvest around me. Help me to trust Your word even when I cannot yet see the answer, like the official who believed and went his way. Thank You that You are the Savior of the world. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
