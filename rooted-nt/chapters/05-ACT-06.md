# Acts 6
theme: Serving Tables, Spreading the Word, and the Courage of Stephen
main: As the church grows, Greek-speaking believers complain that their widows are being overlooked in the daily distribution of food. The apostles respond wisely, asking the church to choose seven Spirit-filled, wise men to oversee the work so that the apostles can devote themselves to prayer and the ministry of the word. The word of God spreads, and even many priests believe. One of the seven, Stephen, performs great signs and speaks with such wisdom that his opponents cannot answer him, so they bring false charges against him before the council, where his face shines like an angel's.

## Chapter Background and Story

Acts 6 is a transition chapter. It shows the church solving an internal problem and then introduces Stephen, whose speech and martyrdom in chapter 7 will lead to persecution that scatters the believers beyond Jerusalem (8:1). In this way, a dispute about food distribution leads indirectly to the spread of the gospel to Samaria and beyond.

The chapter also shows the growing diversity of the church. The early believers were all Jewish, but they came from different backgrounds. Luke calls one group "Hellenists" and the other "Hebrews." The Hellenists were Greek-speaking Jews, many of whom had grown up outside Israel in places like Egypt, Asia Minor, or Cyprus, and had settled in Jerusalem. The Hebrews were Aramaic-speaking Jews native to Judea and Galilee. The two groups often worshiped in different synagogues and could feel culturally distant from each other.

### A Complaint about Widows (6:1–7)

[[v1|Now in those days, when the number of the disciples was multiplying, a complaint arose from the Hellenists against the Hebrews, because their widows were neglected in the daily service]]. Widows in the ancient world were especially vulnerable. Without a husband's income, many depended on family or charity to survive. The Old Testament repeatedly commanded care for widows (Deuteronomy 10:18; 24:19–21; Isaiah 1:17). The early church provided daily food for widows in need, but the Greek-speaking widows were being overlooked. Whether this was due to language barriers, cultural prejudice, or simple oversight as the church grew rapidly, it was a real problem.

The apostles' response is studied below. They called the whole community together and asked them to choose seven men to oversee this ministry. The qualifications were spiritual and practical: [[v3|seven men of good report, full of the Holy Spirit and of wisdom]]. Meanwhile, the apostles would [[v4|continue steadfastly in prayer and in the ministry of the word]].

The proposal [[v5|pleased the whole multitude]]. They chose seven men: Stephen, Philip, Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas, and Nicolaus. All seven have Greek names, suggesting that most or all of them came from the Hellenist community, the very group whose widows had been neglected. The church responded to the complaint generously by placing the care of the neglected group in the hands of leaders who understood them. Nicolaus is described as [[v5|a proselyte of Antioch]], a Gentile who had converted to Judaism before becoming a follower of Jesus. Already, the church's leadership included someone from a Gentile background.

The seven were presented to the apostles, who [[v6|prayed, they laid their hands on them]]. The laying on of hands was a sign of commissioning and blessing, recalling Moses laying his hands on Joshua (Numbers 27:18–23). These seven are often regarded as the first deacons, since the Greek word for "service" (diakonia) is related to the word "deacon," although Luke does not use that title for them here.

Luke concludes with another summary of growth, studied below.

### Stephen's Ministry and Arrest (6:8–15)

Stephen, [[v8|full of faith and power, performed great wonders and signs among the people]]. Though he had been chosen to serve tables, his ministry was not limited to administration. God used him in powerful ways.

Opposition arose from members of [[v9|the synagogue called “The Libertines”]], meaning "Freedmen," probably descendants of Jews who had been taken to Rome as slaves and later freed. Others came from Cyrene and Alexandria in North Africa, and from Cilicia and Asia in what is now Turkey. Cilicia was the home province of Saul of Tarsus, who may well have been among those who debated Stephen.

[[v10|They weren’t able to withstand the wisdom and the Spirit by which he spoke]]. This fulfilled Jesus' promise: [[Luke 21:15|for I will give you a mouth and wisdom which all your adversaries will not be able to withstand or to contradict]].

Unable to win the argument, his opponents resorted to deception. They secretly persuaded men to say, [[v11|We have heard him speak blasphemous words against Moses and God]]. They stirred up the people, the elders, and the scribes, seized Stephen, and brought him before the council. False witnesses testified, [[v13|This man never stops speaking blasphemous words against this holy place and the law]]. They claimed he had said that Jesus would destroy the temple and change the customs of Moses.

The parallels with Jesus' trial are striking. Jesus too was accused by false witnesses of speaking against the temple (Mark 14:57–58). Stephen's experience mirrors his Lord's. There may also be a kernel of truth behind the distorted charges: Stephen likely taught, as Jesus had, that God's presence was not confined to the temple and that Jesus fulfilled the Law. Chapter 7 shows his reasoning.

The chapter ends with a remarkable image, studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### Acts 6:1–3 — Choosing Servants Full of the Spirit and Wisdom

verse: Acts 6:1-3

#### Simple Explanation

The church in Jerusalem was growing rapidly. Thousands had come to faith, and the community was sharing its resources so that no one was in need. Part of this care involved a daily distribution of food to widows. But a problem arose: [[v1|a complaint arose from the Hellenists against the Hebrews, because their widows were neglected in the daily service]].

This complaint could have divided the young church along cultural lines. The Greek-speaking believers felt that their widows were being treated unfairly compared to those of the Aramaic-speaking majority.

The apostles took the complaint seriously. They called the whole community together and said, [[v2|It is not appropriate for us to forsake the word of God and serve tables]]. They were not saying that serving tables was beneath them. Jesus had taught them to serve, even washing their feet. Rather, they recognized that their particular calling was to teach and pray, and that they could not do everything.

They proposed a solution: [[v3|Therefore, select from among you, brothers, seven men of good report, full of the Holy Spirit and of wisdom, whom we may appoint over this business]]. The church would choose qualified people to take responsibility for this ministry.

Notice the qualifications. The men were to be "of good report," meaning they had a good reputation and were trusted. They were to be "full of the Holy Spirit," spiritually mature and led by God. And they were to be "full of wisdom," able to handle practical matters fairly and wisely. Even a task like distributing food required spiritual leaders.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage shows that the church is called to care for the vulnerable fairly. God's people have always been commanded to care for widows, orphans, and strangers. James writes, [[James 1:27|Pure religion and undefiled before our God and Father is this: to visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction]]. When some are neglected, the church must act.

It also shows that practical service is spiritual work. The seven were chosen for their spiritual qualities, not merely their administrative skills. All service in the church, whether teaching or serving meals, is ministry done in the power of the Spirit. Paul teaches that the church is one body with many members, each with different gifts (1 Corinthians 12:4–7).

Finally, the passage shows healthy leadership. The apostles listened to the complaint, involved the whole community in the solution, and shared responsibility with others. They did not ignore the problem, dismiss it, or try to handle everything themselves.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage encourages churches to pay attention to those who may be overlooked. In every community, some people, perhaps those from a minority culture, those who speak a different language, the elderly, or the disabled, can be neglected. Churches can ask whether everyone is being cared for fairly and take steps to address any gaps.

It also shows the value of listening to complaints. The apostles did not dismiss the Hellenists' concern as divisive. They listened and responded. Leaders in churches, families, and organizations can learn from this example, treating concerns seriously and seeking fair solutions.

Finally, the passage reminds believers that every form of service matters. Those who serve in practical ways, such as cooking, cleaning, organizing, or caring for others, are doing spiritual work. Churches can honor and support these ministries and look for people of good character, spiritual maturity, and wisdom to lead them.

### Acts 6:4 — Prayer and the Ministry of the Word

verse: Acts 6:4

#### Simple Explanation

After proposing that seven men be chosen to oversee the distribution of food, the apostles explained their own focus: [[v4|But we will continue steadfastly in prayer and in the ministry of the word]].

The apostles had been entrusted by Jesus with a unique responsibility. They were eyewitnesses of His life, death, and resurrection, and they had been commissioned to teach and proclaim His message (Acts 1:8, 21–22). With thousands of new believers in Jerusalem, the need for teaching was enormous.

They named two priorities. First, [[v4|prayer]]. The apostles knew that their ministry depended on God. Without prayer, their teaching would lack power. The church had been born in prayer (1:14) and continued to grow through prayer (2:42; 4:24–31).

Second, [[v4|the ministry of the word]]. The word "ministry" here is the same Greek word used for the "service" of tables in verse 1. Both are forms of service. The apostles' service was to teach and proclaim God's word.

The phrase "continue steadfastly" means to be devoted, to persist, to keep at something. The apostles would not merely fit prayer and teaching into their schedule; they would give themselves fully to these tasks.

This was not a statement that serving tables was unimportant, but a recognition that the apostles needed to focus on the work God had especially given them.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse highlights the central importance of prayer and Scripture in the life of the church. Without the word, the church would lose its foundation. Without prayer, it would lose its dependence on God. Paul urged Timothy, a church leader, to [[2 Timothy 4:2|preach the word; be urgent in season and out of season]].

It also shows that different people have different callings within the body of Christ. The apostles were called to prayer and the word; the seven were called to oversee practical care. Both callings were necessary. Paul writes that God gave some as apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors, and teachers, [[Ephesians 4:12|for the perfecting of the saints, to the work of serving, to the building up of the body of Christ]].

Finally, the verse shows wisdom in managing time and energy. Even the apostles could not do everything. Recognizing one's priorities and sharing responsibilities is part of faithful ministry.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages pastors and church leaders to prioritize prayer and the teaching of Scripture. Leaders are often pulled in many directions by administration, meetings, and urgent needs. The apostles' example reminds them to protect time for prayer and for studying and teaching God's word.

It also encourages churches to support their leaders in this focus. Congregations can share practical responsibilities so that those called to teach have time to prepare and pray.

Finally, the verse applies to every believer. All believers are called to pray and to know God's word. Whatever their role, believers can make prayer and Scripture central in their lives, giving them priority rather than leaving them for whatever time is left over.

### Acts 6:7 — The Word of God Increased

verse: Acts 6:7

#### Simple Explanation

After the church resolved the dispute about the widows and appointed the seven, Luke gives a summary of what happened next: [[v7|The word of God increased and the number of the disciples greatly multiplied in Jerusalem]].

The phrase "the word of God increased" is unusual. Luke describes the word itself as growing, almost like a living thing. This phrase appears several times in Acts (12:24; 19:20). It means that the message about Jesus spread more and more widely and had increasing influence.

The growth was connected to what had just happened. When the church dealt wisely with its internal problem, it was freed to focus on its mission. A divided church would have been distracted; a united church continued to grow.

Then Luke adds a remarkable detail: [[v7|A great company of the priests were obedient to the faith]]. The priests served in the temple. Many of the high priestly leaders were Sadducees who opposed the apostles. But there were thousands of ordinary priests who served in rotation (Luke 1:8–9), many of them not wealthy or powerful. A large number of these priests came to faith in Jesus.

The phrase "obedient to the faith" shows that believing in Jesus involves obedience. Faith is not just agreeing with ideas; it is submitting to Jesus as Lord.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows the power of God's word. The gospel spread not because of clever strategies or human power, but because God's word is living and active (Hebrews 4:12). Isaiah writes that God's word [[Isaiah 55:11|will not return to me void, but it will accomplish that which I please]].

It also shows that unity supports mission. When the church handled its problem with fairness and wisdom, it continued to grow. Jesus prayed that His followers would be one so that the world would believe (John 17:21). Division hinders witness; unity strengthens it.

Finally, the conversion of many priests shows that no group is beyond the reach of the gospel. The priests were closely tied to the temple system that had opposed Jesus. Yet many of them believed. This anticipates the conversion of Saul, a fierce opponent who would become an apostle.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to trust the power of God's word. Believers may wonder whether their witness makes any difference. The word of God has power to grow and spread, often in ways believers cannot see.

It also reminds churches that dealing well with internal problems matters for their mission. Conflicts, complaints, and divisions can distract a church from its purpose. Handling them with wisdom, fairness, and humility allows the church to continue its work.

Finally, the verse encourages believers to pray for those who seem unlikely to believe. Many priests came to faith despite their leaders' opposition. Believers can pray for people in every group, including those who seem resistant, trusting that God's word can reach them.

### Acts 6:15 — The Face of an Angel

verse: Acts 6:15

#### Simple Explanation

Stephen had been brought before the Sanhedrin, the same council that had condemned Jesus and threatened the apostles. False witnesses had accused him of speaking against the temple and the Law of Moses. His life was in danger.

Then Luke records: [[v15|All who sat in the council, fastening their eyes on him, saw his face like it was the face of an angel]]. Everyone in the council stared at Stephen, and what they saw astonished them. His face shone with a heavenly radiance or calm. He did not look like a frightened man facing execution. He looked like a messenger from God's presence.

This recalls Moses, whose face shone after he had been in God's presence on Mount Sinai (Exodus 34:29–35). Stephen had been accused of speaking against Moses, but his face shone as Moses' face had. The irony is powerful.

Stephen's shining face was a sign that God was with him. He was "full of grace and power" (6:8 in some translations), full of the Holy Spirit and wisdom. In this moment of danger, God's presence was visible on him.

The council members saw it, but they did not change their minds. In the next chapter, after Stephen's speech, they will stone him.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that God's presence can sustain His people in the most difficult moments. Stephen faced false accusations and the threat of death, yet he was filled with peace and glory. Paul writes that believers [[2 Corinthians 3:18|with unveiled face seeing the glory of the Lord as in a mirror, are transformed into the same image from glory to glory]].

It also shows that faithfulness to Christ can reflect His glory even in suffering. Stephen's face shone not because his circumstances were good, but because he was close to God. Peter later wrote that when believers are insulted for Christ, [[1 Peter 4:14|the Spirit of glory and of God rests on you]].

Finally, the verse shows that seeing evidence of God is not the same as believing. The council saw Stephen's face, yet they hardened their hearts. Faith requires a willingness to respond to what God reveals.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers who face opposition, false accusations, or danger. God's presence can give them peace and strength. They do not have to face these trials alone.

It also invites believers to spend time in God's presence. Moses' face shone after being with God, and Stephen's face shone because he was full of the Spirit. Believers who spend time with God in prayer, worship, and Scripture may find that His presence shapes their character and even how others see them.

Finally, the verse reminds believers that their calm and grace under pressure can be a witness. When believers respond to hostility with peace and love instead of fear or anger, others may see something of Christ in them, even if they do not immediately respond.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Acts 6 shows that even healthy churches face problems. The early church was growing and generous, yet some widows were being overlooked. The apostles responded by listening to the complaint, involving the whole community, and appointing Spirit-filled, wise leaders to address it. Churches today can learn from this example, paying attention to those who may be neglected and seeking fair, wise solutions.

The chapter also highlights the importance of different callings. The apostles devoted themselves to prayer and the ministry of the word, while the seven oversaw practical care. Both forms of service were essential and spiritual. Every believer has a role in the body of Christ, and all forms of service matter to God.

Acts 6 shows that when the church handles problems well, the word of God continues to spread. The church grew, and many priests came to faith. Believers can trust the power of God's word to reach people, including those who seem least likely to believe.

Finally, the chapter introduces Stephen, a man full of faith, power, the Holy Spirit, and wisdom. When he was falsely accused, his face shone like an angel's. Believers who face opposition or injustice can find in Stephen an example of courage and peace, and can trust that God's presence will sustain them.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord, thank You for caring about the widows who were overlooked, and for the wisdom You gave the early church to meet their needs. Open my eyes to those around me who are neglected or forgotten, and show me how to help. Thank You for the many ways Your people serve, both in teaching and in practical care. Help me to serve faithfully in whatever role You give me, and to honor those whose service is quiet and unseen. Make me devoted to prayer and to Your word. Let Your word increase in my life, in my church, and in my community. Fill me with Your Spirit and wisdom, as You filled Stephen, and when I face opposition or false accusation, give me Your peace and let Your presence be seen in me. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
