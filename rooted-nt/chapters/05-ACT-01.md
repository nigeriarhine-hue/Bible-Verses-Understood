# Acts 1
theme: The Risen Lord Ascends and His Witnesses Wait and Pray
main: For forty days after His resurrection, Jesus appears to His apostles, speaks about God's kingdom, and tells them to wait in Jerusalem for the promised Holy Spirit. He declares that they will receive power and become His witnesses from Jerusalem to the ends of the earth, and then He is taken up into heaven, while angels promise that He will return in the same way. The disciples devote themselves to prayer together, and Matthias is chosen to replace Judas among the Twelve.

## Chapter Background and Story

Acts begins where Luke's Gospel ends. In his first book, Luke wrote about [[v1|all that Jesus began both to do and to teach]]. The word "began" is important. Acts tells what Jesus continued to do and teach after His ascension, through His Spirit and His church. The main character of Acts is not merely Peter or Paul, but the risen Lord Jesus at work through the Holy Spirit.

### Forty Days of Teaching (1:1–5)

Luke reminds Theophilus that Jesus [[v3|showed himself alive after he suffered, by many proofs, appearing to them over a period of forty days and speaking about God’s Kingdom]]. The resurrection was not a vague feeling or a vision. Jesus appeared repeatedly to His followers, ate with them, and taught them. Paul lists many of these appearances, including one to more than five hundred people at once (1 Corinthians 15:3–8). The forty days recall other significant periods in Scripture, such as Israel's forty years in the wilderness and Jesus' forty days of testing.

Jesus' theme during these days was [[v3|God’s Kingdom]], the same message He had preached throughout His ministry. Now the disciples could understand it in light of the cross and resurrection.

Jesus commanded them, [[v4|Don’t depart from Jerusalem, but wait for the promise of the Father]]. The promise was the Holy Spirit, foretold by the prophets (Joel 2:28–29; Ezekiel 36:26–27) and by Jesus Himself (John 14:16–17; Luke 24:49). John the Baptist had baptized with water, but [[v5|you will be baptized in the Holy Spirit not many days from now]].

### The Question about the Kingdom (1:6–8)

The disciples asked, [[v6|Lord, are you now restoring the kingdom to Israel?]] Their question was natural. The prophets had promised a time when God would restore Israel, defeat its enemies, and establish His rule. Now that the Messiah had risen, surely that time had come?

Jesus did not dismiss the question, but He redirected it. This is studied below.

### The Ascension (1:9–11)

[[v9|When he had said these things, as they were looking, he was taken up, and a cloud received him out of their sight]]. In Scripture, a cloud often signals God's presence and glory, as with the cloud that led Israel in the wilderness and filled the tabernacle (Exodus 40:34), and the cloud at Jesus' transfiguration (Luke 9:34–35). The ascension was not Jesus disappearing into outer space, but His entering into the presence and glory of the Father, where He now reigns. The angels' message is studied below.

The ascension is often overlooked, but it is an essential part of the gospel. Because Jesus ascended, He now reigns at the Father's right hand (Ephesians 1:20–22), intercedes for His people (Romans 8:34; Hebrews 7:25), and pours out the Spirit (Acts 2:33).

### Waiting in Prayer (1:12–14)

The disciples returned to Jerusalem from the Mount of Olives, [[v12|a Sabbath day’s journey away]], about two-thirds of a mile, the distance a Jew could walk on the Sabbath according to tradition. They gathered in the upper room where they were staying. Luke lists the eleven apostles, matching the list in Luke 6:14–16 without Judas Iscariot. Their prayerful waiting is studied below.

### Matthias Chosen (1:15–26)

During these days, about one hundred twenty believers were gathered. Peter, who had denied Jesus only weeks before but had been restored (John 21:15–19), now stood up to lead. He explained that Judas's betrayal had fulfilled Scripture, spoken by the Holy Spirit through David.

Luke includes a parenthetical description of Judas's death. Judas [[v18|obtained a field with the reward for his wickedness; and falling headlong, his body burst open]]. Matthew records that Judas hanged himself and that the priests used his returned silver to buy a potter's field (Matthew 27:5–8). The accounts are often harmonized by suggesting that the field was bought in Judas's name with his money, and that after he hanged himself, his body fell and burst open. The field became known as [[v19|Akeldama]], meaning "The field of blood."

Peter quoted two psalms. Psalm 69:25 speaks of the desolation of the dwelling of those who persecute the righteous, and Psalm 109:8 says, [[v20|Let another take his office]]. Peter concluded that someone must take Judas's place among the Twelve.

The qualifications were specific. The new apostle had to be someone who had accompanied them [[v22|beginning from the baptism of John to the day that he was received up from us]], so that he could [[v22|become a witness with us of his resurrection]]. The apostles' unique role was to be eyewitnesses of Jesus' ministry and resurrection.

Two men were nominated: Joseph called Barsabbas (also called Justus) and Matthias. The believers prayed, [[v24|You, Lord, who know the hearts of all men, show which one of these two you have chosen]]. Then they cast lots, an Old Testament practice used to discern God's will (Proverbs 16:33; 1 Chronicles 24:5), and [[v26|the lot fell on Matthias; and he was counted with the eleven apostles]].

The number twelve mattered. Jesus had chosen twelve apostles, corresponding to the twelve tribes of Israel (Luke 22:30). Restoring the Twelve showed that God's plan to renew His people was continuing. After the Spirit came at Pentecost, the church never again is recorded casting lots to make decisions; instead, believers sought the Spirit's guidance through prayer, Scripture, and counsel (Acts 13:2; 15:28).

## Important Verses Explained

### Acts 1:7–8 — Power to Be Witnesses

verse: Acts 1:7-8

#### Simple Explanation

The risen Jesus had spent forty days teaching His disciples about God's kingdom. They gathered with Him on the Mount of Olives and asked whether He was now going to restore the kingdom to Israel. They were eager for God's promises to be fulfilled, and they wanted to know when.

Jesus answered, [[v7|It isn’t for you to know times or seasons which the Father has set within his own authority]]. The timing of God's final plans belongs to the Father. Jesus had said something similar about His return: no one knows the day or the hour (Mark 13:32). The disciples did not need a timetable; they needed a mission.

[[v8|But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you]]. Instead of focusing on when the kingdom would come in fullness, they were to receive the Spirit's power. The word "power" refers to God's strength working through them, enabling them to do what they could not do on their own.

[[v8|You will be witnesses to me]]. A witness is someone who tells what they have seen and heard. The disciples had seen Jesus' life, death, and resurrection. Their task was to testify about Him. Notice that Jesus says "witnesses to me." The focus of their message was not themselves, but Jesus.

[[v8|in Jerusalem, in all Judea and Samaria, and to the uttermost parts of the earth]]. Their witness would spread outward in expanding circles: first Jerusalem, the city where Jesus was crucified; then the surrounding region of Judea; then Samaria, home of the people Jews usually avoided; and finally to the ends of the earth. This verse is the outline of the book of Acts.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal that God's kingdom advances through the Spirit-empowered witness of His people. The disciples expected a political restoration. Jesus pointed them to something larger: a kingdom that would include people of every nation, spreading not by force but by the testimony of those filled with the Spirit.

They also fulfill God's ancient promise. God told Abraham that through his offspring, [[Genesis 22:18|All the nations of the earth will be blessed]]. Isaiah foresaw God's servant as [[Isaiah 49:6|a light to the nations, that you may be my salvation to the end of the earth]]. Through the witness of Jesus' followers, this promise would be fulfilled.

Finally, these verses show that witness depends on the Spirit's power. The disciples were ordinary people, fishermen and tax collectors, who had recently abandoned Jesus in fear. Their transformation into bold witnesses would be the work of the Holy Spirit.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses remind believers that they are called to be witnesses. Every follower of Jesus has a story of what He has done and can tell others about Him. Witnessing is not only for pastors or missionaries; it is the calling of the whole church.

They also encourage believers who feel inadequate. Many people hesitate to share their faith because they feel unqualified or afraid. Jesus' promise is that the Spirit gives power. Believers can depend on Him for the courage and words they need.

Finally, these verses help believers keep a balanced view of the future. It is natural to wonder about the timing of Jesus' return and the end of history. But Jesus redirects attention to the present mission. Rather than speculating about dates, believers are called to live as faithful witnesses wherever God places them, beginning where they live and reaching outward.

### Acts 1:11 — This Jesus Will Come Back

verse: Acts 1:11

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus had just finished speaking to His disciples on the Mount of Olives. As they watched, He was lifted up, and a cloud hid Him from their sight. The disciples kept staring into the sky, perhaps stunned, perhaps hoping He would reappear.

Then two men in white clothing stood beside them. Luke had described two men in dazzling clothing at the empty tomb as well (Luke 24:4). These were angels, God's messengers.

They said, [[v11|You men of Galilee, why do you stand looking into the sky?]] The question gently moved the disciples from staring to acting. They had been given a mission, and they needed to return to Jerusalem and wait for the Spirit.

Then came the promise: [[v11|This Jesus, who was received up from you into the sky, will come back in the same way as you saw him going into the sky]]. Notice the words "This Jesus." The same Jesus who had lived among them, died, and risen would return. He would not be replaced by someone else. And He would come back "in the same way," personally, visibly, and in glory, with the clouds.

Jesus Himself had spoken of His return: [[Luke 21:27|Then they will see the Son of Man coming in a cloud with power and great glory]].

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse affirms one of the central hopes of the Christian faith: the personal, visible return of Jesus Christ. The ancient creeds of the church confess that He "will come again in glory to judge the living and the dead." Christians hold different views about the order of events surrounding His return, but they share the confident hope that He will come.

It also reveals the connection between the ascension and the second coming. Jesus left visibly and will return visibly. The time between His ascension and His return is the age of the church's mission, the time for His witnesses to carry the gospel to the ends of the earth.

Finally, the angels' question shows that hope in Christ's return should lead to faithful action, not passive waiting. Believers are not called to stand staring into the sky but to live as faithful servants, ready for their Master's return (Luke 12:35–40).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse gives believers hope in a troubled world. History is not moving aimlessly. The same Jesus who ascended will return to set everything right, to judge with justice, and to make all things new.

It also calls believers to live with readiness. Jesus' return could happen at any time. This should lead believers to live faithfully, to remain in His love, and to be ready to meet Him.

Finally, the angels' gentle rebuke encourages believers not to become so focused on the future that they neglect the present. Some people become so absorbed in end-time speculation that they lose sight of their calling. The hope of Jesus' return is meant to strengthen believers for faithful witness and service today.

### Acts 1:14 — With One Accord in Prayer

verse: Acts 1:14

#### Simple Explanation

After the ascension, the disciples returned to Jerusalem as Jesus had commanded and gathered in an upper room. Luke lists the eleven apostles by name. Then he tells us what they were doing: [[v14|All these with one accord continued steadfastly in prayer and supplication]].

Two phrases stand out. First, [[v14|with one accord]]. The believers were united in heart and purpose. Only weeks before, the disciples had argued about who was greatest (Luke 22:24). Now they were united. This phrase appears repeatedly in Acts to describe the early church (Acts 2:46; 4:24; 5:12).

Second, [[v14|continued steadfastly in prayer]]. They did not pray once and then stop. They persevered in prayer. Jesus had told them to wait for the Spirit, and they waited by praying.

Luke also names others who were present: [[v14|along with the women and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brothers]]. The women who had followed Jesus, supported His ministry, stood at the cross, and discovered the empty tomb were part of this praying community. Mary, the mother of Jesus, was there. This is the last time she is mentioned by name in the New Testament, and she is found among those praying.

Jesus' brothers were there too. During Jesus' ministry, they had not believed in Him (John 7:5). After the resurrection, they became believers. Jesus had appeared to James (1 Corinthians 15:7), who would later become a leader of the church in Jerusalem.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that the church was born in prayer. Before the Spirit came at Pentecost, the believers prayed together. Throughout Acts, major events are preceded by prayer: the choosing of leaders (6:6), the sending of missionaries (13:3), and deliverance from prison (12:5). Prayer is the atmosphere in which God's work moves forward.

It also reveals the unity of the early church. The group in the upper room included apostles, women, Jesus' mother, and His formerly unbelieving brothers. People with very different backgrounds and histories were united in prayer. This unity was an answer to Jesus' prayer that His followers would be one (John 17:21).

Finally, the verse shows the honor given to women in the early church. Women were not on the margins of this community; they were among its faithful members, praying alongside the apostles. Luke, who in his Gospel gave special attention to women followers of Jesus, continues to show their place in the church.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to make prayer central in their lives and churches. Before acting, the early church prayed. Believers today can seek God together in prayer, especially when they face decisions, challenges, or periods of waiting.

It also calls believers to unity. The phrase "with one accord" challenges churches divided by conflict, competition, or factions. Believers are called to be united in heart and purpose, setting aside differences for the sake of Christ.

Finally, the verse offers hope for families. Jesus' brothers once doubted Him, but now they were praying among His followers. Believers who pray for family members who do not yet believe can take heart. God can change hearts, and those who once resisted Jesus can become His faithful followers.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Acts 1 reminds believers that Jesus is alive and active. Luke's Gospel told what Jesus began to do and teach; Acts tells what He continues to do through His Spirit and His people. Believers today are part of this ongoing story. The risen Lord is still at work in the world, and He works through those who follow Him.

The chapter also gives believers a clear mission. Jesus did not satisfy His disciples' curiosity about timing; He gave them a calling to be His witnesses, empowered by the Spirit, from their home city to the ends of the earth. Every believer can be a witness in their own setting, telling others about Jesus with the help of the Holy Spirit.

Acts 1 offers hope for the future. The same Jesus who ascended will return in glory. This hope keeps believers from despair and calls them to live faithfully and expectantly. At the same time, the angels' question reminds them not to stand staring into the sky, but to get on with the work Jesus has given.

Finally, the chapter shows the importance of prayer and unity. Before the Spirit came, the believers waited together in prayer, with one accord. Churches and individual believers today can follow their example, seeking God together, waiting on Him, and preparing for what He will do.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for showing Yourself alive after Your suffering, and for continuing Your work through Your Spirit and Your people. Thank You that You reign now at the Father's right hand. When I am curious about times and seasons, help me to trust the Father's plan and to focus on the mission You have given me. Fill me with the power of Your Holy Spirit, and make me Your witness in my home, my community, and wherever You send me. Thank You for the promise that You will come again in glory. Help me to live ready for Your return, not standing idle, but serving You faithfully. Teach me to pray with perseverance, and unite Your church in heart and purpose. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
