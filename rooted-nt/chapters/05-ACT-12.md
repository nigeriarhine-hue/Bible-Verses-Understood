# Acts 12
theme: A Praying Church, a Delivered Apostle, and a Proud King Brought Low
main: King Herod Agrippa I executes the apostle James and imprisons Peter, planning to put him on trial after Passover. While the church prays earnestly, an angel wakes Peter in his cell, his chains fall off, and he is led out through locked gates. Peter goes to the house where believers are praying, but they are slow to believe the servant girl who announces him. Later, Herod accepts the crowd's praise as a god and is struck down. Herod dies, but the word of God continues to grow and multiply.

## Chapter Background and Story

Acts 12 brings the first half of the book to a close. The focus has been on the church in Jerusalem and on Peter. After this chapter, Peter largely disappears from Acts (he reappears briefly at the Jerusalem Council in chapter 15), and the focus shifts to Paul and the mission to the Gentiles. The chapter shows the church facing persecution from the highest political authority in the region and shows God's power over even the mightiest earthly rulers.

The "King Herod" in this chapter is Herod Agrippa I, grandson of Herod the Great, who had tried to kill the infant Jesus (Matthew 2), and nephew of Herod Antipas, who had executed John the Baptist and mocked Jesus at His trial (Luke 23:6–12). Herod Agrippa I was a friend of the Roman emperors Caligula and Claudius and ruled over a territory nearly as large as his grandfather's from A.D. 41 to 44. He worked hard to please the Jewish population and present himself as devout.

### James Killed, Peter Arrested (12:1–5)

[[v1|Now about that time, King Herod stretched out his hands to oppress some of the assembly]]. Herod began a new wave of persecution. [[v2|He killed James, the brother of John, with the sword]]. James was one of the first disciples Jesus called and one of the inner three, with Peter and John, who witnessed the transfiguration and Jesus' prayer in Gethsemane. Years earlier, James and John had asked to sit at Jesus' right and left in His glory, and Jesus had asked whether they could drink the cup He would drink. They said they could, and Jesus said, "You will indeed drink my cup" (Matthew 20:20–23). James became the first of the Twelve to be martyred. Luke records his death in a single verse.

[[v3|When he saw that it pleased the Jews, he proceeded to seize Peter also]]. Herod's motive was political: he wanted popularity. It was during [[v3|the days of unleavened bread]], the week following Passover. Herod put Peter in prison under the guard of four squads of four soldiers each, intending to bring him out for public trial after the festival. He may have heard that the apostles had escaped from prison before (5:19) and taken extra precautions. The church's response is studied below.

### Peter's Miraculous Escape (12:6–11)

The night before Herod planned to bring him out, Peter was [[v6|sleeping between two soldiers, bound with two chains]]. Peter was asleep. Facing likely execution the next day, he slept peacefully, perhaps remembering Jesus' words that he would live to old age (John 21:18).

[[v7|And behold, an angel of the Lord stood by him, and a light shone in the cell]]. The angel struck Peter on the side to wake him and said, [[v7|Stand up quickly!]] Peter's chains fell from his wrists. The angel told him to dress, put on his sandals and cloak, and follow. Peter obeyed, thinking he was seeing a vision. They passed the first and second guards and came to the iron gate leading to the city, [[v10|which opened to them by itself]]. They walked down a street, and the angel suddenly left. Peter's realization is studied below.

### At the House of Mary (12:12–17)

Peter went to [[v12|the house of Mary, the mother of John who was called Mark, where many were gathered together and were praying]]. This John Mark would later travel with Paul and Barnabas and is traditionally regarded as the author of the Gospel of Mark. Mary's house was large enough for many believers to gather, and it may have been a regular meeting place for the Jerusalem church.

A servant girl named Rhoda came to the gate. The scene that follows, one of the most charming in Acts, is studied below.

Peter told them how the Lord had brought him out of prison and said, [[v17|Tell these things to James and to the brothers]]. This James was the brother of Jesus, who had become a leader in the Jerusalem church (15:13; Galatians 1:19; 2:9). Then Peter [[v17|departed and went to another place]]. Luke does not say where Peter went. He left Jerusalem to avoid Herod.

### Herod's Death (12:18–25)

In the morning, there was great commotion among the soldiers. Herod searched for Peter and could not find him. He questioned the guards and ordered them executed, as Roman law often required when guards lost a prisoner. Then Herod went to Caesarea.

Herod had been angry with the people of Tyre and Sidon, coastal cities that depended on his territory for food. They sought peace through Blastus, the king's chamberlain. On an appointed day, Herod put on his royal robes, sat on his throne, and gave a speech. The crowd shouted, [[v22|The voice of a god, and not of a man!]] Herod's response and its consequence are studied below.

The Jewish historian Josephus also records Herod Agrippa's death in Caesarea in A.D. 44. He describes Herod wearing a robe made of silver that shone in the morning sun, the crowd hailing him as a god, Herod not rebuking them, and being struck immediately with severe abdominal pain, dying five days later. Josephus's account closely matches Luke's.

Barnabas and Saul returned from Jerusalem, where they had delivered the famine relief (11:30), and brought John Mark with them to Antioch. This sets the stage for the first missionary journey in chapter 13.

## Important Verses Explained

### Acts 12:5 — Constant Prayer Was Made for Him

verse: Acts 12:5

#### Simple Explanation

King Herod had just executed the apostle James and arrested Peter. Herod planned to bring Peter before the people after Passover, almost certainly to execute him as well. Peter was held in prison under heavy guard, with sixteen soldiers assigned to him in rotating shifts. Escape seemed impossible.

[[v5|Peter therefore was kept in the prison, but constant prayer was made by the assembly to God for him]]. Notice the contrast: "Peter was kept in prison, but..." On one side was the power of the king, his soldiers, his chains, and his prison. On the other side was the church, praying.

The word "constant" can also be translated "earnestly" or "fervently." It describes prayer that is intense and persistent, stretched out like a runner reaching for the finish line. Luke uses a related word to describe Jesus' prayer in Gethsemane, when He prayed [[Luke 22:44|more earnestly]].

The church had no political power, no army, and no influence with Herod. But they had access to God through prayer. They did not organize a protest or a rescue attempt. They prayed.

Their prayer was also communal: "by the assembly." The believers gathered together to pray, as they had done from the beginning (1:14; 4:24).

It is worth remembering that James had just been killed. The church had prayed, no doubt, for James too, and he had died. Yet they did not give up on prayer. They continued to pray earnestly for Peter.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that prayer is the church's most powerful resource in times of crisis. Earthly rulers may seem all-powerful, but God rules over them. James writes, [[James 5:16|The insistent prayer of a righteous person is powerfully effective]]. The church in Jerusalem trusted that God could act when no human help was possible.

It also raises a difficult question: why was Peter delivered while James was killed? Scripture does not give a full answer. God's purposes are sometimes beyond human understanding. Both men were faithful, and both were loved by God. James glorified God through his death, and Peter through his continued ministry. Hebrews 11 describes believers who by faith escaped the sword and others who by faith were killed by the sword (Hebrews 11:34, 37). Both outcomes can be faithful, and God is present in both.

Finally, the verse shows the importance of persevering in prayer even after disappointment. The church had seen James die, yet they continued to pray. Faith keeps praying, even when previous prayers seem unanswered.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to pray earnestly in times of crisis. When believers face situations beyond their control, such as illness, injustice, or danger, they can turn to God in fervent, persistent prayer.

It also encourages believers to pray together. The church gathered to pray for Peter. Believers today can join with others to pray for those in need, especially for believers who are persecuted or imprisoned for their faith.

Finally, the verse offers comfort to those who have prayed and not received the answer they hoped for. God does not always answer as we wish, and some faithful believers suffer and die. Yet God is still good, still sovereign, and still worthy of trust. Believers can continue to pray, trusting God's wisdom and love.

### Acts 12:11 — Now I Truly Know

verse: Acts 12:11

#### Simple Explanation

Peter had been asleep in prison, chained between two soldiers, on the night before his likely execution. An angel woke him, his chains fell off, and he followed the angel past two sets of guards and through an iron gate that opened by itself. Throughout this, Peter thought he was dreaming or seeing a vision. Then the angel suddenly left him, and Peter found himself standing alone in a street of Jerusalem.

[[v11|When Peter had come to himself]]. The phrase means that Peter came to his senses, fully awake and aware. The cool night air and the empty street made it clear that this was really happening.

[[v11|he said, “Now I truly know that the Lord has sent out his angel and delivered me out of the hand of Herod]]. Peter recognized that his rescue was the Lord's work. It was not luck or human cleverness. God had sent His angel.

[[v11|and from everything the Jewish people were expecting]]. Many people, including those who opposed the church, were expecting Peter's execution. Herod had arrested him to please them. God had overturned their expectations.

Peter's statement recalls Psalm 34:7: [[Psalm 34:7|The LORD’s angel encamps around those who fear him, and delivers them]].

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that God's deliverance can be so surprising that it seems unreal. Peter thought he was dreaming. Sometimes God's answers to prayer are beyond what believers can imagine. Paul writes that God is able to do [[Ephesians 3:20|exceedingly abundantly above all that we ask or think]].

It also shows that God is sovereign over earthly powers. Herod was a powerful king, supported by Rome, with soldiers and prisons at his command. Yet he could not hold Peter when God decided to release him. No human power can stand against God's purposes.

Finally, the verse shows the importance of recognizing God's work. Peter did not take credit for his escape or attribute it to chance. He acknowledged the Lord's hand. Gratitude begins with recognizing what God has done.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to recognize God's hand in their lives. Sometimes God's help comes in ways that seem ordinary; sometimes it comes in extraordinary ways. Either way, believers can learn to say, "Now I truly know that the Lord has done this," and give Him thanks.

It also gives hope to those who feel trapped. Peter was chained, guarded, and behind locked gates. Believers who feel trapped by circumstances can remember that God is able to open doors that seem permanently shut.

Finally, the verse reminds believers that human expectations do not limit God. Everyone expected Peter to die. God had other plans. Believers can trust that God's purposes are not bound by what others predict or fear.

### Acts 12:15–16 — "You Are Crazy!"

verse: Acts 12:15-16

#### Simple Explanation

After his miraculous escape, Peter went to the house of Mary, the mother of John Mark, where many believers were gathered, praying. They were praying for Peter at that very moment. He knocked at the gate.

A servant girl named Rhoda came to answer. When she recognized Peter's voice, she was so overjoyed that she forgot to open the gate. Instead, she ran inside to tell everyone that Peter was standing outside.

[[v15|They said to her, “You are crazy!”]] The believers who were praying for Peter's release did not believe that their prayer had been answered. They thought Rhoda was out of her mind.

[[v15|But she insisted that it was so]]. Rhoda kept insisting. She knew what she had heard.

[[v15|They said, “It is his angel.”]] Some Jews believed that each person had a guardian angel who might look or sound like them (compare Matthew 18:10). Perhaps they thought Peter had already been killed and his angel had come.

[[v16|But Peter continued knocking. When they had opened, they saw him and were amazed]]. Peter was still standing outside, knocking. When they finally opened the gate, they were astonished.

This scene is both humorous and humbling. The believers were praying earnestly for Peter, but when God answered, they could not believe it.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This scene shows that God answers prayer even when believers' faith is imperfect. The church prayed, but they did not fully expect God to answer. God answered anyway. His answers are not limited by the strength of human faith. Jesus taught that even faith as small as a mustard seed can move mountains (Matthew 17:20).

It also shows the honesty of Scripture. Luke does not present the early church as perfect. He shows their struggles and doubts. Believers today can take comfort in knowing that the early Christians, too, sometimes struggled to believe.

Finally, the scene honors a servant girl. Rhoda, a person of low social status, was the first to recognize the answer to the church's prayer, and she held to the truth even when others dismissed her. Like the women at the empty tomb whose report was dismissed (Luke 24:11), Rhoda was right. God often reveals His work through those whom others overlook.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage encourages believers to pray with expectation. It is possible to pray faithfully while not really expecting God to answer. Believers can ask God to increase their faith so that they pray believing He will act.

It also comforts believers who struggle with doubt. God answered the church's prayer despite their unbelief. Believers do not need perfect faith for God to hear them. They can bring their doubts to Him, like the father who said to Jesus, [[Mark 9:24|I believe. Help my unbelief!]].

Finally, the passage reminds believers to listen to those whose voices are often ignored. Rhoda's report was dismissed because she was young and a servant. Believers can learn to take seriously the testimony of all members of the community, regardless of their status.

### Acts 12:23–24 — He Didn't Give God the Glory

verse: Acts 12:23-24

#### Simple Explanation

King Herod Agrippa had executed James and imprisoned Peter. When Peter escaped, Herod had the guards executed and went to Caesarea. There, on an appointed day, he put on his royal robes, sat on his throne, and gave a speech to the people of Tyre and Sidon, who were seeking peace with him.

The people shouted, [[v22|The voice of a god, and not of a man!]] They flattered Herod, perhaps because they depended on him for food. Herod did not reject their praise. He accepted being called a god.

[[v23|Immediately an angel of the Lord struck him, because he didn’t give God the glory. Then he was eaten by worms and died]]. Luke explains clearly why Herod died: he accepted the glory that belongs only to God. The same angel of the Lord who delivered Peter now struck Herod.

Contrast Herod with Peter, who refused worship when Cornelius bowed before him (10:25–26), and with Paul and Barnabas, who later tore their clothes when the people of Lystra tried to worship them (14:14–15).

Then comes a powerful contrast: [[v24|But the word of God grew and multiplied]]. Herod, the persecutor, died. The church he tried to destroy continued to grow. The king who wanted to stop the word of God was gone, but the word lived on.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that God will not share His glory with anyone. Through Isaiah, God declared, [[Isaiah 42:8|I will not give my glory to another]]. Herod's pride brought judgment. Pride, especially the desire to be worshiped, is a grave sin because it takes what belongs to God alone.

They also show the outcome of opposing God's people. Herod began the chapter killing James and imprisoning Peter. He ended the chapter dead. The chapter shows a dramatic reversal: the persecutor fell, and the persecuted church thrived.

Finally, verse 24 summarizes the message of Acts: God's word cannot be stopped. Rulers rise and fall. Persecution comes and goes. But the word of God continues to grow and multiply. Isaiah writes, [[Isaiah 40:8|The grass withers, the flower fades; but the word of our God stands forever]].

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses warn believers against pride. It is easy to accept praise that should go to God, taking credit for accomplishments, gifts, or successes that come from Him. Believers can practice giving God the glory, acknowledging that all good things come from Him.

They also encourage believers facing persecution. Those who oppose God's people may seem powerful, but they are not in ultimate control. God is sovereign, and His purposes will prevail.

Finally, these verses give believers confidence in God's word. No matter what happens in the world, God's word will continue to grow. Believers can invest their lives in spreading His word, knowing that it will outlast every earthly power.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Acts 12 shows the power of a praying church. Facing the power of a king who had already killed one apostle, the believers turned to God in earnest prayer. God answered in a way they could hardly believe. Believers today can bring every crisis to God in prayer, together with other believers, trusting that He is able to act when no human help is possible.

The chapter also holds together two different outcomes: James was killed, and Peter was delivered. Believers will not always understand why God allows some to suffer and rescues others. But both outcomes are held within God's loving purposes. Believers can trust Him when prayers are answered as they hoped, and when they are not.

Acts 12 humbly reminds believers that even faithful pray-ers can struggle to believe. The church was astonished when Peter appeared at the door. God answers prayer even when faith is weak. Believers can bring their doubts to Him and ask Him to increase their faith, and they can learn to listen to the testimony of those, like Rhoda, whom others might overlook.

Finally, the chapter contrasts the fate of a proud king with the growth of God's word. Herod accepted glory that belonged to God and was struck down, but the word of God grew and multiplied. Believers are called to give God the glory in everything, and they can live with confidence that His word will outlast every power that opposes it.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord God, thank You that You hear the prayers of Your people. When I face situations beyond my control, teach me to pray earnestly and to keep praying, together with my brothers and sisters. Thank You for delivering Peter from prison, and thank You for the faithfulness of James, who glorified You in his death. Help me to trust You when Your answers are not what I hoped, and to rejoice when You do more than I imagined. Forgive me for the times I have prayed without really expecting You to answer, and increase my faith. Guard my heart from pride, and help me always to give You the glory that belongs to You alone. Thank You that Your word grows and multiplies, and that no power on earth can stop it. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
