# Colossians 3
theme: Set Your Minds on Things Above
main: Since believers have been raised with Christ, Paul calls them to seek and set their minds on the things above, where Christ is seated, because their life is hidden with Christ in God and they will appear with Him in glory. They are to put to death the earthly ways of sexual immorality, impurity, evil desire, and greed, and to put away anger, malice, slander, and lies, because they have put off the old self and put on the new self, renewed in the image of its Creator, in whom ethnic and social divisions lose their power, for Christ is all and in all. As God's chosen and beloved people, they are to clothe themselves with compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, patience, forgiveness, and above all love, letting Christ's peace rule and His word dwell richly among them, and doing everything in the name of the Lord Jesus with thanksgiving. Paul then applies this new life to households and to work, calling all to serve the Lord Christ.

## Chapter Background and Story

In chapter 2, Paul warned the Colossians against teachers who promoted rules and rituals as the path to spiritual maturity: [[Colossians 2:21|Don’t handle, nor taste, nor touch]]. Paul said these regulations look wise but have no power to restrain sinful desires (2:23). That raises a natural question: If rules cannot transform the heart, what can?

Chapter 3 answers that question. The power for holy living comes not from human rules but from union with Christ. Believers have died with Christ and been raised with Him. Their life is now bound up with His. As they set their minds on Christ, they are empowered to put off the old way of life and put on the new.

This chapter closely parallels Ephesians 4–6, which was likely written at about the same time. Both letters describe putting off the old self and putting on the new, the importance of worship and thanksgiving, and the shape of Christian households. Colossians expresses many of these truths in a shorter, more compact form.

### Seek the Things Above (3:1–4)

The call to seek and set the mind on things above is studied below. [[v4|When Christ, our life, is revealed, then you will also be revealed with him in glory]]. Christ is not merely part of believers' lives; He is their life. Right now, their true life is hidden. But when Christ returns, it will be revealed, and they will share His glory.

### Put to Death and Put Away (3:5–11)

[[v5|Put to death therefore your members which are on the earth: sexual immorality, uncleanness, depraved passion, evil desire, and covetousness, which is idolatry]]. "Members which are on the earth" refers to the parts of the old nature still at work in believers. Paul uses strong language: "put to death." These are not habits to be managed but sins to be killed. The list moves from outward acts to inner desires. As in Ephesians 5:5, greed is called idolatry because it makes things into a god.

[[v6|For these things’ sake the wrath of God comes on the children of disobedience]]. [[v7|You also once walked in those, when you lived in them]]. The Colossians had once lived this way, but no longer.

[[v8|but now you must put them all away: anger, wrath, malice, slander, and shameful speaking out of your mouth]]. The second list focuses on sins of the temper and tongue, those that destroy relationships. "Put away" uses the image of taking off dirty clothes.

[[v9|Don’t lie to one another, seeing that you have put off the old man with his doings]], [[v10|and have put on the new man, who is being renewed in knowledge after the image of his Creator]]. Here Paul says believers have already put off the old self and put on the new, decisively at conversion. Now they are to live in line with what is already true. The new self is "being renewed," an ongoing process of becoming more like Christ, who is the image of God (Colossians 1:15). God is restoring in believers the image that sin had damaged (Genesis 1:26–27).

[[v11|where there can’t be Greek and Jew, circumcision and uncircumcision, barbarian, Scythian, bondservant, or free person; but Christ is all, and in all]]. In the new humanity, the divisions of the old world lose their power. "Greek and Jew" was a religious and ethnic divide. "Barbarian" was a Greek term for anyone who did not speak Greek. "Scythian" referred to people from the region north of the Black Sea, regarded by Greeks and Romans as the most uncivilized of all. "Bondservant or free person" was the deepest social divide. In Christ, none of these labels determines a person's worth or standing. Christ is everything, and He lives in all His people equally.

### Put On the New Clothing (3:12–17)

The qualities believers are to put on, the role of peace and the word of Christ, and the call to do all in Jesus' name are studied below.

### Christian Households (3:18–4:1)

[[v18|Wives, be in subjection to your husbands, as is fitting in the Lord]]. [[v19|Husbands, love your wives, and don’t be bitter against them]]. This is a shorter version of Paul's teaching in Ephesians 5:22–33, which is discussed more fully there. "As is fitting in the Lord" places the wife's response within her relationship with Christ, which means it can never require her to sin or to endure abuse. Husbands are commanded to love their wives and not to be harsh or bitter toward them. In a culture where husbands held great power, this was a significant limit on that power. Christians hold different views on the roles of husbands and wives, but all agree that marriage must be marked by love, respect, and safety, never by control or harm.

[[v20|Children, obey your parents in all things, for this pleases the Lord]]. [[v21|Fathers, don’t provoke your children, so that they won’t be discouraged]]. As in Ephesians 6:1–4, children are addressed as members of the church, and fathers are warned against harshness that crushes a child's spirit. "In all things" assumes parents who are guiding their children in the Lord; it never requires a child to do wrong or to submit to abuse.

[[v22|Servants, obey in all things those who are your masters according to the flesh, not just when they are looking, as men pleasers, but in singleness of heart, fearing God]]. As in Ephesians 6:5–9, Paul addresses believers living within the Roman system of slavery. This passage does not endorse slavery as God's design; the principles behind the gospel, including the equality of all in Christ that Paul has just declared in verse 11, eventually led many Christians to oppose slavery. Paul's words are pastoral guidance for those living within a system they could not change. Their lasting principle for work is studied below. [[v25|But he who does wrong will receive again for the wrong that he has done, and there is no partiality]]. God judges impartially. This warning applies to both slaves and masters, as Colossians 4:1 shows.

## Important Verses Explained

### Colossians 3:1–3 — Your Life Is Hidden with Christ in God

verse: Colossians 3:1-3

#### Simple Explanation

"If then you were raised together with Christ." The "if" means "since." Believers have been united with Christ in His resurrection (Colossians 2:12). This is not a future hope only; it is already true in a spiritual sense.

"Seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated on the right hand of God." Since believers have been raised with Christ, they should pursue the things of His kingdom. "Seated on the right hand of God" describes Christ's position of supreme authority (Psalm 110:1). The things "above" are the realities of His kingdom: His righteousness, His love, His purposes.

"Set your mind on the things that are above, not on the things that are on the earth." This goes deeper than seeking; it concerns the whole direction of one's thinking. "Things that are on the earth" does not mean the physical world is evil. It means the values and pursuits of life apart from God, such as the sinful desires listed in verse 5, or the human regulations criticized in chapter 2.

"For you died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God." Believers died with Christ to their old way of life. Their new life is now "hidden with Christ in God." "Hidden" means both secure and not yet fully visible. Their life is kept safe in God, where no one can take it. And its full glory is not yet seen; it will be revealed when Christ returns (v4).

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses describe the foundation of Christian ethics. Believers are not trying to become new people through their own efforts. They already are new people, raised with Christ. Their task is to live out who they already are. This is why rules alone cannot produce holiness: holiness flows from identity, from union with the risen Christ.

Setting the mind on things above is not escapism. It does not mean ignoring earthly responsibilities or the needs of the world. In fact, the rest of the chapter shows that a heavenly mind leads to very practical, earthly changes: kindness, forgiveness, faithful work, and loving relationships. Those whose minds are set on Christ become more useful on earth, not less.

The phrase "your life is hidden with Christ in God" is deeply comforting. A believer's true life cannot be destroyed by suffering, failure, loss, or even death. It is safe with Christ, held in God Himself. Jesus said that no one can snatch His sheep out of His hand or the Father's hand (John 10:28–29).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Every day, believers' minds are pulled toward many concerns: work, money, relationships, news, entertainment, and worries. These verses invite them to deliberately turn their minds toward Christ and His kingdom. This might involve beginning the day with prayer and Scripture, pausing during the day to remember Christ's presence, and evaluating priorities in light of eternity.

When believers feel insecure, these verses remind them that their life is hidden with Christ in God. Their identity and future are secure in Him, regardless of what happens on earth.

Setting the mind on things above also helps in moments of temptation. When the pull of old desires is strong, remembering that one has died and been raised with Christ can bring strength to choose differently.

### Colossians 3:12–14 — Clothed with Compassion and Love

verse: Colossians 3:12-14

#### Simple Explanation

Having described what to "put away," Paul now describes what to "put on." He begins with believers' identity: "as God’s chosen ones, holy and beloved." These three titles were once used for Israel (Deuteronomy 7:6–8). Now they belong to all who are in Christ, Jew and Gentile. Believers are chosen by God, set apart for Him, and deeply loved. Their new behavior flows from this identity.

Paul lists qualities to "put on," like clothing. "A heart of compassion": deep, heartfelt mercy for others. "Kindness": goodness shown in practical ways. "Lowliness" or humility: not thinking too highly of oneself. "Humility," which may also be translated "gentleness" or "meekness": a gentle, considerate spirit. "Perseverance" or patience: enduring others' faults without anger.

Verse 13 describes how these qualities work in practice: "bearing with one another, and forgiving each other, if any man has a complaint against any; even as Christ forgave you, so you also do." Believers will have complaints against one another. Paul does not pretend otherwise. But they are called to bear with each other's weaknesses and to forgive, following Christ's example.

Verse 14: "Above all these things, walk in love, which is the bond of perfection." Love is like the outer garment or belt that holds all the others together. It is "the bond of perfection," the tie that binds everything into complete unity.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These qualities are the character of Christ Himself. He was compassionate toward the suffering, kind to the outcast, humble and gentle in heart, and patient with His slow-to-learn disciples. When believers put on these qualities, they are putting on Christ (Romans 13:14; Galatians 3:27).

The command to forgive "even as Christ forgave you" sets the measure for forgiveness. Christ forgave completely, freely, and at great cost. Believers who have received such forgiveness are called to extend it to others. As discussed in Ephesians 4:32, forgiveness does not mean excusing wrongdoing, immediately restoring trust, or remaining in danger. It means releasing the desire for revenge and entrusting justice to God, while still seeking safety and accountability where needed.

Love as "the bond of perfection" echoes 1 Corinthians 13, where Paul says that without love, even the greatest gifts are nothing. Love is not just one virtue among many; it is the one that unites all the others and makes them complete.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

The image of putting on clothing suggests a daily choice. Each morning, believers can ask God to help them clothe themselves with compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, and patience for the day ahead.

These qualities are especially tested in close relationships, where complaints and irritations naturally arise. Believers can practice bearing with others' quirks and weaknesses, letting go of minor offenses, and forgiving more serious ones, remembering how much Christ has forgiven them.

When forgiveness feels hard, believers can begin by praying for the person who hurt them, asking God for help, and seeking wise counsel. Forgiveness is often a process, and God's grace is sufficient for each step.

### Colossians 3:15–16 — The Peace and Word of Christ

verse: Colossians 3:15-16

#### Simple Explanation

"And let the peace of God rule in your hearts." The word "rule" was used of an umpire in athletic contests, the one who made decisions and settled disputes. Paul pictures God's peace acting as the umpire in believers' hearts, guiding decisions and settling conflicts. Some manuscripts read "the peace of Christ."

"To which also you were called in one body." This peace is not only personal but communal. Believers were called into one body, the church, and peace is meant to govern their relationships with one another.

"And be thankful." Again, Paul emphasizes thanksgiving.

Verse 16: "Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly." "The word of Christ" may mean the message about Christ, the gospel, or Christ's own teaching. It is to "dwell" in believers, to live there, to be at home, and to do so "richly," abundantly.

"In all wisdom teaching and admonishing one another." When Christ's word dwells richly in believers, they are able to teach and encourage one another, and to warn and correct one another, with wisdom.

"With psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs, singing with grace in your heart to the Lord." Worship through song is one way the word of Christ dwells in believers and is shared among them. Singing teaches, encourages, and expresses gratitude. "With grace in your heart" can also mean "with thankfulness in your hearts."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses describe a community shaped by Christ. His peace governs their relationships, His word fills their hearts, and their worship overflows in song and thanksgiving. This is a picture of a healthy church.

The parallel passage in Ephesians 5:18–19 says that being "filled with the Spirit" leads to speaking to one another in psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs. Here, letting the word of Christ dwell richly produces the same result. The Spirit and the word work together. The Spirit fills believers as they are saturated in Christ's word.

Singing has always been central to the worship of God's people. Israel sang the Psalms; the early church sang hymns about Christ (perhaps including Colossians 1:15–20 and Philippians 2:6–11). Songs carry truth into the heart and memory in a way that teaching alone sometimes cannot.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

When facing decisions or conflicts, believers can ask whether a choice leads toward the peace of Christ. Sometimes a lack of peace signals that something needs to be reconsidered. This is not merely a feeling, but a discernment grounded in Scripture and prayer.

Letting the word of Christ dwell richly involves regular, deep engagement with Scripture: reading, studying, memorizing, and meditating on it. As it fills the heart, it shapes thinking, speech, and relationships.

Believers can also make singing part of their spiritual life, both in church and in private. Songs of worship can teach, comfort, and strengthen faith. Encouraging one another through words of Scripture and songs of praise builds up the whole community.

### Colossians 3:23–24 — Work Heartily as for the Lord

verse: Colossians 3:23-24

#### Simple Explanation

Paul's words here were addressed first to enslaved believers, whose work was often unrewarded and unnoticed. But the principle applies to all believers in every kind of work. "And whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for men."

"Whatever you do" covers every kind of work: paid or unpaid, important or ordinary, seen or unseen. "Work heartily" means wholeheartedly, with energy and sincerity, from the soul. "As for the Lord and not for men." The ultimate audience of a believer's work is the Lord. Even when human supervisors are unfair or unappreciative, believers can work as an offering to Christ.

Verse 24 gives the motivation: "knowing that from the Lord you will receive the reward of the inheritance; for you serve the Lord Christ." Enslaved people in the Roman world could not legally inherit property. Paul tells them that they will receive an inheritance from the Lord. Their faithful work is seen by God and will be rewarded. "For you serve the Lord Christ": whatever their earthly status, their true Master is Christ.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage transforms the meaning of work. In the ancient world, manual labor was often looked down upon, especially by the wealthy. Paul declares that all work done for Christ has dignity and eternal significance. Cooking, cleaning, building, farming, caring for children, managing accounts: all can be done as service to the Lord.

This passage also brings justice into view. Verse 25 warns that wrongdoers will be repaid, and Colossians 4:1 commands masters to treat their servants justly and fairly, knowing that they too have a Master in heaven. Paul does not ask the oppressed to work faithfully while ignoring the sins of the oppressor. God sees both, and He judges impartially.

Working "as for the Lord" does not mean accepting exploitation as God's will. Believers may seek fair treatment, better conditions, and justice in the workplace. But whatever their circumstances, they can find dignity and purpose in offering their work to Christ.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many people find their work tedious, unappreciated, or frustrating. These verses offer a new perspective: every task can be an act of worship. Believers can work with excellence and integrity, not to impress others, but to honor Christ.

This truth also brings freedom from the need for recognition. When work goes unnoticed by others, believers can remember that the Lord sees and will reward faithful service.

For those in positions of authority, these verses and the following verse call for fair and just treatment of those who work for them. Employers, managers, and leaders answer to Christ for how they treat others.

Finally, this passage encourages believers who do unpaid work, such as caring for family members, volunteering, or serving in church, that their labor matters deeply to God.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Colossians 3 reveals the true source of holy living: union with the risen Christ. Rules and rituals cannot change the heart, but believers who have died and been raised with Christ have a new life. That life is hidden with Christ in God, secure and safe, and it will be revealed in glory when He returns. Believers are called to seek the things above and to set their minds on Christ.

From that heavenly focus come very practical changes. Believers are to put to death sexual immorality, impurity, evil desire, and greed, and to put away anger, malice, slander, and lies. They have already put off the old self and put on the new, which is being renewed in the image of its Creator. In this new humanity, ethnic and social divisions lose their power, for Christ is all and in all.

As God's chosen, holy, and beloved people, believers are to clothe themselves with compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, and patience, bearing with one another and forgiving as Christ forgave them. Above all, they are to put on love, which binds everything together. Christ's peace is to rule their hearts, His word is to dwell in them richly, and their worship is to overflow in songs and thanksgiving. Whatever they do, in word or deed, they are to do it in the name of the Lord Jesus.

This new life reaches into homes and workplaces. Marriages are to be marked by love and respect, never by harshness or harm. Parents are to raise children without provoking them. And in every kind of work, believers can work wholeheartedly as for the Lord, knowing that He sees, He rewards, and He judges with perfect fairness. All of life becomes worship when it is offered to Christ.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You that I have been raised with You and that my life is hidden with You in God. Help me to seek the things above and set my mind on You. Give me grace to put to death what belongs to my old life and to put away anger, malice, slander, and lies. Thank You that I am being renewed in Your image, and that in You there are no divisions that separate Your people. As one chosen, holy, and beloved, help me to clothe myself with compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, and patience. Teach me to bear with others and to forgive as You have forgiven me. Above all, fill me with love. Let Your peace rule in my heart and Your word dwell in me richly. Whatever I do today, in word or deed, let me do it in Your name, giving thanks to the Father through You. Help me to work heartily, as for You and not for others. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
