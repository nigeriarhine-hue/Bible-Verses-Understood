# Revelation 7
theme: Sealed by God and Standing Before the Throne
main: Between the sixth and seventh seals, the action pauses. John sees four angels holding back the four winds of the earth so that no harm comes to land, sea, or trees. Another angel rises from the east with the seal of the living God and calls out that no harm should be done until the servants of God have been sealed on their foreheads. John hears the number of those sealed: 144,000, twelve thousand from each of the twelve tribes of Israel. Then he looks and sees a great multitude that no one could count, from every nation, tribe, people, and language, standing before the throne and the Lamb, wearing white robes and holding palm branches. They cry out that salvation belongs to God on the throne and to the Lamb. All the angels, elders, and living creatures fall on their faces and worship God. One of the elders asks John who these people in white robes are, and then explains: they have come out of the great tribulation; they have washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb. Therefore they are before God's throne and serve Him day and night, and He will shelter them with His presence. They will never again hunger or thirst, nor will the sun or scorching heat strike them, for the Lamb at the center of the throne will be their shepherd and lead them to springs of living water, and God will wipe away every tear from their eyes.

## Chapter Background and Story

Revelation 6 ended with a terrifying question: [[Revelation 6:17|for the great day of his wrath has come, and who is able to stand?]] Chapter 7 answers that question. Before the seventh seal is opened, the action pauses to show that God's people are protected and that a vast multitude will stand before His throne.

This pattern of pausing between the sixth and seventh items appears again with the trumpets (10:1–11:14). These interludes reassure believers that, whatever judgments fall on the world, God knows and keeps His own.

### The Winds Held Back (7:1)

[[v1|After this, I saw four angels standing at the four corners of the earth, holding the four winds of the earth, so that no wind would blow on the earth, or on the sea, or on any tree]]. The "four corners" and "four winds" are ways of describing the whole earth in every direction (compare Jeremiah 49:36; Zechariah 6:5). The winds may represent the destructive forces of judgment, perhaps connected with the four horsemen of chapter 6. The angels hold them back. Judgment is restrained until God's purposes for His people are accomplished.

### The Sealing of God's Servants (7:2–8)

The sealing of God's servants and the 144,000 are studied below.

[[v5|of the tribe of Judah twelve thousand were sealed, of the tribe of Reuben twelve thousand, of the tribe of Gad twelve thousand,]] The list of tribes in verses 5–8 is unusual. Judah is listed first, rather than Reuben, Jacob's firstborn, probably because the Messiah, the Lion of the tribe of Judah (5:5), came from Judah. The tribe of Dan is missing, and Joseph and his son Manasseh are both included, while Ephraim is not named. Interpreters have suggested several reasons for Dan's absence. Dan was associated with idolatry in Israel's history (Judges 18:30; 1 Kings 12:28–30), and some early Christian writers connected Dan with the coming of antichrist, though Scripture does not teach this. Others think the list simply reflects one of the many ways the tribes were arranged in the Old Testament, which vary considerably. The point of the list is clearly the completeness of God's people, with twelve tribes of twelve thousand each.

### The Great Multitude (7:9–12)

The vision of the great multitude is studied below.

[[v11|All the angels were standing around the throne, the elders, and the four living creatures; and they fell on their faces before his throne, and worshiped God,]] [[v12|saying, “Amen! Blessing, glory, wisdom, thanksgiving, honor, power, and might, be to our God forever and ever! Amen.”]] The whole heavenly court responds to the multitude's praise with worship of its own. Seven qualities are ascribed to God, expressing complete and perfect praise, just as seven were ascribed to the Lamb in 5:12.

### Who Are These? (7:13–17)

[[v13|One of the elders answered, saying to me, “These who are arrayed in the white robes, who are they, and where did they come from?”]] The elder asks a question so that he can explain the vision. John responds humbly: [[v14|I told him, “My lord, you know.”]] The elder's explanation is studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### Revelation 7:2–4 — Sealed on Their Foreheads

verse: Revelation 7:2-4

#### Simple Explanation

John sees "another angel ascend from the sunrise, having the seal of the living God." In the ancient world, a seal was a mark of ownership and protection. Kings and officials used seals to mark documents and property as their own and to guarantee that they were not tampered with.

The angel cries out to the four angels holding back the winds: "Don’t harm the earth, the sea, or the trees, until we have sealed the bondservants of our God on their foreheads!" Before judgment falls, God marks His servants as His own.

Then John hears the number of those sealed: "one hundred forty-four thousand, sealed out of every tribe of the children of Israel." Twelve thousand are sealed from each of the twelve tribes.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The sealing recalls Ezekiel 9, where a man with a writing kit marks the foreheads of those in Jerusalem who grieve over its sins, so that they are spared when judgment comes. It also recalls the Passover, when the blood of the lamb on the doorposts protected Israel's households (Exodus 12:13). In the New Testament, believers are said to be sealed with the Holy Spirit as a mark that they belong to God (2 Corinthians 1:22; Ephesians 1:13; 4:30).

The seal does not mean that God's servants will escape all suffering. Many believers in Revelation suffer and even die for their faith. But it means that they belong to God, that He knows them, and that nothing can separate them from Him or destroy their eternal life. Later in the book, those who follow the beast receive a different mark on their foreheads or hands (13:16–17). Every person ultimately bears the mark of the one they serve.

The identity of the 144,000 has been understood in several ways. Some futurist interpreters understand them as a specific group of Jewish believers in Jesus during a future time of tribulation, perhaps serving as witnesses to the world. Others see them as Jewish Christians in the early church. Many interpreters, across several traditions, understand the 144,000 as a symbolic picture of the whole people of God, both Jewish and Gentile believers, described in terms of Israel's twelve tribes. They note that the number is built from twelve (the tribes) times twelve (perhaps the apostles) times a thousand (a vast number), expressing completeness. They also note that John hears the number of the 144,000 and then sees a countless multitude, just as he heard about a Lion and then saw a Lamb (5:5–6). On this view, the 144,000 and the great multitude are two ways of describing the same people: God's complete and ordered people, and His vast, international family.

Whatever view one holds, this passage affirms God's faithfulness to Israel and His intention to keep a complete people for Himself. It should never be used to diminish the place of the Jewish people in God's purposes, nor to suggest that only a limited number of people can be saved.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can take great comfort in knowing that they are sealed by God. They belong to Him, and He knows them by name. Whatever happens in the world, their identity and destiny are secure.

This passage also reminds believers that God's protection is not always protection from suffering, but protection through suffering. Believers may face trials, but they will never be lost.

Finally, the contrast between the seal of God and the mark of the beast invites believers to consider whose mark they bear. Every life is shaped by some ultimate loyalty. Believers are called to live as those who belong wholly to God.

### Revelation 7:9–10 — A Great Multitude from Every Nation

verse: Revelation 7:9-10

#### Simple Explanation

After hearing the number of the sealed, John looks and sees "a great multitude which no man could count." The crowd is so vast that it cannot be numbered.

This multitude comes "out of every nation and of all tribes, peoples, and languages." Every ethnic group, every culture, and every language is represented.

They are "standing before the throne and before the Lamb." In chapter 6, people cried out, "who is able to stand?" Here is the answer: these people stand, not in fear, but in worship.

They are "dressed in white robes," symbols of purity and victory, "with palm branches in their hands." Palm branches were used in celebrations of victory and in the Jewish Feast of Tabernacles. They were also waved when Jesus entered Jerusalem (John 12:13).

They cry out with a loud voice: "Salvation be to our God, who sits on the throne, and to the Lamb!" They give all the credit for their salvation to God and the Lamb.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This vision is the fulfillment of God's promise to Abraham that through him all the families of the earth would be blessed (Genesis 12:3). It also fulfills the promise that Abraham's descendants would be as countless as the stars (Genesis 15:5). The people of God are not small or limited. They form a vast multitude beyond counting.

The diversity of the multitude is important. People from every nation, tribe, people, and language stand together before the throne. Their cultural differences are not erased; they are still recognized as coming from every nation and language. But they are united in worship of the one God and the Lamb. This is the goal toward which the church moves: a diverse family united in Christ.

The song of the multitude places salvation entirely in God's hands. They do not praise themselves for their faithfulness or endurance. "Salvation" belongs to God and to the Lamb. This is the heart of the gospel: salvation is a gift of grace.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This vision gives believers a glimpse of their future and their family. They will one day stand with believers from every nation and language before God's throne. This can shape how they view people from other cultures now: as fellow members of the same family, or as people whom God invites into it.

Churches can reflect this vision by welcoming people of all backgrounds, rejecting prejudice, and supporting the work of sharing the gospel with every people group.

This passage also reminds believers to give God all the credit for their salvation. Their place before the throne will not be earned by their own goodness but given by God's grace through the Lamb.

### Revelation 7:14–17 — Washed in the Blood of the Lamb

verse: Revelation 7:14-17

#### Simple Explanation

The elder explains who the multitude are: "These are those who came out of the great suffering." They have passed through great trial and have come out on the other side.

Then he says: "They washed their robes and made them white in the Lamb’s blood." It is a startling image: blood that makes robes white. Their purity comes not from their own efforts but from the sacrifice of Jesus.

Verse 15 describes their new life: "Therefore they are before the throne of God, and they serve him day and night in his temple." And God "will spread his tabernacle over them," sheltering them with His presence.

Verse 16 promises: "They will never be hungry or thirsty any more. The sun won’t beat on them, nor any heat." Every form of suffering will be removed.

Verse 17 gives the reason: "for the Lamb who is in the middle of the throne shepherds them and leads them to springs of life-giving waters. And God will wipe away every tear from their eyes."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

"The great suffering," often translated "the great tribulation," has been understood in different ways. Futurist interpreters often see it as a specific period of intense tribulation before Christ's return. Others understand it as the suffering that God's people experience throughout the church age, which Jesus said His followers would face (John 16:33; Acts 14:22). Preterists connect it with the persecutions of the first century. In every view, the multitude has endured suffering and come through it into God's presence.

The image of robes made white in blood expresses the heart of the gospel. Sin stains, and no human effort can make a person clean. But the blood of Jesus, His sacrificial death, cleanses completely (1 John 1:7). Isaiah had promised: [[Isaiah 1:18|Though your sins are as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow]].

The promises of verses 15–17 draw on Isaiah 49:10 and Isaiah 25:8. God's presence shelters His people like the tabernacle in the wilderness. Their suffering ends. And the Lamb becomes their Shepherd. The image is beautiful: the Lamb who was slain now shepherds His flock, leading them to springs of living water, as in Psalm 23. And God Himself wipes away every tear. This tender promise is repeated at the climax of the book (Revelation 21:4).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage offers deep comfort to those who are suffering. Whatever trials believers face now, there will be a day when God wipes every tear from their eyes. Hunger, thirst, pain, and sorrow will be no more.

Believers who feel stained by sin or shame can find hope in the image of robes washed white in the Lamb's blood. No stain is too deep for Christ's cleansing. They can come to Him in faith and receive His forgiveness.

The picture of the Lamb as Shepherd invites believers to trust Him now. He leads His people to springs of living water, not only in the future but in the present, through His word, His Spirit, and His presence. Believers can follow Him daily, confident that He knows the way.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Revelation 7 answers the question raised at the end of chapter 6: Who is able to stand? Those who belong to God and have been washed in the blood of the Lamb will stand before His throne. This truth gives believers deep security and hope.

The sealing of God's servants reminds believers that they belong to God. They are marked as His own. Whatever judgments fall on the world, God knows and keeps His people. His protection may not spare them from all suffering, but it guarantees that they will never be lost.

The vision of the great multitude shows the wonderful scope of God's salvation. People from every nation, tribe, people, and language will stand together before the throne. Believers are part of this vast, diverse family. They can welcome others, reject prejudice, and join in God's mission to every people.

The elder's explanation reminds believers that their salvation rests on the Lamb's sacrifice. Their robes are made white not by their own goodness but by His blood. And their future is glorious: they will serve God in His presence, sheltered by Him, free from all suffering, shepherded by the Lamb, and comforted by God, who will wipe away every tear.

## Chapter Prayer

Father, thank You that You have sealed me as Your own, and that nothing can separate me from Your care. Thank You for the vast multitude from every nation, tribe, people, and language whom You have redeemed through the Lamb. Help me to love all who belong to Your family and to share Your good news with every people. Thank You that my robes have been washed white in the blood of the Lamb, and that my salvation comes from You alone. When I face suffering, remind me that one day I will stand before Your throne, sheltered by Your presence, free from hunger, thirst, and pain. Lord Jesus, be my Shepherd now; lead me to springs of living water. And until the day You wipe away every tear, hold me close and give me hope. Salvation belongs to You, our God, and to the Lamb. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
