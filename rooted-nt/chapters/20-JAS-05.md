# James 5
theme: Patience, Prayer, and Restoring the Wanderer
main: James issues a fierce warning to the rich who hoard wealth and exploit others: their riches are rotting, their clothes moth-eaten, their gold corroded, and the withheld wages of their workers cry out to the Lord of Armies, while they live in luxury and condemn the innocent. He then turns to the believers who suffer and urges them to be patient until the Lord's coming, like a farmer waiting for the rain and harvest, strengthening their hearts because the Lord's coming is near. They should not grumble against one another, but follow the example of the prophets and of Job, whose endurance revealed that the Lord is full of compassion and mercy. Their yes should be yes and their no, no, without oaths. Those who suffer should pray, the cheerful should sing, and the sick should call the elders to pray over them and anoint them with oil in the Lord's name; the prayer of faith will restore the sick, and any sins will be forgiven. Believers are to confess their sins to one another and pray for one another, because the prayer of a righteous person is powerful, as Elijah's prayers were. Finally, whoever brings back a wanderer from the truth saves a soul from death and covers a multitude of sins.

## Chapter Background and Story

James's readers were largely poor believers, many of them working in fields owned by wealthy landowners. In the first-century world, agricultural laborers were often paid daily, and withholding their wages could mean their families went hungry. The Law of Moses commanded that workers be paid promptly (Leviticus 19:13; Deuteronomy 24:14–15). Yet many wealthy landowners exploited their workers, sometimes using the courts to oppress them (James 2:6).

The first section of this chapter (vv1–6) is addressed to these wealthy oppressors, in the style of the Old Testament prophets, who denounced injustice against the poor. Whether or not the rich would ever hear James's words, the believers who did would be reassured that God sees and will judge.

The rest of the chapter turns to the believers themselves. They are to be patient in suffering, honest in their speech, and faithful in prayer. James ends his letter not with a formal greeting but with a call to restore those who wander from the truth.

### Warning to the Rich (5:1–6)

[[v1|Come now, you rich, weep and howl for your miseries that are coming on you]]. James echoes the prophets who warned of coming judgment on oppressors (Isaiah 13:6; Amos 6:1–7). [[v2|Your riches are corrupted and your garments are moth-eaten]]. [[v3|Your gold and your silver are corroded, and their corrosion will be for a testimony against you and will eat your flesh like fire. You have laid up your treasure in the last days]]. Wealth that is hoarded decays. Jesus warned against storing up treasure on earth, where moth and rust destroy (Matthew 6:19–20). Gold and silver do not literally rust, but James uses the image to show that hoarded wealth is worthless. The corrosion is evidence against the rich, showing that they kept what should have been used to help others. "In the last days" means they are hoarding when the end is near, foolishly preparing for the wrong future. The cry of the unpaid workers is studied below.

[[v5|You have lived in luxury on the earth, and taken your pleasure. You have nourished your hearts as in a day of slaughter]]. The rich indulged themselves without restraint, like cattle fattening themselves on the very day they will be slaughtered, unaware of the judgment coming.

[[v6|You have condemned and you have murdered the righteous one. He doesn’t resist you]]. The rich used their power, including courts, to condemn and even cause the death of innocent people who had no power to resist. Some interpreters also see an allusion to Jesus, the Righteous One, who did not resist those who condemned Him (Acts 3:14; 7:52).

James is not condemning wealth itself, but the hoarding, exploitation, luxury, and injustice that often accompany it. Scripture elsewhere calls the wealthy to be generous and rich in good works (1 Timothy 6:17–19).

### Patience Until the Lord Comes (5:7–11)

The call to patience is studied below. [[v9|Don’t grumble, brothers, against one another, so that you won’t be judged. Behold, the judge stands at the door]]. Suffering can make people irritable and quick to complain about one another. James warns against this. The Judge is near, "at the door," ready to enter.

[[v10|Take, brothers, for an example of suffering and of perseverance, the prophets who spoke in the name of the Lord]]. The prophets suffered for faithfully speaking God's word (Matthew 5:12; Hebrews 11:36–38). They are examples of patient endurance.

[[v11|Behold, we call them blessed who endured. You have heard of the perseverance of Job and have seen the Lord in the outcome, and how the Lord is full of compassion and mercy]]. Job lost his wealth, his children, and his health. He questioned and lamented, but he did not abandon God. At the end of his story, God restored him (Job 42:10–17). "The outcome" shows God's purpose: He is full of compassion and mercy. Job's example shows that endurance does not mean silent, emotionless acceptance. Job cried out honestly to God. Endurance means holding on to God even while struggling.

### Honest Speech (5:12)

[[v12|But above all things, my brothers, don’t swear—not by heaven, or by the earth, or by any other oath; but let your “yes” be “yes”, and your “no”, “no”, so that you don’t fall into hypocrisy]]. This echoes Jesus' teaching in Matthew 5:33–37. People often swore oaths to make their statements seem more trustworthy, or used clever oaths to avoid commitment. Jesus and James call for simple, consistent honesty. Believers should be so truthful that their word needs no oath to back it up. Christians have differed on whether this forbids all oaths, such as in a court of law, or only careless and deceptive oaths; most understand the main point to be honesty in everyday speech.

### Prayer, Healing, and Confession (5:13–18)

[[v13|Is any among you suffering? Let him pray. Is any cheerful? Let him sing praises]]. Every circumstance of life can be brought to God. In hardship, believers pray. In joy, they praise. The study below explores prayer for the sick and the power of prayer.

[[v17|Elijah was a man with a nature like ours, and he prayed earnestly that it might not rain, and it didn’t rain on the earth for three years and six months]]. [[v18|He prayed again, and the sky gave rain, and the earth produced its fruit]]. Elijah was an ordinary human being, with weaknesses and fears like everyone else. After his great victory on Mount Carmel, he became so discouraged that he asked to die (1 Kings 19:4). Yet his prayers were powerful. 1 Kings 17–18 records the drought and the rain that followed his prayers. James's point is that God answers the prayers of ordinary believers, not only spiritual heroes.

## Important Verses Explained

### James 5:4 — The Cry of the Unpaid Worker

verse: James 5:4

#### Simple Explanation

"Behold, the wages of the laborers who mowed your fields, which you have kept back by fraud, cry out." The rich landowners had hired workers to harvest their fields but had refused to pay them, or had cheated them. James pictures the withheld wages themselves crying out, like a witness testifying against the rich.

"And the cries of those who reaped have entered into the ears of the Lord of Armies." The workers' cries for justice have reached God. "The Lord of Armies" (often translated "the Lord of Hosts") is a title emphasizing God's supreme power. He commands the armies of heaven. The oppressed may seem powerless, but their cause is heard by the most powerful One of all.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

God's law specifically protected workers. [[Deuteronomy 24:14|You shall not oppress a hired servant who is poor and needy]]... [[Deuteronomy 24:15|In his day you shall give him his wages, neither shall the sun go down on it, for he is poor and sets his heart on it, lest he cry against you to the LORD, and it be sin to you]]. James echoes this law. When workers are cheated, their cry rises to God.

The image of a cry reaching God's ears appears throughout Scripture. Abel's blood cried out from the ground (Genesis 4:10). Israel's cry in Egypt reached God, and He came down to rescue them (Exodus 3:7–8). God hears the cries of the oppressed.

This passage shows that economic injustice is a spiritual issue. How people treat workers, pay wages, and use wealth matters to God. The prophets repeatedly condemned those who exploited the poor (Amos 2:6–7; Micah 2:1–2; Jeremiah 22:13).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers who employ others, whether as business owners, managers, or simply as people who hire help, are called to pay fair wages promptly and treat workers justly.

Believers can also consider how their purchasing and investment choices affect workers elsewhere. Supporting fair labor practices and opposing exploitation reflect God's concern for the worker.

For those who have been cheated or exploited, this verse brings comfort: God hears their cry. They can seek justice through proper means and trust that the Lord of Armies is on the side of the oppressed.

### James 5:7–8 — Patient Like a Farmer

verse: James 5:7-8

#### Simple Explanation

"Be patient therefore, brothers, until the coming of the Lord." After warning the rich, James turns to the suffering believers. They are to be patient, enduring hardship without giving up, until Christ returns. The word for patience suggests a long-suffering endurance, the ability to hold on for a long time.

"Behold, the farmer waits for the precious fruit of the earth, being patient over it, until it receives the early and late rain." In the land of Israel, the "early rain" came in autumn, softening the ground for planting. The "late rain" came in spring, helping the crops mature before harvest. Farmers could not hurry the rain or the harvest. They had to wait patiently, trusting that it would come.

Verse 8: "You also be patient. Establish your hearts, for the coming of the Lord is at hand." Believers are to strengthen their hearts, standing firm and steady. The reason: "the coming of the Lord is at hand." Christ's return is near, and with it, justice and reward.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The image of the farmer teaches several lessons about patience. First, patience involves waiting for something that is certain. The farmer knows the rain will come; believers know Christ will return. Second, patience involves active faithfulness. The farmer does not sit idle; he works while he waits. Believers continue serving God while they wait. Third, patience trusts in God's timing. The farmer cannot control the rain; believers cannot control the timing of Christ's return or the end of their suffering.

"The coming of the Lord is at hand" has been the hope of believers in every generation. The New Testament writers lived with eager expectation of Christ's return, without knowing its timing (Matthew 24:36). From God's perspective, the time is short (2 Peter 3:8–9). The nearness of His coming gives believers strength to endure.

The call to "establish your hearts" reminds believers that patience is not passive resignation. It requires inner strength, rooted in trust in God.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers facing long seasons of hardship, illness, waiting, or injustice can take encouragement from the farmer. God's timing is not theirs, but His promises are sure.

Patience can be cultivated through prayer, remembering God's past faithfulness, and fixing hope on Christ's return. Believers can ask God to strengthen their hearts when they feel like giving up.

Finally, believers can continue to be faithful in the meantime, working, serving, and loving others, as the farmer continues to work while waiting for the harvest.

### James 5:14–16 — The Prayer of Faith

verse: James 5:14-16

#### Simple Explanation

"Is any among you sick? Let him call for the elders of the assembly." Those who are sick are encouraged to ask the church's leaders to come and pray. The initiative is with the sick person, and the response comes from the community's leaders.

"And let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord." Oil was used in the ancient world both as medicine (Luke 10:34) and as a symbol of God's presence and blessing. Jesus' disciples anointed the sick with oil and healed them (Mark 6:13). The anointing is done "in the name of the Lord," that is, by His authority and in dependence on Him.

Verse 15: "and the prayer of faith will heal him who is sick, and the Lord will raise him up." The healing comes from the Lord in response to prayer offered in faith. "Raise him up" may mean restore to health or lift from the sickbed.

"If he has committed sins, he will be forgiven." Sometimes, though not always, sickness may be connected with sin. If so, God offers forgiveness.

Verse 16: "Confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, that you may be healed." Healing is connected with confession and mutual prayer. Honest confession among believers brings healing, spiritual and sometimes physical.

"The insistent prayer of a righteous person is powerfully effective." The prayers of those who are right with God are powerful. "Righteous" here does not mean perfect but one who lives in faithful relationship with God.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses teach that God cares about the sick and responds to prayer. Christians have practiced anointing and prayer for the sick throughout history, though in different ways. Some traditions see anointing of the sick as a sacrament; others practice it as an act of prayer and faith; others emphasize prayer without anointing. All take seriously the call to pray for the sick.

It is important to read these verses alongside the rest of Scripture. Not every sickness is healed in this life, even when believers pray in faith. Paul left Trophimus sick in Miletus (2 Timothy 4:20); Timothy had frequent illnesses (1 Timothy 5:23); Paul himself lived with a thorn in the flesh that God did not remove (2 Corinthians 12:7–9). When healing does not come, it is not necessarily because of weak faith or unconfessed sin. Jesus rejected the assumption that sickness is always caused by personal sin (John 9:1–3). Ultimately, every believer will be "raised up" at the resurrection, when all sickness will be healed forever.

Prayer for healing also does not exclude medical care. Oil was itself a common medicine. Luke was a physician (Colossians 4:14). Seeking medical treatment and praying for healing go together; God often heals through doctors, medicine, and care.

The call to confess sins to one another highlights the healing power of honesty and community. Hidden sin can weigh heavily on a person, affecting their spiritual, emotional, and even physical well-being (Psalm 32:3–5). Confession to trusted believers, combined with prayer, brings release and healing. Such confession should take place in safe, trustworthy relationships, never under pressure or in settings where it could be misused.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers who are sick can ask church leaders and fellow believers to pray for them. There is no shame in asking for prayer. And believers can respond when others ask, coming alongside the sick with prayer and care.

Believers can pray in faith for healing while also seeking medical help, trusting God to work through both. When healing does not come, they can hold onto God's compassion and the hope of the resurrection, and they should never blame the sick person for a lack of faith.

Finally, believers can practice honest confession in trusted relationships, such as with a close friend, mentor, or pastor, and pray for one another. Confession and prayer bring healing and strengthen community.

### James 5:19–20 — Turning Back the Wanderer

verse: James 5:19-20

#### Simple Explanation

James ends his letter with a call to restoration. "Brothers, if any among you wanders from the truth." "Wanders" suggests drifting or straying, like a sheep wandering from the flock. James speaks of someone "among you," a member of the community who has drifted from the truth, whether in belief or in behavior.

"And someone turns him back." Another believer helps bring the wanderer back. Every believer can play this role.

Verse 20: "let him know that he who turns a sinner from the error of his way will save a soul from death." Bringing someone back from wandering is a matter of life and death. Persisting in sin leads to spiritual death (James 1:15). Restoring a wanderer rescues them.

"And will cover a multitude of sins." The wanderer's sins are covered, that is, forgiven, when they turn back to God. Proverbs 10:12 says, [[Proverbs 10:12|love covers all wrongs]], and Peter writes that [[1 Peter 4:8|love covers a multitude of sins]].

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

James ends his letter not with a formal farewell but with a pastoral concern for those who stray. This reflects God's heart. Jesus told the parable of the shepherd who leaves the ninety-nine sheep to search for the one that is lost (Luke 15:3–7). God does not give up on those who wander.

This passage also shows that believers are responsible for one another. As Hebrews 3:13 says, believers are to encourage one another daily so that no one is hardened by sin. Paul writes that those who are spiritual should gently restore someone caught in sin (Galatians 6:1).

Restoration requires love, humility, and courage. It is not about condemning or shaming the wanderer, but about lovingly helping them find their way back. The goal is life and forgiveness.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can watch for those in their community who are drifting from faith, perhaps no longer attending, struggling with doubt, or slipping into harmful patterns. A phone call, a visit, a kind word, or an invitation can make a difference.

Restoring a wanderer requires gentleness and patience. Believers can listen, show love, share truth humbly, and pray for them, trusting God to work.

Those who have wandered themselves can take comfort that the door is always open. God welcomes those who return, and their sins are covered by His grace.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

James 5 begins with a strong warning against wealthy people who hoard riches, live in luxury, and exploit their workers. God hears the cries of the oppressed, and He will judge injustice. Believers are called to use their resources justly and generously, to pay fair wages, and to care for those who are exploited.

The chapter then encourages suffering believers to be patient until the Lord comes, like a farmer waiting for rain and harvest. They are to strengthen their hearts, avoid grumbling against one another, and follow the example of the prophets and of Job, whose story shows that the Lord is full of compassion and mercy. Endurance means holding on to God even while struggling honestly.

James calls for simple honesty in speech, letting "yes" mean yes and "no" mean no. He encourages believers to bring every situation to God: to pray in suffering, to praise in joy, and to call for prayer when sick. The prayer of a righteous person is powerful, as Elijah's prayers were. Believers can pray for healing while also seeking medical care, trusting God's compassion even when healing does not come in this life.

Confession and mutual prayer bring healing to individuals and communities. And the letter ends with a call to restore those who wander from the truth. Believers are responsible for one another. Those who lovingly turn a wanderer back save a soul from death and cover a multitude of sins. In this way, James's practical wisdom comes to its goal: a community of genuine faith, marked by justice, patience, honesty, prayer, and restoring love.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord of Armies, You hear the cries of the oppressed. Give me a heart for justice, and help me to use whatever resources I have generously and fairly. Keep me from hoarding, greed, and self-indulgence. When I suffer, give me patience like a farmer waiting for the rain, and strengthen my heart, for Your coming is near. Keep me from grumbling against others. Thank You that You are full of compassion and mercy, as You showed to Job. Make my words honest and simple. Teach me to pray in every circumstance, to praise You in joy, and to ask for prayer when I am weak. I pray for all who are sick; heal them according to Your will, and comfort those who are not healed in this life with the hope of the resurrection. Give me courage to confess my sins and to pray for others. And make me a restorer of those who wander, bringing them back to You with love. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
