# Hebrews 10
theme: One Sacrifice Forever, So Let Us Draw Near
main: The Law was only a shadow of the good things to come, and its sacrifices, repeated year after year, could never make worshipers perfect; in fact, they served as an annual reminder of sins, because the blood of bulls and goats cannot take sins away. Quoting Psalm 40, the author shows that Christ came to do God's will by offering His body, and by that will believers have been made holy through His offering once for all. While earthly priests stood daily offering the same sacrifices, Christ offered one sacrifice for sins forever and sat down at God's right hand, for by one offering He has perfected forever those being made holy, as the new covenant promised: "I will remember their sins and their iniquities no more." Since believers now have confidence to enter the Most Holy Place by the blood of Jesus, through the new and living way He opened through the curtain of His flesh, they should draw near to God with sincere hearts and full assurance, hold firmly to their hope, and stir one another up to love and good works, not neglecting to meet together. The author warns that those who deliberately keep on sinning after knowing the truth have no other sacrifice left, only judgment. He reminds his readers of their earlier endurance under persecution and urges them not to throw away their confidence, for they need endurance, and they are not among those who shrink back but among those who have faith and are saved.

## Chapter Background and Story

Hebrews 10 brings the author's long explanation of Christ's priesthood and sacrifice to its climax. Chapters 7–9 compared Christ's priesthood, covenant, and sanctuary with those of the old covenant. Now the focus is on the sacrifice itself. Why were the old sacrifices repeated endlessly? Because they could not truly deal with sin. Why does Christ's sacrifice never need to be repeated? Because it is perfect and complete.

Then, at verse 19, the author turns from teaching to application. If all this is true, what should believers do? The answer is a series of encouragements: draw near to God, hold fast to hope, and encourage one another. This is followed by the fourth warning in Hebrews, and then by a warm reminder of the readers' past faithfulness and a call to endurance.

The readers had once suffered public persecution, including insults, imprisonment, and the confiscation of their property, and they had endured it joyfully (vv32–34). But now, years later, some were growing weary, and some had stopped gathering with other believers (v25). The author wants them to remember who they are and to keep going.

### The Inadequacy of the Old Sacrifices (10:1–4)

[[v1|For the law, having a shadow of the good to come, not the very image of the things, can never with the same sacrifices year by year, which they offer continually, make perfect those who draw near]]. As Hebrews 8:5 explained, the Law was a shadow. It outlined the shape of good things to come but did not contain their reality.

[[v2|Or else wouldn’t they have ceased to be offered, because the worshipers, having been once cleansed, would have had no more consciousness of sins?]] If the sacrifices had truly removed sin, there would have been no need to repeat them. Their repetition proved their inadequacy.

[[v3|But in those sacrifices there is a yearly reminder of sins]]. Rather than removing guilt, the annual Day of Atonement reminded people that sin remained.

[[v4|For it is impossible that the blood of bulls and goats should take away sins]]. Animal sacrifices could not ultimately remove human sin. They were signs pointing to the sacrifice that could.

### I Have Come to Do Your Will (10:5–10)

[[v5|Therefore when he comes into the world, he says, “You didn’t desire sacrifice and offering, but you prepared a body for me.]] [[v6|You had no pleasure in whole burnt offerings and sacrifices for sin.]] [[v7|Then I said, ‘Behold, I have come (in the scroll of the book it is written of me) to do your will, O God.’”]] The author quotes Psalm 40:6–8, following the Greek version, which reads "you prepared a body for me" where the Hebrew reads "my ears you have opened." He places these words in the mouth of Christ as He comes into the world. God did not ultimately desire animal sacrifices; He desired obedience. Christ came with a body prepared for Him, to do God's will perfectly, even to the point of offering that body on the cross.

[[v8|Previously saying, “Sacrifices and offerings and whole burnt offerings and sacrifices for sin you didn’t desire, neither had pleasure in them” (those which are offered according to the law)]], [[v9|then he has said, “Behold, I have come to do your will.” He takes away the first, that he may establish the second]]. Christ set aside the old system of sacrifices and established the new way: His own obedient sacrifice. The result is studied below.

### One Sacrifice Forever (10:11–18)

[[v11|Every priest indeed stands day by day serving and offering often the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins]], [[v12|but he, when he had offered one sacrifice for sins forever, sat down on the right hand of God]], [[v13|from that time waiting until his enemies are made the footstool of his feet]]. The contrast is vivid. Priests stood, because their work was never finished. There were no chairs in the tabernacle. Christ sat down, because His work was complete. He now waits for the final defeat of all His enemies, as Psalm 110:1 promised. The result of His one offering is studied below.

[[v15|The Holy Spirit also testifies to us, for after saying]], [[v16|“This is the covenant that I will make with them after those days,” says the Lord, “I will put my laws on their heart, I will also write them on their mind;” then he says]], [[v17|“I will remember their sins and their iniquities no more.”]] The author returns to Jeremiah 31, quoted in Hebrews 8, and emphasizes its final promise: complete forgiveness.

[[v18|Now where remission of these is, there is no more offering for sin]]. If sins are completely forgiven, no further sacrifice is needed. This is the conclusion of the whole argument of chapters 7–10.

### Let Us Draw Near (10:19–25)

The great call to draw near, hold fast, and encourage one another is studied below. [[v23|let’s hold fast the confession of our hope without wavering; for he who promised is faithful]]. Believers can hold firmly to their hope because their hope rests on God's faithfulness, not their own.

### A Warning Against Deliberate Sin (10:26–31)

[[v26|For if we sin willfully after we have received the knowledge of the truth, there remains no more a sacrifice for sins]], [[v27|but a certain fearful expectation of judgment, and a fierceness of fire which will devour the adversaries]]. This is the fourth warning in Hebrews, closely related to Hebrews 6:4–6. "Sin willfully" describes deliberate, persistent sin, continuing to sin knowingly as a settled way of life, ultimately rejecting Christ. Since Christ's sacrifice is the only sacrifice for sin, those who reject it have nowhere else to turn.

[[v28|A man who disregards Moses’ law dies without compassion on the word of two or three witnesses]]. [[v29|How much worse punishment do you think he will be judged worthy of who has trodden under foot the Son of God, and has counted the blood of the covenant with which he was sanctified an unholy thing, and has insulted the Spirit of grace?]] The author describes this sin in the strongest terms: trampling the Son of God, treating His blood as worthless, and insulting the Spirit of grace. This is not the stumbling of a weak believer but the defiant rejection of Christ.

[[v30|For we know him who said, “Vengeance belongs to me. I will repay,” says the Lord. Again, “The Lord will judge his people.”]] [[v31|It is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God]]. The author quotes Deuteronomy 32:35–36. God is the living God, and His judgment is real.

As with Hebrews 6:4–6, Christians understand this warning in different ways: as describing true believers who can fall away, as describing those who professed faith without truly possessing it, or as a real warning that God uses to keep His people from falling away. All agree that it describes deliberate, settled rejection of Christ, not the ordinary failures that every believer experiences and confesses. Believers who are grieved by their sins and want to turn back to God are not the people this passage describes. For them, the promise of 1 John 1:9 stands: if we confess our sins, He is faithful and righteous to forgive us.

### Remember and Endure (10:32–39)

[[v32|But remember the former days, in which, after you were enlightened, you endured a great struggle with sufferings]]: [[v33|partly, being exposed to both reproaches and oppressions, and partly, becoming partakers with those who were treated so]]. Soon after coming to faith, the readers had endured public insult and persecution, and they had stood with others who were suffering.

[[v34|For you both had compassion on me in my chains and joyfully accepted the plundering of your possessions, knowing that you have for yourselves a better possession and an enduring one in the heavens]]. They had cared for prisoners and joyfully accepted the loss of their property, because they knew they had a better and lasting possession in heaven. (Some manuscripts read "compassion on the prisoners" rather than "on me in my chains.")

[[v35|Therefore don’t throw away your boldness, which has a great reward]]. [[v36|For you need endurance so that, having done the will of God, you may receive the promise]]. The readers had shown great courage before. Now they must not abandon it. What they need is endurance.

[[v37|“In a very little while, he who comes will come and will not wait.]] [[v38|But the righteous one will live by faith. If he shrinks back, my soul has no pleasure in him.”]] The author quotes Habakkuk 2:3–4, the same passage Paul quotes in Romans 1:17 and Galatians 3:11. Christ's return is near. The righteous live by faith and do not shrink back.

[[v39|But we are not of those who shrink back to destruction, but of those who have faith to the saving of the soul]]. The author ends with confidence. He believes his readers are among those who will persevere in faith. This verse leads directly into the great chapter on faith that follows.

## Important Verses Explained

### Hebrews 10:10 — Sanctified Once for All

verse: Hebrews 10:10

#### Simple Explanation

"By which will we have been sanctified." "Which will" refers to God's will, which Christ came to do (v9). Because Christ obeyed the Father's will perfectly, believers have been "sanctified," made holy, set apart for God, cleansed from sin.

"Through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ." The means of sanctification is Christ's sacrifice, the offering of His physical body on the cross. Psalm 40 spoke of a body prepared for Him (v5); He offered that body for His people.

"Once for all." This phrase appears again. Christ's sacrifice does not need to be repeated. It was offered once, and its effect is permanent.

Notice the tense: "we have been sanctified." This is a completed action. Believers are already set apart as holy in God's sight because of what Christ has done.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

In Hebrews, "sanctified" often refers to the definitive act by which believers are set apart and made acceptable to God through Christ's sacrifice. This is sometimes called "positional" or "definitive" sanctification. It is complete and secure. Believers are holy in God's sight because of Christ.

At the same time, verse 14 speaks of "those who are being sanctified," an ongoing process. Believers are already holy in their standing before God, and they are being made holy in their character day by day. Both are true, and both rest on Christ's sacrifice.

This verse also connects Christ's obedience with His sacrifice. Christ did not merely die; He came to do God's will, and His death was the climax of a life of perfect obedience. God desired obedience more than sacrifice (1 Samuel 15:22). In Christ, perfect obedience and perfect sacrifice come together.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can rest in the truth that they have been made holy in God's sight through Christ's sacrifice. Their standing before God does not depend on how well they performed today. It rests on what Christ did once for all.

This truth also motivates holy living. Those who have been set apart for God are called to live as people who belong to Him. Their identity shapes their behavior.

Finally, believers can follow Christ's example of doing God's will. Like Him, they can say, "I have come to do your will," offering their own bodies as living sacrifices (Romans 12:1).

### Hebrews 10:14 — Perfected Forever

verse: Hebrews 10:14

#### Simple Explanation

"For by one offering he has perfected forever those who are being sanctified."

"By one offering": a single sacrifice, Christ's death on the cross.

"He has perfected forever": He has made believers complete, fully qualified to stand in God's presence, permanently. "Perfected" here means brought to the goal God intended: a right relationship with Him, with sin fully dealt with.

"Those who are being sanctified": believers who are in the process of being made holy. The perfection is complete; the sanctification is ongoing.

This verse holds together two truths. Believers are already perfected in their standing before God, forever. And they are still being made holy in their daily lives.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This is one of the most remarkable statements in the New Testament. One offering accomplished what centuries of sacrifices could not. The old sacrifices could never make worshipers perfect (Hebrews 10:1). Christ's single sacrifice has made believers perfect forever.

The combination of "perfected forever" and "being sanctified" gives believers both security and direction. Security, because their acceptance with God is complete and permanent, resting on Christ's finished work. Direction, because they are on a journey of growth, becoming in practice what they already are in Christ.

This truth guards against two errors. One is anxiety, fearing that one's standing with God depends on one's performance. The other is complacency, thinking that because one is accepted, growth does not matter. Hebrews affirms both full acceptance and ongoing transformation.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers who struggle with insecurity before God can hold onto this verse. By one offering, Christ has perfected them forever. Their failures do not undo His work.

At the same time, believers can embrace the ongoing process of being made holy. They can grow in love, obedience, and character, not to earn acceptance, but because they are already accepted.

When believers fall, they can return to this truth: Christ's offering is sufficient. They do not need another sacrifice. They need only to confess and receive the forgiveness already secured.

### Hebrews 10:19–22 — A New and Living Way

verse: Hebrews 10:19-22

#### Simple Explanation

The author now turns from teaching to application. "Having therefore, brothers, boldness to enter into the holy place by the blood of Jesus." Under the old covenant, only the high priest could enter the Most Holy Place, once a year, trembling. Now every believer has "boldness," confidence and freedom, to enter God's presence. The basis is "the blood of Jesus."

Verse 20: "by the way which he dedicated for us, a new and living way, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh." Jesus opened a way into God's presence. It is "new," never available before, and "living," because it is through the living Christ. The curtain that once blocked the way is compared to Jesus' flesh. When His body was torn on the cross, the way was opened. The Gospels record that at the moment of Jesus' death, the temple curtain was torn from top to bottom (Mark 15:38).

Verse 21: "and having a great priest over God’s house." Believers have Jesus as their high priest, who rules over God's household.

Verse 22 gives the call: "let’s draw near with a true heart in fullness of faith." Believers should approach God with sincerity and full confidence.

"Having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience." Just as priests were sprinkled with blood to be consecrated, believers' hearts have been cleansed by Christ's blood from a guilty conscience.

"And having our body washed with pure water." This likely refers to baptism, the outward sign of inner cleansing, or more broadly to the cleansing that comes through Christ.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses describe the greatest privilege of the new covenant: direct access to God. What was once reserved for one man on one day a year is now open to every believer at every moment. This is the fulfillment of everything the tabernacle pointed toward.

The image of the curtain as Christ's flesh is profound. The barrier between God and humanity was removed through Christ's body, offered on the cross. The way into God's presence passes through the cross.

The call to "draw near" is an invitation to intimate fellowship with God. Believers do not need to stand at a distance. They can come close, speak freely, and enjoy His presence. This is what Hebrews 4:16 described as approaching the throne of grace with boldness.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can draw near to God in prayer and worship with confidence, not because they are worthy, but because Jesus has opened the way. They can come to Him at any time, in any circumstance.

This passage invites believers to come with sincerity, a "true heart," and with full assurance of faith. They do not need to approach God with doubt about whether they are welcome. They are welcome through Christ.

Believers who feel unclean or unworthy can remember that their hearts have been sprinkled clean by Christ's blood. Their conscience can be at rest.

### Hebrews 10:24–25 — Stir Up One Another to Love and Good Works

verse: Hebrews 10:24-25

#### Simple Explanation

After calling believers to draw near to God and hold fast to hope, the author calls them to care for one another.

"Let's consider how to provoke one another to love and good works." "Consider" means to think carefully, to give attention to. Believers are to think about how to encourage one another. "Provoke" usually has a negative meaning, but here it is positive: to stir up, to stimulate, to spur on. Believers are to inspire one another toward love and good deeds.

Verse 25: "not forsaking our own assembling together, as the custom of some is." Some of the readers had stopped meeting with other believers, perhaps out of fear of persecution, discouragement, or indifference. The author urges them not to give up gathering together.

"But exhorting one another." When believers gather, they encourage one another.

"And so much the more as you see the Day approaching." "The Day" is the day of Christ's return. As it draws nearer, gathering and encouragement become even more important.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that the Christian life is not lived alone. Believers need one another. Drawing near to God (v22) and holding fast to hope (v23) are sustained by community (vv24–25). Hebrews 3:13 made a similar point: "exhort one another day by day... lest any one of you be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin."

The three verses form a pattern: faith (v22), hope (v23), and love (v24). These are the three great Christian virtues (1 Corinthians 13:13), and they flourish in community.

Gathering together was not merely a religious duty for the early Christians. It was where they worshiped, prayed, heard the Scriptures, shared the Lord's Supper, and supported one another (Acts 2:42–47). To abandon the gathering was to cut oneself off from the means God uses to sustain faith.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can take regular gathering with other believers seriously. Participating in a local church, through worship, small groups, and fellowship, provides encouragement and accountability that are hard to find alone.

Believers can also think intentionally about how to encourage others: a word of affirmation, an invitation to serve together, a reminder of God's promises, or an example of love and generosity.

For those who have drifted from Christian community because of hurt, busyness, or discouragement, this passage is a gentle invitation to return. And for churches, it is a call to be communities where people are genuinely encouraged and spurred on, where those who have been wounded find care, and where no one is left alone.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Hebrews 10 brings the great argument of Hebrews to its climax. The old sacrifices, repeated year after year, could never take away sins; they were only a shadow. But Christ came to do God's will, offering His own body once for all. By that single sacrifice, believers have been made holy, and by one offering He has perfected forever those who are being sanctified. He has sat down at God's right hand because His work is complete. Where sins are forgiven, no further sacrifice is needed.

This truth gives believers both security and freedom. They do not need to earn God's acceptance or repeat sacrifices. Their standing before God is complete and permanent in Christ. At the same time, they are being made holy day by day, growing into what they already are.

Because of what Christ has done, believers can draw near to God with confidence, through the new and living way He opened through His flesh. They can come with sincere hearts and full assurance, cleansed from a guilty conscience. They can hold fast their hope, because God is faithful. And they can stir one another up to love and good works, faithfully gathering together and encouraging one another, especially as the day of Christ's return approaches.

The chapter also contains a solemn warning against deliberately and persistently rejecting Christ, because there is no other sacrifice for sin. This warning is meant to keep believers from drifting, not to crush those who struggle and long for God. Finally, the author reminds his readers of their past courage in suffering and calls them to endure. They are not those who shrink back, but those who have faith and are saved.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for coming into the world to do the Father's will and for offering Your body once for all. Thank You that by Your one sacrifice I have been made holy and perfected forever, and that You are making me holy day by day. Thank You that You sat down at the right hand of God because Your work is finished, and that my sins are remembered no more. Thank You for opening a new and living way into God's presence through Your flesh. Help me to draw near with a sincere heart and full assurance of faith. Help me to hold fast to my hope, for You who promised are faithful. Show me how to stir up others to love and good works, and keep me faithful in gathering with Your people. Guard me from drifting, give me endurance, and keep me among those who have faith and are saved. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
