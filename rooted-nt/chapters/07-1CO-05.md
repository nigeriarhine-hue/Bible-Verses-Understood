# 1 Corinthians 5
theme: A Holy Church: Discipline for Restoration and Christ Our Passover
main: Paul confronts a shocking case of sexual immorality that the Corinthian church has tolerated, a man living with his father's wife, and rebukes the church for being proud instead of grieving. He instructs them to remove the man from their fellowship, handing him over to Satan with the hope that he will ultimately be saved. Using the image of yeast at Passover, Paul explains that tolerated sin spreads through the whole community, and that because Christ our Passover lamb has been sacrificed, the church must live in sincerity and truth. He clarifies that believers are not to withdraw from unbelievers in the world, but must not tolerate unrepentant, flagrant sin among those who claim to follow Christ.

## Chapter Background and Story

In chapters 1–4, Paul addressed the problem of divisions in the church. Now he turns to another serious problem: moral failure that the church was ignoring. Corinth was known for sexual immorality, and some believers apparently thought that Christian freedom meant they could live however they pleased. Others may have been so proud of their spiritual gifts and knowledge that they overlooked sin among them.

Paul's instructions in this chapter are about church discipline, the process by which a church responds to a member who persists in serious, unrepentant sin. This is a difficult subject. Throughout history, church discipline has sometimes been misused harshly, and sometimes neglected entirely. Paul's teaching shows that discipline is meant to protect the church's holiness and, ultimately, to restore the sinner. Jesus Himself gave instructions for addressing sin within the community (Matthew 18:15–17), beginning privately and gently.

It is also important to note what this chapter does not teach. Church discipline is not a substitute for reporting crimes to proper authorities, and it must never be used to protect abusers or silence victims. In cases of abuse, the safety of the vulnerable comes first, and civil authorities must be involved (Romans 13:1–4).

### Judging Those Inside, Not Outside (5:9–13)

Paul clarifies a misunderstanding about an earlier letter, studied below. He concludes, [[v12|For what do I have to do with also judging those who are outside? Don’t you judge those who are within?]] [[v13|But those who are outside, God judges. “Put away the wicked man from among yourselves.”]] Paul quotes a phrase that appears several times in Deuteronomy (Deuteronomy 17:7; 19:19; 22:21, 24). God's people are responsible for holiness within their own community. People outside the church are under God's judgment, not the church's.

This chapter's story has a hopeful sequel. Many interpreters believe that 2 Corinthians 2:5–11 refers to this same man. If so, the discipline worked. The man repented, and Paul urged the church to forgive him, comfort him, and reaffirm their love for him, [[2 Corinthians 2:7|lest by any means such a one should be swallowed up with his excessive sorrow]]. The goal of discipline is always restoration.

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 Corinthians 5:1–2 — Mourning Rather Than Pride

verse: 1 Corinthians 5:1-2

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had heard a report about the church in Corinth that shocked him. [[v1|It is actually reported that there is sexual immorality among you, and such sexual immorality as is not even named among the Gentiles, that one has his father’s wife]]. A man in the church was in a sexual relationship with his father's wife, probably his stepmother. This was forbidden in the Law of Moses (Leviticus 18:8) and was considered shameful even in pagan Roman society, where such relationships were illegal. The Corinthian church was tolerating something that even unbelievers condemned.

Paul says that the woman was "his father's wife," not "his mother," which suggests she was his stepmother. Paul does not address her, which suggests she was not a member of the church.

[[v2|You are arrogant]]. The church's response made the problem worse. They were "arrogant," puffed up. Perhaps they were proud of their tolerance, thinking that their freedom in Christ allowed anything. Perhaps they were so proud of their spiritual gifts that they ignored moral failures. Whatever the reason, their pride blinded them to the seriousness of sin.

[[v2|and didn’t mourn instead, that he who had done this deed might be removed from among you]]. Paul says they should have "mourned." The right response to serious sin in the church is grief, the kind of sorrow people feel at a death. Instead of boasting, they should have grieved and taken action to remove the man from their fellowship.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that sin in the church is serious. The church is God's holy temple (3:16–17). Tolerating serious, unrepentant sin damages the community and dishonors God.

They also show that pride can blind people to sin. The Corinthians were so focused on their own status that they did not see the danger in their midst. Pride often leads people to excuse sin, either in themselves or in others.

Finally, these verses show the right response to sin: grief. Sin should not be celebrated, excused, or ignored. It should be mourned. Jesus said, [[Matthew 5:4|Blessed are those who mourn, for they shall be comforted]]. Godly sorrow over sin leads to repentance and healing.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses challenge churches that ignore serious sin. Some churches avoid addressing sin because they fear conflict or want to appear tolerant. Paul's words remind them that love sometimes requires confronting sin.

They also challenge believers to examine their own attitudes. Do they excuse sin in themselves or others? Do they feel grief over sin, or have they become indifferent? Believers can ask God to give them His heart toward sin.

Finally, these verses call for humility. The Corinthians' pride kept them from seeing clearly. Believers can ask God to keep them humble, so that they can see sin for what it is and respond with sorrow and appropriate action.

### 1 Corinthians 5:5 — Delivered to Satan, That the Spirit May Be Saved

verse: 1 Corinthians 5:5

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had told the Corinthians that they should have removed the immoral man from their fellowship. Now he gives specific instructions. When the church gathered in the name of the Lord Jesus, with Paul present in spirit and the power of the Lord among them, they were to take action.

[[v5|you are to deliver such a one to Satan]]. This is a strong expression. It means to remove the man from the fellowship of the church, putting him outside the community where God's people gather and are protected. Outside the church is the world, which Paul elsewhere describes as under the influence of Satan (2 Corinthians 4:4; 1 John 5:19). The man would no longer enjoy the protection and fellowship of God's people.

[[v5|for the destruction of the flesh]]. This phrase has been understood in different ways. Some interpret "flesh" as the sinful nature: being removed from the church would lead the man to see the destructive results of his sin, so that his sinful desires would be "destroyed" through repentance. Others interpret it as physical suffering or illness that might result from being outside God's protection, which could lead him to repentance. Either way, the purpose is redemptive.

[[v5|that the spirit may be saved in the day of the Lord Jesus]]. This is the ultimate goal. Paul hoped that the discipline would bring the man to repentance, so that he would be saved on the day of Christ's return. The purpose of discipline was not to destroy the man but to save him.

This shows that church discipline, rightly practiced, is an act of love. Like a doctor who must sometimes cause pain to heal, the church sometimes must take difficult action for the good of the person who is in danger.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that the goal of discipline is restoration. Paul does not want the man to be lost; he wants him to be saved. Hebrews writes, [[Hebrews 12:11|All chastening seems for the present to be not joyous but grievous; yet afterward it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness to those who have been trained by it]].

It also shows the seriousness of being outside the church. The community of believers is a place of protection, fellowship, and spiritual nourishment. Being removed from it is a serious matter, meant to awaken a person to their danger.

Finally, the verse shows that discipline must be done with authority and care. Paul instructed the church to act "in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ" and "with the power of our Lord Jesus Christ." Discipline is not about personal revenge or power; it is a solemn act done under Christ's authority, with the goal of healing.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse reminds churches that discipline is sometimes necessary. When a member persists in serious, unrepentant sin, the church may need to take loving but firm action. This should be done carefully, following Jesus' instructions in Matthew 18:15–17, beginning with private conversation and moving to more formal steps only if necessary.

It also reminds churches that discipline must always aim at restoration. The goal is never to punish or shame but to bring the person to repentance and back into fellowship. Churches can pray for those under discipline and welcome them back when they repent.

Finally, this verse warns against misusing discipline. Discipline should never be used to control people, silence those who raise legitimate concerns, or protect leaders from accountability. It should be practiced humbly, fairly, and in the spirit of Christ.

### 1 Corinthians 5:6–8 — Christ Our Passover

verse: 1 Corinthians 5:6-8

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had instructed the Corinthians to remove the immoral man from their fellowship. Now he explains why this was so important, using an image from the Jewish Passover.

[[v6|Your boasting is not good. Don’t you know that a little yeast leavens the whole lump?]] Yeast (or leaven) spreads through dough. Just a small amount can make a whole batch rise. Paul uses this image to show that tolerated sin spreads. If the church allowed this sin to continue, it would influence others and corrupt the whole community.

[[v7|Purge out the old yeast, that you may be a new lump, even as you are unleavened]]. Before Passover, Jewish families searched their homes and removed all yeast. During the Feast of Unleavened Bread that followed Passover, they ate bread made without yeast, remembering how Israel left Egypt in haste (Exodus 12:15–20). Paul tells the Corinthians to remove the "old yeast," the sin in their midst, so that they can be a "new lump," a fresh batch of dough. He adds, "even as you are unleavened." In Christ, they had already been made new. Now they needed to live like it.

[[v7|For indeed Christ, our Passover, has been sacrificed in our place]]. Here is the reason. At the first Passover, a lamb was sacrificed, and its blood protected Israel from judgment (Exodus 12). Christ is the true Passover lamb. He was sacrificed so that His people would be delivered from sin and judgment. Since Christ has been sacrificed, believers are now celebrating the feast, living in the new life He has given.

[[v8|Therefore let’s keep the feast, not with old yeast, neither with the yeast of malice and wickedness, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth]]. The whole Christian life is like a festival, a celebration of Christ's sacrifice. Believers celebrate it not with the "yeast" of evil and wickedness, but with the "unleavened bread" of sincerity and truth, living honest and holy lives.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show the deep connection between Christ's death and the Passover. Jesus was crucified at Passover time. John the Baptist called Him [[John 1:29|the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world]]. His blood delivers believers from judgment, just as the lamb's blood delivered Israel in Egypt.

They also show that sin has a spreading influence. Sin is never purely private; it affects others. Paul writes elsewhere, [[Galatians 5:9|A little yeast grows through the whole lump]]. Churches and individuals must take sin seriously because of its power to spread.

Finally, these verses show that the Christian life is a celebration. Because Christ our Passover has been sacrificed, believers live in joy and freedom. Holiness is not a burden but a fitting way to celebrate the feast.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to remove "old yeast" from their lives. Believers can ask God to show them sinful attitudes and habits that need to be removed, knowing that these can spread and affect others.

They also encourage churches to take holiness seriously. Tolerating sin is not love; it allows sin to spread and harm the whole community. Churches can address sin with humility and grace, seeking the health of the whole body.

Finally, these verses invite believers to celebrate Christ's sacrifice. Every day, believers can remember that Christ, their Passover, was sacrificed for them. They can live with sincerity and truth as an expression of joy and gratitude.

### 1 Corinthians 5:9–11 — Not Leaving the World

verse: 1 Corinthians 5:9-11

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had written an earlier letter to the Corinthians, which has not survived. In it, he had told them not to associate with sexually immoral people. Apparently, the Corinthians had misunderstood him.

[[v9|I wrote to you in my letter to have no company with sexual sinners]]. Some Corinthians may have thought Paul meant they should avoid all immoral people, including unbelievers. That would have been impossible in Corinth.

[[v10|yet not at all meaning with the sexual sinners of this world, or with the covetous and extortionists, or with idolaters, for then you would have to leave the world]]. Paul clarifies that he did not mean believers should cut themselves off from unbelievers. If they tried to avoid every immoral, greedy, dishonest, or idolatrous person in the world, they would have to leave the world entirely. Believers are called to live in the world and to be a light to it. Jesus Himself ate with tax collectors and sinners.

[[v11|But as it is, I wrote to you not to associate with anyone who is called a brother who is a sexual sinner, or covetous, or an idolater, or a slanderer, or a drunkard, or an extortionist. Don’t even eat with such a person]]. Paul's instruction was about people who claim to be believers, "anyone who is called a brother," but who persist in serious, unrepentant sin. Notice that the list includes not only sexual sins but also greed, idolatry, slander, drunkenness, and swindling. All these are serious.

"Don't even eat with such a person" means not sharing close fellowship with them, including the fellowship meals of the church, which often included the Lord's Supper. The goal was to make clear that their behavior was incompatible with following Christ, in the hope that they would repent.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that believers are called to be in the world but not of the world. Jesus prayed, [[John 17:15|I pray not that you would take them from the world, but that you would keep them from the evil one]]. Believers are not to withdraw from society but to be a holy presence within it.

They also show the difference between how the church relates to unbelievers and to those who claim to be believers. The church does not judge unbelievers; it loves them and shares the gospel with them. But within the church, believers are accountable to one another.

Finally, these verses show that a variety of sins are serious. Paul's list includes greed, slander, and drunkenness alongside sexual immorality. Churches sometimes focus on certain sins while ignoring others. Paul's list shows that all persistent, unrepentant sin is serious.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to engage with the world. Believers should not isolate themselves from unbelievers. They can build friendships, show love, and share the gospel with people of all kinds, just as Jesus did.

They also call believers to hold one another accountable. Within the church, believers are responsible to help one another live faithfully. This involves loving correction, encouragement, and, when necessary, discipline.

Finally, these verses warn against hypocrisy. Churches can be quick to condemn some sins while tolerating others, such as greed, gossip, and slander. Believers can take all sin seriously, beginning with their own.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

1 Corinthians 5 teaches that the church must take sin seriously. The Corinthian church tolerated shocking immorality and was even proud of its tolerance. Paul called them to mourn instead. Believers today can ask God to give them His heart toward sin: not indifference, not harsh condemnation, but grief that leads to repentance and healing.

The chapter also teaches that church discipline, rightly practiced, is an act of love aimed at restoration. Removing an unrepentant member from fellowship is a serious step, meant to awaken them to their danger and lead them back to God. Churches can practice discipline humbly and carefully, following Jesus' teaching, praying for restoration, and welcoming back those who repent. Discipline must never be used to control, silence, or protect wrongdoers, and abuse must always be reported to proper authorities.

1 Corinthians 5 reminds believers that Christ our Passover has been sacrificed. Because of His sacrifice, believers are a new lump, free from the old yeast of sin. Sin spreads like yeast, so believers can remove it from their lives and communities, celebrating Christ's sacrifice with sincerity and truth.

Finally, the chapter clarifies that believers should not withdraw from unbelievers in the world. They are called to live among all kinds of people, showing love and sharing the gospel. Within the church, however, believers are accountable to one another. Believers can engage the world with love while holding one another to the standards of Christ, beginning with themselves.

## Chapter Prayer

Holy God, You are pure and righteous, and Your church is Your holy temple. Forgive me for the times I have excused sin, in myself or in others, or have been proud when I should have mourned. Give me Your heart toward sin, a heart that grieves and seeks healing. Help Your church to practice correction with humility, love, and wisdom, always aiming at restoration. Protect the vulnerable, and guard Your church from those who would misuse authority. Lord Jesus, thank You for being sacrificed as our Passover Lamb. Help me to remove the old yeast of sin from my life, and to celebrate Your sacrifice with sincerity and truth. Help me to live in the world as a light, loving those who do not know You, while walking faithfully with Your people. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
