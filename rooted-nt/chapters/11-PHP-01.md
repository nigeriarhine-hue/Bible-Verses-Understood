# Philippians 1
theme: To Live Is Christ
main: Writing from prison, Paul thanks God with joy for the Philippians' partnership in the gospel and expresses confidence that God, who began a good work in them, will complete it. He prays that their love will abound in knowledge and discernment. Paul explains that his imprisonment has actually advanced the gospel, and he rejoices that Christ is being proclaimed, even by some with poor motives. Facing the possibility of death, he declares that for him to live is Christ and to die is gain, though he expects to remain for their sake. He urges them to live worthily of the gospel, standing firm together without fear, knowing that suffering for Christ is part of their calling.

## Chapter Background and Story

When Paul wrote this letter, he was in chains, probably under house arrest in Rome, awaiting a trial that could end in his execution (Acts 28:16, 30–31). Yet the letter overflows with joy. Paul's circumstances were uncertain and difficult, but his confidence in Christ was not shaken.

The church at Philippi held a special place in Paul's heart. From the very beginning, they had supported his ministry with their prayers and gifts (Philippians 4:15–16). They had recently sent Epaphroditus with another gift, and Paul writes partly to thank them. He addresses them with deep affection, and his tone throughout is warm and personal.

The Philippians also faced challenges. They were experiencing opposition from outsiders (1:28–30), and there were tensions within the church that threatened their unity (2:1–4; 4:2–3). Paul's words in this chapter encourage them to see their circumstances, and his, in light of God's purposes.

### Greeting (1:1–2)

[[v1|Paul and Timothy, servants of Jesus Christ, to all the saints in Christ Jesus who are at Philippi, with the overseers and servants]]. Paul does not call himself an apostle here, as he does in most letters. With this beloved church, he did not need to assert his authority. He simply calls himself a servant, or slave, of Christ. "Overseers" (sometimes translated bishops) and "servants" (sometimes translated deacons) were the church's leaders; this is one of the earliest references to these roles. [[v2|Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ]].

### Thanksgiving and Confidence (1:3–8)

[[v3|I thank my God whenever I remember you]], [[v4|always in every request of mine on behalf of you all, making my requests with joy]], [[v5|for your partnership in furtherance of the Good News from the first day until now]]. "Partnership" translates *koinonia*, often rendered "fellowship." It means sharing in something together. The Philippians had shared in Paul's mission through prayer, financial support, and their own witness. Paul's confidence about God's continuing work in them is studied below.

[[v7|It is even right for me to think this way on behalf of all of you, because I have you in my heart, because both in my bonds and in the defense and confirmation of the Good News, you all are partakers with me of grace]]. Even in prison and on trial, Paul felt the Philippians were with him. [[v8|For God is my witness, how I long after all of you in the tender mercies of Christ Jesus]]. Paul's love for them was not merely his own; it was the deep compassion of Christ flowing through him.

### Paul's Prayer (1:9–11)

Paul's prayer for their growth in love and discernment is studied below.

### The Gospel Advances Through Chains (1:12–18)

[[v12|Now I desire to have you know, brothers, that the things which happened to me have turned out rather to the progress of the Good News]]. The Philippians may have worried that Paul's imprisonment was a disaster for the gospel. Paul assures them the opposite is true. "Progress" was a word used for an army clearing a path through difficult terrain.

[[v13|so that it became evident to the whole palace guard, and to all the rest, that my bonds are in Christ]]. The "palace guard" was the Praetorian Guard, the elite soldiers of the emperor. Paul was chained to these soldiers in shifts, and they came to understand that he was imprisoned not for a crime but for Christ. The gospel was reaching the heart of the empire. Later in the letter, Paul even sends greetings from believers [[Philippians 4:22|of Caesar’s household]].

[[v14|and that most of the brothers in the Lord, being confident through my bonds, are more abundantly bold to speak the word of God without fear]]. Paul's courage in chains inspired other believers to speak boldly.

[[v15|Some indeed preach Christ even out of envy and strife, and some also out of good will]]. Not everyone preaching in Rome had pure motives. Some were rivals of Paul who hoped to increase his distress. Others preached out of love. [[v18|What does it matter? Only that in every way, whether in pretense or in truth, Christ is proclaimed. I rejoice in this, yes, and will rejoice]]. Paul's response is remarkable. These preachers were not teaching false doctrine, as the troublemakers in Galatia were; they were preaching Christ, though with selfish motives. Paul cared more about Christ being known than about his own reputation.

### To Live Is Christ (1:19–26)

[[v19|For I know that this will turn out to my salvation through your prayers and the supply of the Spirit of Jesus Christ]]. "Salvation" here may mean deliverance from prison or, more broadly, his final vindication before God, whatever the outcome of his trial. [[v20|according to my earnest expectation and hope, that I will in no way be disappointed, but with all boldness, as always, now also Christ will be magnified in my body, whether by life or by death]]. Paul's one concern was that Christ would be honored through him, whether he lived or died. His famous statement about life and death is studied below.

[[v22|But if I live on in the flesh, this will bring fruit from my work; yet I don’t know what I will choose]]. [[v23|But I am hard pressed between the two, having the desire to depart and be with Christ, which is far better]]. [[v24|Yet to remain in the flesh is more needful for your sake]]. Paul is torn. Personally, he longs to be with Christ. But for the sake of the churches, it would be better for him to remain. [[v25|Having this confidence, I know that I will remain, yes, and remain with you all for your progress and joy in the faith]]. Paul expected to be released, and many believe he was, for a time, before a later imprisonment and death.

### Living Worthy of the Gospel (1:27–30)

Paul's call to live worthily of the gospel is studied below. [[v28|and in nothing frightened by the adversaries, which is for them a proof of destruction, but to you of salvation, and that from God]]. The Philippians' courage in the face of opposition was itself a sign. It showed that God was with them.

[[v29|Because it has been granted to you on behalf of Christ, not only to believe in him, but also to suffer on his behalf]]. This is a surprising statement. Paul says suffering for Christ is something "granted," a gift of grace, just as faith is. To suffer for Christ is to share in His sufferings and to be counted worthy of His name (Acts 5:41). [[v30|having the same conflict which you saw in me and now hear is in me]]. The Philippians had seen Paul beaten and imprisoned in their own city (Acts 16:22–24). Now they shared the same struggle.

## Important Verses Explained

### Philippians 1:6 — He Who Began a Good Work

verse: Philippians 1:6

#### Simple Explanation

Paul expresses his confidence about the Philippians' future. "Being confident of this very thing, that he who began a good work in you will complete it until the day of Jesus Christ."

"He who began a good work" is God. The "good work" is God's work of salvation and transformation in their lives. It began when they first believed the gospel, as told in Acts 16, when Lydia's heart was opened by the Lord and the jailer and his household believed.

God "will complete it." The word means to bring to completion, to finish. God does not abandon what He starts. He will continue His work in them until it is finished.

"Until the day of Jesus Christ" refers to Christ's return. God's work in believers continues throughout their lives and will be brought to perfection when Christ comes again.

Paul's confidence is not in the Philippians' strength but in God's faithfulness.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse expresses the faithfulness of God in salvation. Salvation is not something God starts and then leaves believers to finish on their own. He is committed to completing it. Romans 8:29–30 describes the same unbreakable chain: those whom God called, He justified, and those He justified, He also glorified.

Christian traditions have discussed how this promise relates to human responsibility and whether believers can fall away. Some emphasize God's preserving grace, holding that God will certainly keep all true believers to the end. Others emphasize that believers must continue in faith, while God faithfully supplies the grace to do so. Both affirm that God is faithful and that believers' hope rests in Him, not in themselves. Paul himself holds both together: in Philippians 2:12–13, he calls believers to work out their salvation, because God is the one working in them.

This verse also reminds believers that they are works in progress. God's work in them is real but not yet finished. They should expect growth over time, not instant perfection.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many believers become discouraged by their slow progress or repeated failures. This verse offers hope: God has not given up on them. The work He began, He will finish. Their growth depends ultimately on His faithfulness, not on their perfection.

This verse also encourages patience with others. Fellow believers, children, friends, and new Christians are all works in progress. God is at work in them too, even when change seems slow. Believers can pray for one another with confidence, trusting God to complete what He has started.

### Philippians 1:9–11 — Love Abounding in Knowledge and Discernment

verse: Philippians 1:9-11

#### Simple Explanation

Paul tells the Philippians what he prays for them. "This I pray, that your love may abound yet more and more in knowledge and all discernment." The Philippians already showed love, but Paul prays for it to overflow even more. And he wants their love to be guided by "knowledge" and "discernment." Love is not merely a feeling; it needs understanding and wisdom to act well.

"So that you may approve the things that are excellent." Discerning love helps believers recognize what truly matters, to test and choose what is best, not just what is good or acceptable.

"That you may be sincere and without offense to the day of Christ." "Sincere" means pure, with no hidden mixture. "Without offense" means not causing others to stumble and living blamelessly. Paul looks ahead to Christ's return as the time when their lives will be fully revealed.

"Being filled with the fruits of righteousness which are through Jesus Christ, to the glory and praise of God." The goal is a life full of the good results of right living, produced through Christ's work in them, all bringing glory to God.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This prayer shows that love and knowledge are not opposed. Some think of love as purely emotional and knowledge as cold and intellectual. Paul unites them. Love without knowledge can be naïve or misguided. Knowledge without love is empty and prideful (1 Corinthians 8:1; 13:2). Mature Christian love is wise, discerning, and thoughtful.

"Discernment" is the ability to perceive moral and spiritual realities, to recognize what is true, good, and wise in particular situations. It is developed through Scripture, prayer, the Spirit's guidance, and experience (Hebrews 5:14). Discerning love knows how to help without enabling harm, how to forgive while still protecting the vulnerable, and how to speak truth graciously.

The phrase "through Jesus Christ" reminds believers that the fruit of righteousness is not produced by their own efforts alone. It comes through union with Christ, the vine (John 15:5).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This prayer can shape how believers pray for themselves and others. Rather than praying only for circumstances, they can pray for overflowing love guided by wisdom, for the ability to recognize what is best, and for lives that bring glory to God.

Discerning love has practical applications. It helps parents know when to say yes and when to say no. It helps friends know how to support someone without enabling destructive behavior. It helps churches care for people wisely and protect the vulnerable. Love that grows in knowledge and discernment makes wise choices about time, money, relationships, and priorities.

### Philippians 1:21 — To Live Is Christ, and to Die Is Gain

verse: Philippians 1:21

#### Simple Explanation

This short verse captures the heart of Paul's life. "For to me to live is Christ." For Paul, life meant Christ. Christ was the center, purpose, and joy of his existence. Everything he did, he did for Christ, through Christ, and with Christ.

"And to die is gain." If Paul's life was all about Christ, then death could only bring him more of Christ. Death would mean being with Christ in a fuller way (v23). So even death, which most people fear as the ultimate loss, was gain for Paul.

Paul was not being morbid or wishing for death. He was expressing complete confidence in Christ. Whether he lived or died, he could not lose, because Christ was his treasure.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse challenges every believer to ask what fills in the blank: "For to me to live is ____." If the answer is money, success, comfort, or relationships, then death is loss, because those things are left behind. Only when Christ is the center of life can death be gain.

Paul's confidence rests on the resurrection of Christ. Because Jesus conquered death, death has lost its sting (1 Corinthians 15:55–57). For those who belong to Christ, death is not the end but a passage into His presence. Paul says in verse 23 that to depart and be with Christ is "far better." In 2 Corinthians 5:8, he says that to be absent from the body is to be at home with the Lord.

At the same time, Paul valued life. He wanted to remain for the sake of others. This verse does not encourage despising life or seeking death. It expresses a perspective in which both life and death are held in Christ's hands. It is important to say clearly that this verse is not about ending one's own life. Paul chose to remain and serve, and God calls His people to cherish the life He has given. Anyone who is struggling with thoughts of death or self-harm should reach out right away to a trusted person, a pastor, a doctor, or a crisis line for help.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites believers to make Christ the center of their lives. That does not mean neglecting work, family, or responsibilities, but living all of them for Christ, with Christ at the heart.

It also brings comfort in the face of death. Believers who are facing serious illness, aging, or the loss of loved ones can hold on to this truth: for those in Christ, death is gain. It is not the end but the doorway to being with Christ. Grief is still real, and it is right to mourn, but believers grieve with hope (1 Thessalonians 4:13).

Finally, this verse frees believers from fear. If neither life nor death can separate them from Christ, they can live boldly, serving God and others without being controlled by fear of loss.

### Philippians 1:27 — Living Worthy of the Gospel

verse: Philippians 1:27

#### Simple Explanation

"Only let your way of life be worthy of the Good News of Christ." The phrase translated "way of life" comes from a word related to citizenship. It means to live as a citizen. Philippi was a Roman colony, and its citizens were proud of their Roman citizenship. Paul calls the believers to live as citizens of a greater kingdom, in a way that fits the gospel. He will develop this idea further in Philippians 3:20: [[Philippians 3:20|our citizenship is in heaven]].

"That whether I come and see you or am absent, I may hear of your state." Paul wants them to live faithfully whether or not he is present to see it.

"That you stand firm in one spirit, with one soul striving for the faith of the Good News." Paul describes the church as a united team. "Stand firm" suggests soldiers holding their position. "With one soul" means sharing the same purpose and commitment. "Striving" was used for athletes competing together. The Philippians were to work side by side, like teammates, for the sake of the gospel.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The gospel is not only a message to believe but a way of life to live. Those who have received the good news of Christ are called to live in a way that reflects it. This includes personal holiness, but Paul especially emphasizes unity. A divided church contradicts the gospel of reconciliation.

The image of citizenship is powerful. Just as Roman citizens in Philippi lived by Roman laws and customs far from Rome, believers live by the values of God's kingdom while living in the world. Their loyalty is ultimately to Christ, their King.

Unity in this verse is not uniformity. It is a shared commitment to the gospel that allows believers to stand together and work together despite differences in personality, background, or opinion. Paul will develop this further in chapter 2.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can ask whether their way of life fits the gospel they believe. Does their conduct at home, work, and in the community reflect the grace, love, and truth of Christ? Living worthily of the gospel is not about earning salvation but about letting the gospel shape everything.

This verse also calls churches to unity in mission. Believers are not meant to compete with one another but to work together, side by side, for the sake of the gospel. Small disagreements can be set aside for the sake of shared purpose. When churches work together in love, they bear powerful witness to Christ.

Finally, living worthily "whether I come... or am absent" reminds believers that faithfulness does not depend on being watched. Integrity means living the same way in private as in public.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Philippians 1 shows that joy and confidence in Christ do not depend on circumstances. Paul was in prison, facing a possible death sentence, and some of his fellow preachers were trying to make his situation worse. Yet he overflowed with thanksgiving, prayer, and joy. His secret was that Christ was the center of his life.

Believers can share Paul's confidence that God, who began a good work in them, will complete it. This truth gives hope when they feel discouraged by slow progress or repeated failure, and it encourages patience with others who are still growing. Like Paul, they can pray for themselves and others to grow in love that is wise and discerning, able to recognize what is best and to produce the fruit of righteousness.

Paul's perspective on his imprisonment invites believers to see their own difficulties differently. What seems like a setback may become an opportunity for the gospel. Paul's chains opened doors to the emperor's guard and gave courage to other believers. God can use hard circumstances for His purposes.

At the heart of the chapter is Paul's declaration: "For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain." When Christ is the center of life, death loses its terror, and life gains meaning. Believers can live boldly, serving others, standing firm together, and striving side by side for the gospel. Even suffering for Christ can be seen as a privilege. Whatever comes, life or death, joy or hardship, Christ is enough.

## Chapter Prayer

Father, thank You for the people You have given me as partners in the gospel. I am confident that You, who began a good work in me, will carry it on to completion until the day of Christ Jesus. Let my love abound more and more in knowledge and discernment, so that I may approve what is excellent and be filled with the fruit of righteousness that comes through Jesus Christ, to Your glory and praise. When my circumstances are hard, help me to trust that You can use them to advance the gospel. Make Christ the center of my life, so that I can say, "For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain." Help me to live as a citizen of Your kingdom, standing firm with my brothers and sisters in one spirit, and to be unafraid in the face of opposition. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
