# 1 Timothy 6
theme: Godliness with Contentment Is Great Gain
main: Paul instructs believing servants to honor their masters so that God's name and the teaching are not slandered, especially when their masters are fellow believers. He warns again against false teachers who are proud, love controversy, and think godliness is a way to get rich. In contrast, godliness with contentment is great gain, because we brought nothing into the world and can take nothing out; with food and clothing we can be content. Those who want to be rich fall into temptation and harmful desires, for the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil. Paul charges Timothy, as a man of God, to flee these things, pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, endurance, and gentleness, and fight the good fight of faith until Christ appears. He tells Timothy to command the rich not to be proud or trust in uncertain riches but to hope in God, be rich in good works, and be generous. He ends by urging Timothy to guard what has been entrusted to him.

## Chapter Background and Story

The final chapter of 1 Timothy gathers several themes from the letter: the conduct of believers in society, the danger of false teachers, and Timothy's personal faithfulness. But the theme that dominates the chapter is money. The false teachers in Ephesus apparently saw religion as a path to financial gain (v5). Ephesus was a wealthy commercial city, and the temple of Artemis functioned as a major bank. The pull of wealth was strong.

Paul responds with some of the most important teaching in the New Testament on contentment, greed, and generosity. He addresses both those who want to become rich and those who already are. His words are as relevant today, in a world shaped by consumerism and the pursuit of wealth, as they were in first-century Ephesus.

### Servants and Masters (6:1–2)

[[v1|Let as many as are bondservants under the yoke count their own masters worthy of all honor, that the name of God and the doctrine not be blasphemed]]. As in Ephesians 6:5–9 and Colossians 3:22–4:1, Paul addresses believers living within the system of Roman slavery. His concern is that the conduct of Christian slaves would not bring dishonor on the gospel. If believing slaves became rebellious or disrespectful, outsiders might accuse Christianity of undermining society, and the gospel's reputation would suffer.

[[v2|Those who have believing masters, let them not despise them because they are brothers, but rather let them serve them, because those who partake of the benefit are believing and beloved. Teach and exhort these things]]. Some slaves with Christian masters may have thought that, since they were now brothers in Christ, they no longer needed to serve faithfully. Paul says their shared faith should make them serve even more willingly.

As discussed in the commentary on Ephesians 6, these instructions are pastoral guidance for believers living in an unjust system that they could not change. They are not an endorsement of slavery. The same letter condemns slave-trading (1 Timothy 1:10), and Paul encouraged slaves to gain their freedom when possible (1 Corinthians 7:21). The gospel's teaching that all are equal in Christ eventually helped lead many Christians to work for the abolition of slavery. Today, these verses can guide believers in how to work faithfully and honorably, always with the understanding that exploitation and abuse in any workplace are wrong.

### False Teachers and the Love of Gain (6:3–5)

[[v3|If anyone teaches a different doctrine and doesn’t consent to sound words, the words of our Lord Jesus Christ, and to the doctrine which is according to godliness]], [[v4|he is conceited, knowing nothing, but obsessed with arguments, disputes, and word battles, from which come envy, strife, insulting, evil suspicions]], [[v5|constant friction of people of corrupt minds and destitute of the truth, who suppose that godliness is a means of gain. Withdraw yourself from such]]. Paul describes the false teachers' character. They were proud, ignorant, and obsessed with controversy. Their teaching produced envy, conflict, insults, and suspicion. And they used religion to make money. "Sound words" means healthy teaching, the opposite of the sickly, quarrelsome teaching of these men. Notice that Paul specifically mentions "the words of our Lord Jesus Christ" as the standard.

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 Timothy 6:6–8 — Godliness with Contentment

verse: 1 Timothy 6:6-8

#### Simple Explanation

The false teachers thought godliness was a means of financial gain (v5). Paul turns this idea around. "But godliness with contentment is great gain." Godliness does bring great gain, not necessarily money, but something far more valuable, when it is combined with contentment.

"Contentment" means being satisfied with what one has, free from the restless desire for more. The word was used by Greek philosophers for self-sufficiency. For Paul, contentment comes not from self-sufficiency but from Christ's sufficiency (Philippians 4:11–13).

Verse 7 gives the reason: "For we brought nothing into the world, and we certainly can’t carry anything out." Every person enters life with nothing and leaves with nothing. Job said the same thing after losing everything: [[Job 1:21|Naked I came out of my mother’s womb, and naked will I return there]]. Possessions are temporary.

Verse 8: "But having food and clothing, we will be content with that." The word for "clothing" can include shelter. Paul is describing basic necessities. Having what one needs to live, believers can be content.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Contentment is a rare and precious virtue. Our culture often encourages the opposite: constant dissatisfaction, the desire for more and better, and comparison with others. Advertising thrives on making people feel that they lack something. The Bible offers a different way.

Contentment is not laziness or indifference to legitimate needs. Nor does it mean that poverty is good or that believers should not work to improve their circumstances or help others out of poverty. It means not being controlled by the desire for more. Hebrews 13:5 urges believers to be free from the love of money and content with what they have, because God has promised never to leave or forsake them. The deepest source of contentment is God's presence.

Jesus taught His followers not to store up treasures on earth but in heaven (Matthew 6:19–21), and not to worry about food and clothing, because their heavenly Father knows what they need (Matthew 6:25–33). Contentment flows from trust in God's provision.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can cultivate contentment by practicing gratitude for what they have, resisting the pressure to compare themselves with others, and reflecting on the temporary nature of possessions.

Practical steps might include limiting exposure to advertising, simplifying one's lifestyle, distinguishing between needs and wants, and regularly thanking God for His provision. Contentment can also be strengthened by remembering that one's identity and security come from God, not from what one owns.

For those who struggle with real financial hardship, this passage is not a call to accept injustice or neglect. Believers and churches are called to help meet one another's needs. But even in hardship, contentment in God's presence can bring peace.

### 1 Timothy 6:9–10 — The Love of Money

verse: 1 Timothy 6:9-10

#### Simple Explanation

Paul now warns about the dangers of wanting to be rich. "But those who are determined to be rich fall into a temptation, a snare, and many foolish and harmful lusts, such as drown men in ruin and destruction."

Notice that Paul speaks of those "determined to be rich," those whose goal and desire is wealth. This desire leads to three dangers. It brings "temptation," pressures to compromise and cut corners. It becomes "a snare," a trap that catches and holds. And it leads to "many foolish and harmful lusts," desires that are both senseless and destructive. The end result is like drowning: "ruin and destruction."

Verse 10: "For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil." This verse is often misquoted as "money is the root of all evil." But Paul does not say money itself is evil. He says the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil. Greed can lead to lying, stealing, exploitation, broken relationships, and countless other sins.

"Some have been led astray from the faith in their greed, and have pierced themselves through with many sorrows." The pursuit of money has caused some to abandon their faith. And it brings pain: anxiety, broken relationships, regret, and emptiness.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Jesus warned repeatedly about the dangers of wealth. He said, [[Matthew 6:24|You can't serve both God and Mammon]]. He told the parable of the rich fool, who stored up wealth but was not rich toward God (Luke 12:16–21). He said it is hard for the rich to enter the kingdom of God (Mark 10:23–25). And He described how the deceitfulness of riches can choke the word of God (Mark 4:19).

The love of money is a form of idolatry (Colossians 3:5). It places wealth in the position that belongs to God, as the source of security, identity, and happiness. When money becomes an idol, it demands sacrifices: time, relationships, integrity, and faith.

The image of those who have [[v10|pierced themselves through with many sorrows]] is vivid. Those who pursue wealth often discover that it does not satisfy. Instead, it brings pain. Ecclesiastes 5:10 says that whoever loves money will never be satisfied with money.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can examine their hearts for the love of money. Signs might include constant worry about finances, envy of others' wealth, willingness to compromise integrity for financial gain, neglect of relationships for the sake of work, or difficulty being generous.

This passage does not condemn working hard, earning a good income, or saving responsibly. Scripture commends diligence and wise planning (Proverbs 6:6–8; 21:20). The problem is when money becomes the goal rather than a tool.

Generosity is one of the most effective antidotes to the love of money. Regularly giving to God's work and to those in need loosens money's grip on the heart.

### 1 Timothy 6:11–12 — Flee, Follow, Fight

verse: 1 Timothy 6:11-12

#### Simple Explanation

Paul turns to Timothy personally. "But you, man of God." This title was used in the Old Testament for prophets and leaders like Moses, Samuel, and Elijah. Paul honors Timothy as one who belongs to God and serves Him.

"Flee these things." Timothy must run away from the love of money and the corrupt behavior of the false teachers.

"And follow after righteousness, godliness, faith, love, perseverance, and gentleness." Fleeing is not enough. Timothy must pursue godly qualities. "Righteousness" is doing what is right. "Godliness" is reverence for God shown in life. "Faith" is trust in God, or faithfulness. "Love" is self-giving care for others. "Perseverance" is steadfast endurance. "Gentleness" is a meek and humble spirit.

Verse 12: "Fight the good fight of faith." The Christian life involves struggle. The word for "fight" was used for athletic contests, suggesting effort, discipline, and determination.

"Take hold of the eternal life to which you were called." Timothy is to grasp firmly the eternal life God has given him, living in light of it.

"And you confessed the good confession in the sight of many witnesses." Timothy had publicly declared his faith, perhaps at his baptism or ordination. He is to remain faithful to that confession.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses describe the Christian life with three verbs: flee, follow, and fight. Believers must flee from sin and temptation (2 Timothy 2:22; 1 Corinthians 6:18; 10:14). They must pursue godly character. And they must fight the good fight of faith, standing firm against everything that opposes it.

The list of qualities to pursue overlaps with the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23). These are not achievements earned by effort alone but qualities the Spirit produces as believers cooperate with His work.

"Take hold of the eternal life" is a striking phrase. Eternal life is already given to believers, yet they are called to grasp it, to live in its reality. This is the tension of the Christian life: believers already possess salvation, and they are called to live it out fully.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can ask what they need to flee from: greed, pride, sexual temptation, bitterness, or other sins. Fleeing may mean avoiding certain places, people, media, or situations.

They can also ask what qualities they need to pursue. Rather than simply avoiding sin, the Christian life is an active pursuit of godliness, love, and faithfulness.

The call to "fight the good fight" reminds believers that the Christian life is not always easy. There will be struggles, temptations, and discouragement. But it is a good fight, worth fighting, and God gives strength for it.

### 1 Timothy 6:17–19 — Rich in Good Works

verse: 1 Timothy 6:17-19

#### Simple Explanation

Paul now addresses those who are already wealthy. He does not tell them to give away everything, nor does he condemn them for being rich. Instead, he gives them instructions.

"Charge those who are rich in this present age that they not be arrogant." Wealth can lead to pride, a sense of superiority over others. The rich must guard against this.

"Nor have their hope set on the uncertainty of riches." Wealth is uncertain. It can be lost through economic collapse, theft, illness, or disaster. Proverbs 23:5 says that riches sprout wings and fly away. The rich should not trust in their wealth.

"But on the living God, who richly provides us with everything to enjoy." Hope should be placed in God, who is the source of every good gift. Notice that God gives things "to enjoy." Paul does not say wealth is evil or that believers should feel guilty about enjoying God's gifts.

Verse 18: "that they do good, that they be rich in good works, that they be ready to distribute, willing to share." The rich are to use their wealth to do good, becoming "rich" in a different sense, rich in good deeds. They should be generous and eager to share.

Verse 19: "laying up in store for themselves a good foundation against the time to come, that they may lay hold of eternal life." Generosity is like storing up treasure for eternity. It reflects and strengthens a life focused on what truly lasts.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage gives a balanced view of wealth. Being rich is not a sin in itself, but it brings special temptations and responsibilities. The rich must avoid pride and misplaced trust and must use their wealth generously.

Jesus taught similar principles. He told His followers to store up treasures in heaven (Matthew 6:19–20) and to use worldly wealth to make friends for eternity (Luke 16:9). Zacchaeus, after meeting Jesus, gave half his possessions to the poor and repaid those he had cheated (Luke 19:8). His generosity was a sign of his transformed heart.

The phrase "who richly provides us with everything to enjoy" is a beautiful reminder of God's generosity. God is not stingy. He delights to give good gifts. Believers who recognize this become generous themselves.

"Laying up in store... a good foundation against the time to come" does not mean buying salvation through good works. Salvation is by grace. Rather, generosity shows where one's heart is and reflects a life oriented toward eternity.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

By global standards, many people in wealthy nations are "rich in this present age." This passage speaks directly to them. Believers can guard against pride, place their hope in God rather than in savings or investments, and use their resources generously.

Generosity can take many forms: giving to the church, supporting missions, helping the poor, funding ministries, sharing possessions, and showing hospitality. It can also include giving time, skills, and attention.

Believers can also enjoy God's gifts with gratitude. Paul does not call for guilt over every pleasure, but for a heart that receives God's gifts thankfully and shares them freely.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

First Timothy 6 speaks powerfully to a world shaped by the pursuit of wealth. Paul warns against false teachers who saw religion as a way to make money, and he offers a better path: godliness with contentment is great gain. Since people bring nothing into the world and take nothing out, believers can be content with what they need, trusting God's provision.

The chapter also warns that the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil. Those who are determined to become rich fall into temptation, traps, and harmful desires that can destroy their lives and lead them away from faith. Believers can examine their hearts, resist greed, and practice generosity as a remedy against money's grip.

Paul's charge to Timothy applies to every believer: flee from sin, pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, endurance, and gentleness, and fight the good fight of faith. Believers are called to take hold of the eternal life God has given them and to remain faithful to their confession until Christ appears.

For those who are wealthy, the instructions are clear: do not be proud, do not trust in uncertain riches, hope in God who richly provides everything to enjoy, and be rich in good works, generous, and willing to share. In this way, believers lay up a foundation for the future and take hold of true life.

Finally, Paul urges Timothy to guard what has been entrusted to him. Every believer has received the treasure of the gospel. Guarding it faithfully, living it out, and passing it on are among the greatest responsibilities and privileges of the Christian life.

Free me from the love of money and the restless desire for more. Remind me that I brought nothing into this world and can take nothing out. Help me to be content with what I have and to trust You for what I need. Guard me from pride and from placing my hope in uncertain riches. Make me rich in good works, generous, and eager to share. Help me to flee from sin and to pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, endurance, and gentleness. Give me strength to fight the good fight of faith and to take hold of eternal life. You are the blessed and only Ruler, the King of kings and Lord of lords. To You be honor and eternal power. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
