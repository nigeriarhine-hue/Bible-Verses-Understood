# Luke 5
theme: From Empty Nets to Full Lives: Jesus Calls Sinners
main: After a fruitless night of fishing, Simon obeys Jesus' word, catches an overwhelming number of fish, and falls at Jesus' knees confessing his sinfulness; Jesus calls him to catch people instead. Jesus cleanses a leper, regularly withdraws to pray, forgives and heals a paralyzed man, calls the tax collector Levi, and eats with sinners. He explains that He came for the sick, and that the new life He brings cannot be squeezed into old patterns.

## Chapter Background and Story

Luke 5 continues Jesus' early ministry in Galilee. In chapter 4, He announced His mission in Nazareth, was rejected there, and then taught and healed in Capernaum with great authority. Now Luke shows Jesus calling His first disciples and encountering people at the margins: a fisherman who feels unworthy, a man with leprosy, a paralyzed man, and a tax collector. At the same time, the Pharisees and teachers of the law begin to watch Jesus closely and raise objections. Several of these stories also appear in Matthew and Mark (see Matthew 8:3, Matthew 9:6, Matthew 9:13, Mark 2:5, and Mark 2:22), but Luke tells the calling of Peter in a distinctive way that is found only in his Gospel.

### The Miraculous Catch and the Call of Simon (5:1–11)

Jesus was standing beside the [[v1|lake of Gennesaret]], Luke's name for the Sea of Galilee, taken from the fertile plain on its northwest shore. The crowd was pressing in on Him to hear the word of God. He saw two boats at the water's edge; the fishermen had left them and were washing their nets. Fishermen on the lake typically worked at night, when fish came closer to the surface, and cleaned and repaired their nets in the morning.

Jesus got into one of the boats, which belonged to Simon (later called Peter), and asked him to push out a little from shore. Then He sat down and taught the crowd from the boat. Sitting in a boat slightly offshore allowed His voice to carry over the water to the people on the beach.

When He finished speaking, He said to Simon, [[v4|Put out into the deep and let down your nets for a catch]]. Simon answered with honest reluctance, then obedience. This is studied below. They caught so many fish that their nets began to tear. They signaled their partners in the other boat to come help, and they filled both boats so full that they began to sink.

When Simon Peter saw this, he fell at Jesus' knees and said, [[v8|Depart from me, for I am a sinful man, Lord]]. This response is studied below. Simon and all his companions, including James and John, the sons of Zebedee, were astonished. Jesus said to Simon, [[v10|Don’t be afraid. From now on you will be catching people alive]]. The phrase "catching people alive" uses a word that means to capture alive, rather than to kill, as fish were. Simon would gather people into God's kingdom for life.

They brought their boats to shore, [[v11|left everything, and followed him]]. Having just made the greatest catch of their careers, they left it all behind. The miracle that could have made them wealthy became the moment they gave up their business to follow Jesus.

(Mark and Matthew record a briefer call of these fishermen by the lake, studied at Matthew 4:19 and Mark 1:16–20. Luke's fuller account may describe the same event in more detail or a related occasion. Many readers see Luke's version as explaining why the fishermen were willing to follow so readily: they had witnessed Jesus' power and been confronted with their own unworthiness.)

### Cleansing a Man With Leprosy (5:12–16)

In one of the towns, a man [[v12|full of leprosy]] came to Jesus. Luke the physician notes the severity of the disease. The man fell on his face and begged, "Lord, if you want to, you can make me clean." Jesus reached out, touched him, and said, "I want to. Be made clean." Immediately the leprosy left him. (This healing is studied at Matthew 8:3.) Jesus told him not to tell anyone but to show himself to the priest and offer the sacrifices Moses commanded as a testimony.

Despite this, news about Jesus spread even more, and large crowds came to hear Him and to be healed. Then Luke adds a sentence that reveals something essential about Jesus' life: [[v16|But he withdrew himself into the desert and prayed]]. This is studied below.

### Forgiving and Healing a Paralyzed Man (5:17–26)

One day as Jesus was teaching, Pharisees and teachers of the law were sitting there. They had come from every village of Galilee, Judea, and Jerusalem. Their presence shows that Jesus' ministry was attracting the attention of religious authorities from far and wide. Luke adds, [[v17|The power of the Lord was with him to heal them]].

Some men brought a paralyzed man on a mat, but they could not get him in because of the crowd. So they went up on the roof and lowered him through the tiles, right in front of Jesus. Luke mentions "tiles," which suggests a roof of clay tiles, a type more familiar to his Greek readers than the packed-earth roofs common in Galilee.

Seeing their faith, Jesus said, [[v20|Man, your sins are forgiven you]]. The scribes and Pharisees began reasoning that this was blasphemy, since only God can forgive sins. Jesus knew their thoughts and asked which was easier to say: "Your sins are forgiven" or "Get up and walk." To prove that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins, He told the man to get up, take his mat, and go home. Immediately the man stood up, picked up the mat, and went home [[v25|glorifying God]]. Everyone was amazed and gave glory to God, saying, [[v26|We have seen strange things today]]. (This event is studied at Matthew 9:6 and Mark 2:5.)

### The Call of Levi (5:27–32)

Jesus went out and saw a tax collector named Levi sitting at his tax booth. He said, "Follow me!" Luke adds that Levi [[v28|left everything]]. For a tax collector, this was a dramatic step. Fishermen could return to their boats, but a tax collector who abandoned his post could not easily get it back.

Levi then held a great banquet for Jesus at his house, with a large crowd of tax collectors and others. The Pharisees and their scribes complained to Jesus' disciples: [[v30|Why do you eat and drink with the tax collectors and sinners?]] Jesus answered, [[v31|Those who are healthy have no need for a physician, but those who are sick do]]. Then He added, [[v32|I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners, to repentance]]. Luke alone includes the words "to repentance," making clear that Jesus' welcome of sinners was an invitation to turn to God and be changed. (This saying is studied at Matthew 9:13.)

### The Question About Fasting and the New Wine (5:33–39)

The Pharisees then said that John's disciples and their own disciples often fasted and prayed, but Jesus' disciples ate and drank. Jesus answered that the friends of the bridegroom cannot fast while He is with them, but the days would come when the bridegroom would be taken away, and then they would fast.

He told a parable about new and old. No one tears a patch from a new garment to fix an old one; doing so ruins the new garment, and the patch does not match the old. No one puts new wine into old wineskins, or the skins will burst. New wine must go into new wineskins. (This saying is studied at Mark 2:22.)

Luke alone adds a final saying: [[v39|No man having drunk old wine immediately desires new, for he says, ‘The old is better.’]] This is a gentle, perhaps humorous, observation about human nature. People who are used to the old often resist the new, preferring what is familiar. Jesus was explaining why many religious people resisted His message. They were comfortable with their traditions and did not want the new thing God was doing. The saying is both a warning and an expression of understanding: resistance to change is natural, but it can cause people to miss God's work.

## Important Verses Explained

### Luke 5:5 — [[Luke 5:5|But at Your Word I Will Let Down the Net]]

verse: Luke 5:5

#### Simple Explanation

This verse records Simon's response when Jesus told him to go out into deep water and let down his nets for a catch. The setting is important. Simon was a professional fisherman on the Sea of Galilee. He and his partners had worked hard all night, the best time for fishing, and had caught nothing. Now it was morning, and they were cleaning their nets, ready to go home. Jesus had just used Simon's boat as a platform to teach the crowds.

When Jesus told him to put out into the deep and let down the nets, Simon answered, [[v5|Master, we worked all night and caught nothing]]. This was an honest statement of the facts. Fishing in the daytime, in deep water, after a night of failure, made no sense from a fisherman's perspective. Jesus was a teacher, perhaps a carpenter's son, not a fisherman. Simon could have dismissed His instruction.

But Simon continued, [[v5|but at your word I will let down the net]]. He obeyed, not because the instruction made sense to him, but because Jesus said it. Simon called Jesus "Master," a word used for a person in authority, such as a commander or overseer. Luke uses this word only on the lips of disciples.

The result was overwhelming. They caught so many fish that their nets began to break, and they needed their partners' help. Both boats were filled so full that they began to sink.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that obedience to Jesus' word is the path to fruitfulness. Simon's own expertise and effort had produced nothing. But when he obeyed Jesus' word, even against his own judgment, the result was abundance. This echoes Jesus' teaching that apart from Him, His followers can do nothing (John 15:5).

It also shows that faith and honest doubt can coexist. Simon did not pretend that Jesus' instruction made sense. He stated his reservations honestly. But he chose to obey anyway. Faith is not the absence of questions; it is trust that acts on Jesus' word even when questions remain.

Finally, the abundant catch is a picture of what Jesus would do through Simon's life. Jesus told him that from now on he would catch people. In Acts 2, after Peter preached on the day of Pentecost, about three thousand people believed and were baptized, an abundant "catch" that came through obedience to Jesus.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers who feel that their efforts have been fruitless. Many people work hard in their jobs, families, ministries, or personal growth and see little result. Jesus' word to Simon invites them to keep listening to Him and to obey, trusting that He can bring fruit where their efforts alone have failed.

It also invites believers to obey Jesus even when His instructions do not make sense to them. Scripture sometimes calls believers to forgive when it seems foolish, to give when they feel they have little, or to wait when they want to act. Like Simon, they can say, "At your word, I will," trusting that Jesus knows better than they do.

Finally, this verse shows that honest prayer is welcome. Believers can tell Jesus their doubts and frustrations, as Simon did, and then choose to trust and obey. Honesty and obedience can go together.

### Luke 5:8 — [[Luke 5:8|Depart From Me, for I Am a Sinful Man]]

verse: Luke 5:8

#### Simple Explanation

This verse records Simon Peter's reaction to the miraculous catch of fish. Moments earlier, Simon had told Jesus that he and his partners had fished all night and caught nothing, but he obeyed Jesus' instruction to let down the nets again. The result was a catch so large that the nets began to tear and two boats began to sink.

Simon's response was not excitement about the profit or pride in the catch. Instead, [[v8|when he saw it, fell down at Jesus’ knees]]. Falling to his knees in a crowded, fish-filled boat shows how overwhelmed he was. He said, [[v8|Depart from me, for I am a sinful man, Lord]].

Notice that Simon changed how he addressed Jesus. In verse 5, he called Him "Master." Now he called Him "Lord." Something about the miracle revealed to Simon that Jesus was more than a teacher. He sensed God's presence and power in Jesus, and that awareness made him painfully aware of his own sinfulness.

This kind of reaction appears elsewhere in Scripture. When Isaiah saw the Lord in the temple, he cried, [[Isaiah 6:5|Woe is me! For I am undone, because I am a man of unclean lips]]. When people encounter God's holiness, they become aware of their own unworthiness.

Simon asked Jesus to leave him, not because he did not want Jesus, but because he felt unworthy to be near Him. Jesus did not leave. Instead, He said, [[v10|Don’t be afraid. From now on you will be catching people alive]]. Jesus responded to Simon's confession of sin not with rejection but with a calling.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that a true encounter with Jesus reveals both His holiness and our sinfulness. Simon did not need a sermon about sin; he simply saw Jesus' power and recognized his own unworthiness. Genuine spiritual awakening often begins with this kind of humbling realization.

It also shows Jesus' grace. Simon asked Jesus to go away, but Jesus drew closer and called him into His service. Jesus did not wait for Simon to become worthy before calling him. He called him in his unworthiness. This is the pattern of grace throughout Scripture: God calls sinners, forgives them, and uses them for His purposes.

Finally, Simon's story shows that those who know their sinfulness are often the ones God uses most. Later in Luke, Jesus says that one who has been forgiven much loves much (Luke 7:47). Peter would fail again, denying Jesus three times, and again Jesus would restore him and use him to lead the early church.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites believers to approach Jesus with humility. Recognizing one's sinfulness is not a sign of weak faith; it is often the beginning of genuine faith. Believers can come to Jesus honestly, acknowledging their sins, and trust that He responds with grace.

It also encourages those who feel too sinful to be used by God. Many people think they must clean up their lives before God can use them. Simon's story shows that Jesus calls people in their unworthiness and transforms them. The proper response is not to push Jesus away but to receive His call.

Finally, this verse shows that fear can be replaced by purpose. Jesus told Simon, "Don't be afraid," and then gave him a mission. Believers who feel unworthy or afraid can hear Jesus' words of reassurance and step into the purpose He has for them.

### Luke 5:16 — Jesus Withdrew to Pray

verse: Luke 5:16

#### Simple Explanation

This verse appears in the middle of a period of great popularity in Jesus' ministry. He had just healed a man full of leprosy and told him not to tell anyone. Instead, news about Jesus spread even more, and [[v15|great multitudes came together to hear and to be healed by him of their infirmities]]. The demands on Jesus were increasing. People came from everywhere, wanting healing and teaching.

Then Luke adds, [[v16|But he withdrew himself into the desert and prayed]]. The word "but" shows a contrast: while crowds were gathering, Jesus withdrew. "Desert" refers to lonely, uninhabited places, away from towns and crowds.

The verb forms in Greek suggest that this was a regular habit, not a one-time event. A natural way to translate it is, "But he would often withdraw to lonely places and pray." Jesus made a practice of seeking solitude with His Father, especially when the demands of ministry were greatest.

Luke emphasizes Jesus' prayer life more than any other Gospel writer. He records Jesus praying at His baptism (3:21), before choosing the twelve apostles (6:12), before Peter's confession (9:18), at the transfiguration (9:28–29), before teaching the Lord's Prayer (11:1), in Gethsemane (22:41–44), and on the cross (23:34, 46). Prayer was the foundation of Jesus' ministry.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse reveals that Jesus, though He was the Son of God, lived in dependence on His Father through prayer. His power to heal and teach flowed from His relationship with the Father. If Jesus needed regular time alone with God, His followers need it even more.

It also shows that popularity and success can be spiritually dangerous. Crowds can create pressure, distraction, and the temptation to measure success by numbers or approval. Jesus' habit of withdrawing kept Him focused on His Father's will rather than on public demands. Prayer was His anchor in the midst of activity.

Finally, the verse shows the importance of rhythms in spiritual life. Jesus moved between public ministry and private prayer. Both were essential. Service flowed from communion with God, and communion with God prepared Him for service.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to make prayer a regular habit, not just something done in emergencies. Setting aside consistent time to be alone with God, away from distractions, helps believers stay connected to Him and receive strength and direction.

It is especially relevant for busy seasons. When responsibilities increase, prayer is often the first thing to be neglected. Jesus' example shows that busy times are when prayer is most needed. Believers can protect time for prayer, even when it feels like there is too much to do.

Finally, this verse invites believers to seek solitude. Modern life is filled with noise, screens, and constant connection. Finding a quiet place and time, whether early in the morning, during a walk, or in a quiet room, can create space to hear God and be renewed by Him.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Luke 5 shows Jesus calling unlikely people into His kingdom: a fisherman who knew he was sinful, a man with leprosy who was excluded from community, a paralyzed man who could not come on his own, and a tax collector who was despised by others. In every case, Jesus responded with grace, power, and a call to new life. Believers can take encouragement that Jesus calls people as they are and transforms them.

The miraculous catch teaches that obedience to Jesus' word leads to fruitfulness, even when His instructions seem unreasonable. Believers can bring their honest doubts to Him and then choose to obey, trusting that He knows better. The catch also shows that encountering Jesus' power reveals human sinfulness, and that Jesus meets confession with mercy and calling rather than rejection.

Jesus' habit of withdrawing to pray provides a model for balancing activity and devotion. In busy seasons especially, believers need time alone with God to stay grounded and to discern His will. Prayer is not a luxury but the foundation of fruitful service.

The call of Levi and the banquet with sinners show that Jesus came for those who know they need Him. Believers can welcome people who feel far from God and invite them to repentance and new life, rather than keeping them at a distance. Finally, the sayings about new wine remind believers that Jesus' new life cannot be contained in old patterns, and that people often prefer the familiar. Believers can ask God to keep them open to His work, even when it challenges their comfort.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for meeting Simon in his empty boat and filling his nets beyond what he could imagine. When my efforts seem fruitless, help me to listen to Your word and to obey, even when I do not understand. Like Simon, I confess that I am a sinful person. Thank You that You do not turn away from sinners, but draw near and call us to follow You.

Teach me to follow Your example of withdrawing to pray, especially when life is busy and demanding. Thank You for touching the untouchable, forgiving the paralyzed man, and calling Levi. Help me to welcome those who feel far from You and to invite them to repentance and new life. Keep me from clinging to old patterns when You are doing something new, and make my heart a fresh wineskin for Your new life. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
