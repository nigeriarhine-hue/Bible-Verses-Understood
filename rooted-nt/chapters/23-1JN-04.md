# 1 John 4
theme: God Is Love
main: John warns believers not to believe every spirit but to test the spirits, because many false prophets have gone out into the world. Every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is from God, while every spirit that denies this is the spirit of antichrist. Believers belong to God and have overcome the false prophets, because the One who is in them is greater than the one who is in the world. Then John turns to love: love comes from God, and everyone who loves has been born of God and knows God, while whoever does not love does not know God, because God is love. God showed His love by sending His one and only Son into the world so that we might live through Him; this is love, not that we loved God, but that He loved us and sent His Son as the atoning sacrifice for our sins. Since God loved us so much, we ought to love one another. No one has seen God, but when we love one another, God lives in us and His love is made complete in us, and we know this by the Spirit He has given. Whoever lives in love lives in God, and love made complete gives confidence on the day of judgment. There is no fear in love; perfect love drives out fear. We love because He first loved us. Anyone who claims to love God but hates a brother or sister is a liar, for whoever loves God must also love their brother and sister.

## Chapter Background and Story

First John 4 contains some of the most beautiful words ever written about love. It also contains a sober warning about spiritual deception. The two belong together. The false teachers had distorted the truth about Jesus and had shown no love for the believers. John responds by teaching believers how to recognize false spirits and by describing the true nature of God's love.

In the ancient world, many people claimed to speak by the inspiration of spirits. Prophets, teachers, and religious leaders often claimed special spiritual insight. Some of the false teachers in John's community may have claimed that the Spirit gave them new revelations about Jesus. John tells believers not to accept every spiritual claim but to test it by its teaching about Christ.

The second half of the chapter is a meditation on love. Its central statement, "God is love," appears twice (vv8, 16). John shows that God's love was revealed most clearly when He sent His Son, that this love should flow through believers to one another, and that love casts out fear.

### Test the Spirits (4:1–6)

The call to test the spirits and the victory of believers over false prophets are studied below. [[v5|They are of the world. Therefore they speak of the world, and the world hears them]]. False teachers speak from the world's perspective, and so the world listens to them. Their message is popular because it matches what people already want to hear.

[[v6|We are of God. He who knows God listens to us. He who is not of God doesn’t listen to us. By this we know the spirit of truth, and the spirit of error]]. "We" here refers to the apostles, who bore witness to Christ. Those who belong to God receive the apostolic teaching. This becomes another test: does a teaching agree with the apostles' message, preserved in the New Testament?

### God Is Love (4:7–12)

[[v7|Beloved, let’s love one another, for love is of God; and everyone who loves has been born of God and knows God]]. Love comes from God. Those who truly love, with the self-giving love God shows, reveal that they have been born of God.

[[v8|He who doesn’t love doesn’t know God, for God is love]]. This is one of the most profound statements in Scripture. God does not merely love; He is love. Love is His very nature. Everything He does flows from love, including His justice and His holiness. Because God is love, those who know Him will love. A person who does not love has not truly come to know Him. The way God revealed His love is studied below.

[[v11|Beloved, if God loved us in this way, we also ought to love one another]]. God's love for us is both the model and the motive for our love for one another.

[[v12|No one has seen God at any time. If we love one another, God remains in us, and his love has been perfected in us]]. God is invisible, but His love becomes visible when believers love one another. Love among believers is how the invisible God is made known in the world. Jesus said that all people would know His disciples by their love for one another (John 13:35).

### Living in Love (4:13–17)

[[v13|By this we know that we remain in him and he in us, because he has given us of his Spirit]]. The Holy Spirit within believers assures them that they belong to God.

[[v14|We have seen and testify that the Father has sent the Son as the Savior of the world]]. John again appeals to eyewitness testimony. Jesus is "the Savior of the world," a title the Samaritans gave Him (John 4:42) and one that Roman emperors claimed for themselves.

[[v15|Whoever confesses that Jesus is the Son of God, God remains in him, and he in God]]. Right belief about Jesus is a mark of genuine relationship with God.

[[v16|We know and have believed the love which God has for us. God is love, and he who remains in love remains in God, and God remains in him]]. Believers know God's love by experience and trust it by faith. To live in love is to live in God.

[[v17|In this, love has been made perfect among us, that we may have boldness in the day of judgment, because as he is, even so we are in this world]]. When God's love reaches its goal in believers, they can face the day of judgment with confidence rather than fear. "As he is, even so we are" may mean that believers, united with Christ, share His standing before the Father, or that they reflect His love in the world.

### Love Casts Out Fear (4:18–21)

The power of love over fear is studied below. [[v20|If a man says, “I love God,” and hates his brother, he is a liar; for he who doesn’t love his brother whom he has seen, how can he love God whom he has not seen?]] [[v21|This commandment we have from him, that he who loves God should also love his brother]]. Love for God and love for others cannot be separated. It is easy to claim love for an invisible God, but that love is tested by how one treats visible people. Jesus joined the two great commandments, love for God and love for neighbor (Mark 12:29–31).

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 John 4:1–3 — Test the Spirits

verse: 1 John 4:1-3

#### Simple Explanation

"Beloved, don’t believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God." "Spirit" here refers to the spiritual source behind a teacher or prophet. Not every claim of spiritual inspiration comes from God. Believers are to test such claims.

"Because many false prophets have gone out into the world." False prophets were already active, as Jesus had warned (Matthew 24:11, 24).

Verse 2: "By this you know the Spirit of God: every spirit who confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is of God." The key test is what a teacher says about Jesus. The true Spirit leads people to confess that Jesus Christ came in a real human body. This truth was being denied by the false teachers, who apparently taught that the divine Christ only appeared to be human or was separate from the man Jesus.

Verse 3: "and every spirit who doesn’t confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is not of God; and this is the spirit of the Antichrist, of whom you have heard that it comes. Now it is in the world already." Denying the incarnation is the mark of the spirit of antichrist. This spirit was already at work in John's day.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage teaches that spiritual experiences and claims must be tested. Paul gave similar instructions: [[1 Thessalonians 5:21|Test all things, and hold firmly that which is good]]. Not every voice that claims to speak for God actually does.

The test John gives centers on Jesus. The incarnation, God becoming truly human in Jesus Christ, is at the heart of the gospel. If Jesus did not truly become human, He could not truly die for human sins or be raised as the firstfruits of a new humanity. Any teaching that undermines the truth about Jesus' identity is not from God.

This test applies broadly. Believers can evaluate teachings, spiritual movements, and claims of revelation by asking: What does this say about Jesus? Does it affirm that He is fully God and fully human, crucified and risen? Does it exalt Him or diminish Him? Does it agree with the apostolic witness in Scripture?

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers encounter many spiritual voices today: teachers, books, podcasts, social media influencers, and movements claiming spiritual insight. Not all of them are from God. Believers can test what they hear by Scripture, especially by what it teaches about Jesus.

Testing the spirits is not about being suspicious of everything. It is about being wise and discerning, holding fast to what is true and rejecting what is false.

Believers can also be humble in this process, seeking the counsel of mature believers and the wisdom of the wider church when evaluating new teachings.

### 1 John 4:4 — Greater Is He Who Is in You

verse: 1 John 4:4

#### Simple Explanation

"You are of God, little children." Believers belong to God. They are His children.

"And have overcome them." Believers have overcome the false prophets. They did not fall for the false teaching. They remained faithful to the truth.

"Because greater is he who is in you than he who is in the world." The reason for their victory is not their own strength. It is because God, who lives in them by His Spirit, is greater than the evil one, "he who is in the world," who inspires the false prophets.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse is one of the great promises of Scripture. Believers may feel weak and vulnerable in the face of spiritual opposition, deception, temptation, and hostility. But they are not alone. God Himself dwells in them, and He is infinitely greater than any spiritual power opposed to Him.

The victory described here is already won. Believers "have overcome." This is because Christ has already defeated the devil (Colossians 2:15; Hebrews 2:14). Believers share in His victory. Jesus said, [[John 16:33|Cheer up! I have overcome the world]].

This does not mean believers will never face struggles or that they can be careless. But it does mean they can face spiritual opposition with confidence rather than fear. The One in them is greater.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

When believers face temptation, deception, or spiritual attack, they can remember that God in them is greater than any opposing force. They do not need to fear.

This verse also brings comfort to believers who feel overwhelmed by the evil in the world. Evil is real, but it is not supreme. God is greater.

Finally, believers can rely on God's strength rather than their own. Their victory comes from Him.

### 1 John 4:9–10 — This Is Love

verse: 1 John 4:9-10

#### Simple Explanation

"By this God’s love was revealed in us, that God has sent his only born Son into the world that we might live through him." God's love was made visible in a specific act: He sent His only Son into the world. The purpose was that we might "live through him," receiving eternal life through Jesus.

Verse 10: "In this is love, not that we loved God, but that he loved us." True love is defined not by human love for God but by God's love for humanity. God took the initiative. He loved us first, even when we did not love Him.

"And sent his Son as the atoning sacrifice for our sins." The greatest expression of God's love is the cross, where Jesus became the sacrifice that dealt with our sins.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses echo John 3:16: [[John 3:16|For God so loved the world, that he gave his only born Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish, but have eternal life.]] God's love is not abstract or sentimental. It is shown in a costly, concrete act: the gift of His Son.

The phrase "not that we loved God" is humbling. Human love for God is always a response. God's love came first, and it came to people who were not seeking Him. Paul writes that [[Romans 5:8|while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us]].

The connection between love and atonement is significant. Some people think of God's love and His justice as opposites. But the cross shows them united. God's love provided the sacrifice that satisfied His justice. Love is not ignoring sin; love is dealing with sin at great cost.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can find the measure of God's love for them at the cross. When they doubt that God loves them, they can look at what He gave.

This truth also shapes how believers love others. True love is self-giving, initiating, and costly. It does not wait for others to love first. It reaches out, even to those who do not deserve it.

Finally, believers can rest in the fact that their relationship with God is grounded in His love, not theirs. Their love for Him may waver, but His love for them is steadfast.

### 1 John 4:18–19 — Perfect Love Casts Out Fear

verse: 1 John 4:18-19

#### Simple Explanation

"There is no fear in love." Love and fear are opposites in this context. The fear John has in mind is the fear of punishment, the fear of standing before God in judgment (v17).

"But perfect love casts out fear." "Perfect" means complete, mature, fully developed. When God's love reaches its goal in a believer's life, it drives out the dread of punishment. Those who know they are fully loved by God need not be afraid of Him.

"Because fear has punishment." Fear is connected with the expectation of punishment. It belongs to those who expect condemnation.

"He who fears is not made perfect in love." Someone still gripped by the fear of punishment has not yet fully grasped or experienced God's love.

Verse 19: "We love him, because he first loved us." Some ancient manuscripts lack the word "him," which makes the statement even broader. All human love, for God and for others, is a response to God's prior love.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses describe the freedom that God's love brings. Many people relate to God out of fear, afraid of His anger, afraid of failing, afraid of rejection. John says that God's love removes that kind of fear. Romans 8:15 says believers did not receive a spirit of slavery to fear but the Spirit of adoption. Romans 8:1 says there is no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus.

This does not remove the fear of the Lord in the sense of reverence and awe (Proverbs 9:10; Hebrews 12:28). Believers still honor God as holy and majestic. What is removed is the terror of punishment, because Christ has borne the punishment.

"We love him, because he first loved us" is a simple but profound summary of the Christian life. Believers do not generate love by their own efforts. They receive God's love and then reflect it. The more they understand how much God loves them, the more they are able to love.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers who relate to God mainly out of fear can bring that fear to Him and ask Him to fill them with the assurance of His love. Meditating on passages about God's love, such as this one, John 3:16, and Romans 8:31–39, can help.

As believers grow in confidence of God's love, they can approach Him freely in prayer, confess their sins without dread, and live without the constant fear of rejection.

Finally, believers can let God's love flow through them to others. Those who know they are deeply loved are free to love others generously, without fear.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

First John 4 begins with a call to discernment. Not every spiritual voice is from God. Believers are to test the spirits by what they teach about Jesus: Do they confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh? Do they agree with the apostolic witness? The spirit of antichrist is already at work in the world, but believers have overcome it, because the One who is in them is greater than the one who is in the world.

The heart of the chapter is the declaration that God is love. His love was revealed when He sent His only Son into the world, that we might live through Him, and gave Him as the atoning sacrifice for our sins. This is what love is: not that we loved God, but that He loved us first. Because God loved us this way, we ought to love one another. When we do, the invisible God becomes visible in His people.

Love also brings confidence. Those who live in God's love can face the day of judgment without fear, because perfect love drives out fear. Believers can approach God not with dread, but with the trust of children who know they are loved.

Finally, the chapter insists that love for God and love for others cannot be separated. Anyone who claims to love God but hates a brother or sister is a liar. Believers love because God first loved them, and they show that love in how they treat the people around them.

## Chapter Prayer

Father, give me discernment to test every spirit and to hold fast to the truth that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh. Thank You that the One who is in me is greater than the one who is in the world. Thank You that You are love, and that You showed Your love by sending Your only Son into the world so that I might live through Him. Thank You that You loved me first, and that Jesus is the atoning sacrifice for my sins. Fill me with Your love so that I may love others as You have loved me. Let Your love be made complete in me, and cast out every fear of punishment. Help me to approach You with confidence as Your beloved child. Keep me from claiming to love You while failing to love my brothers and sisters. I love because You first loved me. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
