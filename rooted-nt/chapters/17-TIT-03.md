# Titus 3
theme: Saved by Mercy, Devoted to Good Works
main: Paul tells Titus to remind believers to be subject to rulers and authorities, to be obedient and ready for every good work, to speak evil of no one, to avoid quarreling, and to be gentle and courteous toward all people. They should remember that they too were once foolish, disobedient, enslaved to passions, and full of malice and envy. But when the kindness and love of God our Savior appeared, He saved them, not because of righteous things they had done, but because of His mercy, through the washing of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit, whom He poured out richly through Jesus Christ, so that, justified by His grace, they might become heirs with the hope of eternal life. Paul wants Titus to stress these truths so that believers will devote themselves to good works, while avoiding foolish controversies and warning divisive people. He closes with travel plans, a request to help Zenas and Apollos, and a final encouragement for believers to do good works that meet urgent needs.

## Chapter Background and Story

In the final chapter of Titus, Paul turns from relationships within the church to relationships with the wider world. How should believers in Crete relate to their rulers, their neighbors, and those outside the faith? Crete, like much of the Roman world, had a reputation for unruliness, and its people may have resented Roman rule. Believers were to be different: peaceful, gentle, and devoted to doing good.

The motivation for this kind of life is not human goodness but God's mercy. In verses 3–7, Paul gives one of the richest summaries of salvation in the New Testament. He reminds believers what they once were, what God has done, and what they now have. Because God has been kind to them, they are to be kind to others.

The chapter, and the letter, ends with an emphasis on good works. Paul mentions them three times (vv1, 8, 14). Believers are saved not by good works, but for good works.

### Good Citizens and Gentle Neighbors (3:1–2)

Paul's instructions about relating to authorities and to all people are studied below.

### What God Has Done (3:3–7)

[[v3|For we were also once foolish, disobedient, deceived, serving various lusts and pleasures, living in malice and envy, hateful, and hating one another]]. Paul includes himself: "we were also once." Before God's grace, believers were foolish, rebellious, deceived, enslaved to their desires, and filled with hatred. This memory keeps them humble and gentle toward those who are still in that condition. God's saving work is studied below.

### Devoted to Good Works (3:8–11)

The call to devote oneself to good works is studied below. [[v9|but shun foolish questionings, genealogies, strife, and disputes about the law; for they are unprofitable and vain]]. As in 1 Timothy 1:4 and 2 Timothy 2:23, Paul warns against pointless debates. Speculative arguments about genealogies and fine points of the Law were producing conflict, not godliness.

[[v10|Avoid a factious man after a first and second warning]], [[v11|knowing that such a one is perverted and sinful, being self-condemned]]. A "factious man" is someone who stirs up division, creating parties and splits in the church. Paul gives a clear process: warn him once, then a second time. If he persists, have nothing more to do with him. This follows the pattern Jesus taught in Matthew 18:15–17. The person who continues in divisiveness after repeated warnings shows by his own behavior that something is wrong.

### Final Instructions (3:12–15)

[[v12|When I send Artemas to you, or Tychicus, be diligent to come to me to Nicopolis, for I have determined to winter there]]. Paul planned to send a replacement so that Titus could join him. Nicopolis was a city on the western coast of Greece. Tychicus is mentioned in several of Paul's letters as a trusted messenger (Ephesians 6:21; Colossians 4:7; 2 Timothy 4:12). Artemas is mentioned only here.

[[v13|Send Zenas the lawyer and Apollos on their journey speedily, that nothing may be lacking for them]]. Zenas was a lawyer, perhaps an expert in Roman law or in the Jewish Law. Apollos was the eloquent teacher from Alexandria who had ministered in Ephesus and Corinth (Acts 18:24–28; 1 Corinthians 3:6). Titus and the Cretan believers were to help them on their journey with supplies and support, a practical expression of Christian hospitality.

[[v14|Let our people also learn to maintain good works to meet necessary needs, that they may not be unfruitful]]. Supporting traveling ministers was one example of good works. Believers were to "learn" to do good, to make it a habit, so that their lives would be fruitful.

[[v15|All who are with me greet you. Greet those who love us in faith. Grace be with you all. Amen]]. Paul ends with greetings and a blessing of grace for "all," the whole church.

## Important Verses Explained

### Titus 3:1–2 — Ready for Every Good Work

verse: Titus 3:1-2

#### Simple Explanation

Paul tells Titus to remind believers of their responsibilities in society. "Remind them to be in subjection to rulers and to authorities, to be obedient." Believers should respect and obey the government in matters of civil order. Paul gives similar teaching in Romans 13:1–7.

"To be ready for every good work." Believers should be eager to contribute to the good of their communities.

Verse 2 describes how they should treat everyone. "To speak evil of no one": not slandering or insulting anyone, including rulers and unbelievers. "Not to be contentious": not argumentative or quarrelsome. "To be gentle": reasonable and gracious. "Showing all humility toward all men": treating everyone with courtesy, gentleness, and consideration.

The phrase "toward all men" is important. These attitudes are not just for fellow believers but for everyone, including those who disagree with them, oppose them, or behave badly.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses describe a Christian presence in society that is respectful, constructive, and gracious. Believers are not to be rebellious troublemakers but people who contribute to the common good. Jeremiah 29:7 instructed the exiles in Babylon to seek the peace of the city and pray for it. Peter wrote that believers should honor everyone and honor the king (1 Peter 2:17).

Submission to authorities has limits. When rulers command what God forbids, believers must obey God rather than people (Acts 5:29). Christians throughout history have resisted unjust laws and protected the oppressed. But in ordinary matters, believers are to be law-abiding and respectful.

The command to "speak evil of no one" is challenging, especially in a culture where harsh speech about leaders and opponents is common. Believers can disagree, advocate for justice, and oppose wrongdoing, but they are to do so without slander, insults, or contempt.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can be good citizens: obeying laws, paying taxes, participating in their communities, and seeking the common good. Being "ready for every good work" might mean volunteering, helping neighbors, supporting local schools, or caring for those in need.

These verses also challenge how believers speak about others, especially in political discussions and on social media. Believers can express their views honestly while avoiding slander, insults, and contempt. Gentleness and courtesy toward everyone, including opponents, reflect the character of Christ.

When believers must oppose injustice, they can do so with courage and with grace, standing firmly for what is right while treating others with respect.

### Titus 3:4–5 — Saved by His Mercy

verse: Titus 3:4-5

#### Simple Explanation

After describing what believers once were (v3), Paul describes what God did. "But when the kindness of God our Savior and his love toward mankind appeared." The words "kindness" and "love toward mankind" describe God's generous goodwill toward humanity. The second word is the root of the English word "philanthropy." God's love for humanity "appeared" in the coming of Jesus Christ.

Verse 5: "not by works of righteousness which we did ourselves." God did not save people because of their good deeds. Even their best efforts could not earn salvation.

"But according to his mercy." Salvation flows from God's mercy, His compassion toward those who do not deserve it.

"He saved us through the washing of regeneration and renewing by the Holy Spirit." God saved believers by washing them and giving them new life. "Regeneration" means new birth, being born again (John 3:3–5). "Renewing" means being made new. Both are the work of the Holy Spirit.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses are among the clearest statements in Scripture that salvation is by God's mercy, not human works. They echo Ephesians 2:8–9: "for by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God, not of works."

"The washing of regeneration" has been understood in different ways. Many Christian traditions, including Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran, and Anglican traditions, see a reference to baptism, through which God works to cleanse and give new life. Other traditions, including many Baptist and evangelical churches, understand the washing as the inner cleansing of the heart by the Holy Spirit, which baptism outwardly pictures. Both views agree that new birth and renewal are God's work by the Holy Spirit, not human achievement.

The contrast between verse 3 and verses 4–5 is striking. Believers were once hateful and hated, but God was kind and loving toward them. They did not deserve His mercy, yet He saved them. This is the gospel: God's undeserved love reaching people at their worst.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can rest in the truth that their salvation does not depend on their performance. On days when they feel they have failed, they can remember that God saved them according to His mercy, not their works.

This truth also shapes how believers treat others. Remembering that they too were once foolish and disobedient, they can be gentle and patient with those who are still far from God. Their own salvation by mercy calls them to show mercy.

Believers can also give thanks for the work of the Holy Spirit, who has given them new birth and continues to renew them day by day.

### Titus 3:6–7 — Poured Out Richly, Heirs of Hope

verse: Titus 3:6-7

#### Simple Explanation

Paul continues describing God's saving work. The Holy Spirit is the one "whom he poured out on us richly through Jesus Christ our Savior." God did not give the Spirit sparingly. He poured Him out abundantly, as He promised through the prophet Joel (Joel 2:28) and fulfilled at Pentecost (Acts 2:17, 33). The Spirit comes "through Jesus Christ," who sends Him to His people.

Notice that all three persons of the Trinity are involved: God the Father saves (v4–5), the Holy Spirit renews (v5–6), and Jesus Christ is the one through whom the Spirit is poured out (v6).

Verse 7 gives the purpose: "that being justified by his grace, we might be made heirs according to the hope of eternal life." "Justified" means declared righteous, put right with God. This happens "by his grace," as a gift. And the result is that believers become "heirs." They will inherit eternal life. Right now, they have the hope of it; one day they will possess it fully.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses summarize the whole of salvation: God's kindness, mercy, new birth, the Spirit's renewal, justification by grace, and the inheritance of eternal life. It is a complete picture, from beginning to end, all God's gracious work.

Being "heirs" is a rich image. Paul uses it often (Romans 8:16–17; Galatians 4:7). Heirs receive their inheritance not because they earned it but because of their relationship with the one who gives it. Believers are God's children and therefore heirs of all He has promised.

The word "richly" emphasizes God's generosity. He does not hold back. The Spirit is given in abundance, and with Him comes every spiritual blessing. Believers do not need to fear that God's supply will run out.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can find assurance in these verses. They have been justified by grace, made heirs, and given a sure hope. Their standing with God is secure, based on His grace rather than their achievements.

The rich outpouring of the Spirit invites believers to depend on Him daily. He is not given sparingly. They can ask for His help, guidance, and strength with confidence.

Finally, the hope of eternal life can shape how believers face difficulties. Whatever they lose or suffer now, they are heirs of an inheritance that cannot be taken away.

### Titus 3:8 — Careful to Maintain Good Works

verse: Titus 3:8

#### Simple Explanation

"This saying is faithful." This phrase likely refers to the summary of salvation in verses 4–7. It is trustworthy and true.

"And concerning these things I desire that you insist confidently." Paul wants Titus to teach these truths firmly and clearly. The gospel of grace is not to be mentioned once and then left behind; it must be stressed repeatedly.

"So that those who have believed God may be careful to maintain good works." The purpose of emphasizing grace is that believers will devote themselves to doing good. The word "careful" means to think about, to give attention to. Believers are to be intentional about good works, not leaving them to chance.

"These things are good and profitable to men." Good works benefit others and the community. They are not merely private acts of piety but practical help to people.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows the right relationship between grace and good works. Grace comes first. Believers are saved by mercy, not works (v5). But grace leads to good works. In fact, Paul says that emphasizing grace is what produces good works. Some fear that teaching grace will make people careless about holiness. Paul teaches the opposite: grace, rightly understood, motivates believers to do good.

This echoes Ephesians 2:10: [[Ephesians 2:10|For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared before that we would walk in them]]. Believers are saved by grace for good works.

The phrase "profitable to men" connects good works with love for others. Christian good works are not mainly about religious rituals but about meeting real needs, as verse 14 emphasizes. They are the practical expression of the kindness and love that God has shown.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can be intentional about doing good. This might involve setting aside time and resources for serving others, looking for needs in their community, supporting ministries that help the poor, or showing kindness to neighbors and coworkers.

Churches can emphasize both grace and good works, teaching that salvation is God's free gift and encouraging believers to respond with lives of practical love.

This verse also offers a test of genuine faith. Those who truly believe God's grace will be marked by care for others. A faith that produces no good works should lead to honest self-examination.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Titus 3 shows how believers should live in the world: as good citizens, ready for every good work, speaking evil of no one, avoiding quarrels, and showing gentleness and courtesy to all people. This kind of life stands out in a world full of conflict and harsh words. Believers can respect authorities, contribute to the common good, and engage even their opponents with grace, while still standing firmly for justice.

The motivation for this life is the memory of God's mercy. Believers were once foolish, disobedient, and enslaved to their desires, filled with malice and hatred. But God's kindness and love appeared in Jesus Christ. He saved them not because of their righteous works, but because of His mercy, through new birth and renewal by the Holy Spirit, poured out richly through Christ. Justified by grace, they are now heirs with the hope of eternal life.

This gospel of grace does not make believers careless. On the contrary, Paul insists on it so that believers will devote themselves to good works that meet real needs. Grace saves, and grace produces a life of practical love.

Finally, the chapter calls believers to avoid pointless controversies and to deal wisely with those who cause division. Instead, they are to focus on what is good and profitable: the gospel and the good works it produces. In this way, the kindness of God that appeared in Christ continues to appear through His people in the world.

## Chapter Prayer

God our Savior, thank You for the kindness and love You showed when You appeared in Jesus Christ. I remember that I was once foolish, disobedient, and enslaved to my own desires, yet You saved me, not because of anything I had done, but because of Your mercy. Thank You for washing me, giving me new birth, and renewing me by Your Holy Spirit, whom You poured out richly through Jesus Christ. Thank You that I am justified by Your grace and an heir of eternal life. Help me to be a good citizen and a gentle neighbor, ready for every good work. Guard my tongue from speaking evil of anyone, and keep me from quarrels. Make me courteous and kind to all people. Let Your grace move me to devote myself to good works that meet real needs, so that my life may be fruitful for Your glory. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
