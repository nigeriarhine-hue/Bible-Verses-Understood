# Matthew 28
theme: The Risen King Sends His Disciples to All Nations
main: Early on the first day of the week, women who come to the tomb find it empty and hear from an angel that Jesus has risen, just as He said. Jesus meets them, and the religious leaders bribe the guards to spread a false story. On a mountain in Galilee, the risen Jesus declares that all authority belongs to Him, sends His followers to make disciples of all nations, and promises to be with them always.

## Chapter Background and Story

Matthew 28 is the climax and conclusion of Matthew's Gospel. Jesus had been crucified on Friday and buried in Joseph of Arimathea's tomb before sunset (Matthew 27:57–61). The chief priests and Pharisees, remembering that Jesus had predicted His resurrection, persuaded Pilate to seal the tomb and post a guard (27:62–66). Saturday was the Sabbath, a day of rest. Matthew 28 opens at dawn on Sunday, the first day of the week.

Jesus had repeatedly told His disciples that He would be killed and raised on the third day (Matthew 16:21; 17:23; 20:19). He had also promised that after He was raised, He would go ahead of them into Galilee (26:32). Yet none of the disciples seem to have expected the resurrection. The women came to visit a grave, and the men were in hiding. The events of this chapter took them by surprise.

### The Empty Tomb (28:1–8)

As the first day of the week was dawning, Mary Magdalene and the other Mary (the mother of James and Joseph, 27:56) went to see the tomb. These were the same women who had watched Jesus die and seen where He was buried (27:55–61). Mark and Luke tell us they brought spices to anoint the body (Mark 16:1; Luke 24:1).

Suddenly there was a great earthquake. An angel of the Lord came down from heaven, rolled back the stone, and sat on it. His appearance was like lightning, and his clothes were white as snow. The guards were so afraid that they shook and became like dead men. The irony is striking: the guards posted to keep the dead man in the tomb became like dead men, while the one who had died was alive.

The angel did not roll away the stone to let Jesus out, but to let the witnesses in. He said to the women, [[v5|Don’t be afraid, for I know that you seek Jesus, who has been crucified]]. Then he announced the news that is studied below: [[v6|He is not here, for he has risen, just like he said]]. He invited them to see the place where He had lain, and then told them to go quickly and tell the disciples that Jesus had risen and was going ahead of them to Galilee.

The women hurried away from the tomb [[v8|with fear and great joy]], running to tell the disciples. In that culture, women's testimony was often given less weight than men's, especially in legal matters. If the early church had invented the resurrection story, it would have been unlikely to make women the first witnesses. Their prominence in all four Gospels is one of the marks of historical honesty.

### Jesus Appears to the Women (28:9–10)

As the women went, Jesus Himself met them and greeted them with the ordinary word, [[v9|Rejoice!]], which was also a common greeting meaning something like "Hello" or "Greetings." They came to Him, took hold of His feet, and worshiped Him. Holding His feet shows that He was physically present, not a ghost or vision. Their worship shows that they recognized Him as more than a teacher.

Jesus repeated the angel's message with a tender change: [[v10|Don’t be afraid. Go tell my brothers that they should go into Galilee, and there they will see me]]. He called the disciples who had deserted Him "my brothers." Their failure had not ended their relationship with Him.

### The Guards' Report (28:11–15)

While the women were on their way, some of the guards went into the city and reported to the chief priests everything that had happened. The chief priests met with the elders and devised a plan. They gave the soldiers a large sum of money and told them to say, [[v13|His disciples came by night and stole him away while we slept]]. They promised to protect the soldiers if the story reached Pilate, since sleeping on duty could be punished severely.

The story had obvious problems. If the guards were asleep, how could they know who took the body? And it is hard to imagine that the frightened disciples, who had fled at Jesus' arrest, would overpower or sneak past a guard and break a seal. Matthew notes that this story was still being told among some Jews at the time he wrote his Gospel. His account explains where the rumor came from. Notably, the story assumes that the tomb was empty; the dispute was over how it became empty.

The religious leaders had paid thirty pieces of silver to arrange Jesus' death. Now they paid a large sum to cover up His resurrection. Money could buy a betrayal and a lie, but it could not keep Jesus in the grave.

### The Great Commission (28:16–20)

The eleven disciples, now without Judas, went to Galilee, to the mountain Jesus had designated. Matthew's Gospel often places important moments on mountains: the temptation (4:8), the Sermon on the Mount (5:1), the feeding of the four thousand (15:29), and the transfiguration (17:1). This final mountain scene brings the Gospel to its conclusion.

When they saw Jesus, they worshiped Him, [[v17|but some doubted]]. This honest detail is studied below. Jesus came to them and spoke words that form the climax of the whole Gospel: [[v18|All authority has been given to me in heaven and on earth]]. In the wilderness, the devil had offered Jesus the kingdoms of the world if He would worship him (4:8–9). Jesus refused and took the path of the cross. Now, through His obedience, death, and resurrection, He has received all authority from the Father, not only over the earth but over heaven as well. This fulfills Daniel 7:13–14, where the Son of Man receives dominion over all peoples.

On the basis of this authority, Jesus gave His disciples their mission: to go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and teaching them to obey everything He commanded. Then He gave a promise: [[v20|Behold, I am with you always, even to the end of the age]]. These verses are studied below.

Matthew's Gospel began with the promise that Jesus would be called "Immanuel," meaning [[Matthew 1:23|God with us]]. It ends with Jesus' promise to be with His disciples always. The whole Gospel is framed by the truth that in Jesus, God is with His people.

## Important Verses Explained

### Matthew 28:6 — "He Has Risen, Just Like He Said"

verse: Matthew 28:6

#### Simple Explanation

These words are spoken by an angel to Mary Magdalene and the other Mary at Jesus' tomb early on Sunday morning. The women had come to visit the grave, expecting to find Jesus' body. Instead, there was a great earthquake, and an angel rolled back the stone and sat on it. The Roman guards were terrified and became like dead men. The angel told the women not to be afraid, because he knew they were looking for Jesus, who had been crucified.

Then came the announcement: [[v6|He is not here, for he has risen, just like he said]]. Each part of this statement is important. "He is not here" declares the empty tomb. "He has risen" explains why; the body was not stolen or moved, but Jesus had been raised from the dead by God's power. The verb form emphasizes that God raised Him. "Just like he said" reminds the women that Jesus had predicted this. Three times He had told His disciples that He would be killed and raised on the third day (Matthew 16:21; 17:23; 20:19). His words had come true.

The angel then invited them, [[v6|Come, see the place where the Lord was lying]]. The women could see for themselves that the body was gone. The resurrection was not just a spiritual idea but a bodily event that left an empty tomb.

Immediately afterward, the angel sent them to tell the disciples. Then Jesus Himself met them, and they took hold of His feet and worshiped Him, confirming that He was truly and physically alive.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The resurrection is the foundation of the Christian faith. Paul writes, [[1 Corinthians 15:17|If Christ has not been raised, your faith is vain; you are still in your sins]]. The resurrection shows that God accepted Jesus' sacrifice, that His death really accomplished forgiveness, and that death has been defeated. It vindicates Jesus' claims to be the Messiah and Son of God.

The phrase "just like he said" also teaches that Jesus' words can be trusted. Everything He predicted about His death and resurrection came to pass. If He kept His word about this, believers can trust His other promises, including His promise to return and to raise His people.

The resurrection also gives hope for believers' future. Jesus' resurrection is the beginning of the resurrection of all who belong to Him. Because He lives, they will live. Death is not the end for those who trust in Christ.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse calls believers to live with resurrection hope. Grief, suffering, and death are real, but they are not final. Believers can face loss and their own mortality with confidence that Jesus has conquered death and will raise His people.

It also encourages believers to trust Jesus' words. When His promises seem slow to be fulfilled, believers can remember that He did exactly what He said about rising from the dead. His faithfulness in the past gives confidence for the future.

Finally, the angel's message led immediately to a mission: go and tell. The good news of the resurrection is meant to be shared. Believers can share this hope with others, especially those who are grieving or searching for meaning, by speaking about the risen Jesus and living in a way that reflects His victory.

### Matthew 28:17 — Worship and Doubt on the Mountain

verse: Matthew 28:17

#### Simple Explanation

This verse describes the moment when the eleven disciples saw the risen Jesus on the mountain in Galilee. The women had delivered the message from the angel and from Jesus, and the disciples had obeyed by going to Galilee. Now Jesus appeared to them.

[[v17|When they saw him, they bowed down to him]]. The word means they worshiped Him, falling down before Him as one worships God. These men had followed Jesus for years, and now they recognized Him as far more than a teacher.

Then Matthew adds a surprising phrase: [[v17|but some doubted]]. The word translated "doubted" is the same word used when Peter began to sink in the water: [[Matthew 14:31|why did you doubt?]] It describes hesitation or being divided in mind, rather than outright unbelief. Some of the disciples, even while seeing Jesus, struggled to take it all in. Perhaps they were overwhelmed, uncertain, or still processing their own failure and the shock of the past days. Matthew does not say who doubted or how long their doubt lasted.

What Jesus did next is significant. He did not send away the doubters or wait until everyone was certain. [[v18|Jesus came to them and spoke to them]]. He drew near to the whole group, worshipers and doubters alike, and gave them His commission.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse reveals the honesty of Matthew's Gospel. Matthew could have presented the disciples as perfectly confident at this climactic moment, but he records that some doubted. This honesty strengthens the credibility of the account. The early church did not hide the weaknesses of its leaders.

The verse also shows that worship and doubt can exist together in the same group and even in the same heart. Faith is not always free of questions. The disciples had experienced trauma, failure, and an event unlike anything in human experience. Their hesitation is understandable.

Most importantly, the verse shows Jesus' response to doubt. He came near and spoke to them. He entrusted His mission to people who were still growing in faith. Jesus' work in the world does not depend on His followers' perfect certainty but on His authority and His presence with them.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers who struggle with doubt. Many sincere believers have times of questioning, uncertainty, or hesitation. These experiences do not mean they are excluded from Jesus' presence or purposes. Like the disciples, they can continue to worship, bring their questions to Him, and stay close to the community of faith.

It also encourages believers to welcome others who doubt. Churches can be places where honest questions are allowed and where people with uncertain faith are invited to keep seeking Jesus. Jesus did not wait for perfect faith before drawing near.

Finally, this verse reminds believers that Jesus' mission is carried out by imperfect people. Believers do not need to have all their questions answered before they serve and share their faith. They can step forward in obedience, trusting Jesus' authority and His promise to be with them.

### Matthew 28:19–20 — Make Disciples of All Nations

verse: Matthew 28:19-20

#### Simple Explanation

These verses contain Jesus' final words in Matthew's Gospel, often called the Great Commission. Jesus has just declared, [[v18|All authority has been given to me in heaven and on earth]]. The word "Go" in verse 19 is connected to that authority: because Jesus has all authority, He sends His followers out with His mission.

The main command is [[v19|make disciples]]. A disciple is a learner and follower of a teacher. Jesus is not only telling His followers to win converts but to make people who follow Him in their whole lives. The command includes three supporting actions: going, baptizing, and teaching.

"Go" means that disciples must move outward to reach others rather than waiting for people to come to them. [[v19|of all nations]] marks a major expansion. During His earthly ministry, Jesus had told the Twelve to go only to the lost sheep of Israel (Matthew 10:5–6). Now the mission extends to every people group in the world, fulfilling God's promise to Abraham that through his offspring [[Genesis 22:18|all the nations of the earth will be blessed]].

"Baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" describes the act by which new disciples are publicly identified with the triune God and with His people. "Name" is singular, suggesting the one God revealed as Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

[[v20|teaching them to observe all things that I commanded you]]. Discipleship includes ongoing instruction in Jesus' teaching. Matthew's Gospel contains five major blocks of Jesus' teaching (chapters 5–7, 10, 13, 18, 24–25), and these provide much of the content disciples are to learn and obey. The goal is not merely to know His commands but to "observe" or obey them.

Finally comes the promise: [[v20|Behold, I am with you always, even to the end of the age]]. Jesus will not be physically present in the same way, but He will be with His disciples through every day until His return.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal the global scope of God's plan. From Genesis onward, God intended to bless all nations through Abraham's family. Jesus, the son of Abraham (Matthew 1:1), now sends His disciples to bring that blessing to the whole world. The church's mission is rooted in God's eternal purpose.

The Great Commission also shows that discipleship is comprehensive. It begins with going to people where they are, continues with baptism into the life of God's people, and grows through lifelong learning and obedience. It is not merely a decision but a transformation of life under Jesus' authority.

The promise of Jesus' presence is the foundation of the whole mission. The disciples were not sent out alone or in their own strength. The same Jesus who was "God with us" at His birth (1:23) promises to be "with you always." This presence is fulfilled through the Holy Spirit, whom Jesus promised to send (John 14:16–18; Acts 1:8). The mission is sustained by His authority and His presence.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses call every believer to participate in making disciples. Not everyone is called to travel to distant countries, but every believer can help others know and follow Jesus. This might include sharing their faith with friends, family, and coworkers; supporting missionaries; welcoming people from other cultures; and helping new believers grow.

They also call believers to take discipleship seriously in their own lives. Being a disciple means continually learning and obeying Jesus' teaching. Believers can make a habit of studying His words, applying them to daily decisions, and helping others do the same. Churches can focus on helping people grow in obedience, not merely in knowledge.

Finally, these verses give believers confidence. The task of reaching all nations is enormous, and believers may feel inadequate. But Jesus has all authority, and He has promised to be with His people always. Believers can obey His commission with courage and joy, knowing that He goes with them every day, until the end of the age.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Matthew 28 transforms everything that came before it. The crucified King is alive. The tomb is empty. Jesus' words have proven true. This means that believers can trust His promises, face death with hope, and know that His sacrifice has truly accomplished forgiveness. The resurrection is not only a past event but a present reality: Jesus is alive and active today.

The chapter shows the right responses to the risen Jesus. The women responded with fear and great joy, worship, and obedience as they ran to tell others. The disciples worshiped, even with some doubt. The religious leaders responded with bribery and lies to suppress the truth. Every person must decide how to respond to the claim that Jesus has risen. Believers can respond with worship, trust, and joyful obedience.

Jesus' tenderness toward His failed disciples is also striking. He called them "my brothers" after they had deserted Him, and He entrusted His mission to them even though some still doubted. Believers who feel unworthy or uncertain can take heart: Jesus does not wait for perfect people to carry out His work.

Finally, the Great Commission gives every believer a purpose. Jesus has all authority, and He sends His followers to make disciples of all nations, baptizing them and teaching them to obey His commands. This mission shapes how believers live, how churches use their resources, and how Christians relate to people of other nations and cultures. And it is sustained by His promise: "I am with you always, even to the end of the age." The story Matthew began with "God with us" ends with Jesus' promise to remain with His people forever.

## Chapter Prayer

Risen Lord Jesus, thank You that the tomb is empty and that You have risen just as You said. Thank You for conquering death and for proving that Your sacrifice was accepted for my sins. Fill me with the same fear and great joy that the women felt, and help me to run and tell others the good news.

Thank You for calling Your failing disciples "my brothers" and for drawing near even to those who doubted. When I struggle with doubt, help me to keep worshiping You and to trust Your presence. You have all authority in heaven and on earth. Send me out to help others become Your disciples, to welcome them into Your family, and to teach them to obey everything You have commanded. Give me courage, compassion for people of every nation, and confidence in Your promise that You are with me always, even to the end of the age. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
