# 1 John 1
theme: Walking in the Light
main: John begins by testifying to what he and the other apostles heard, saw with their own eyes, and touched with their hands: the Word of life, the eternal life that was with the Father and was revealed to them. They proclaim it so that their readers may share in their fellowship, which is fellowship with the Father and His Son Jesus Christ, and so that their joy may be complete. The message they heard from Christ is that God is light, and in Him there is no darkness at all. Anyone who claims fellowship with God but walks in darkness is lying. But if believers walk in the light, as God is in the light, they have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus cleanses them from all sin. Those who claim to have no sin deceive themselves, but if they confess their sins, God is faithful and just to forgive them and cleanse them from all unrighteousness. Those who claim they have not sinned make God a liar.

## Chapter Background and Story

First John begins without a greeting, much like the opening of John's Gospel. In fact, the first verses deliberately echo the Gospel's prologue: [[John 1:1|In the beginning was the Word]]. John wants his readers to know from the start that the message they believe is rooted in real, physical encounters with Jesus, the eternal Word who became flesh.

This emphasis was important because some false teachers were denying that Jesus truly came in a physical body (1 John 4:2–3). Influenced by ideas that treated the physical world as inferior, they may have taught that the divine Christ only appeared to be human, or that He was separate from the man Jesus. John counters this by stressing that the apostles heard, saw, looked at, and touched Him.

The second half of the chapter addresses another error. The false teachers apparently claimed to have special fellowship with God while living in moral darkness, and they claimed to be without sin. John insists that fellowship with God, who is light, requires walking in the light, and that honesty about sin is essential.

### The Word of Life (1:1–4)

John's testimony about the Word of life is studied below. [[v4|And we write these things to you, that our joy may be fulfilled]]. Some manuscripts read "your joy." Either way, John's joy, and his readers', is made complete when they share in fellowship with God and with one another. Jesus spoke of His joy being made full in His disciples (John 15:11; 17:13).

### God Is Light (1:5–7)

The message that God is light, and what it means to walk in the light, are studied below. [[v6|If we say that we have fellowship with him and walk in the darkness, we lie and don’t tell the truth]]. This is the first of three "if we say" statements (vv6, 8, 10), each exposing a false claim. Claiming to know God while living in sin is a lie. John is blunt because the false teachers were making exactly this claim.

### Confessing Our Sins (1:8–10)

[[v8|If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us]]. The second false claim is to have no sin, perhaps meaning to have no sinful nature, or to think sin no longer matters. Such a claim is self-deception. The promise of forgiveness for those who confess is studied below.

[[v10|If we say that we haven’t sinned, we make him a liar, and his word is not in us]]. The third false claim is to have never sinned. This contradicts God's word, which declares that all have sinned (Romans 3:23). To deny one's sin is to call God a liar, since He has said all people sin and has sent His Son to deal with it.

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 John 1:1–3 — That Which We Have Seen and Touched

verse: 1 John 1:1-3

#### Simple Explanation

"That which was from the beginning." The Word existed from the beginning, before creation, as John 1:1 says. He is eternal.

"That which we have heard, that which we have seen with our eyes, that which we saw, and our hands touched." John piles up sensory experiences. The apostles heard Jesus teach. They saw Him with their own eyes. They looked at Him carefully, observing Him over time. They even touched Him. After the resurrection, Jesus invited His disciples to touch Him to prove He was not a ghost (Luke 24:39; John 20:27).

"Concerning the Word of life." Jesus is the Word who gives life and who is life itself.

Verse 2: "and the life was revealed, and we have seen, and testify, and declare to you the life, the eternal life, which was with the Father, and was revealed to us." The eternal life that existed with the Father became visible in Jesus. The apostles witnessed it and now proclaim it.

Verse 3: "that which we have seen and heard we declare to you, that you also may have fellowship with us." The apostles share their testimony so that others can join their fellowship. "Fellowship" means sharing, partnership, a common life.

"Yes, and our fellowship is with the Father and with his Son, Jesus Christ." This fellowship is not merely human friendship. It is a shared life with God Himself.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses affirm two essential truths about Jesus. First, He is eternal: He existed "from the beginning" and was "with the Father." Second, He is fully human: He could be heard, seen, and touched. Jesus is both God and man. The false teachers denied His true humanity; John insists on it.

The Christian faith is grounded in eyewitness testimony. The apostles were not sharing ideas or theories but reporting what they had experienced. Peter made the same claim (2 Peter 1:16). This historical foundation gives believers confidence.

The goal of the apostolic testimony is fellowship. God's purpose in sending His Son was not only to forgive sins but to bring people into relationship with Himself and with one another. The Christian life is a shared life, with the Father, the Son, and fellow believers.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can be confident that their faith rests on real events witnessed by real people. When doubts arise, they can return to the eyewitness testimony recorded in Scripture.

This passage also invites believers to experience fellowship with God. Faith is not merely believing facts but entering into a relationship with the Father through the Son.

Finally, believers can value fellowship with other believers. The Christian life is not meant to be lived alone. Sharing life with other believers, in worship, prayer, and friendship, is part of God's design.

### 1 John 1:5 — God Is Light

verse: 1 John 1:5

#### Simple Explanation

"This is the message which we have heard from him and announce to you." The apostles received this message from Jesus and pass it on.

"That God is light." Light is one of Scripture's great images for God. Light represents truth, purity, holiness, goodness, and revelation. Where light shines, darkness is exposed and driven away.

"And in him is no darkness at all." There is no evil, falsehood, or impurity in God. He is completely good, completely true, completely holy. The emphasis is strong: "no darkness at all."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This short verse is foundational for everything that follows in the letter. Because God is light, those who have fellowship with Him must walk in the light. Darkness and light cannot coexist.

The image of light runs throughout Scripture. At creation, God said, [[Genesis 1:3|Let there be light]]. The psalmist wrote, [[Psalm 27:1|The LORD is my light and my salvation]]. Jesus said, [[John 8:12|I am the light of the world]]. In the new creation, there will be no need for the sun, because God's glory will be its light (Revelation 21:23).

The truth that God has "no darkness at all" brings great comfort. God is never deceptive, never cruel, never unjust. He can be trusted completely. His character is purely good.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can trust God completely, knowing there is no darkness in Him. When life is confusing or painful, they can hold onto the truth that God is good and true.

This verse also calls believers to examine their own lives. Are there areas of darkness, hidden sin, deception, or dishonesty, that need to be brought into God's light?

Finally, believers can reflect God's light in the world, living lives of truth, goodness, and love that point others to Him.

### 1 John 1:7 — Cleansed by the Blood of Jesus

verse: 1 John 1:7

#### Simple Explanation

"But if we walk in the light as he is in the light." To "walk" means to live, to conduct one's daily life. Walking in the light means living in honesty, openness, and obedience before God, with nothing hidden.

"We have fellowship with one another." When believers live in the light, they can have genuine fellowship with one another. Honesty and openness make true relationships possible.

"And the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanses us from all sin." Walking in the light does not mean being sinless. It means living honestly before God, including being honest about sin. And as believers do so, Jesus' blood, His sacrificial death, keeps cleansing them from all sin. The verb is in the present tense: it continually cleanses.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse holds together two truths that might seem to be in tension: walking in the light and needing cleansing from sin. Walking in the light is not about perfection; it is about honesty and direction. Believers who walk in the light still sin, but they do not hide it. They bring it into the light, where Christ's blood cleanses it.

"The blood of Jesus" refers to His sacrificial death on the cross. As Hebrews explains, His blood was offered once for all and is sufficient to cleanse completely (Hebrews 9:14; 10:10). That cleansing is ongoing for believers as they walk with Him.

The connection between walking in the light and fellowship with one another is significant. Hidden sin damages relationships. Honesty, confession, and forgiveness build genuine community. James 5:16 also connects confession to one another with healing.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can practice walking in the light by being honest with God about their sins, struggles, and failures, rather than hiding them or pretending.

This openness can also extend to trusted fellow believers. Having a few people with whom one can be honest about struggles brings support, accountability, and deeper fellowship.

Finally, believers can rest in the ongoing cleansing of Christ's blood. They do not need to carry guilt. As they walk in the light, Christ keeps cleansing them.

### 1 John 1:9 — If We Confess Our Sins

verse: 1 John 1:9

#### Simple Explanation

"If we confess our sins." To "confess" means to say the same thing as God about our sins: to agree with Him that they are wrong, to acknowledge them honestly, without excuses.

"He is faithful." God keeps His promises. He has promised to forgive those who confess, and He will.

"And righteous." God's forgiveness is also just. This might seem surprising, since forgiving sin might seem to overlook justice. But God forgives justly, because Christ has already paid the penalty for sin (1 John 2:2). God is not ignoring sin; He has dealt with it in Christ.

"To forgive us the sins." God releases believers from the guilt of their sins.

"And to cleanse us from all unrighteousness." Beyond forgiveness, God cleanses, purifying believers from the stain and effects of sin. "All" means nothing is left out.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse is one of the most comforting promises in Scripture. It assures believers that confession leads to forgiveness and cleansing every time, not because they deserve it, but because God is faithful and just.

The words "faithful and righteous" show that forgiveness rests on God's character and on Christ's work. God is faithful to His promise, and He is just because the penalty has already been paid. Believers can be completely confident that confessed sin is forgiven.

Confession is not a way of earning forgiveness. It is the honest acknowledgment that opens the heart to receive what God freely gives. David experienced this: [[Psalms 32:5|I acknowledged my sin to you]]... [[Psalms 32:5|and you forgave the iniquity of my sin]]. Proverbs 28:13 says, "He who conceals his sins doesn't prosper, but whoever confesses and renounces them finds mercy."

This verse is written to believers. It shows that believers still sin and still need to confess. Confession is a regular part of the Christian life, not a one-time event at conversion.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can make confession a regular habit, bringing their sins honestly to God in prayer, naming them specifically, and receiving His forgiveness.

When believers struggle with guilt over confessed sin, they can return to this promise. God is faithful and just to forgive. They do not need to keep punishing themselves for what God has forgiven.

Finally, believers can extend the same forgiveness to others. Having received such generous forgiveness, they are called to forgive those who wrong them (Ephesians 4:32). This does not mean ignoring harm or abuse, but it does mean releasing bitterness and revenge.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

First John 1 grounds the Christian faith in what the apostles heard, saw, and touched: Jesus Christ, the eternal Word of life who became truly human. Believers can rest their faith on this historical testimony. The goal of the apostles' message was fellowship, a shared life with the Father, with His Son Jesus Christ, and with one another, leading to complete joy.

The central message is that God is light, and in Him there is no darkness at all. He is completely good, true, and holy. Those who claim to know Him must walk in the light, living in honesty and openness before Him. Claiming fellowship with God while living in darkness is a lie.

Walking in the light does not mean being sinless. John is clear that anyone who claims to be without sin is self-deceived. Rather, walking in the light means honesty about sin. As believers live openly before God, they enjoy fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus keeps cleansing them from all sin.

The chapter offers a beautiful promise: if we confess our sins, God is faithful and just to forgive us and cleanse us from all unrighteousness. Believers can bring their sins honestly to God, confident that He will forgive. They can live free from hidden guilt, in the light of His love, and extend the same forgiveness to others.

Thank You for bringing me into fellowship with the Father and with You, and with Your people. Father, You are light, and in You there is no darkness at all. Help me to walk in the light, living honestly before You, with nothing hidden. Keep me from deceiving myself about my sin. Help me to forgive others as You have forgiven me. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
