# Romans 10
theme: The Word of Faith Is Near: Whoever Calls on the Lord Will Be Saved
main: Paul prays for Israel's salvation, acknowledging their zeal for God but explaining that they sought to establish their own righteousness rather than submitting to God's righteousness, which is found in Christ, the goal of the Law. The righteousness of faith does not require impossible journeys to heaven or the depths; the word is near: confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, and you will be saved. There is no distinction between Jew and Gentile, for whoever calls on the Lord's name will be saved. This requires messengers to be sent, because faith comes from hearing the message about Christ, and God continues to stretch out His hands to His people.

## Chapter Background and Story

Romans 10 continues Paul's discussion of Israel that began in chapter 9. In chapter 9, Paul emphasized God's sovereign freedom and mercy. In chapter 10, he emphasizes human responsibility: Israel's failure to believe, and the need for the gospel to be proclaimed and received by faith. Both truths are held together in Paul's thinking.

The chapter also contains one of the clearest summaries of how a person is saved (10:9–10) and one of the strongest calls to mission in the New Testament (10:14–15).

### Zeal Without Knowledge (10:1–4)

Paul begins with his heart's desire: [[v1|Brothers, my heart’s desire and my prayer to God is for Israel, that they may be saved]]. Paul continued to pray for his people. His teaching about God's sovereignty in chapter 9 did not stop him from praying earnestly for their salvation.

[[v2|For I testify about them that they have a zeal for God, but not according to knowledge]]. Paul knew this from experience. He had been zealous for God himself, persecuting the church (Galatians 1:14; Philippians 3:6). Zeal is admirable, but it is not enough. It must be guided by true knowledge.

[[v3|For being ignorant of God’s righteousness, and seeking to establish their own righteousness, they didn’t subject themselves to the righteousness of God]]. Many in Israel did not understand that God's righteousness is a gift received by faith. Instead, they tried to build their own righteousness through keeping the Law. In doing so, they did not submit to God's way. Paul's statement about Christ and the Law is studied below.

### The Word Is Near (10:5–13)

Paul contrasts two approaches to righteousness. [[v5|For Moses writes about the righteousness of the law, “The one who does them will live by them.”]] (Leviticus 18:5). The way of the Law requires doing everything it commands. If anyone perfectly obeyed, they would live, but no one does.

[[v6|But the righteousness which is of faith says this, “Don’t say in your heart, ‘Who will ascend into heaven?’ (that is, to bring Christ down);]] [[v7|or, ‘Who will descend into the abyss?’ (that is, to bring Christ up from the dead.)”]]. Paul draws on Deuteronomy 30:11–14, where Moses told Israel that God's command was not too hard or far away; it was near them. Paul applies this to Christ. No one needs to climb to heaven to bring Christ down, because He has already come. No one needs to descend into the depths to bring Him back from the dead, because God has already raised Him. Salvation is not a matter of heroic human achievement.

[[v8|But what does it say? “The word is near you, in your mouth and in your heart;” that is, the word of faith which we preach]]. The gospel is near, accessible to everyone. Paul then explains what it calls for, studied below. He continues, [[v11|For the Scripture says, “Whoever believes in him will not be disappointed.”]] (Isaiah 28:16). [[v12|For there is no distinction between Jew and Greek; for the same Lord is Lord of all, and is rich to all who call on him]]. One Lord rules over all people, and He is generous to all who call on Him. The universal promise is studied below.

### How Will They Hear? (10:14–17)

Paul then shows the necessity of proclaiming the gospel, studied below. [[v16|But they didn’t all listen to the glad news. For Isaiah says, “Lord, who has believed our report?”]] (Isaiah 53:1). Not everyone who hears believes. [[v17|So faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God]]. Some manuscripts read "the word of Christ," meaning the message about Christ. Faith is born when people hear the gospel.

### Israel Heard but Did Not Respond (10:18–21)

Has Israel heard? [[v18|Yes, most certainly, “Their sound went out into all the earth, their words to the ends of the world.”]] Paul quotes Psalm 19:4, which speaks of creation's witness to God, and applies it to the spread of the gospel. The message had gone out widely.

Did Israel understand? Paul quotes Moses: [[v19|I will provoke you to jealousy with that which is no nation. I will make you angry with a nation void of understanding.]] (Deuteronomy 32:21). God would use the Gentiles' faith to provoke Israel to jealousy, a theme Paul develops in chapter 11. And Isaiah: [[v20|I was found by those who didn’t seek me. I was revealed to those who didn’t ask for me.]] (Isaiah 65:1). The Gentiles found God without seeking Him.

The chapter ends with a moving picture of God's patience: [[v21|But about Israel he says, “All day long I stretched out my hands to a disobedient and contrary people.”]] (Isaiah 65:2). God has not given up on His people. He stretches out His hands like a parent pleading with a child, inviting them to come. This prepares for chapter 11, where Paul shows that God has not rejected Israel.

## Important Verses Explained

### Romans 10:4 — Christ, the Fulfillment of the Law

verse: Romans 10:4

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been explaining why many of his fellow Jews had not believed in Jesus. They had a zeal for God, but they did not understand God's way of righteousness. Instead of receiving righteousness as a gift, they tried to establish their own righteousness by keeping the Law.

Then Paul declared, [[v4|For Christ is the fulfillment of the law for righteousness to everyone who believes]]. The word translated "fulfillment" (telos in Greek) can mean "end," "goal," or "completion." Both meanings are important.

Christ is the "goal" of the Law. The Law pointed forward to Him. The sacrifices, the priesthood, the temple, and the promises all found their fulfillment in Him. The Law was like a road leading to a destination, and Christ is that destination.

Christ is also the "end" of the Law as a way of gaining righteousness. Since Christ has come, people no longer try to become righteous by keeping the Law. Instead, they receive righteousness through faith in Him.

[[v4|for righteousness to everyone who believes]]. This righteousness is available to "everyone who believes," Jew and Gentile alike. No one is excluded.

This does not mean the Law has no value. Paul has said that the Law is holy, righteous, and good (7:12). The moral teaching of the Law still reveals God's will. But the Law is no longer the means of becoming right with God. That role belongs to Christ alone.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that the whole Old Testament points to Christ. Jesus said, [[Matthew 5:17|Don’t think that I came to destroy the law or the prophets. I didn’t come to destroy, but to fulfill]]. Christ is the center of God's plan, and the Law finds its meaning in Him.

It also shows that righteousness is found only in Christ. Paul wrote to the Philippians that he wanted to be found in Christ, [[Philippians 3:9|not having a righteousness of my own, that which is of the law, but that which is through faith in Christ, the righteousness which is from God by faith]].

Finally, the verse shows the universal scope of the gospel. Righteousness comes to "everyone who believes." The Law had separated Jews from Gentiles; Christ unites them through faith.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse frees believers from trying to make themselves righteous through rule-keeping. Christ has done what the Law could not. Believers can rest in His righteousness.

It also encourages believers to read the Old Testament with Christ in view. The Law, the sacrifices, and the promises all point to Him. Seeing Christ in the Old Testament deepens understanding and worship.

Finally, the verse reminds believers that righteousness is available to everyone. No one needs to meet religious requirements first. Anyone who believes in Christ receives His righteousness.

### Romans 10:9–10 — Confess and Believe

verse: Romans 10:9-10

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been explaining that righteousness by faith does not require impossible achievements. No one needs to climb to heaven or descend into the depths. The message is near, "in your mouth and in your heart." Now Paul explains what this means.

[[v9|that if you will confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord]]. To "confess" means to declare openly, to say publicly. The confession is "Jesus is Lord." In the Roman world, people were expected to say "Caesar is Lord." For Christians to say "Jesus is Lord" was a bold declaration of loyalty to Jesus above all. In the Greek Old Testament, "Lord" was the word used for God's name. To confess Jesus as Lord means to acknowledge His divine authority and to submit to Him.

[[v9|and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead]]. The heart, in Scripture, is the center of a person's thoughts, feelings, and will. Believing "in your heart" means trusting with one's whole being, not merely agreeing with an idea. The content of this belief is that God raised Jesus from the dead. The resurrection is central to the gospel.

[[v9|you will be saved]]. The result is salvation. This is God's promise to all who confess and believe.

[[v10|For with the heart one believes resulting in righteousness; and with the mouth confession is made resulting in salvation]]. Paul explains the connection. Inner faith leads to righteousness, being right with God. Outer confession leads to salvation. These are not two separate steps but two sides of the same response: true faith in the heart naturally expresses itself in confession with the mouth.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses summarize how a person is saved. Salvation comes through faith in the risen Jesus as Lord. It is simple, but it is not shallow. Confessing Jesus as Lord means giving Him one's whole life.

They also show that faith is both inward and outward. Genuine faith is not hidden; it is confessed. Jesus said, [[Matthew 10:32|Everyone therefore who confesses me before men, I will also confess him before my Father who is in heaven]].

Finally, these verses show the centrality of the resurrection. Believing that God raised Jesus from the dead is essential. The resurrection proves that Jesus is who He claimed to be and that His sacrifice was accepted.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses give a clear answer to anyone who asks how to be saved. Believers can share this simple message: confess that Jesus is Lord, and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead.

They also encourage believers to confess their faith openly. Many believers keep their faith private. Paul's words remind them that true faith is expressed in confession. Believers can speak openly about Jesus with gentleness and respect.

Finally, these verses invite believers to examine whether Jesus is truly Lord in their lives. Confessing Jesus as Lord means giving Him authority over every area: relationships, work, money, and choices. Believers can ask whether there are areas they have not yet surrendered to Him.

### Romans 10:13 — Whoever Will Call on the Name of the Lord

verse: Romans 10:13

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had just said that there is no distinction between Jew and Greek, because the same Lord is Lord of all and is generous to all who call on Him. Now he supports this with a quotation from Scripture.

[[v13|For, “Whoever will call on the name of the Lord will be saved.”]] This verse comes from Joel 2:32. Peter also quoted it in his sermon on the day of Pentecost (Acts 2:21).

"Whoever" means anyone. There are no exceptions based on nationality, background, past sins, or social status. The promise is open to all.

"Call on the name of the Lord" means to cry out to God for help, to pray to Him in trust. In the Old Testament, it describes worship and dependence on God. In Joel, "the Lord" refers to Yahweh, the God of Israel. Paul applies it to Jesus, whom he has just called Lord (10:9). This shows that Paul regarded Jesus as fully divine, sharing the name and identity of Israel's God.

"Will be saved" is the promise. Salvation is certain for those who call on the Lord.

This verse is beautifully simple. Salvation is not reserved for the religious elite or for those who have achieved great things. Anyone who calls on Jesus in faith will be saved.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows the universal offer of the gospel. God's salvation is not limited to one people. It is offered to "whoever." This fulfills God's promise to Abraham that all nations would be blessed through him (Genesis 12:3).

It also shows the deity of Christ. By applying Joel's words about Yahweh to Jesus, Paul shows that Jesus shares the identity of the one true God. Calling on Jesus is calling on God.

Finally, the verse shows that salvation requires a response. People must "call on the name of the Lord." God offers salvation freely, but it must be received. This prepares for Paul's questions in the next verses about how people can call on someone they have not heard of.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse brings hope to anyone who feels far from God. No matter what someone has done or where they come from, if they call on the name of the Lord, they will be saved.

It also encourages believers to pray with confidence. Believers can call on the Lord in every need, trusting that He hears and responds.

Finally, the verse motivates believers to share the gospel. If "whoever" calls on the Lord will be saved, then every person needs to hear about Him. Believers can share Jesus' name with everyone they meet.

### Romans 10:14–15 — How Beautiful Are the Feet

verse: Romans 10:14-15

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had just declared that whoever calls on the name of the Lord will be saved. But this raises a practical question: how can people call on someone they do not know? Paul asks a series of questions, each building on the one before.

[[v14|How then will they call on him in whom they have not believed?]] People will not call on the Lord unless they believe in Him.

[[v14|How will they believe in him whom they have not heard?]] People cannot believe in someone they have never heard of.

[[v14|How will they hear without a preacher?]] People cannot hear unless someone tells them. "Preacher" here means anyone who proclaims the message, not only ordained ministers.

[[v15|And how will they preach unless they are sent?]] Messengers must be sent. This is the beginning of the chain.

So the chain is: sent, preach, hear, believe, call, be saved. If any link is missing, the chain breaks.

[[v15|As it is written: “How beautiful are the feet of those who preach the Good News of peace, who bring glad tidings of good things!”]] Paul quotes Isaiah 52:7. In Isaiah, the image is of a messenger running across the mountains to bring news of victory and the return from exile. To those waiting for good news, the dusty feet of the messenger look beautiful. Paul applies this to those who bring the gospel.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that God uses people to spread the gospel. God could have chosen to reveal Himself directly to every person, but He chose to use messengers. This is a great privilege and responsibility for believers. Jesus commissioned His followers to [[Mark 16:15|Go into all the world, and preach the Good News to the whole creation]].

They also show the importance of sending. Some go, and others send. Churches that send and support missionaries are part of the chain of salvation. Paul relied on churches to send and support him (Romans 15:24; Philippians 4:15–16).

Finally, these verses show the beauty of the gospel. The message is "good news," "glad tidings of good things." Those who bring it are bringing the best news in the world.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses challenge believers to share the gospel. Many people around them have not heard or understood the good news of Jesus. Believers can be the messengers who tell them.

They also encourage believers to support mission. Not everyone is called to go to distant places, but everyone can participate by praying, giving, and encouraging those who go. Churches can make sending a priority.

Finally, these verses honor those who share the gospel. Pastors, missionaries, evangelists, and ordinary believers who share their faith are doing beautiful work. Believers can thank and support them, and they can see their own witness as part of this beautiful calling.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Romans 10 begins with Paul's prayer for his people's salvation. Like Paul, believers can pray earnestly for those who do not yet know Christ, including those who are religious but have not understood God's gift of righteousness. Zeal for God is good, but it must be joined with knowledge of the truth.

The chapter also shows that Christ is the goal and fulfillment of the Law. Righteousness is not achieved by human effort but received through faith in Him. Believers can stop trying to establish their own righteousness and submit to God's righteousness, found in Christ.

Romans 10 gives a clear and simple message of salvation: confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord, and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead. Whoever calls on the name of the Lord will be saved, with no distinction between people. Believers can rest in this promise for themselves and share it confidently with others, while also confessing their faith openly.

Finally, the chapter calls believers to mission. People cannot call on someone they have not believed, believe in someone they have not heard of, or hear without someone telling them. Faith comes by hearing the message about Christ. Believers can share the gospel, support those who are sent, and remember that God, with outstretched hands, still invites all people to come to Him.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, I confess that You are Lord, and I believe in my heart that God raised You from the dead. Thank You for the promise that whoever calls on Your name will be saved. Thank You that You are the fulfillment of the Law and that I can receive Your righteousness by faith. Keep me from trying to establish my own righteousness. Give me a heart like Paul's that prays for the salvation of others, especially my friends, family, and neighbors. Make me willing to share Your good news, and let my feet be beautiful as I bring glad tidings to others. Bless those who are sent to preach the gospel around the world, and show me how to support them. Thank You, Father, that You stretch out Your hands to all people. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
