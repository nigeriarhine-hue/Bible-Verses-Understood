# 1 Peter 4
theme: Living for God's Will and Sharing Christ's Sufferings
main: Since Christ suffered in the flesh, believers are to arm themselves with the same attitude, living the rest of their earthly lives not for human desires but for the will of God. They have spent enough time in the past in the reckless behavior of their surrounding culture, and their former companions are surprised and slander them for no longer joining in, but those slanderers will give account to God, the Judge of the living and the dead. Because the end of all things is near, believers should be clear-minded and self-controlled for prayer, love one another deeply, since love covers a multitude of sins, offer hospitality without grumbling, and use whatever gift they have received to serve others as good stewards of God's grace, speaking as one who speaks God's words and serving with the strength God provides, so that God may be glorified through Jesus Christ. They should not be surprised by the fiery trial testing them, but rejoice that they share in Christ's sufferings, so they will be overjoyed when His glory is revealed. If they are insulted for Christ's name, they are blessed, for the Spirit of glory rests on them. They must not suffer as wrongdoers, but if they suffer as Christians, they should not be ashamed but glorify God. Judgment begins with God's household, so those who suffer according to God's will should entrust themselves to their faithful Creator and keep doing good.

## Chapter Background and Story

First Peter 4 continues the theme of suffering that runs through the letter. Peter has shown that Christ suffered unjustly and was vindicated (1 Peter 3:18–22). Now he applies this to his readers' situation. They were suffering because they had changed their way of life. Former friends who once joined them in drunken parties, immorality, and idol worship now found them strange and spoke against them.

Peter calls this suffering a "fiery trial" (v12). Some scholars think this refers to the intensifying persecution of Christians in the Roman Empire, perhaps under Nero, who blamed Christians for the great fire of Rome in A.D. 64. Others think it describes the ongoing social hostility believers faced. Either way, Peter wants his readers to understand that suffering for Christ is not strange or unexpected. It is a sharing in Christ's own sufferings, and it leads to glory.

In the middle of the chapter, Peter gives practical instructions for life in the Christian community: prayer, love, hospitality, and the use of spiritual gifts. In times of pressure, believers need one another more than ever.

### Living for God's Will (4:1–6)

[[v1|Therefore, since Christ suffered for us in the flesh, arm yourselves also with the same mind; for he who has suffered in the flesh has ceased from sin]], [[v2|that you no longer should live the rest of your time in the flesh for the lusts of men, but for the will of God]]. Believers are to "arm themselves," like soldiers putting on armor, with the same attitude Christ had: a willingness to suffer rather than sin. "He who has suffered in the flesh has ceased from sin" is a difficult phrase. It may mean that those willing to suffer for Christ have made a decisive break with sin, or that union with Christ in His death frees believers from sin's power (Romans 6:7). Either way, the goal is to live for God's will rather than human desires.

[[v3|For we have spent enough of our past time doing the desire of the Gentiles, and having walked in lewdness, lusts, drunken binges, orgies, carousings, and abominable idolatries]]. Peter describes the lifestyle many of his readers had left behind. "Gentiles" here means those who do not know God. Enough time had been wasted on such things.

[[v4|They think it is strange that you don’t run with them into the same excess of riot, speaking evil of you]]. Former companions were surprised and offended that believers no longer joined them. Their surprise turned to slander. [[v5|They will give account to him who is ready to judge the living and the dead]]. Those who slander believers will answer to God.

[[v6|For to this end the Good News was preached even to the dead, that they might be judged indeed as men in the flesh, but live as to God in the spirit]]. This verse is difficult. Many interpreters understand "the dead" to mean believers who heard the gospel while alive and have since died. Though they were "judged" by people, perhaps condemned or mocked, or though they experienced physical death, they now live with God. Others connect this verse with 1 Peter 3:19, seeing a proclamation to the dead. Most favor the first reading, which offers comfort: believers who died, even if mocked or persecuted, are alive to God.

### Love and Service in the Last Days (4:7–11)

[[v7|But the end of all things is near. Therefore be of sound mind, self-controlled, and sober in prayer]]. Since Christ's resurrection, the world has entered its final age. Believers should live with clear minds and self-control so they can pray well. Prayer requires alertness, not panic or distraction. The call to love, hospitality, and service is studied below.

### The Fiery Trial (4:12–19)

The call not to be surprised by suffering is studied below. [[v15|But let none of you suffer as a murderer, or a thief, or an evil doer, or a meddler in other men’s matters]]. Not all suffering is suffering for Christ. Those who suffer because of their own wrongdoing should not claim to be persecuted. Interestingly, Peter includes "meddler" in the list. Even believers can bring trouble on themselves by interfering in others' affairs.

[[v16|But if one of you suffers for being a Christian, let him not be ashamed; but let him glorify God in this matter]]. The name "Christian" was first used in Antioch (Acts 11:26) and was likely a label outsiders used, sometimes with contempt. Peter says believers should not be ashamed of it. Suffering for bearing Christ's name is an honor.

[[v17|For the time has come for judgment to begin with the household of God. If it begins first with us, what will happen to those who don’t obey the Good News of God?]] [[v18|“If it is hard for the righteous to be saved, what will happen to the ungodly and the sinner?”]] God's judgment, like a refining fire, begins with His own people, purifying them through trials (see Ezekiel 9:6; Malachi 3:1–3). If God's people go through refining trials, how much more serious will judgment be for those who reject the gospel? Peter quotes Proverbs 11:31 (in the Greek version). The salvation of the righteous comes through difficulty, not because God is weak, but because the path to glory passes through suffering, as it did for Christ. The chapter's closing call to trust God is studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 Peter 4:8 — Love Covers a Multitude of Sins

verse: 1 Peter 4:8

#### Simple Explanation

"And above all things be earnest in your love among yourselves." Of all the instructions Peter gives, love comes first. "Earnest" means intense, fervent, constant, stretched out, like an athlete straining toward the finish line. Believers are to love one another deeply and wholeheartedly.

"For love covers a multitude of sins." Peter draws on Proverbs 10:12: [[Proverbs 10:12|love covers all wrongs]]. Love covers sins by forgiving them, overlooking minor offenses, and refusing to keep a record of wrongs (1 Corinthians 13:5). Where there is love, people do not hold grudges or expose others' faults unnecessarily.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

In times of pressure and persecution, communities can become tense. People may become irritable, critical, or divided. Peter calls believers to deep love, which holds communities together.

"Love covers a multitude of sins" does not mean that love ignores serious sin or hides wrongdoing that harms others. It does not mean covering up abuse or crime. Scripture calls for confronting sin, protecting the vulnerable, and seeking justice (Matthew 18:15–17; Ephesians 5:11). Rather, it means that love is quick to forgive, slow to take offense, and willing to overlook the everyday faults and irritations that come with living in community. It means not broadcasting others' failures or holding them against them.

Love also covers sin in the sense that God's love, shown in Christ, covers believers' sins. Those who have received such love are called to extend it to others.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can make love their top priority in relationships, especially within the church. This might mean being patient with difficult people, forgiving offenses, and refusing to gossip about others' faults.

When conflicts arise, believers can ask whether the issue is serious enough to address or whether love can simply overlook it. Many minor offenses can be covered by love.

At the same time, believers should distinguish between covering minor faults and covering serious wrongdoing. Love protects the vulnerable, and that sometimes requires bringing harm into the light.

### 1 Peter 4:10–11 — Good Stewards of God's Grace

verse: 1 Peter 4:10-11

#### Simple Explanation

"As each has received a gift." Every believer has received at least one gift from God. No one is left out.

"Employ it in serving one another." Gifts are not for personal advantage or display but for serving others.

"As good managers of the grace of God in its various forms." A "manager" or steward was a household servant entrusted with the master's resources. Believers are stewards of God's grace, which comes in many different forms. Each gift is a piece of God's grace to be used for others.

Verse 11 gives two broad categories of gifts. "If anyone speaks, let it be as it were the very words of God." Those with speaking gifts, such as teaching, preaching, or encouraging, should speak faithfully, as those who are delivering God's message, not their own opinions.

"If anyone serves, let it be as of the strength which God supplies." Those with serving gifts, such as helping, giving, leading, or showing mercy, should serve in God's strength, not their own.

"That in all things God may be glorified through Jesus Christ." The ultimate purpose of every gift is God's glory.

"To whom belong the glory and the dominion forever and ever. Amen."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage teaches that every believer is gifted and responsible. Paul describes spiritual gifts in Romans 12:6–8 and 1 Corinthians 12, emphasizing that every member of the body of Christ has a role. Peter makes the same point more simply: whatever gift you have, use it to serve others.

The image of stewardship is important. Gifts belong to God, not to the believer. They are entrusted for a purpose. Jesus' parable of the talents warns against burying God's gifts out of fear or laziness (Matthew 25:14–30).

The emphasis on God's strength is freeing. Believers do not serve in their own power. When they feel inadequate, they can rely on the strength God supplies. And when they succeed, the glory goes to God, not to themselves.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can ask what gifts God has given them and how they are using them to serve others. Gifts might include teaching, encouraging, hospitality, administration, generosity, compassion, craftsmanship, music, prayer, or many others.

Those who speak, whether in teaching, preaching, or simply encouraging a friend, can seek to speak faithfully to God's word. Those who serve can rely on God's strength rather than their own.

Finally, believers can aim for God's glory in all their service, not seeking recognition for themselves but pointing others to Him.

### 1 Peter 4:12–14 — Do Not Be Surprised by the Fiery Trial

verse: 1 Peter 4:12-14

#### Simple Explanation

"Beloved, don’t be astonished at the fiery trial which has come upon you to test you." Peter addresses his readers tenderly as "beloved." He tells them not to be surprised by intense suffering, a "fiery trial." Like fire refining metal (1 Peter 1:7), this trial tests and purifies their faith.

"As though a strange thing happened to you." Suffering for Christ is not unusual or foreign. It is part of following Him.

Verse 13: "But because you are partakers of Christ’s sufferings, rejoice." Believers who suffer for Christ share in His sufferings. This is a reason for joy, not because suffering is pleasant, but because it unites them with Christ.

"That at the revelation of his glory you also may rejoice with exceeding joy." When Christ returns in glory, those who shared His sufferings will share His joy. Present suffering leads to future joy.

Verse 14: "If you are insulted for the name of Christ, you are blessed." Those who are insulted because they belong to Christ are blessed, echoing Jesus' words in Matthew 5:11–12.

"Because the Spirit of glory and of God rests on you." The Holy Spirit, who brings God's glory, rests on suffering believers. Just as God's glory rested on the tabernacle (Exodus 40:34–35), His Spirit rests on His suffering people.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Jesus warned His followers that they would face persecution (John 15:18–20). Paul wrote that all who want to live godly lives in Christ will be persecuted (2 Timothy 3:12). Suffering for Christ is not a sign of God's absence but of belonging to Him.

The idea of sharing in Christ's sufferings is found throughout the New Testament. Paul longed to know [[Philippians 3:10|the fellowship of his sufferings]]. This does not mean believers add to Christ's atoning work, which was complete. It means they share His experience of rejection by a hostile world and His path from suffering to glory.

The promise that "the Spirit of glory... rests on you" is deeply comforting. In the very moment of insult and suffering, God's presence and glory are with His people. Stephen, as he was being stoned, saw the glory of God and Jesus standing at His right hand (Acts 7:55–56).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers who face ridicule, rejection, or persecution because of their faith can remember that this is not strange. They are sharing in Christ's experience, and God's Spirit rests on them.

Believers can find joy even in difficult circumstances, not by denying the pain, but by remembering that they belong to Christ and that glory awaits.

This passage also calls believers to stand with persecuted Christians around the world, praying for them, supporting them, and speaking up for them.

### 1 Peter 4:19 — Entrust Your Souls to a Faithful Creator

verse: 1 Peter 4:19

#### Simple Explanation

Peter concludes the chapter with practical advice for those who suffer. "Therefore let them also who suffer according to the will of God." Their suffering is not outside God's control. God allows it for His purposes.

"In doing good." While suffering, believers should keep doing good. Suffering is not an excuse to stop living faithfully.

"Entrust their souls to him." "Entrust" means to commit something valuable into someone's safekeeping, like depositing treasure. Believers are to place their whole lives into God's hands.

"As to a faithful Creator." God is the Creator, who made all things and sustains them. And He is faithful, completely trustworthy. The one who made them will take care of them.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse echoes Christ's example. On the cross, Jesus prayed, [[Luke 23:46|Father, into your hands I commit my spirit]], quoting Psalm 31:5. Peter earlier described how Jesus [[1 Peter 2:23|committed himself to him who judges righteously]]. Believers follow His example by entrusting themselves to God.

"Faithful Creator" is a unique title in the New Testament. It combines God's power as Creator with His reliability. The God who created the universe has the power to care for His people, and He is faithful to do so. Nothing is outside His control, and He will never abandon those who trust Him.

The phrase "according to the will of God" does not mean God delights in suffering or that all suffering is His direct will. It means that even suffering is within His sovereign purposes, and He can bring good from it.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

When believers face suffering, they can pray as Jesus did, entrusting their lives into God's hands. This act of trust brings peace, even when circumstances do not change.

Believers can also continue doing good, even when life is hard. Faithfulness in suffering is a powerful testimony.

Finally, believers can remember that their Creator is faithful. The God who made them knows them, loves them, and will care for them. Their lives are safe in His hands.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

First Peter 4 calls believers to live for God's will rather than for human desires, arming themselves with the same mind Christ had when He suffered. When former friends find their new way of life strange and speak against them, believers can remember that those who slander them will give account to God, and that their own lives are hidden with Him.

Because the end of all things is near, believers are called to clear-minded prayer and, above all, to deep, earnest love for one another, love that covers a multitude of sins. They are to show hospitality without grumbling and to use every gift God has given them to serve others as good stewards of His varied grace, speaking His words faithfully and serving in His strength, so that God is glorified in everything.

Peter also prepares believers for suffering. They should not be surprised by fiery trials, as if something strange were happening. Suffering for Christ is a sharing in His sufferings, and it leads to joy when His glory is revealed. Those insulted for His name are blessed, because the Spirit of glory rests on them. They should not suffer for wrongdoing, but if they suffer as Christians, they need not be ashamed.

Finally, those who suffer according to God's will are to entrust their lives to their faithful Creator and keep doing good. Like Christ, who committed His spirit into the Father's hands, believers can rest their lives in the care of the God who made them and will never fail them.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, You suffered in the flesh for me. Help me to arm myself with the same mind, living not for my own desires but for the will of God. When others find my faith strange or speak against me, keep me steady. Because the end of all things is near, make me clear-minded and faithful in prayer. Above all, fill me with deep love for my brothers and sisters, love that covers a multitude of sins. Make me hospitable without grumbling. Show me the gifts You have given me, and help me to use them to serve others in Your strength, so that God may be glorified in everything. When fiery trials come, help me not to be surprised, but to rejoice that I share in Your sufferings. Strengthen all who are insulted and persecuted for Your name. Into the hands of my faithful Creator I entrust my life. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
