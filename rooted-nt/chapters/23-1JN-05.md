# 1 John 5
theme: Faith That Overcomes and the Assurance of Eternal Life
main: John ties together the great threads of his letter: faith, love, and obedience. Everyone who believes that Jesus is the Christ has been born of God, and everyone who loves the Father loves His children. Loving God means keeping His commandments, and His commandments are not burdensome, because everyone born of God overcomes the world through faith. Jesus came by water and blood, and the Spirit, the water, and the blood all testify to Him. God Himself has given testimony about His Son: He has given us eternal life, and this life is in His Son. Whoever has the Son has life. John wrote so that believers may know they have eternal life. He assures them that God hears prayers made according to His will, encourages them to pray for a brother or sister who sins, and closes with three great certainties: those born of God are kept from the evil one, believers belong to God, and the Son of God has come so that we may know the true God. His final word is a tender warning: keep yourselves from idols.

## Chapter Background and Story

The last chapter of 1 John gathers up everything the letter has said. Throughout, John has given believers tests by which they can know they truly belong to God: right belief about Jesus, obedience to God's commands, and love for one another. Here he shows that these three are not separate. Faith in Jesus brings new birth; new birth produces love for God's children; love for God shows itself in obedience. All of it is the work of God in those who trust His Son.

The false teachers who had left the community had unsettled the believers who remained. They may have claimed a superior knowledge of God and implied that ordinary believers lacked something essential. John writes to give the faithful firm ground under their feet. His purpose is stated plainly in verse 13: he wants them to know that they have eternal life.

The chapter also touches on two passages that have prompted much discussion over the centuries: the testimony of "water and blood" (vv6–8) and the "sin leading to death" (vv16–17). John's original readers likely understood these references quickly; modern readers do well to consider the main interpretations with humility.

### Faith, Love, and Obedience Together (5:1–5)

[[v1|Whoever believes that Jesus is the Christ has been born of God. Whoever loves the Father also loves the child who is born of him]]. Faith in Jesus as the Christ is the evidence of new birth. And new birth places a person in God's family. Just as a child who loves a parent will also care for the parent's other children, so the one who loves God the Father will love His other children.

[[v2|By this we know that we love the children of God, when we love God and keep his commandments]]. Earlier John said that love for one another proves love for God (4:20). Now he says that love for God and obedience to His commands prove genuine love for God's children. The two run in both directions. True love for people is not sentimental approval of whatever they do; it is shaped by God's own will. The victory of faith over the world is studied below.

### The Testimony About the Son (5:6–12)

[[v6|This is he who came by water and blood, Jesus Christ; not with the water only, but with the water and the blood. It is the Spirit who testifies, because the Spirit is the truth]]. This verse has been understood in several ways. The most widely held view is that "water" refers to Jesus' baptism, where His public ministry began and the Father declared Him to be His Son, and "blood" refers to His death on the cross. John insists that Jesus Christ came "not with the water only, but with the water and the blood." This may answer a false teaching, associated in early church history with a teacher named Cerinthus, that the divine Christ came upon the man Jesus at His baptism but left Him before His crucifixion, so that only a human being died. John rejects this firmly: the One who was baptized is the same One who died, Jesus Christ the Son of God.

Others connect the water and blood with the water and blood that flowed from Jesus' side when He was pierced on the cross (John 19:34), a sign that He truly died. Still others see a reference to baptism and the Lord's Supper in the church's life. These views are not entirely exclusive, but the first fits best with John's emphasis that Jesus Christ came in the flesh and truly died.

[[v7|For there are three who testify:]] [[v8|the Spirit, the water, and the blood; and the three agree as one]]. In Jewish law, a matter was established by two or three witnesses (Deuteronomy 19:15). The baptism, the cross, and the ongoing witness of the Holy Spirit together confirm who Jesus is.

Some older English translations, such as the King James Version, include additional words in verses 7–8 that speak of the Father, the Word, and the Holy Spirit bearing witness in heaven. These words are not found in the early Greek manuscripts and appear to have been added much later, which is why the World English Bible and most modern translations do not include them. The doctrine of the Trinity does not rest on this addition; it is taught throughout the New Testament (for example, Matthew 28:19; 2 Corinthians 13:14).

[[v9|If we receive the witness of men, the witness of God is greater; for this is God’s testimony which he has testified concerning his Son]]. People accept human testimony every day, in courts, in business, and in ordinary life. How much more should they accept the testimony of God Himself?

[[v10|He who believes in the Son of God has the testimony in himself. He who doesn’t believe God has made him a liar, because he has not believed in the testimony that God has given concerning his Son]]. The believer carries God's testimony within, through the inner witness of the Spirit. To reject that testimony is to treat God as untrustworthy. The content of God's testimony is studied below.

### Confidence in Prayer (5:13–17)

The assurance of eternal life and confidence in prayer are studied below. [[v16|If anyone sees his brother sinning a sin not leading to death, he shall ask, and God will give him life for those who sin not leading to death. There is sin leading to death. I don’t say that he should make a request concerning this]]. [[v17|All unrighteousness is sin, and there is sin not leading to death]].

These verses are among the most debated in John's letters. John encourages believers to pray for a fellow believer caught in sin, and he promises that God will give that person life. But he also mentions a "sin leading to death," and he does not command prayer about it.

Interpreters have understood this in several ways. Many see the "sin leading to death" in light of the letter's own context: the deliberate, settled rejection of Jesus Christ shown by the false teachers who denied that Jesus is the Christ come in the flesh and walked away from the fellowship (2:19, 22). Such persistent rejection of the only source of life leads to spiritual death. Others think John means sin that results in physical death as a divine judgment, as when some believers in Corinth became sick and died (1 Corinthians 11:30) or in the account of Ananias and Sapphira (Acts 5:1–11). Still others connect it with Jesus' warning about blasphemy against the Holy Spirit (Mark 3:28–29).

Several things are clear. John does not give believers a list for deciding who is beyond hope, and he does not forbid prayer for anyone; he simply says he is not commanding it in that case. His main point is positive: believers should pray for one another when they see sin. The context also reassures. A person who is troubled by their sin and wants to turn to God shows that they have not hardened their heart against Him. Every sin is serious, "all unrighteousness is sin," but not every sin places a person beyond the reach of God's mercy.

### Three Certainties and a Final Warning (5:18–21)

[[v18|We know that whoever is born of God doesn’t sin, but he who was born of God keeps himself, and the evil one doesn’t touch him]]. As in chapter 3, John means that the one born of God does not go on living in a settled pattern of sin. The phrase "he who was born of God keeps himself" is read in two ways. Many interpreters understand "he who was born of God" as a reference to Jesus, the eternal Son, who keeps the believer safe; a number of ancient manuscripts read "keeps him" rather than "keeps himself," which supports this reading. Others take it as the believer, who guards their own life. Either way, the evil one cannot lay hold of those who belong to God.

[[v19|We know that we are of God, and the whole world lies in the power of the evil one]]. John draws a clear line. Believers belong to God, while the world system that rejects God remains under the sway of the evil one. This is not a reason for pride or fear, but for clear thinking about where true life is found.

[[v20|We know that the Son of God has come and has given us an understanding, that we know him who is true; and we are in him who is true, in his Son Jesus Christ. This is the true God and eternal life]]. Against the false teachers who claimed special knowledge, John declares that true understanding comes from the Son of God. Believers know the true God and live in union with Him through Jesus Christ. The final sentence, "This is the true God and eternal life," is understood by many interpreters as a direct statement that Jesus Christ is the true God, matching the opening of John's Gospel (John 1:1). Others read it as referring to the Father. Both readings affirm that eternal life is found only in knowing the true God through His Son.

[[v21|Little children, keep yourselves from idols]]. The letter ends abruptly with a tender but serious warning. In a city like Ephesus, filled with temples, idols were everywhere. But John likely also means any false image of God, including the distorted Jesus of the false teachers. An idol is anything that takes the place of the true God. Having shown believers the true God in Jesus Christ, John urges them not to turn aside to any substitute.

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 John 5:3–5 — The Faith That Overcomes the World

verse: 1 John 5:3-5

#### Simple Explanation

John begins by defining love for God in practical terms: "For this is loving God, that we keep his commandments." Love for God is not only a feeling or a profession; it shows itself in a life that follows His ways. Jesus said the same thing: [[John 14:15|If you love me, keep my commandments]].

Then John adds an encouraging word: "His commandments are not grievous." God's commands are not crushing burdens. This does not mean obedience is always easy, but that God's commands are good, wise, and given in love. Jesus said, [[Matthew 11:30|For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light]].

Why are God's commands not burdensome? Verse 4 explains: "For whatever is born of God overcomes the world." The new birth gives believers a new nature and a new power. They are not trying to obey God in their own strength. God's life in them enables them to live for Him.

John then names the means of this victory: "This is the victory that has overcome the world: your faith." Faith in Jesus is the victory. It is not a vague optimism, but trust in a specific Person. Verse 5 makes this explicit: the one who overcomes the world is the one who "believes that Jesus is the Son of God."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

In John's writings, "the world" often means human society organized in rebellion against God, with its values, pressures, and lies (2:15–17). The world tempts believers to love what is passing away, to fear what others think, and to doubt God's truth. Overcoming the world means not being conquered by these pressures.

This victory is rooted in Christ's own victory. On the night before His death, Jesus told His disciples, [[John 16:33|In the world you have trouble; but cheer up! I have overcome the world]]. Believers overcome because they are joined by faith to the One who has already overcome. Notice that John says the victory "has overcome," in the past tense. The decisive battle has already been won at the cross and the empty tomb.

This does not mean believers never struggle or fail. It means the world no longer has final authority over them. Faith keeps returning them to Christ, and in Him they share His victory. Each time a believer chooses trust over fear, truth over deception, and obedience over compromise, that victory is displayed again.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers face many pressures that can feel overwhelming: cultural expectations, temptations, discouragement, and the constant noise of voices that push God aside. This passage reminds them that they do not face these pressures alone or without power. Through faith in Jesus, they already share in His victory.

When obedience to God feels heavy, believers can remember that His commands are not meant to crush them but to lead them into life. God does not ask His children to do what He will not also give them strength to do.

The key to overcoming is not trying harder in one's own strength, but trusting Jesus more deeply. Faith grows as believers spend time with God in His Word, in prayer, and in fellowship with other believers who remind them of who Jesus is.

### 1 John 5:11–13 — That You May Know You Have Eternal Life

verse: 1 John 5:11-13

#### Simple Explanation

After speaking of God's testimony about His Son, John tells us what that testimony says: "that God gave to us eternal life, and this life is in his Son." Eternal life is a gift from God. It is not earned. And it is found in only one place: in Jesus.

Verse 12 states the consequence simply: "He who has the Son has the life. He who doesn’t have God’s Son doesn’t have the life." Eternal life is not a separate thing that can be possessed apart from Jesus. To have Jesus, through faith, is to have life. To be without Him is to be without life.

Verse 13 gives John's purpose in writing the whole letter: "These things I have written to you who believe in the name of the Son of God, that you may know that you have eternal life." John wants believers to have assurance. He does not want them to live in constant uncertainty about their standing with God.

John's Gospel was written so that people might believe and have life (John 20:31). His letter was written so that those who already believe might know they have that life.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

In Scripture, eternal life is not only life that lasts forever. It is a quality of life: the life of God Himself, shared with His people. Jesus defined it in His prayer: [[John 17:3|This is eternal life, that they should know you, the only true God, and him whom you sent, Jesus Christ]]. Eternal life begins now, in a living relationship with God, and continues forever.

Assurance is grounded first in God's own testimony. Believers do not rest their confidence on their feelings, which change, or on their performance, which is imperfect. They rest it on what God has said about His Son. If God has promised life to all who have the Son, then those who trust the Son can know they have life.

The tests John has given throughout the letter, believing the truth about Jesus, obeying God's commands, and loving fellow believers, are not hurdles to earn life. They are signs that confirm life is present. A person who trusts Jesus, wants to obey Him, and loves His people can take comfort that God's life is at work in them.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many sincere believers struggle with doubts about whether they are truly saved. This passage offers a solid foundation. Assurance does not depend on having a perfect life or never feeling doubt. It depends on whether a person has the Son, trusting in Jesus Christ as Savior and Lord.

When doubts arise, believers can return to God's promise rather than looking only inward. They can remind themselves that God has promised life to whoever has the Son, that they have put their trust in His Son, and that they can therefore rest in His word.

At the same time, this passage gives a loving and sober invitation to anyone who does not yet have the Son. Life is offered freely in Jesus, and no one is beyond the reach of this gift.

### 1 John 5:14–15 — Confidence When We Pray

verse: 1 John 5:14-15

#### Simple Explanation

John moves from assurance of eternal life to confidence in prayer: "This is the boldness which we have toward him, that if we ask anything according to his will, he listens to us." The word "boldness" means freedom and confidence in speaking. Believers can come to God openly, without fear, as children come to a loving Father.

The promise has an important qualification: "according to his will." Prayer is not a way of getting God to do whatever we want. It is bringing our requests into line with what God desires. When we ask according to His will, He hears us.

Verse 15 adds: "And if we know that he listens to us, whatever we ask, we know that we have the petitions which we have asked of him." To be heard by God, in the sense John means, is to have the request granted. If God hears a prayer that agrees with His will, the answer is certain, even if it does not come immediately or in the form we expected.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This promise is not a blank check. It is an invitation into partnership with God. Jesus taught His disciples to pray, [[Matthew 6:10|Let your Kingdom come. Let your will be done on earth as it is in heaven]]. He Himself prayed in Gethsemane, [[Mark 14:36|However, not what I desire, but what you desire]]. True prayer seeks God's will above our own.

How do believers know God's will? Much of it is revealed in Scripture: God wills that believers grow in holiness (1 Thessalonians 4:3), that they be filled with the Spirit, that the gospel spread, that His people love one another. When believers pray for these things, they can pray with full confidence. In matters Scripture does not directly address, believers can bring their requests honestly and trust God to answer in the way that is truly best.

The Holy Spirit also helps believers in prayer. Paul writes that the Spirit intercedes for believers according to the will of God (Romans 8:26–27). Prayer is not a lonely effort to guess what God wants; it is a relationship in which God Himself helps His children to pray.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many people pray hesitantly, unsure whether God is listening. This passage assures believers that God does hear them. They can approach Him with boldness, not because they deserve to be heard, but because they are His children through Jesus.

Praying according to God's will can shape what believers pray for. As they grow in knowing Scripture, their prayers increasingly reflect God's priorities: growth in Christlikeness, the good of others, the spread of the gospel, and the coming of His kingdom.

When an answer seems delayed or different from what was hoped, believers can trust that God has heard and that His will is good. Unanswered prayer, from our perspective, is often an answer we do not yet understand.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

First John 5 brings the whole letter to a confident and hopeful close. It shows that faith, love, and obedience belong together. Those who believe that Jesus is the Christ have been born of God; those born of God love God's children; and love for God shows itself in keeping His commandments. These commands are not burdensome, because God's own life in His children gives them strength to obey.

The chapter calls believers to live as overcomers. The world presses hard with its values, temptations, and fears, but faith in Jesus is the victory that overcomes it. Believers are not left to fight in their own strength. They share in the victory Christ has already won.

John also wants believers to rest in assurance. God has testified about His Son, and His testimony is that eternal life is in Jesus. Whoever has the Son has life. Believers do not need to live in anxious uncertainty. They can know that they have eternal life, because God's word is true.

This assurance leads naturally to bold, trusting prayer. Believers can bring their requests to God, knowing that He listens when they pray according to His will. They can also pray for fellow believers who stumble, asking God to restore them, rather than standing at a distance in judgment.

Finally, John reminds believers to guard their hearts. Having come to know the true God through His Son, they are to keep themselves from every idol, anything that would take God's place in their hearts or distort the truth about who He is.

## Chapter Prayer

Father, thank You that everyone who believes that Jesus is the Christ has been born of You, and that You have made me part of Your family. Teach me to love You by keeping Your commandments, and to love Your children with a sincere heart. Thank You that Your commands are not a burden, and that faith in Jesus is the victory that overcomes the world. Thank You for Your testimony about Your Son, and for the gift of eternal life that is found in Him. Help me to rest in the assurance that I have life because I have the Son. Give me boldness in prayer, and shape my desires so that I ask according to Your will. Teach me to pray for my brothers and sisters when they stumble. Keep me safe from the evil one, and guard my heart from every idol, so that I may know You, the true God, and live for You alone. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
