# John 14
theme: Comfort for Troubled Hearts: The Way to the Father and the Promised Counselor
main: On the night of His betrayal, Jesus comforts His anxious disciples. He promises that He is going to prepare a place for them in His Father's house and will come again to take them to Himself. He declares that He is the way, the truth, and the life, that whoever has seen Him has seen the Father, and that He will send another Counselor, the Spirit of truth, to be with them forever. He leaves them His own peace, which is unlike anything the world can give.

## Chapter Background and Story

John 14 continues Jesus' farewell conversation in the upper room. The disciples had just heard devastating news. Jesus had said that one of them would betray Him, that He was going where they could not follow, and that Peter, their boldest member, would deny Him three times before morning (13:21–38). Their hearts were troubled. Everything they had built their lives around for three years seemed to be falling apart.

Jesus' response is not to rebuke them but to comfort them. Much of chapter 14 is a series of promises: a place prepared, a way to the Father, answered prayer, the gift of the Spirit, the presence of the Father and the Son, and lasting peace. Along the way, three disciples, Thomas, Philip, and Judas (not Iscariot), ask questions that reveal their confusion, and each question leads to a deeper revelation.

### A Place Prepared (14:1–4)

Jesus began, [[v1|Don’t let your heart be troubled. Believe in God. Believe also in me]]. He then spoke of His Father's house and of His return, studied below. He added, [[v4|You know where I go, and you know the way]].

### The Way, the Truth, and the Life (14:5–7)

Thomas, honest as always, said, [[v5|Lord, we don’t know where you are going. How can we know the way?]] Thomas thought in terms of places and roads. Jesus answered with one of His greatest statements, studied below. He added, [[v7|If you had known me, you would have known my Father also. From now on, you know him and have seen him]].

### Seeing the Father (14:8–14)

Philip said, [[v8|Lord, show us the Father, and that will be enough for us]]. Perhaps he was thinking of Moses, who asked to see God's glory (Exodus 33:18). Philip wanted a visible manifestation of God.

Jesus' answer combines gentle disappointment with profound revelation: [[v9|Have I been with you such a long time, and do you not know me, Philip? He who has seen me has seen the Father]]. Philip had been looking at the Father's revelation for three years without fully realizing it. Jesus is the perfect image of the Father. His words and works are the Father's words and works: [[v10|the Father who lives in me does his works]]. If Philip found it hard to believe Jesus' words, he could believe because of the works themselves.

Then Jesus made a remarkable promise: [[v12|he who believes in me, the works that I do, he will do also; and he will do greater works than these, because I am going to my Father]]. "Greater works" does not mean more spectacular miracles than raising the dead. It refers to the wider scope of the work the disciples would do after Jesus' return to the Father and the coming of the Spirit. Jesus ministered in a small region for about three years; through His followers, the gospel would spread to the ends of the earth, and thousands would come to faith in a single day (Acts 2:41).

He added, [[v13|Whatever you will ask in my name, I will do it, that the Father may be glorified in the Son]]. To ask "in Jesus' name" is not to use a magic formula but to pray as His representatives, according to His character and will, for the glory of the Father. John later explains, [[1 John 5:14|if we ask anything according to his will, he listens to us]].

### Another Counselor (14:15–24)

[[v15|If you love me, keep my commandments]]. Love for Jesus shows itself in obedience, not as a way to earn His love but as a response to it. Jesus then promised the Holy Spirit, studied below.

He continued, [[v18|I will not leave you orphans. I will come to you]]. The disciples would not be abandoned like children without parents. [[v19|Because I live, you will live also]]. His resurrection would guarantee their life.

Judas, a different disciple from Judas Iscariot (probably the same person called Thaddaeus in Matthew and Mark), asked why Jesus would reveal Himself to them and not to the world. Jesus answered that He reveals Himself to those who love Him and keep His word: [[v23|My Father will love him, and we will come to him and make our home with him]]. The word for "home" is related to the "homes" in the Father's house in verse 2. Jesus is preparing a place for His people in the Father's house, and meanwhile the Father and the Son make their home in His people.

### The Spirit's Teaching and the Gift of Peace (14:25–31)

Jesus said that the Counselor, the Holy Spirit, [[v26|will teach you all things, and will remind you of all that I said to you]]. This promise applied especially to the apostles, who would preserve and explain Jesus' teaching. It helps explain how John could write his Gospel decades later, with the Spirit bringing Jesus' words to his memory and deepening his understanding. Then Jesus gave His peace, studied below.

Jesus said that if they loved Him, they would rejoice that He was going to the Father, [[v28|for the Father is greater than I]]. This does not mean that Jesus is less divine than the Father, since the same Gospel says the Word was God (1:1) and Jesus said, "I and the Father are one" (10:30). Christians have understood this statement as referring to Jesus' role as the Son who was sent, who took on human nature and humbled Himself, and who was now returning to the glory He had with the Father before the world began (17:5).

Jesus said that the ruler of this world was coming, but [[v30|he has nothing in me]]. Satan had no claim on Jesus, because Jesus was without sin. Jesus would go to the cross not because Satan had power over Him but [[v31|that the world may know that I love the Father]], in obedience to the Father's command. Then He said, [[v31|Arise, let’s go from here]]. The conversation may have continued as they walked toward the Mount of Olives.

## Important Verses Explained

### John 14:1–3 — I Will Come Again

verse: John 14:1-3

#### Simple Explanation

The disciples were shaken. Jesus had just told them that He was leaving, that one of them would betray Him, and that Peter would deny Him. Their world was collapsing. Into this moment, Jesus spoke words of comfort: [[v1|Don’t let your heart be troubled]].

The word "troubled" means stirred up, agitated, disturbed. Jesus Himself had been troubled at the tomb of Lazarus (11:33), at the thought of the cross (12:27), and at the prediction of betrayal (13:21). He knew what it felt like. He did not tell the disciples that their situation was easy, but He showed them how to face it.

[[v1|Believe in God. Believe also in me]]. The remedy for a troubled heart is trust. The disciples already believed in God; now Jesus called them to trust Him in the same way. He placed Himself alongside God as the object of faith.

[[v2|In my Father’s house are many homes]]. The image of a father's house suggests family, belonging, and permanence. "Many homes" means there is plenty of room. Older translations used the word "mansions," which originally meant "dwelling places." The emphasis is not on luxury but on belonging. [[v2|If it weren’t so, I would have told you]]. Jesus assures them that He has been honest with them.

[[v2|I am going to prepare a place for you]]. Jesus' departure was not abandonment but preparation. Through His death, resurrection, and ascension, He would open the way into the Father's presence.

[[v3|If I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again and will receive you to myself; that where I am, you may be there also]]. This is a promise of Jesus' return. The greatest joy of heaven is not the place itself but being with Jesus.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses give believers a clear hope for the future. Jesus will come again. Christians have often understood this primarily as His second coming at the end of the age, when He will gather His people (1 Thessalonians 4:16–17). Some also see a secondary application to the moment of a believer's death, when they go to be with Christ (Philippians 1:23). In either case, the promise is that believers will be with Him forever.

The image of a wedding may also lie behind these words. In Jewish custom, after a betrothal, the bridegroom went to prepare a place for his bride, often an addition to his father's house. When it was ready, he came to bring her home. The church is called the bride of Christ (Ephesians 5:25–27; Revelation 19:7).

Finally, these verses show that Jesus' focus is relational. [[v3|I will come again and will receive you to myself; that where I am, you may be there also]]. Heaven is above all the presence of Jesus. This is the deepest longing of the believer and the deepest desire of Christ for His people (John 17:24).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These words bring comfort to troubled hearts. Believers face many situations that stir up fear: illness, loss, uncertainty about the future, and the approach of death. Jesus' remedy is trust in God and in Him. Believers can bring their troubled hearts to Christ and rest in His promises.

They also bring hope in the face of death. Christian funerals often include these verses because they remind mourners that believers who die go to the Father's house, where Jesus has prepared a place for them. Death is not the end but a homecoming.

Finally, these verses help believers keep an eternal perspective. Life in this world is not the whole story. Jesus is preparing a place for His people and will come again. This hope gives strength to endure hardship and to live faithfully while waiting for His return.

### John 14:6 — The Way, the Truth, and the Life

verse: John 14:6

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus had told His disciples that they knew the way to where He was going. Thomas, honest about his confusion, said, [[v5|Lord, we don’t know where you are going. How can we know the way?]] Thomas was thinking of a physical destination and a road to get there.

Jesus answered, [[v6|I am the way, the truth, and the life]]. He did not give directions; He pointed to Himself. Jesus does not merely show the way; He is the way. He does not merely teach the truth; He is the truth. He does not merely give life; He is the life.

"The way": Jesus is the path by which people come to God. Through His life, death, and resurrection, He has opened the way into the Father's presence.

"The truth": Jesus is the full and reliable revelation of God. In a world of confusion and lies, He is the reality of who God is and what God has done.

"The life": Jesus is the source of eternal life. As He said to Martha, He is the resurrection and the life (11:25).

[[v6|No one comes to the Father, except through me]]. This is a clear and exclusive claim. Jesus is not one way among many. He is the only way to the Father. This is not because God is narrow or unkind, but because only Jesus has dealt with sin and opened the way to God.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This is the sixth of Jesus' "I am" sayings in John. It brings together much of the Gospel's message. John began by describing Jesus as the Word who was with God, full of grace and truth (1:14), and in whom was life (1:4). Now Jesus summarizes His identity in relation to the Father: He is the way to the Father, the truth about the Father, and the life of the Father.

The claim of exclusivity is often difficult for modern readers. But it flows from who Jesus is. If He is truly the Son of God who died for sins and rose again, then He alone can reconcile people to God. The apostles proclaimed the same truth: [[Acts 4:12|There is salvation in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven that is given among men, by which we must be saved!]]

The verse is also deeply gracious. The way to God is not a long list of achievements or a secret path known only to a few. It is a Person who welcomes all who come. The exclusive claim is joined with a universal invitation: whoever comes to Him will not be cast out (6:37).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse gives believers confidence. In a world of many religions and philosophies, Christians can trust that Jesus is the way to the Father. They do not need to search endlessly for truth; they can know the One who is the truth.

It also shapes how believers speak about their faith. Jesus' claim is exclusive, but it should be shared with humility and love, never with arrogance. Believers do not claim to be better than others; they point to Jesus, who alone can bring anyone to God. Honest, gentle conversation and respect for people of other beliefs are fully consistent with confidence in Christ.

Finally, the verse invites believers to keep following Jesus as the way. Every day, believers face choices about how to live. Jesus is not only the way into God's family but also the way of life within it. Following Him means trusting His truth and living by His life.

### John 14:16–17 — Another Counselor, the Spirit of Truth

verse: John 14:16-17

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus was preparing His disciples for His departure. They had depended on His presence for three years. How would they continue without Him? Jesus answered with a promise: [[v16|I will pray to the Father, and he will give you another Counselor, that he may be with you forever]].

The word "Counselor" translates the Greek word paraclete, which means "one called alongside." It can refer to a legal advocate, a helper, a comforter, or an encourager. Different translations use different words, such as Helper, Comforter, or Advocate, because no single English word captures its full meaning.

The word "another" is important. Greek has two words for "another": one meaning "another of a different kind," and one meaning "another of the same kind." Jesus uses the second. The Holy Spirit would be a Counselor just like Jesus, continuing His presence and ministry with the disciples.

[[v16|that he may be with you forever]]. Jesus would be with them only a short time longer, but the Spirit would remain with them forever.

[[v17|the Spirit of truth, whom the world can’t receive, for it doesn’t see him and doesn’t know him]]. The Spirit reveals truth, especially the truth about Jesus. The world, meaning humanity in rebellion against God, cannot receive Him because it does not recognize Him.

[[v17|You know him, for he lives with you and will be in you]]. The Spirit was already with the disciples through Jesus' presence. After Jesus' glorification, the Spirit would dwell within them.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal the personal nature of the Holy Spirit. Jesus speaks of Him not as an impersonal force but as a Person, one who teaches, reminds, testifies, guides, and convicts (14:26; 15:26; 16:8, 13). He is the third Person of the Trinity, sent by the Father at the request of the Son.

They also show the intimacy of God's presence with believers. Under the old covenant, God's Spirit came upon certain people for particular tasks. Under the new covenant, the Spirit dwells within every believer. Paul writes that believers' bodies are [[1 Corinthians 6:19|a temple of the Holy Spirit]].

Finally, the promise shows that Jesus' departure was not a loss but a gain. Later He says, [[John 16:7|It is to your advantage that I go away]]. While on earth, Jesus was present in one place at a time. Through the Spirit, He is present with all His people everywhere.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses assure believers that they are never alone. Many people feel isolated, especially in times of grief, fear, or transition. Jesus promised that the Spirit would be with His people forever. Believers can rely on the Spirit's presence in every circumstance.

They also encourage believers to depend on the Spirit's help. The Christian life is not meant to be lived in human strength. The Spirit teaches, guides, comforts, and empowers. Believers can ask for His help in understanding Scripture, resisting temptation, making decisions, and loving others.

Finally, these verses call believers to listen to the Spirit of truth. In a world full of conflicting voices, the Spirit guides believers into the truth about Jesus. He works especially through Scripture, which He inspired, helping believers understand and apply God's Word.

### John 14:27 — My Peace I Give to You

verse: John 14:27

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus was near the end of His conversation with the disciples in the upper room. He had promised them a place in His Father's house, the presence of the Holy Spirit, and the indwelling of the Father and the Son. Now He gave them a parting gift: [[v27|Peace I leave with you. My peace I give to you]].

"Peace" (in Hebrew, shalom) was a common greeting and farewell. But Jesus meant much more than a polite goodbye. In Scripture, shalom means wholeness, well-being, and right relationship with God and others. Jesus was leaving them something real.

"My peace" is the peace Jesus Himself had. On the night of His betrayal, facing the cross, He was at peace because He trusted the Father completely. He was offering His disciples a share in that same peace.

[[v27|not as the world gives, I give to you]]. The world offers a kind of peace that depends on circumstances: the absence of conflict, financial security, good health, or comfort. That peace disappears when circumstances change. Jesus' peace does not depend on circumstances. It comes from His presence and His promises.

[[v27|Don’t let your heart be troubled, neither let it be fearful]]. Jesus repeated the words He spoke at the beginning of the chapter (14:1). Because of His peace, His disciples did not need to be overwhelmed by fear.

The disciples were about to face the most frightening hours of their lives. Jesus' peace was not a promise that nothing bad would happen, but a promise that they would not face it alone.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The peace Jesus gives is rooted in reconciliation with God. Paul writes, [[Romans 5:1|Being therefore justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ]]. Because Jesus has dealt with sin on the cross, those who trust Him are no longer enemies of God. This peace with God becomes the foundation for the peace of God in the heart.

It is also connected with the Holy Spirit, whom Jesus has just promised. Peace is part of the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22). The Spirit's presence in believers produces a peace that surpasses understanding (Philippians 4:7).

Finally, this peace reflects the character of Jesus Himself. He faced betrayal, suffering, and death without losing His trust in the Father. His peace was not denial of reality but confidence in God within reality. That is the peace He gives His people.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse offers hope to people who struggle with anxiety and fear. Jesus' peace is available even when circumstances are difficult. Believers can bring their worries to Him in prayer and receive His peace. This does not mean that feelings of fear disappear instantly, or that anxiety is always a sign of weak faith; anxiety can have many causes, and seeking help from doctors or counselors is wise and compatible with faith. But Jesus offers a deep anchor of peace that the storms of life cannot remove.

It also helps believers recognize false sources of peace. The world promises peace through wealth, entertainment, control, or escape. These things cannot give lasting peace. True peace comes from Christ.

Finally, the verse encourages believers to share Christ's peace with others. Those who have received His peace can bring calm, comfort, and reconciliation into a troubled world, becoming peacemakers who reflect the heart of their Lord (Matthew 5:9).

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

John 14 speaks directly to troubled hearts. Jesus did not ignore His disciples' fear or tell them to pretend everything was fine. He comforted them with promises. Believers today face many sources of fear and grief. This chapter invites them to bring their troubled hearts to Jesus and to trust Him as they trust God.

The chapter also gives believers a firm hope for the future. Jesus is preparing a place for His people in His Father's house, and He will come again to bring them to Himself. This hope changes how believers face death and loss. Those who die in Christ are going home, and those who remain can grieve with hope.

John 14 points to Jesus as the way, the truth, and the life. In a world of many voices and options, believers can rest in the confidence that Jesus is the way to the Father. This truth should be held with humility and shared with love, offering others the same welcome that Jesus offers.

Finally, the chapter promises the presence of the Holy Spirit and the gift of Christ's peace. Believers are not orphans. The Spirit lives in them, teaches them, and reminds them of Jesus' words. The Father and the Son make their home with those who love Jesus. And His peace, unlike the world's peace, remains even in the storm. These promises allow believers to face each day with courage and calm.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, when my heart is troubled, help me to trust in God and to trust in You. Thank You for preparing a place for me in Your Father's house, and for Your promise that You will come again and take me to be with You. You are the way, the truth, and the life. Thank You for opening the way to the Father and for showing me what He is like. Thank You for sending the Holy Spirit to be with me forever, so that I am never alone. Teach me by Your Spirit, remind me of Your words, and help me to love You by keeping Your commandments. Make Your home in my heart. Give me Your peace, the peace the world cannot give or take away, and calm my fears. Help me to share that peace with others who are troubled. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
