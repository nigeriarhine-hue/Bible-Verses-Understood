# Acts 24
theme: Righteousness, Self-Control, and the Judgment to Come: Paul before Felix
main: The high priest and a hired orator named Tertullus accuse Paul before the governor Felix of being a troublemaker, a ringleader of the Nazarene sect, and a temple defiler. Paul calmly answers that the charges cannot be proven and confesses that he worships the God of his ancestors according to the Way, believing the Law and the Prophets and hoping for the resurrection of the righteous and the unrighteous. Felix postpones the case. Later, when Paul speaks to Felix and his wife Drusilla about faith in Christ, righteousness, self-control, and coming judgment, Felix becomes afraid and sends him away, hoping for a bribe, and leaves Paul in prison for two years.

## Chapter Background and Story

Acts 24 records Paul's trial before Antonius Felix, the Roman governor of Judea from about A.D. 52 to 59. Felix had been born a slave and freed, and he rose to power through the influence of his brother Pallas, a favorite of Emperor Claudius. The Roman historian Tacitus described him as cruel and lustful, ruling with the power of a king and the mind of a slave. His wife Drusilla, mentioned in this chapter, was the youngest daughter of Herod Agrippa I (the king who killed James in Acts 12). Felix had persuaded her to leave her first husband to marry him. She was probably only about twenty at the time.

The chapter shows Paul's integrity and courage before a corrupt ruler. Paul answered the charges truthfully and calmly, and when given the opportunity to speak privately, he did not flatter Felix but spoke about righteousness, self-control, and judgment.

### The Accusation (24:1–9)

Five days after Paul arrived in Caesarea, the high priest Ananias came with some elders and a lawyer named Tertullus, a professional speaker familiar with Roman legal procedures. Tertullus began with flattery: [[v2|Seeing that by you we enjoy much peace, and that prosperity is coming to this nation by your foresight]]. This was a common courtroom technique, but it was far from the truth. Felix's rule was marked by violent uprisings and brutal repression.

Then Tertullus made three charges. First, [[v5|we have found this man to be a plague, an instigator of insurrections among all the Jews throughout the world]]. This was a political charge: Paul was a troublemaker who stirred up riots, a serious accusation in the eyes of Rome, which valued order above all. Second, he called Paul [[v5|a ringleader of the sect of the Nazarenes]]. "Nazarenes" was a name for followers of Jesus of Nazareth. Third, [[v6|He even tried to profane the temple, and we arrested him]]. (Verse 7, which adds details about the commander Lysias taking Paul by force, appears in some later manuscripts but not the earliest.) The Jewish leaders joined in, affirming the charges.

### Paul's Defense (24:10–21)

When Felix nodded for him to speak, Paul began respectfully but without flattery: [[v10|Because I know that you have been a judge of this nation for many years, I cheerfully make my defense]]. Paul simply acknowledged Felix's experience.

Paul answered each charge. First, regarding stirring up trouble: it had been only twelve days since he went to Jerusalem to worship. [[v12|In the temple they didn’t find me disputing with anyone or stirring up a crowd, either in the synagogues or in the city]]. [[v13|Nor can they prove to you the things of which they now accuse me]]. Paul's confession about the Way is studied below.

Regarding the temple: Paul had come to Jerusalem [[v17|to bring gifts for the needy to my nation, and offerings]], referring to the collection from the Gentile churches. When the Jews from Asia found him in the temple, he was ritually purified, [[v18|not with a mob, nor with turmoil]]. Paul pointed out that the Jews from Asia, his original accusers, were not even present. Under Roman law, accusers had to appear in person; their absence was a serious weakness in the case.

Finally, Paul challenged his accusers to say what crime the council had found when he stood before them, unless it was his statement about the resurrection of the dead.

### Felix's Response (24:22–27)

[[v22|But Felix, having more exact knowledge concerning the Way, deferred them, saying, “When Lysias, the commanding officer, comes down, I will decide your case.”]] Felix knew something about the Christian movement, perhaps through his Jewish wife or through his years in Judea. He postponed the decision, which effectively left Paul in prison. He ordered that Paul be kept under guard but given some freedom, and that his friends be allowed to visit and care for him. Luke and others probably visited Paul during this time.

Some days later, Felix and Drusilla summoned Paul to hear him speak about faith in Christ Jesus. What followed is studied below. Felix's motive for keeping Paul is also studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### Acts 24:14–15 — Serving the God of Our Fathers According to the Way

verse: Acts 24:14-15

#### Simple Explanation

Paul stood before Felix, the Roman governor, answering charges brought by the high priest and his lawyer. They had called Paul a troublemaker, a ringleader of the Nazarene sect, and a desecrator of the temple. Paul had denied causing any disturbance and pointed out that his accusers could not prove their charges. Then he made a positive statement about his faith.

[[v14|But this I confess to you, that according to the Way, which they call a sect, so I serve the God of our fathers]]. Paul freely admitted that he followed "the Way," the name early Christians used for their faith. His accusers called it a "sect," suggesting it was a dangerous splinter group. But Paul insisted that he worshiped the same God as his ancestors: the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Following Jesus was not abandoning Israel's faith but fulfilling it.

[[v14|believing all things which are according to the law, and which are written in the prophets]]. Paul believed everything written in the Hebrew Scriptures. He was not rejecting the Law or the Prophets. He believed that they pointed to Jesus.

[[v15|having hope toward God, which these also themselves look for, that there will be a resurrection of the dead, both of the just and unjust]]. Paul shared the hope of the Pharisees and many Jews: that God would raise the dead. This resurrection would include both the righteous and the unrighteous. Jesus taught the same (John 5:28–29), as did the prophet Daniel (Daniel 12:2).

Paul's point was that his faith was not a strange new religion but the fulfillment of Israel's Scriptures and hope. This also mattered legally: Judaism was a recognized religion in the Roman Empire, so if Paul's faith was part of the Jewish tradition, it was not illegal.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that Christianity is rooted in the Old Testament. The God of Israel is the God who sent Jesus. The promises of the Law and the Prophets are fulfilled in Him. Jesus Himself said, [[Matthew 5:17|Don’t think that I came to destroy the law or the prophets. I didn’t come to destroy, but to fulfill]].

They also show the centrality of the resurrection hope. Paul's whole message rested on the resurrection: Jesus' resurrection, and the future resurrection of all the dead. Paul wrote that if there is no resurrection, faith is useless (1 Corinthians 15:13–19). The resurrection gives believers hope beyond death.

Finally, the mention of the resurrection of "both of the just and unjust" reminds readers that all people will face God. The resurrection is a hope for the righteous and a warning for the unrighteous. This gives urgency to the call to repent and believe.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to read the whole Bible, including the Old Testament, as God's word that points to Christ. The Law and the Prophets are not outdated; they reveal God's character and prepare the way for Jesus.

They also give believers confidence in their hope. The resurrection of the dead is not wishful thinking; it is a promise rooted in God's word and confirmed by Jesus' resurrection. Believers can face death and loss with hope.

Finally, these verses remind believers that everyone will one day stand before God. This truth calls believers to live faithfully and to share the gospel with love and urgency.

### Acts 24:25 — Righteousness, Self-Control, and the Judgment to Come

verse: Acts 24:25

#### Simple Explanation

Some days after Paul's trial, Felix and his wife Drusilla sent for Paul to hear him speak about faith in Christ Jesus. Felix may have been curious about the movement he had heard about, or Drusilla, who was Jewish, may have been interested. This was a remarkable opportunity: a private audience with the most powerful man in the province.

What did Paul talk about? [[v25|As he reasoned about righteousness, self-control, and the judgment to come]]. Paul spoke about three topics, each of which applied directly to Felix and Drusilla.

"Righteousness": Felix was known for injustice and cruelty. Paul spoke about God's righteousness and the righteous life God requires, and the righteousness that God gives through faith in Christ.

"Self-control": Felix and Drusilla had married after Felix persuaded her to leave her husband. Felix was known for his lust and indulgence. Paul spoke about mastering one's desires.

"The judgment to come": Felix was a judge, but he too would one day stand before God's judgment. No earthly position would protect him.

Paul did not flatter Felix or avoid uncomfortable topics. He spoke the truth with courage.

[[v25|Felix was terrified]]. Felix felt the weight of Paul's words. His conscience was stirred. He realized that he was guilty before God.

[[v25|and answered, “Go your way for this time, and when it is convenient for me, I will summon you.”]] Instead of responding with repentance, Felix sent Paul away. He postponed the decision to a "convenient" time. As far as we know, that convenient time never came.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that the gospel addresses the whole person. Faith in Christ is not only about belief but also about righteousness, self-control, and accountability to God. The gospel calls people to turn from sin and to live under God's rule.

It also shows the work of the Holy Spirit in convicting people. Jesus said that the Spirit would convict the world [[John 16:8|about sin, about righteousness, and about judgment]]. Paul's words, empowered by the Spirit, brought Felix to fear.

Finally, the verse shows the danger of delay. Felix was terrified but postponed his response. Many people feel the conviction of the Spirit but put off responding, waiting for a more convenient time. Scripture warns, [[2 Corinthians 6:2|Behold, now is the acceptable time. Behold, now is the day of salvation]].

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to speak the truth with courage, even to powerful people. Paul did not water down his message to please Felix. Believers can share the gospel faithfully, including its call to righteousness and its warning of judgment, with love and respect.

It also challenges anyone who feels conviction but delays responding. Many people sense that they need to turn to God but put it off. Felix's story warns that delay can become permanent. When God speaks, the time to respond is now.

Finally, the verse invites believers to examine their own lives in light of righteousness, self-control, and judgment. These are not only for unbelievers to hear. Believers too are called to live righteously, practice self-control, and remember that they will give an account to God.

### Acts 24:26–27 — Hoping for a Bribe

verse: Acts 24:26-27

#### Simple Explanation

Felix had been frightened by Paul's words about righteousness, self-control, and judgment. Yet he did not repent. Instead, Luke reveals Felix's true motive for keeping Paul around.

[[v26|Meanwhile, he also hoped that money would be given to him by Paul, that he might release him]]. Felix wanted a bribe. Paul had mentioned bringing a large gift to Jerusalem (24:17), and Felix may have thought Paul or his friends had access to money. Roman law forbade accepting bribes to release prisoners, but corrupt officials often did so.

[[v26|Therefore also he sent for him more often and talked with him]]. Felix sent for Paul frequently. Perhaps he hoped Paul would offer a bribe. Paul apparently never did. He refused to buy his freedom through corruption.

These conversations also show God's patience. Felix heard the gospel again and again. He had many opportunities to respond. Yet he continued to resist.

[[v27|But when two years were fulfilled, Felix was succeeded by Porcius Festus, and desiring to gain favor with the Jews, Felix left Paul in bonds]]. Paul remained in prison for two years. Felix was recalled to Rome, probably because of his harsh handling of a conflict between Jews and Gentiles in Caesarea. Before leaving, Felix wanted to gain favor with the Jewish leaders, so he left Paul in prison instead of releasing him.

Two years is a long time. Paul could not travel, preach in new cities, or visit the churches. Yet his time was not wasted. He may have written letters, received visitors, and prayed. Some scholars think Luke may have gathered material for his Gospel during this time in Judea.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show the corruption of the human heart. Felix heard the gospel many times, yet his love for money and his desire for political advantage kept him from responding. Jesus warned, [[Luke 16:13|You aren’t able to serve God and Mammon]]. Felix chose money and power over God.

They also show Paul's integrity. Paul could have tried to buy his freedom. He refused to compromise. He trusted God with his future rather than resorting to corruption.

Finally, these verses show that God's timing is not always our timing. Paul waited two years in prison. Jesus had promised that Paul would testify in Rome (23:11), but the fulfillment took time. Waiting is part of the life of faith. Isaiah writes, [[Isaiah 40:31|But those who wait for the LORD will renew their strength]].

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses warn against the love of money and the pursuit of power. Felix's greed and desire for favor kept him from God. Believers can examine whether money, status, or approval is hindering their obedience to God.

They also encourage believers to maintain integrity, even when compromise would bring relief. Paul refused to pay a bribe, even though it might have meant freedom. Believers can trust God rather than resorting to dishonest means.

Finally, these verses encourage believers who are waiting. Some believers face long seasons of waiting: in illness, unemployment, difficult circumstances, or unanswered prayer. Paul's two years in prison remind them that God's purposes continue even in seasons of delay. Believers can use these times faithfully, trusting that God's promises will be fulfilled.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Acts 24 shows how to respond to false accusations with truth and calm. Paul did not flatter Felix or attack his accusers. He answered each charge honestly and pointed out that the charges could not be proven. Believers facing unjust criticism can respond with the same honesty, respect, and confidence.

The chapter also shows that Christian faith is rooted in God's earlier revelation. Paul worshiped the God of his ancestors, believed the Law and the Prophets, and hoped for the resurrection. Believers can read the whole Bible as one story pointing to Christ, and can hold firmly to the hope of the resurrection.

Acts 24 contains a powerful warning about delay. Felix heard about righteousness, self-control, and the judgment to come, and he was terrified. Yet he said, "When it is convenient for me, I will summon you." He never responded. Anyone who feels God's conviction can respond today, not waiting for a convenient time that may never come.

Finally, the chapter shows Paul's integrity during a long, unjust imprisonment. He refused to pay a bribe and waited two years, trusting God's timing. Believers can maintain integrity under pressure, resist the love of money and power, and trust that God's purposes continue even in seasons of waiting.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord God, thank You that the faith I have in Jesus is rooted in Your promises from the beginning. Thank You for the hope of the resurrection. When I face false accusations, give me calm and truthfulness, as You gave Paul. Give me courage to speak about righteousness, self-control, and the judgment to come, even to those who may not want to hear. Search my own heart, and help me to live righteously and with self-control, remembering that I will give an account to You. Keep me from delaying when Your Spirit convicts me; help me to respond to You now. Guard me from the love of money and the desire for power. When I must wait, help me to trust Your timing and to remain faithful. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
