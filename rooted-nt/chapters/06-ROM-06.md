# Romans 6
theme: Dead to Sin, Alive to God: Freedom from Sin's Reign
main: Paul answers a dangerous misunderstanding of grace: if grace increases where sin increases, should believers keep sinning? Absolutely not. Through baptism, believers have been united with Christ in His death and resurrection; their old self was crucified with Him so that they might walk in newness of life. They are to consider themselves dead to sin and alive to God, offering their bodies to God as instruments of righteousness, because sin is no longer their master. Everyone serves someone; once slaves of sin, believers are now set free to serve righteousness, and while the wages of sin is death, God's free gift is eternal life in Christ Jesus.

## Chapter Background and Story

In Romans 5:20, Paul wrote, [[Romans 5:20|where sin abounded, grace abounded more exceedingly]]. Some might twist this truth to mean that sin does not matter, or even that sinning more would bring more grace. In Romans 6, Paul addresses this misunderstanding directly. He asks two similar questions: "Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound?" (6:1) and "Shall we sin because we are not under law but under grace?" (6:15). He answers both with a strong "May it never be!"

Paul's answer is not merely to forbid sin. He explains that believers have been fundamentally changed through their union with Christ. They are no longer the same people. Their relationship to sin has been broken. This chapter is foundational for understanding Christian holiness: believers obey God not to earn salvation, but because they have been made new.

### Dead to Sin through Union with Christ (6:1–11)

Paul's opening question and answer are studied below. He explains what happened to believers: [[v3|Or don’t you know that all of us who were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into his death?]] To be baptized "into Christ" means to be united with Him. And union with Christ includes union with His death. The meaning of this union is studied below.

[[v5|For if we have become united with him in the likeness of his death, we will also be part of his resurrection]]. The word "united" was used of plants grafted together so that they grow as one. Believers are joined to Christ in His death and will share in His resurrection.

[[v6|knowing this, that our old man was crucified with him, that the body of sin might be done away with, so that we would no longer be in bondage to sin]]. The "old man" refers to the person believers used to be before Christ, their old identity in Adam, under the power of sin. That old self was crucified with Christ. The "body of sin" may refer to human existence dominated by sin. The purpose is that believers would no longer be slaves to sin. [[v7|For he who has died has been freed from sin]]. Death ends all obligations. A dead person cannot be commanded by a former master.

[[v8|But if we died with Christ, we believe that we will also live with him]]. Christ's resurrection was final: [[v9|knowing that Christ, being raised from the dead, dies no more. Death no longer has dominion over him!]] [[v10|For the death that he died, he died to sin one time; but the life that he lives, he lives to God]]. Christ dealt with sin once for all and now lives forever to God. Paul's application of this truth is studied below.

### Offering Yourselves to God (6:12–14)

Because of this new identity, Paul gives commands: [[v12|Therefore don’t let sin reign in your mortal body, that you should obey it in its lusts]]. Sin is no longer the rightful king, but it still tries to rule. Believers must refuse to let it.

[[v13|Also, do not present your members to sin as instruments of unrighteousness, but present yourselves to God as alive from the dead, and your members as instruments of righteousness to God]]. "Members" refers to the parts of the body, such as hands, feet, eyes, and tongue, and by extension all of one's abilities. "Instruments" can also mean "weapons." Believers can use their bodies either as weapons for sin or as weapons for righteousness. Each day, they choose whom they will serve with their bodies.

Then comes a promise: [[v14|For sin will not have dominion over you, for you are not under law, but under grace]]. Sin will not be the master of believers. Why? Because they are under grace, not law. The Law commanded but did not give power to obey. Grace frees believers from sin's dominion and gives them a new life.

### Slaves of Righteousness (6:15–23)

Paul asks again, [[v15|Shall we sin because we are not under law but under grace? May it never be!]] He uses the image of slavery, common in the Roman world, where perhaps a third of the population were slaves. [[v16|Don’t you know that when you present yourselves as servants and obey someone, you are the servants of whomever you obey, whether of sin to death, or of obedience to righteousness?]] Everyone serves someone. There is no neutral ground. People either serve sin, which leads to death, or serve God, which leads to righteousness.

[[v17|But thanks be to God that, whereas you were bondservants of sin, you became obedient from the heart to that form of teaching to which you were delivered]]. Believers were once slaves to sin, but they have responded wholeheartedly to the gospel. Notice the phrase "from the heart." Their obedience was not mere outward conformity but heartfelt.

[[v18|Being made free from sin, you became bondservants of righteousness]]. Freedom from sin does not mean freedom to do anything one wants. It means freedom to serve righteousness. True freedom is found in serving God.

Paul admits that he is using a human illustration [[v19|because of the weakness of your flesh]]. Slavery is an imperfect picture, since serving God is not oppressive but life-giving. Still, the illustration helps. Just as believers once gave themselves fully to sin, they should now give themselves fully to righteousness, [[v19|for sanctification]], the process of becoming holy.

Paul asks them to consider the results of their former life: [[v21|What fruit then did you have at that time in the things of which you are now ashamed? For the end of those things is death]]. Sin promised much but produced shame and death. [[v22|But now, being made free from sin and having become servants of God, you have your fruit of sanctification and the result of eternal life]]. Serving God produces holiness and leads to eternal life. Paul's final summary is studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### Romans 6:1–2 — Shall We Continue in Sin?

verse: Romans 6:1-2

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had just written that where sin increased, grace overflowed even more (5:20). God's grace is greater than any sin. This is wonderful news. But Paul knew that some people might misuse it.

[[v1|What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound?]] Someone might reason: "If God's grace is shown most when there is the most sin, then the more I sin, the more grace I will receive. So why not keep sinning?" Paul had apparently been accused of teaching this (3:8).

[[v2|May it never be!]] This is the strongest negative expression in Greek. It can be translated "Absolutely not!" or "God forbid!" Paul rejects the idea completely.

[[v2|We who died to sin, how could we live in it any longer?]] Paul's reason is that believers have "died to sin." This does not mean that believers never sin or that they no longer feel the pull of temptation. It means that their relationship to sin has fundamentally changed. Sin is no longer their master. They have been separated from sin's power. Living in sin would contradict who they now are.

The question is not "May I continue in sin?" but "How could I?" For someone who has died to sin, continuing to live in it makes no sense.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that grace is not a license to sin. True grace does not make people careless about sin; it changes them. Paul writes to Titus that God's grace [[Titus 2:12|instructing us to the intent that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we would live soberly, righteously, and godly in this present age]].

They also show that the gospel creates a new identity. Believers are not merely forgiven sinners who continue as before. They have died to sin and been raised to new life. Their identity has changed, and their behavior is meant to flow from that new identity.

Finally, these verses show that justification and sanctification belong together. God does not declare people righteous and then leave them unchanged. Those whom He justifies, He also begins to transform. Faith that does not change a person's life is not genuine faith.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses warn believers against taking God's grace for granted. It is possible to think, "God will forgive me anyway, so it doesn't matter what I do." Paul's response is clear: those who have truly received grace do not want to keep living in sin.

They also encourage believers who struggle with sin. Believers have died to sin, which means sin's power has been broken. They can fight against sin with confidence, knowing that they are no longer its slaves.

Finally, these verses invite believers to examine their attitude toward sin. Do they hate sin, or do they excuse it? A heart changed by grace grows to hate sin and love righteousness.

### Romans 6:4 — Walking in Newness of Life

verse: Romans 6:4

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had asked whether believers should continue in sin so that grace might increase, and he had answered, "Absolutely not!" He explained that believers have died to sin. Now he explains how this happened: through union with Christ, pictured in baptism.

In the early church, baptism was often done by immersion, plunging the person under water and bringing them up again. This vividly pictured death, burial, and resurrection.

[[v4|We were buried therefore with him through baptism into death]]. When believers are baptized, they are identified with Christ's death and burial. Just as Christ died and was buried, believers' old way of life has died and been buried.

[[v4|that just as Christ was raised from the dead through the glory of the Father]]. Christ did not stay in the tomb. He was raised by the glorious power of God the Father.

[[v4|so we also might walk in newness of life]]. Just as Christ rose to new life, believers are raised to live a new kind of life. "Walk" is a common biblical word for the way a person lives day by day. "Newness of life" means a life that is fresh and different, empowered by the risen Christ.

Christians understand baptism in different ways. Some see it as a means through which God unites believers with Christ. Others see it as an outward sign of a union with Christ that happens through faith. All agree that union with Christ in His death and resurrection is the foundation of the Christian life, and that baptism is the visible sign of this union.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that believers share in Christ's death and resurrection. This is not just a symbol; it is a spiritual reality. Paul writes, [[Galatians 2:20|I have been crucified with Christ, and it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me]].

It also shows that the Christian life is a new life. Believers do not merely try to improve their old life. They have been given a new life, powered by the same God who raised Jesus from the dead. Paul writes, [[2 Corinthians 5:17|Therefore if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old things have passed away. Behold, all things have become new]].

Finally, the verse shows the importance of baptism. Baptism is not merely a ritual; it is a powerful picture of the believer's union with Christ. It marks the beginning of a new life and identity.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to remember their baptism. Baptism marks the moment of identification with Christ's death and resurrection. Believers can recall that their old life has been buried and that they have been raised to walk in newness of life.

It also encourages believers to live differently. The new life is not merely a future hope; it is meant to be lived now, every day. Believers can ask God to help them walk in newness of life in their relationships, habits, words, and choices.

Finally, the verse brings hope to those who feel stuck in old patterns. The same power that raised Jesus from the dead is at work in believers. Change is possible. Believers can trust God to give them new life.

### Romans 6:11 — Consider Yourselves Dead to Sin and Alive to God

verse: Romans 6:11

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been explaining that believers were united with Christ in His death and resurrection. Christ died to sin once for all, and now He lives to God. Death no longer has any power over Him.

[[v11|Thus consider yourselves also to be dead to sin, but alive to God in Christ Jesus our Lord]]. This is the first command in Romans 6. Paul tells believers to "consider" themselves dead to sin and alive to God. The word "consider" is the same bookkeeping word used in Romans 4 for "accounted" or "credited." It means to count something as true, to reckon it to be so.

Believers are not told to make themselves dead to sin by their own effort. They are told to recognize what is already true because of their union with Christ. They have died to sin. They are alive to God. Now they must live in light of this reality.

"Dead to sin" means that sin no longer has authority over them. A dead person does not respond to commands. Sin may still call to believers, but they are no longer obligated to obey.

"Alive to God" means that believers now live in relationship with God, responsive to Him, living for Him.

"In Christ Jesus our Lord" shows that this new identity comes only through union with Christ.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that the Christian life begins with knowing who one is in Christ. Before Paul gives commands about behavior (6:12–13), he tells believers to understand their identity. Right living flows from right understanding. Believers do not obey to become new; they obey because they are new.

It also shows the importance of faith in the ongoing Christian life. Believers must believe what God says about them, even when their feelings or experiences seem to contradict it. Faith counts God's word as true.

Finally, the verse shows that believers share in Christ's victory. Just as Christ has defeated sin and death, believers share in His triumph. Their struggle against sin is not a battle to be won from scratch, but a battle in which the decisive victory has already been won.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers facing temptation to remember who they are. When temptation comes, believers can say, "I am dead to sin and alive to God in Christ. Sin is not my master." This truth gives strength to resist.

It also encourages believers to think rightly about themselves. Many believers see themselves mainly as sinners who will always fail. While believers still struggle with sin, their deepest identity is that they are alive to God in Christ. This truth can bring hope and confidence.

Finally, the verse invites believers to live for God daily. Being "alive to God" means paying attention to Him, responding to Him, and living for His glory. Believers can ask each day how they can live for God in their particular circumstances.

### Romans 6:23 — The Wages of Sin and the Free Gift of God

verse: Romans 6:23

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been comparing two kinds of servitude: slavery to sin and slavery to God. He had shown that serving sin produces shame and leads to death, while serving God produces holiness and leads to eternal life. Now he summarizes the whole chapter in one powerful verse.

[[v23|For the wages of sin is death]]. "Wages" were the regular payment given to soldiers or workers. They were earned. Sin pays its servants, and what it pays is death. Death here includes physical death, spiritual separation from God, and ultimately eternal separation from Him. Sin always pays what it promises, and its payment is death.

[[v23|but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord]]. In contrast to wages, which are earned, eternal life is a "free gift." It cannot be earned. God gives it graciously. "Eternal life" is not only endless existence; it is the life of God, a relationship with Him that begins now and lasts forever. And it is found "in Christ Jesus our Lord," through union with Him.

The contrast is striking: wages versus gift, sin versus God, death versus eternal life. Sin pays what is deserved; God gives what is not deserved.

This verse is one of the most well-known in the Bible because it summarizes the gospel so clearly.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows the seriousness of sin. Sin is not a minor problem; it leads to death. Genesis shows that death entered the world through sin (Genesis 2:17; 3:19). Every death is a reminder of sin's terrible cost.

It also shows the generosity of God. Eternal life is a gift. God does not wait for people to earn it, because they never could. He gives it freely through Christ. This gift cost God the life of His Son.

Finally, the verse shows that there are only two paths. Every person is either receiving sin's wages or God's gift. There is no middle ground. The choice is between death and life.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse helps believers share the gospel simply. Many people do not understand that sin leads to death or that eternal life is a free gift. This verse explains both truths in a few words.

It also reminds believers of the seriousness of sin. Even believers can treat sin lightly. Remembering that the wages of sin is death can motivate believers to turn from sin and live for God.

Finally, the verse fills believers with gratitude. They have received the free gift of eternal life, which they could never have earned. They can thank God every day for this gift and live in a way that honors the Giver.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Romans 6 makes clear that grace is not a license to sin. Those who have truly received God's grace have died to sin and cannot continue to live in it as before. Believers can reject the idea that sin does not matter, and instead let God's grace transform how they live.

The chapter also explains the foundation of the Christian life: union with Christ. Through faith, pictured in baptism, believers have died with Christ and been raised with Him to walk in newness of life. Their old self was crucified, so that they are no longer slaves to sin. Believers can remember their baptism and live in the reality of their new identity.

Romans 6 calls believers to consider themselves dead to sin and alive to God, and to offer their bodies to God as instruments of righteousness. Each day, believers choose whom they will serve with their hands, eyes, tongues, and abilities. Because they are under grace, sin will not be their master. Believers can face temptation with confidence, knowing that sin's power has been broken.

Finally, the chapter contrasts two kinds of servitude. Serving sin produces shame and leads to death; serving God produces holiness and leads to eternal life. The wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord. Believers can respond with gratitude for this gift and give themselves gladly to serving the God who has set them free.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You that I have been united with You in Your death and resurrection. Thank You that my old self was crucified with You, so that I no longer have to be a slave to sin. Keep me from ever treating Your grace as an excuse to sin. Help me to consider myself dead to sin and alive to God in You. Today I offer myself to You, and I offer my body, my hands, my eyes, my tongue, and my abilities as instruments of righteousness. When temptation comes, remind me that sin is not my master, because I am under grace. Thank You for setting me free to serve You. Thank You that though the wages of sin is death, You have given me the free gift of eternal life. Help me to walk in newness of life each day. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
