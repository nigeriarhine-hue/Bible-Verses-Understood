# Revelation 1
theme: The Risen Christ Among His Churches
main: Revelation begins by announcing itself as the revelation of Jesus Christ, given by God to show His servants what must soon take place, and promising a blessing to those who read, hear, and keep its words. John greets the seven churches in the province of Asia with grace and peace from the eternal God, from the sevenfold Spirit before His throne, and from Jesus Christ, the faithful witness, the firstborn from the dead, and the ruler of the kings of the earth. He praises Jesus, who loves us, freed us from our sins by His blood, and made us a kingdom and priests to God. Jesus is coming with the clouds, and every eye will see Him. The Lord God declares that He is the Alpha and the Omega, the Almighty. John, exiled on Patmos for the word of God and the testimony of Jesus, hears a loud voice on the Lord's Day, commanding him to write what he sees and send it to seven churches. Turning, he sees seven golden lampstands and, among them, one like a son of man, clothed in majesty, with eyes like fire, a voice like rushing waters, a sharp sword coming from His mouth, and a face shining like the sun. John falls at His feet as though dead, but Jesus places His right hand on him and says, Do not be afraid. He is the First and the Last, the Living One who was dead and is alive forevermore, and He holds the keys of death and Hades. He explains that the seven stars are the angels of the seven churches and the seven lampstands are the seven churches.

## Chapter Background and Story

Revelation 1 sets the stage for the whole book. Before John describes any beasts, plagues, or battles, he shows his readers Jesus. The risen Christ is alive, glorious, and present among His churches. Everything that follows in the book must be read in light of this opening vision.

John wrote to believers who were feeling the pressure of living as faithful Christians in the Roman Empire. Cities in Asia competed to honor the emperor, building temples and holding festivals in his name. Christians who refused to participate could be treated as disloyal, lose business, or face persecution. John himself had been banished to Patmos. Into this situation, Revelation declares that the true Lord of history is not Caesar but Jesus Christ.

### The Prologue (1:1–3)

[[v1|This is the Revelation of Jesus Christ, which God gave him to show to his servants the things which must happen soon, which he sent and made known by his angel to his servant, John,]] The revelation comes from God, through Jesus Christ, through an angel, to John, and through John to the churches. It is a revelation "of Jesus Christ," meaning it both comes from Him and is about Him.

The phrase "the things which must happen soon" has been understood in several ways. Preterist interpreters take it to mean that much of the book was fulfilled in the near future of the first readers. Futurists often understand "soon" as describing the certainty and suddenness of the events, or God's perspective on time (2 Peter 3:8). Idealists and many others note that the events of the book began to unfold in John's day and continue throughout the church age. All agree that John's readers were to live with a sense of urgency and expectation.

[[v2|who testified to God’s word and of the testimony of Jesus Christ, about everything that he saw]]. John presents himself as a faithful witness, recording exactly what he was shown. The promised blessing for readers is studied below.

### Greeting and Praise (1:4–8)

[[v4|John, to the seven assemblies that are in Asia: Grace to you and peace from God, who is and who was and who is to come; and from the seven Spirits who are before his throne;]] Revelation is written as a letter, with a greeting like those in Paul's letters. The seven churches were real congregations in cities along a postal route in western Asia Minor. Because seven often signifies completeness, many interpreters believe these seven churches also represent the whole church in every place.

God is described as the One "who is and who was and who is to come." This echoes God's name revealed to Moses, [[Exodus 3:14|I AM WHO I AM]], and expresses His eternal existence and His certain coming. "The seven Spirits who are before his throne" is understood by most interpreters as a symbolic reference to the Holy Spirit in His fullness, perhaps drawing on Isaiah 11:2 and Zechariah 4:2–10. Others think it refers to seven chief angels. The first reading fits the greeting well, since grace and peace come from the Father, the Spirit, and the Son. The description of Jesus and John's praise to Him are studied below.

[[v7|Behold, he is coming with the clouds, and every eye will see him, including those who pierced him. All the tribes of the earth will mourn over him. Even so, Amen]]. This verse combines two Old Testament prophecies: Daniel's vision of one like a son of man coming with the clouds of heaven (Daniel 7:13) and Zechariah's prophecy that people will look on the one they pierced and mourn (Zechariah 12:10). Jesus' return will be public and unmistakable. The mourning of the nations may express remorse leading to repentance, or grief at facing judgment; interpreters understand it both ways.

[[v8|“I am the Alpha and the Omega,” says the Lord God, “who is and who was and who is to come, the Almighty.”]] Alpha and Omega are the first and last letters of the Greek alphabet. God is the beginning and end of all things, and everything in between is under His authority. "The Almighty" emphasizes His supreme power. Whatever the churches face, they serve the God who rules over all.

### John's Vision of the Son of Man (1:9–16)

[[v9|I John, your brother and partner with you in the oppression, Kingdom, and perseverance in Christ Jesus, was on the isle that is called Patmos because of God’s Word and the testimony of Jesus Christ]]. John does not present himself as a distant authority but as a fellow sufferer. He shares with his readers in three things: "oppression," the suffering that comes with following Jesus; "Kingdom," the reign of God in which they already participate; and "perseverance," the patient endurance required until Christ returns. He was on Patmos because of his faithfulness to God's word.

[[v10|I was in the Spirit on the Lord’s day, and I heard behind me a loud voice, like a trumpet]] [[v11|saying, “What you see, write in a book and send to the seven assemblies: to Ephesus, Smyrna, Pergamum, Thyatira, Sardis, Philadelphia, and to Laodicea.”]] "The Lord’s day" is the earliest reference to Sunday by this name, the day of Christ's resurrection, when Christians gathered for worship. While worshiping, John was caught up "in the Spirit," a state of prophetic vision.

[[v12|I turned to see the voice that spoke with me. Having turned, I saw seven golden lamp stands]]. [[v13|And among the lamp stands was one like a son of man, clothed with a robe reaching down to his feet, and with a golden sash around his chest]]. The lampstands are later identified as the churches (v20). The risen Christ stands among them. He is not absent from His people; He is present with them, watching over them.

"One like a son of man" recalls Daniel 7:13–14, where a heavenly figure receives everlasting dominion from God. Jesus often called Himself the Son of Man. The long robe and golden sash suggest the clothing of a priest or king.

[[v14|His head and his hair were white as white wool, like snow. His eyes were like a flame of fire]]. In Daniel 7:9, the [[Daniel 7:9|Ancient of Days]], God Himself, has hair white like pure wool. John applies this description to Jesus, showing that He shares the eternal majesty of God. His eyes like fire suggest that He sees everything and judges with perfect insight.

[[v15|His feet were like burnished brass, as if it had been refined in a furnace. His voice was like the voice of many waters]]. Feet like glowing bronze suggest strength and stability. His voice, like a mighty waterfall or the roar of the sea, echoes the description of God's glory in Ezekiel 43:2.

[[v16|He had seven stars in his right hand. Out of his mouth proceeded a sharp two-edged sword. His face was like the sun shining at its brightest]]. He holds the seven stars, the angels of the churches, in His right hand, a picture of His authority over and care for His churches. The sword from His mouth represents His word, which judges and cuts to the heart (Hebrews 4:12; Isaiah 49:2). His face shining like the sun recalls the transfiguration (Matthew 17:2). This is not a literal portrait to be painted, but a symbolic description of Christ's glory, wisdom, power, and authority.

### Christ's Word to John (1:17–20)

Jesus' comforting words to John are studied below. [[v19|Write therefore the things which you have seen, and the things which are, and the things which will happen hereafter]]. Some interpreters see this verse as an outline of the book: what John has seen (chapter 1), the things that are (the churches in chapters 2–3), and what will take place later (chapters 4–22). Others see it more generally as a command to record the whole vision, which concerns both the present and the future.

[[v20|The mystery of the seven stars which you saw in my right hand, and the seven golden lamp stands is this: The seven stars are the angels of the seven assemblies. The seven lamp stands are seven assemblies]]. Here Jesus Himself interprets two symbols. The lampstands are the churches, which are meant to shine the light of Christ in the world. The "angels" of the churches have been understood as guardian angels, as human messengers or leaders, or as a way of representing the spiritual character of each church. Whatever the precise meaning, the churches are held securely in Christ's hand.

## Important Verses Explained

### Revelation 1:3 — The Blessing of Hearing and Keeping

verse: Revelation 1:3

#### Simple Explanation

Revelation begins with a promise of blessing: "Blessed is he who reads and those who hear the words of the prophecy." In the early church, few people owned copies of Scripture. One person would read aloud while the congregation listened. This verse pictures Revelation being read to the gathered church, and it promises blessing to both the reader and the hearers.

But hearing alone is not enough. The blessing also belongs to those who "keep the things that are written in it." To keep means to hold onto, obey, and live by the message. Revelation is not given merely to satisfy curiosity about the future. It is given to shape how believers live now.

The reason for urgency is given at the end of the verse: "for the time is near." The events the book describes are close at hand, so its message demands a response.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This is the first of seven blessings, or beatitudes, in Revelation (1:3; 14:13; 16:15; 19:9; 20:6; 22:7, 14). The book that contains frightening images of judgment also contains repeated promises of blessing for God's faithful people. Revelation's purpose is ultimately to comfort and encourage.

The word "prophecy" is important. Biblical prophecy is not only prediction of the future. It is God's word spoken to His people, often calling them to repentance, faith, and faithfulness. Revelation does tell about the future, but it does so to strengthen believers in the present.

The emphasis on keeping the words of the prophecy reminds readers that the right response to Revelation is obedience. Some people treat the book as a puzzle to be solved or a timetable to be decoded. But the book itself calls readers to worship God, resist evil, endure suffering, and remain faithful to Jesus.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Revelation can seem intimidating or confusing, and some believers avoid it altogether. This verse is an invitation to read it, hear it, and expect blessing from it. Even when parts of the book are difficult to understand, its main message is clear: Jesus is Lord, evil will be defeated, and those who remain faithful will share in His victory.

Believers can read Revelation in the setting where it was first heard: in the gathered church, read aloud, and received together. Hearing God's word with others guards against private speculation and encourages shared obedience.

The phrase "the time is near" encourages believers to live with readiness. Every generation is called to live faithfully, as if the Lord could return at any time.

### Revelation 1:5–6 — To Him Who Loves Us

verse: Revelation 1:5-6

#### Simple Explanation

Verse 5 describes Jesus with three titles. He is "the faithful witness," who faithfully revealed God and remained true even to death. He is "the firstborn of the dead," the first to rise from the dead to never die again, and the guarantee that His people will rise too. And He is "the ruler of the kings of the earth," the true King above every earthly ruler, including the Roman emperor.

Then John bursts into praise: "To him who loves us, and washed us from our sins by his blood." Jesus' love is ongoing; He "loves" us now. And His love has acted: He freed us from our sins through His death on the cross. Many manuscripts read "freed us" rather than "washed us," but both express the same truth: Jesus' blood releases us from the guilt and power of sin.

Verse 6 adds: "and he made us to be a Kingdom, priests to his God and Father." Those who belong to Jesus are not only forgiven. They are made into a royal people who serve God as priests. John ends with praise: "to him be the glory and the dominion forever and ever. Amen."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The three titles in verse 5 speak directly to persecuted believers. Like Jesus, they are called to be faithful witnesses, even when it costs them. Like Jesus, they may face death, but He is the firstborn from the dead, so death will not have the final word. And though earthly rulers may seem powerful, Jesus is the true ruler over them all.

The language of "Kingdom" and "priests" comes from Exodus 19:6, where God called Israel to be [[Exodus 19:6|a kingdom of priests, and a holy nation]]. Through Jesus, this calling now extends to all who belong to Him, from every nation (1 Peter 2:9). Believers have direct access to God, they offer Him worship and service, and they represent Him to the world.

This passage places love before everything else. Before John describes Jesus' glory or His judgments, he reminds believers that Jesus loves them. His love is the foundation of their salvation and their identity. Everything else in the book flows from the love of the One who gave His life for them.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers today can draw strength from knowing that Jesus loves them now, in the middle of whatever they are facing. His love is not a distant memory; it is present and active.

They can also take courage from Jesus' titles. When faithfulness is costly, they follow the faithful witness. When they face death or loss, they trust the firstborn from the dead. When world events seem out of control, they remember that Jesus is ruler of the kings of the earth.

Being made a kingdom and priests gives believers a sense of purpose. Every believer, not just church leaders, has the privilege of worshiping God, praying for others, and representing Him in daily life.

### Revelation 1:17–18 — Do Not Be Afraid

verse: Revelation 1:17-18

#### Simple Explanation

John's response to the vision of Christ's glory is overwhelming awe: "When I saw him, I fell at his feet like a dead man." This was the same John who once leaned against Jesus at the Last Supper. But now he sees Jesus in His full heavenly glory, and he cannot stand.

Jesus responds with tenderness: "He laid his right hand on me." The same right hand that holds the seven stars now touches John to comfort him. Then Jesus speaks: "Don’t be afraid. I am the first and the last."

Verse 18 continues: "and the Living one. I was dead, and behold, I am alive forever and ever." Jesus truly died, and He truly rose. He will never die again. And He adds: "I have the keys of Death and of Hades." Hades refers to the realm of the dead. Jesus has authority over death and the grave.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses combine majesty and gentleness. The glorified Christ is awe-inspiring, but He is not cold or distant. He reaches out to the trembling believer with comfort. This is the same Jesus who touched lepers, lifted up the fallen, and told His frightened disciples, "Don't be afraid," many times in the Gospels.

"The first and the last" is a title God uses for Himself in Isaiah: [[Isaiah 44:6|I am the first, and I am the last; and besides me there is no God]]. By applying this title to Himself, Jesus claims full divine identity. He shares the eternal existence and authority of God.

The keys of Death and Hades are a powerful image. Keys represent authority to open and shut. Death had seemed like a locked prison from which no one returned. But Jesus has gone into death and come out victorious, and He now holds the keys. For believers who faced persecution and possible martyrdom, this was profound comfort. Death could not hold them, because their Lord holds its keys.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many people today live with fear: fear of the future, of illness, of loss, of death itself. Jesus' words to John are also His words to every believer: "Don’t be afraid." This is not a command to deny real dangers, but an invitation to trust the One who is greater than all of them.

Believers can find comfort especially when facing death, whether their own or that of someone they love. Jesus has conquered death. He holds the keys. Those who belong to Him will share in His resurrection life.

This passage also shapes how believers approach Jesus. They come with reverence, recognizing His majesty, but also with confidence, knowing that He welcomes them with love.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Revelation 1 begins the book by showing believers who Jesus is. Before any visions of conflict or judgment, John's readers see the risen Christ in His glory, standing among His churches. This sets the tone for the whole book. Whatever the church faces, Jesus is present, He is in control, and He cares for His people.

The chapter reminds believers that Revelation is meant to bless those who hear and keep its words. It is not a book of fear or speculation, but of hope and faithfulness. Believers can approach it with confidence, expecting to meet Jesus there.

It also reminds believers of their identity. Jesus loves them, has freed them from their sins by His blood, and has made them a kingdom and priests to God. They share in His kingdom even while they share in suffering and endurance, as John did.

The vision of Christ calls for worship and awe. He is the Son of Man, clothed in majesty, with eyes that see everything and a word that judges truly. He is the Alpha and the Omega, the First and the Last. Yet He is also the One who places His hand on the trembling believer and says, "Don’t be afraid." Because He is alive forevermore and holds the keys of death and Hades, believers can face any trial, even death itself, with courage and hope.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for revealing Yourself in Your glory. You are the faithful witness, the firstborn from the dead, and the ruler of the kings of the earth. Thank You that You love me, that You freed me from my sins by Your blood, and that You have made me part of Your kingdom and a priest to serve God. Help me to hear and keep Your word, and to live with readiness for Your coming. When I feel afraid, place Your hand on me and remind me that You are the First and the Last, the Living One who was dead and is alive forevermore. Thank You that You hold the keys of death and Hades, and that nothing can separate me from Your life. Let me walk faithfully, endure patiently, and worship You with my whole heart. To You be glory and dominion forever and ever. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
