# Acts 14
theme: Through Many Hardships: Faithful Ministry, Rejected Worship, and a Door of Faith Opened
main: In Iconium, Paul and Barnabas preach boldly, and many Jews and Greeks believe, until a plot to stone them drives them on. In Lystra, Paul heals a man lame from birth, and the crowd hails them as the gods Zeus and Hermes. Horrified, the apostles tear their clothes and urge the people to turn from idols to the living God who made all things and shows His kindness through rain and harvests. Soon after, opponents stone Paul and leave him for dead, but he rises and continues. Retracing their steps, Paul and Barnabas strengthen the new churches, appoint elders, and return to Antioch to report that God has opened a door of faith to the Gentiles.

## Chapter Background and Story

Acts 14 completes the account of Paul's first missionary journey. Paul and Barnabas travel through the Roman province of Galatia, visiting the cities of Iconium, Lystra, and Derbe, before retracing their route and returning to Antioch in Syria. These are likely the churches to which Paul later wrote his letter to the Galatians.

The chapter shows two important aspects of the mission. First, it shows how Paul addressed a pagan audience with no background in the Scriptures, beginning with God as Creator. Second, it shows Paul's care for the new churches, strengthening them, warning them about suffering, and appointing leaders.

### Iconium: Belief and Division (14:1–7)

In Iconium, about ninety miles east of Pisidian Antioch, Paul and Barnabas went to the synagogue as usual, [[v1|and so spoke that a great multitude both of Jews and of Greeks believed]]. But unbelieving Jews stirred up the Gentiles and poisoned their minds against the believers.

Remarkably, opposition did not make them leave immediately: [[v3|Therefore they stayed there a long time, speaking boldly in the Lord, who testified to the word of his grace, granting signs and wonders to be done by their hands]]. The Lord confirmed the message of grace with miracles. Note the description of the gospel as [[v3|the word of his grace]]. At its heart, the gospel is a message of God's undeserved kindness.

The city became divided. Some sided with the Jewish opponents, others with the apostles. When both Gentiles and Jews, along with their leaders, plotted to mistreat and stone them, Paul and Barnabas learned of it and fled to Lystra, Derbe, and the surrounding region, [[v7|There they preached the Good News]]. Jesus had told His disciples, "When they persecute you in this city, flee into the next" (Matthew 10:23). Fleeing danger was not cowardice but wisdom, and the mission continued in new places.

### Lystra: Healing and Mistaken Worship (14:8–18)

In Lystra, there was a man [[v8|impotent in his feet, a cripple from his mother’s womb, who never had walked]]. Luke uses three phrases to emphasize the severity of his condition. The man listened to Paul preaching. Paul [[v9|fastening eyes on him and seeing that he had faith to be made whole]], said loudly, [[v10|Stand upright on your feet!]] The man jumped up and began to walk. This healing closely parallels Peter's healing of the lame man at the temple gate (3:1–10), showing that the same power worked through both apostles.

The crowd's reaction was dramatic. They shouted in their local language, [[v11|The gods have come down to us in the likeness of men!]] They called Barnabas "Jupiter" (Zeus in Greek), the chief of the gods, and Paul "Mercury" (Hermes), the messenger of the gods, because Paul did most of the speaking. There was a local legend that Zeus and Hermes had once visited the region disguised as humans and were refused hospitality by everyone except an elderly couple, who were rewarded while the others were punished. The people of Lystra may have been determined not to make the same mistake.

The priest of Zeus, whose temple stood at the city's entrance, brought bulls and garlands to the gates to offer sacrifices. Because the crowd spoke Lycaonian, Paul and Barnabas may not have understood at first what was happening. When they realized, they [[v14|tore their clothes]], a Jewish sign of grief and horror at blasphemy, and rushed into the crowd. Their message is studied below. Even so, [[v18|they hardly stopped the multitudes from making a sacrifice to them]].

### Paul Stoned (14:19–20)

Then the mood turned. Opponents from Antioch and Iconium arrived and persuaded the crowd. The people who had wanted to worship Paul now stoned him and dragged him out of the city, thinking he was dead. This is studied below.

### Strengthening the Churches (14:21–28)

After preaching in Derbe and making many disciples, Paul and Barnabas did something surprising. Derbe was near the eastern edge of Galatia, and from there they could have taken a shorter route home through Paul's home region of Cilicia. Instead, they went back through Lystra, Iconium, and Antioch, the very cities where they had been persecuted. Their purpose and their message are studied below.

[[v23|When they had appointed elders for them in every assembly, and had prayed with fasting, they commended them to the Lord on whom they had believed]]. The new churches needed leaders. Paul and Barnabas appointed elders, mature believers who would shepherd and oversee each congregation. The word "appointed" can describe a process that involved the church; some interpreters note that it can mean choosing by a show of hands. Before leaving, the apostles prayed and fasted, entrusting the churches to the Lord. They knew that the churches ultimately belonged to Christ, not to them.

They traveled back through Pisidia and Pamphylia, preached in Perga (where they had apparently not preached on the way in), and sailed from Attalia to Antioch in Syria, [[v26|from where they had been committed to the grace of God for the work which they had fulfilled]]. Their report to the church is studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### Acts 14:15–17 — Turn to the Living God

verse: Acts 14:15-17

#### Simple Explanation

After Paul healed a lame man in Lystra, the crowd decided that Paul and Barnabas were gods in human form. The priest of Zeus prepared to offer sacrifices to them. Paul and Barnabas were horrified. They tore their clothes and rushed into the crowd.

[[v15|Men, why are you doing these things? We also are men of the same nature as you]]. Paul insisted that they were ordinary human beings, with the same weaknesses as everyone else. They were not to be worshiped.

[[v15|and bring you good news, that you should turn from these vain things to the living God]]. "Vain things" means worthless or empty things, referring to the idols the people worshiped. Paul called them to turn from these empty idols to "the living God," the one true God who is alive and active.

[[v15|who made the sky, the earth, the sea, and all that is in them]]. Paul began with God as Creator. His audience in Lystra did not know the Jewish Scriptures, so Paul did not quote the prophets as he did in synagogues. He started with what they could see: the creation around them. This language echoes Exodus 20:11 and Psalm 146:6.

[[v16|who in the generations gone by allowed all the nations to walk in their own ways]]. In the past, God allowed the nations to go their own way without the special revelation He had given Israel.

[[v17|Yet he didn’t leave himself without witness, in that he did good and gave you rains from the sky and fruitful seasons, filling our hearts with food and gladness]]. Even so, God had given evidence of Himself through His kindness in nature. Every rainfall, every harvest, every meal, and every moment of joy was a gift from the living God. Lystra was an agricultural region, and the people knew how much they depended on rain and good harvests.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that God reveals Himself through creation. Paul writes in Romans, [[Romans 1:20|For the invisible things of him since the creation of the world are clearly seen, being perceived through the things that are made, even his everlasting power and divinity]]. Even people without the Scriptures can see evidence of God in the world He made. This is often called "general revelation."

They also reveal God's kindness to all people. Jesus said that God [[Matthew 5:45|makes his sun to rise on the evil and the good, and sends rain on the just and the unjust]]. God's goodness in providing food and joy is meant to lead people to seek Him.

Finally, these verses show the danger of idolatry. The people of Lystra wanted to worship Paul and Barnabas instead of the God who had healed the man. Humans are always tempted to worship created things instead of the Creator. Paul's message calls everyone to turn from idols to the living God.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to see God's goodness in everyday life. Food, rain, seasons, and gladness are all gifts from Him. Believers can cultivate gratitude by recognizing God's hand in ordinary blessings.

They also show a wise way to share the gospel with people who do not know the Bible. Paul began with what his listeners could understand: creation and God's kindness. Believers can start conversations about God by pointing to evidence of His goodness that others can see and experience.

Finally, these verses warn against the temptation to idolize people. Christians can sometimes put leaders, celebrities, or gifted individuals on pedestals. Paul and Barnabas refused to be worshiped. All glory belongs to God alone. Leaders, too, must resist the temptation to receive praise that belongs to God.

### Acts 14:19–20 — Stoned, but Rising Again

verse: Acts 14:19-20

#### Simple Explanation

The people of Lystra had tried to worship Paul and Barnabas as gods. Shortly afterward, opponents arrived from Pisidian Antioch and Iconium, cities where Paul had preached and faced opposition. They had traveled long distances to stop Paul's ministry.

[[v19|But some Jews from Antioch and Iconium came there, and having persuaded the multitudes, they stoned Paul and dragged him out of the city, supposing that he was dead]]. The same crowd that had wanted to offer sacrifices to Paul now threw stones at him. Crowds can be fickle; praise can quickly turn to violence. They stoned Paul until they thought he was dead and dragged his body outside the city.

Paul later referred to this event in his list of sufferings: [[2 Corinthians 11:25|Once I was stoned]]. He may have carried scars from it for the rest of his life (Galatians 6:17).

[[v20|But as the disciples stood around him, he rose up, and entered into the city]]. The new believers gathered around Paul's body, perhaps grieving, perhaps praying. Then Paul got up. Whether he had been unconscious or near death, his recovery was remarkable. And he went back into the city that had just tried to kill him.

[[v20|On the next day he went out with Barnabas to Derbe]]. The very next day, Paul continued his journey, traveling about sixty miles to Derbe to preach again.

It is worth remembering that Paul himself had once approved of the stoning of Stephen (8:1). Now he experienced stoning for the sake of the same Lord.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show the cost of faithful ministry. Jesus had said that Paul would suffer much for His name (9:16). Paul's stoning in Lystra was one fulfillment of that prophecy. Following Jesus can bring suffering.

They also show God's sustaining power. Paul was left for dead, yet he rose and continued his mission. Paul later wrote, [[2 Corinthians 4:8|We are pressed on every side, yet not crushed; perplexed, yet not to despair]], [[2 Corinthians 4:9|pursued, yet not forsaken; struck down, yet not destroyed]].

Finally, the image of the disciples gathered around Paul is significant. The new believers did not abandon him. They stood with him in his suffering. The church is a community that surrounds and supports those who suffer.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers who face hardship for their faith. Suffering for Christ is not a sign of failure. Paul was stoned, yet he continued. Believers can trust that God will sustain them through difficult times.

They also remind believers not to depend on the approval of crowds. Praise and popularity can quickly change. Believers are called to faithfulness to God, not to seeking human approval.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to stand with those who suffer. When fellow believers face persecution, illness, or loss, believers can gather around them, offering prayer, presence, and practical help.

### Acts 14:22 — Through Many Afflictions into the Kingdom

verse: Acts 14:22

#### Simple Explanation

After preaching in Derbe, Paul and Barnabas made a courageous decision. Instead of taking a shorter route home, they went back through Lystra, Iconium, and Pisidian Antioch, the very cities where they had faced persecution and where Paul had been stoned. They returned because the new believers needed them.

[[v22|strengthening the souls of the disciples]]. The new believers were young in the faith and surrounded by opposition. Paul and Barnabas strengthened them through teaching and encouragement.

[[v22|exhorting them to continue in the faith]]. They urged the believers to keep going, to remain faithful to Jesus. Faith is not only about beginning well but continuing to the end.

[[v22|and that through many afflictions we must enter into God’s Kingdom]]. Paul and Barnabas were honest with the new believers. They did not promise them an easy life. They told them that the path to God's kingdom would involve many hardships. The word "afflictions" refers to pressures, troubles, and persecutions.

Notice the word "we." Paul included himself. He was not telling others to endure what he himself avoided. He had just been stoned.

"God's Kingdom" here refers to the full, future experience of God's reign, which believers will enter at the end of their journey. Hardships are part of the road that leads there.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that suffering is a normal part of the Christian life. Jesus said, [[John 16:33|In the world you have trouble]]. Paul wrote to Timothy that all who want to live godly lives in Christ will be persecuted (2 Timothy 3:12). The Christian life is not a promise of comfort but of faithfulness and future glory.

It also shows that suffering has purpose. Paul writes, [[Romans 5:3|we also rejoice in our sufferings, knowing that suffering produces perseverance]]. Hardships shape believers' character and strengthen their hope. Paul also wrote that [[Romans 8:18|the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which will be revealed toward us]].

Finally, the verse shows the importance of honest discipleship. Paul and Barnabas did not hide the cost of following Jesus. They prepared new believers for what was ahead. Honest teaching helps believers persevere when trouble comes.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse helps believers understand their struggles. When hardship comes, believers can remember that this is part of the journey, not a sign that God has abandoned them. The path to God's kingdom goes through difficulty, but it leads to glory.

It also encourages believers to strengthen one another. Paul and Barnabas returned to dangerous places to encourage the new believers. Believers today can support and encourage those who are struggling, especially new believers who may not yet understand that hardships are part of following Jesus.

Finally, the verse challenges churches to teach honestly. Some messages promise that faith will bring health, wealth, and ease. Paul and Barnabas taught that the road to God's kingdom involves many afflictions. Honest teaching prepares believers to endure and to find their hope in God rather than in comfortable circumstances.

### Acts 14:27 — A Door of Faith to the Nations

verse: Acts 14:27

#### Simple Explanation

After their long journey, Paul and Barnabas returned to Antioch in Syria, the church that had sent them out. They had traveled for perhaps two years, covering many hundreds of miles by land and sea. They had preached in many cities, seen many people come to faith, faced fierce opposition, and established new churches.

[[v27|When they had arrived and had gathered the assembly together]]. They called the whole church together. The church had sent them, prayed for them, and committed them to God's grace. Now they deserved to hear what had happened.

[[v27|they reported all the things that God had done with them]]. Notice the emphasis. They did not report what they had done, but what God had done with them. They recognized that the mission's success was God's work. They were His instruments.

[[v27|and that he had opened a door of faith to the nations]]. The most important news was that God had opened a door for the Gentiles to come to faith. People who had worshiped idols had turned to the living God. The way into God's family was open to all nations through faith in Jesus.

The image of a door is used elsewhere by Paul to describe opportunities for the gospel (1 Corinthians 16:9; Colossians 4:3). God opens doors that no one can shut (Revelation 3:8).

[[v28|They stayed there with the disciples for a long time]]. Paul and Barnabas rested and ministered in Antioch.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that mission is God's work. Paul and Barnabas reported what God had done. Paul later wrote, [[1 Corinthians 3:6|I planted. Apollos watered. But God gave the increase]]. Missionaries and ministers plant and water, but God gives the growth.

It also shows that faith is the door into God's family. The door was opened "to the nations," and it was a door "of faith." Gentiles did not need to become Jews first. They entered through faith in Jesus. This would become the central issue in the next chapter, at the Jerusalem Council.

Finally, the verse shows the importance of accountability and partnership in mission. Paul and Barnabas reported back to the church that sent them. Missionaries and sending churches are partners. The church shares in the joy of what God has done through those it sends.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to give God the credit for what He does through them. When believers see good results from their efforts, they can recognize that God has worked with them and give Him the glory.

It also encourages churches to celebrate what God is doing in mission. Hearing reports of how God is working around the world can strengthen faith and inspire prayer and support.

Finally, the verse invites believers to look for doors God is opening. God opens opportunities for the gospel in neighborhoods, workplaces, and nations. Believers can pray for open doors and be ready to walk through them when God provides them.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Acts 14 shows that faithful ministry often involves both fruit and opposition. In Iconium, many believed, but the city was divided. In Lystra, a man was healed, but Paul was stoned. Believers today can expect both joy and difficulty as they share their faith. They can continue boldly, trusting that the Lord confirms the word of His grace.

The chapter also shows the importance of pointing people to the living God. When the crowd in Lystra tried to worship Paul and Barnabas, the apostles refused and pointed them to the Creator who gives rain, harvests, food, and gladness. Believers can help others see God's goodness in everyday life, and they can resist every temptation to receive glory that belongs to God alone.

Acts 14 teaches that hardship is part of the Christian journey. Paul and Barnabas told the new believers that through many afflictions they must enter God's kingdom. Believers today can face struggles with honest expectations and confident hope, knowing that God sustains them and that glory awaits.

Finally, the chapter shows the care of Paul and Barnabas for the new churches. They returned to dangerous cities to strengthen believers, appointed elders, and entrusted the churches to the Lord. Then they reported to their sending church what God had done. Believers can follow this example, caring for others' spiritual growth, supporting church leadership, and celebrating what God is doing in His mission.

## Chapter Prayer

Living God, You made the heavens, the earth, the sea, and everything in them. Thank You for the rain, the harvests, the food, and the gladness You give every day. Open my eyes to see Your kindness in ordinary things, and help me to point others to You. Guard me from seeking praise that belongs to You, and from placing any person on a pedestal. When following You brings hardship, strengthen me as You strengthened Paul, so that I can rise and continue. Help me to remember that through many afflictions I will enter Your kingdom, and that the glory to come is worth it all. Make me an encourager of other believers, and bless the leaders of Your church. Thank You for opening the door of faith to all nations. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
