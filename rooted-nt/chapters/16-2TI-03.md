# 2 Timothy 3
theme: Difficult Times and the God-Breathed Scriptures
main: Paul warns Timothy that in the last days there will be difficult times, when people will be lovers of themselves, lovers of money, proud, abusive, ungrateful, unholy, heartless, and lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God, having an outward form of godliness but denying its power. Such people prey on the vulnerable and oppose the truth, as Jannes and Jambres opposed Moses, but their folly will be exposed. In contrast, Timothy has followed Paul's teaching, way of life, faith, patience, love, and endurance through persecutions, from which the Lord rescued him. All who want to live a godly life in Christ will be persecuted, while evil people go from bad to worse. Timothy must continue in what he has learned, remembering the holy Scriptures he has known from childhood, which are able to make him wise for salvation through faith in Christ. All Scripture is God-breathed and useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting, and training in righteousness, so that the servant of God may be thoroughly equipped for every good work.

## Chapter Background and Story

In chapter 2, Paul urged Timothy to remain faithful amid false teaching. In chapter 3, he describes the broader moral and spiritual climate in which Timothy is serving, and he points Timothy to the two great resources that will keep him steady: the example of faithful believers and the Scriptures.

"The last days" in the New Testament refers to the whole period between Christ's first coming and His return (Acts 2:16–17; Hebrews 1:2). Paul is not describing only a distant future. He tells Timothy to "turn away from these" (v5), showing that such people were already present. Every generation of believers has seen these patterns, though they may intensify as the end approaches.

This chapter contains one of the most important statements in the Bible about the nature and purpose of Scripture (vv16–17). Paul, facing death, wanted Timothy to know that God's word would sustain him when Paul was gone.

### Difficult Times (3:1–5)

The description of people in the last days is studied below.

### Predators and Opponents of the Truth (3:6–9)

[[v6|For some of these are people who creep into houses and take captive gullible women loaded down with sins, led away by various lusts]], [[v7|always learning and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth]]. The false teachers used manipulative methods. They "crept into" households, perhaps when husbands were away, and targeted people who were spiritually vulnerable, weighed down by guilt and easily swayed. Paul is not making a statement about women in general; in this setting, many women had limited access to education and were therefore more vulnerable to manipulative teachers. Paul's anger is directed at those who prey on the vulnerable. Spiritual predators who exploit people's guilt, loneliness, or need are a serious danger in every age. Such people were "always learning," always hearing new ideas, but never coming to the truth.

[[v8|Even as Jannes and Jambres opposed Moses, so these also oppose the truth, men corrupted in mind, who concerning the faith are rejected]]. Jannes and Jambres are not named in the Old Testament, but Jewish tradition gave these names to Pharaoh's magicians who opposed Moses by imitating his miracles (Exodus 7:11–12, 22). The false teachers were like them: counterfeits who opposed God's truth.

[[v9|But they will proceed no further. For their folly will be evident to all men, as theirs also came to be]]. Pharaoh's magicians eventually failed; they could not reproduce the plague of gnats and admitted, [[Exodus 8:19|This is God’s finger]] (Exodus 8:18–19). Likewise, false teachers will eventually be exposed.

### Paul's Example (3:10–13)

[[v10|But you followed my teaching, conduct, purpose, faith, patience, love, steadfastness]], [[v11|persecutions, and sufferings—those things that happened to me at Antioch, Iconium, and Lystra. I endured those persecutions. The Lord delivered me out of them all]]. In contrast to the false teachers, Timothy had followed Paul closely. He knew Paul's teaching, his way of life, his goals, and his character. Antioch in Pisidia, Iconium, and Lystra were cities on Paul's first missionary journey, where he faced intense opposition and was even stoned and left for dead (Acts 13:50; 14:5, 19). Lystra was Timothy's hometown, so he may have witnessed these sufferings himself. The Lord rescued Paul each time. The reality of persecution for all believers is studied below.

[[v13|But evil men and impostors will grow worse and worse, deceiving and being deceived]]. Deception is a downward spiral. Those who deceive others end up deceived themselves.

## Important Verses Explained

### 2 Timothy 3:1–5 — A Form of Godliness Without Its Power

verse: 2 Timothy 3:1-5

#### Simple Explanation

"But know this: that in the last days, grievous times will come." "Grievous" means difficult, dangerous, or hard to bear. The word was used for wild, fierce animals and for dangerous seas.

Paul then lists nineteen characteristics of people in these times. The list begins and ends with misplaced love: "lovers of self," "lovers of money," and "lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God." At the root of these problems is love turned in the wrong direction, away from God and others and toward self.

The list includes pride ("boastful, arrogant"), harmful speech ("blasphemers," "slanderers"), broken family relationships ("disobedient to parents," "without natural affection"), ingratitude ("unthankful"), lack of holiness ("unholy"), hard hearts ("unforgiving"), lack of self-control, cruelty ("fierce"), hatred of good, betrayal ("traitors"), recklessness ("headstrong"), and conceit.

Verse 5 is especially striking: "holding a form of godliness but having denied its power." These people look religious on the outside. They may attend worship, use spiritual language, and claim to follow God. But their lives deny the transforming power of true faith. Their religion is an outward shell without inner reality.

"Turn away from these, also." Timothy is to avoid being influenced by such people.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This list is sobering because it describes not only the world outside the church but also people who claim to be religious. The most dangerous form of godlessness is the one that wears the mask of godliness. Jesus warned against hypocrisy, comparing religious hypocrites to whitened tombs that look beautiful outside but are full of death inside (Matthew 23:27–28).

The list also shows that sin is fundamentally a problem of love. When people love themselves, money, and pleasure more than God, every other relationship becomes distorted. Jesus taught that the greatest commandments are to love God with all one's heart and to love one's neighbor as oneself (Mark 12:29–31). The sins in this list are the opposite of those two commandments.

Believers can recognize these patterns in the world around them, and humbly, in their own hearts as well. Self-love, greed, pride, and ingratitude are temptations for everyone. The remedy is the "power" of true godliness: the transforming work of the Holy Spirit, who produces love for God and others.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many of the traits Paul lists are visible in modern culture: self-promotion, consumerism, broken families, harsh speech, ingratitude, and the pursuit of pleasure. Believers can recognize these patterns without despair, knowing that Scripture anticipated them.

This passage also calls for self-examination. Believers can ask whether their faith is merely outward or whether it has transforming power in their lives. Does their faith make them more loving, grateful, self-controlled, and humble?

Finally, "turn away from these" calls for wisdom in relationships. Believers are called to love everyone, but they should be careful about being influenced by people whose lives deny the power of godliness, and they should protect themselves and others from those who are manipulative or abusive.

### 2 Timothy 3:12 — All Who Desire to Live Godly Will Suffer Persecution

verse: 2 Timothy 3:12

#### Simple Explanation

Paul makes a simple but challenging statement. "Yes, and all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution."

"All" means every believer, not just apostles or leaders. "Who desire to live godly" refers to those who are serious about following Christ, who want their lives to reflect His character. "In Christ Jesus" means in union with Him.

Such people "will suffer persecution." Persecution can take many forms: physical violence, imprisonment, loss of property, social rejection, ridicule, discrimination, or pressure from family or society. Its intensity varies greatly from place to place and time to time. But some form of opposition is a normal part of following Christ.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Jesus told His followers to expect persecution. He said, [[John 15:20|If they persecuted me, they will also persecute you]]. He also said, [[Matthew 5:10|Blessed are those who have been persecuted for righteousness' sake, for theirs is the Kingdom of Heaven]]. The world that rejected Christ will often reject those who follow Him.

Why does godliness provoke opposition? A godly life exposes sin by contrast (John 3:19–20). It challenges the values of a world that loves self, money, and pleasure. And there is a spiritual enemy who opposes God's people (Ephesians 6:12).

This truth should not lead believers to seek persecution or to see every difficulty as persecution. Some hardships come from one's own foolishness or unkindness (1 Peter 4:15). True persecution comes because of faithfulness to Christ. Paul also shows that God often delivers His people (v11), though sometimes deliverance comes through death into His presence (2 Timothy 4:18).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers in many parts of the world face severe persecution for their faith. This verse calls Christians everywhere to remember them, pray for them, and support them (Hebrews 13:3).

For those who face milder forms of opposition, such as ridicule, exclusion, or pressure to compromise, this verse is a reminder that such experiences are not strange. They are part of following Christ. Believers can respond with courage, grace, and love.

This verse also offers perspective. If following Christ were always easy and comfortable, it might be a sign that faith had become merely a "form of godliness." Faithful living may bring cost, but it also brings the joy of sharing in Christ's sufferings and the promise of His reward.

### 2 Timothy 3:14–15 — Remain in What You Have Learned

verse: 2 Timothy 3:14-15

#### Simple Explanation

In contrast to the deceivers who keep going from bad to worse, Paul urges Timothy: "But you remain in the things which you have learned and have been assured of." Timothy should hold firmly to the truth he has received, not be swayed by new or false teaching.

"Knowing from whom you have learned them." Timothy learned the faith from trustworthy people: his grandmother Lois, his mother Eunice (2 Timothy 1:5), and Paul. The character of his teachers confirmed the truth of their teaching.

Verse 15: "From infancy, you have known the holy Scriptures." Since early childhood, Timothy had been taught the Scriptures, which at that time meant the Old Testament. Jewish children often began learning the Scriptures at a very young age.

"Which are able to make you wise for salvation through faith which is in Christ Jesus." The Scriptures point to Christ. They give wisdom that leads to salvation, not through mere knowledge, but through faith in Jesus.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show the importance of continuing in the faith one has received. The false teachers offered novelty; Paul urges faithfulness. This does not mean refusing to learn or grow, but it does mean staying rooted in the truth of the gospel rather than chasing every new idea.

The phrase "from infancy" highlights the value of teaching children Scripture. Seeds planted in childhood can bear fruit throughout life. Proverbs 22:6 says, [[Proverbs 22:6|Train up a child in the way he should go, and when he is old he will not depart from it]].

Paul's description of the Scriptures as able to make one "wise for salvation through faith which is in Christ Jesus" shows how the Old Testament points to Christ. Jesus said that the Scriptures testify about Him (John 5:39), and after His resurrection He explained to His disciples how all the Scriptures spoke of Him (Luke 24:27, 44–47). The purpose of Scripture is to lead people to faith in Christ.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can stay grounded in the truths they have learned, especially in times when new teachings or cultural pressures challenge the faith. This involves regularly returning to Scripture and to the core truths of the gospel.

Parents, grandparents, and churches can invest in teaching children Scripture from an early age. Reading Bible stories, memorizing verses, and talking about God's word at home can shape a child's life.

Believers can also read the whole Bible, including the Old Testament, with an eye toward Christ, seeing how its stories, laws, and prophecies prepare for and point to Him.

### 2 Timothy 3:16–17 — All Scripture Is God-Breathed

verse: 2 Timothy 3:16-17

#### Simple Explanation

This is one of the most important statements in the Bible about Scripture itself.

"Every Scripture is God-breathed." The Greek word, *theopneustos*, literally means "breathed out by God." Scripture is not merely human writing that God approves; it comes from God Himself. Though written by human authors in their own styles and contexts, its ultimate source is God. Peter says that the prophets spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit (2 Peter 1:20–21).

"And profitable." Because Scripture comes from God, it is useful. Paul names four ways.

"For teaching": instructing in truth, showing what to believe.

"For reproof": exposing error and sin, showing what is wrong.

"For correction": restoring to the right path, showing how to make it right.

"For instruction in righteousness": training in godly living, showing how to stay on the right path.

Verse 17 gives the purpose: "that each person who belongs to God may be complete, thoroughly equipped for every good work." Scripture prepares believers fully for whatever God calls them to do. "Thoroughly equipped" was used for a ship fully outfitted for a voyage or a wagon fully loaded for a journey.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

When Paul wrote these words, "Scripture" primarily referred to the Old Testament. But the early church soon recognized the writings of the apostles as Scripture as well. Peter refers to Paul's letters alongside [[2 Peter 3:16|the other Scriptures]] (2 Peter 3:15–16), and Paul quotes a saying of Jesus as Scripture (1 Timothy 5:18). Christians believe that the whole Bible, Old and New Testaments, is God-breathed.

Christians have described the nature of Scripture's authority and inspiration in various ways, but they broadly agree that Scripture is God's word, trustworthy, and the authority for Christian faith and life. The fact that Scripture is "God-breathed" means that, when believers read it, they hear God speaking.

The four uses of Scripture form a complete cycle: teaching shows the right path, reproof shows where one has left it, correction shows how to get back, and training shows how to stay on it. Scripture is not merely information; it is God's tool for shaping His people.

The goal is not just knowledge but good works. Scripture equips believers to live and serve well. Knowledge of the Bible that does not lead to transformed living misses its purpose.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Because Scripture is God-breathed, believers can approach it with reverence, expecting God to speak through it. Regular reading, study, meditation, and memorization of Scripture allow it to shape their minds and hearts.

Believers can welcome all four functions of Scripture: learning truth, being confronted with sin, receiving correction, and being trained in righteousness. This requires humility and openness, especially when Scripture challenges one's thinking or behavior.

Finally, believers can remember that the purpose of Scripture is to equip them for good works. As they read, they can ask not only "What does this mean?" but also "How is God calling me to live?"

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Second Timothy 3 describes difficult times, marked by people who love themselves, money, and pleasure more than God, who display a form of godliness while denying its power. These patterns appear in every age, and believers can recognize them in the world and, with humility, in their own hearts. The remedy is not mere religious appearance but the transforming power of true faith.

The chapter also warns about manipulative teachers who prey on the vulnerable. Churches can protect people from such predators by teaching the truth clearly, caring for those who are struggling, and being watchful for those who would exploit others.

Paul's example offers a different path. Timothy had followed Paul's teaching, conduct, faith, patience, love, and endurance in suffering. All who want to live godly lives in Christ will face persecution in some form, but the Lord is faithful to deliver. Believers can endure opposition with courage and pray for persecuted Christians around the world.

Finally, the chapter points to the great resource that sustains believers: the Scriptures. They are able to make people wise for salvation through faith in Christ. All Scripture is God-breathed and useful for teaching, reproof, correction, and training in righteousness, equipping God's people for every good work. In times of confusion, deception, and difficulty, believers can remain in what they have learned, staying rooted in God's word and letting it shape their lives.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord, the times are often difficult, and many love themselves, money, and pleasure more than You. Guard my heart from these things, and let my faith be not merely an outward form but a life filled with Your power. Protect the vulnerable from those who would deceive and exploit them. Thank You for faithful examples who have shown me what it means to follow You. When I face opposition for living a godly life, give me courage and grace, and strengthen my brothers and sisters who suffer persecution around the world. Help me to remain in what I have learned and to treasure Your Scriptures, which make me wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus. Thank You that all Scripture is breathed out by You. Teach me, correct me, and train me in righteousness through Your word, so that I may be fully equipped for every good work. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
