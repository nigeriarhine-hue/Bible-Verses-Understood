# Revelation 2
theme: Messages to Ephesus, Smyrna, Pergamum, and Thyatira
main: The risen Christ speaks to four of the seven churches in Asia. To Ephesus, He commends their hard work, endurance, and refusal to tolerate false apostles, but rebukes them for abandoning the love they had at first; He calls them to remember, repent, and do the works they did at first, or else He will remove their lampstand. To Smyrna, a poor and persecuted church, He speaks no rebuke at all: He knows their suffering and poverty, yet they are rich; He tells them not to fear what they are about to suffer and to be faithful to death, promising them the crown of life. To Pergamum, which lives where Satan's throne is and has remained faithful even when Antipas was martyred, He warns against those who hold the teaching of Balaam and the Nicolaitans, leading people into idolatry and sexual immorality, and calls them to repent. To Thyatira, whose love, faith, service, and endurance are growing, He warns against tolerating a false prophetess He calls Jezebel, who leads His servants into immorality and idolatry; He searches minds and hearts and will repay each according to their deeds. To each church He gives promises for those who overcome: the tree of life, protection from the second death, hidden manna and a white stone with a new name, and authority over the nations and the morning star.

## Chapter Background and Story

Revelation 2 and 3 contain seven messages from the risen Christ to seven churches. Each message follows a similar pattern. Christ introduces Himself with a description drawn from the vision of chapter 1. He says, "I know your works," showing that He sees each church clearly. He offers commendation where it is deserved and correction where it is needed. He calls for repentance or perseverance. He urges everyone with ears to hear what the Spirit says to the churches. And He gives a promise to the one who "overcomes," the believer who remains faithful to the end.

These were real churches in real cities, and the details of each message fit what is known about those places. At the same time, every message ends with a call to all the churches: [[v7|He who has an ear, let him hear what the Spirit says to the assemblies]]. The messages were meant to be heard by every congregation, and they continue to speak to the church in every age. Some historicist interpreters have also seen the seven churches as representing seven successive periods of church history, though most interpreters today focus on the churches as representative of conditions that can be found in the church at any time.

### Ephesus: Faithful but Loveless (2:1–7)

[[v1|“To the angel of the assembly in Ephesus write: “He who holds the seven stars in his right hand, he who walks among the seven golden lamp stands says these things:]] Ephesus was the most important city in the province, a major port and home to the great temple of Artemis. Paul had spent nearly three years there (Acts 19; 20:31), and tradition connects John with the city as well. Christ presents Himself as the One who holds the churches in His hand and walks among them.

[[v2|“I know your works, and your toil and perseverance, and that you can’t tolerate evil men, and have tested those who call themselves apostles, and they are not, and found them false]]. [[v3|You have perseverance and have endured for my name’s sake, and have not grown weary]]. The Ephesian church was hardworking, persevering, and doctrinally careful. They had tested false teachers and exposed them. Paul had warned the Ephesian elders that wolves would come (Acts 20:29–30), and the church had stood firm.

Christ's rebuke and call to repentance are studied below. [[v6|But this you have, that you hate the works of the Nicolaitans, which I also hate]]. Little is known for certain about the Nicolaitans. They appear again in the message to Pergamum, where they are linked with idolatry and sexual immorality (2:14–15). They seem to have taught that believers could freely participate in pagan practices. Christ commends Ephesus for hating their works; notice that it is the works that are hated, not the people.

[[v7|He who has an ear, let him hear what the Spirit says to the assemblies. To him who overcomes I will give to eat from the tree of life, which is in the Paradise of my God]]. The tree of life first appears in the Garden of Eden (Genesis 2:9; 3:22–24). Humanity was barred from it after the fall. Christ promises that those who overcome will eat from it again. This promise is fulfilled in the new creation (Revelation 22:2).

### Smyrna: Poor but Rich (2:8–11)

[[v8|“To the angel of the assembly in Smyrna write: “The first and the last, who was dead, and has come to life says these things:]] Smyrna was a beautiful and wealthy city, known for its loyalty to Rome and its temple to the emperor. Christians there faced severe pressure. Christ introduces Himself as the One who died and came back to life, a fitting description for a church facing suffering and possible death.

[[v9|“I know your works, oppression, and your poverty (but you are rich), and the blasphemy of those who say they are Jews, and they are not, but are a synagogue of Satan]]. The Christians in Smyrna were poor, perhaps because they had lost work or property for their faith. Yet Christ says they are rich, spiritually wealthy in God's sight.

The phrase about "those who say they are Jews" requires careful handling. It refers to a specific local conflict in which some members of the synagogue in Smyrna were apparently slandering the Christians, possibly reporting them to Roman authorities. In that setting, Christ says that those who oppose His people in this way are not living out the true calling of Israel. John, like Jesus and the first apostles, was himself Jewish, and this statement is a rebuke of particular actions in a particular place. It must never be used to condemn Jewish people as a whole or to justify hostility or prejudice of any kind. Sadly, passages like this have been misused in history to fuel antisemitism, which is a grave sin and entirely contrary to the love of Christ.

Christ's encouragement to Smyrna is studied below. [[v11|He who has an ear, let him hear what the Spirit says to the assemblies. He who overcomes won’t be harmed by the second death]]. The "second death" is later identified as final judgment, the lake of fire (20:14). Believers may suffer physical death, but they will never experience the second death.

### Pergamum: Faithful but Compromising (2:12–17)

[[v12|“To the angel of the assembly in Pergamum write: “He who has the sharp two-edged sword says these things:]] Pergamum was the official center of emperor worship in the province and was home to many pagan temples, including a great altar to Zeus. The Roman governor there held "the right of the sword," the power of life and death. Christ reminds the church that He holds the true sword.

[[v13|“I know your works and where you dwell, where Satan’s throne is. You hold firmly to my name, and didn’t deny my faith in the days of Antipas my witness, my faithful one, who was killed among you, where Satan dwells]]. "Satan’s throne" may refer to the altar of Zeus, the center of emperor worship, or the city's overall role as a stronghold of idolatry. Despite the pressure, the church remained faithful, even when one of its members, Antipas, was killed. Christ honors him with the same title He bears: "my witness, my faithful one" (compare 1:5).

[[v14|But I have a few things against you, because you have there some who hold the teaching of Balaam, who taught Balak to throw a stumbling block before the children of Israel, to eat things sacrificed to idols, and to commit sexual immorality]]. [[v15|So also you likewise have some who hold to the teaching of the Nicolaitans]]. In Numbers 22–25, Balaam was hired to curse Israel. When he could not, he advised the Moabite king to lead Israel into idolatry and immorality (Numbers 31:16). Some in Pergamum were teaching a similar compromise. They may have argued that believers could attend pagan festivals and feasts in temples, where sexual immorality was often part of the celebration.

[[v16|Repent therefore, or else I am coming to you quickly and I will make war against them with the sword of my mouth]]. Christ calls the whole church to repent, since it has tolerated this teaching. His judgment will fall especially on those who teach it. The promise to the overcomer is studied below.

### Thyatira: Loving but Tolerating Evil (2:18–29)

[[v18|“To the angel of the assembly in Thyatira write: “The Son of God, who has his eyes like a flame of fire, and his feet are like burnished brass, says these things:]] Thyatira was a smaller city known for its trade guilds, including those for dyers, metalworkers, and others. Lydia, a seller of purple cloth whom Paul met in Philippi, came from Thyatira (Acts 16:14). Trade guilds often held feasts honoring pagan gods, which created difficult pressures for Christians who depended on their trades.

[[v19|“I know your works, your love, faith, service, patient endurance, and that your last works are more than the first]]. This church had the very thing Ephesus lacked: growing love and service. Its later works exceeded its first.

[[v20|But I have this against you, that you tolerate your woman Jezebel, who calls herself a prophetess. She teaches and seduces my servants to commit sexual immorality and to eat things sacrificed to idols]]. "Jezebel" is likely a symbolic name for a real woman in the church who claimed prophetic authority. The name recalls Queen Jezebel, who led Israel into idolatry (1 Kings 16:31; 21:25). Her teaching, like that of the Nicolaitans, encouraged compromise with pagan worship and immorality, perhaps arguing that it was necessary to keep one's trade.

[[v21|I gave her time to repent, but she refuses to repent of her sexual immorality]]. [[v22|Behold, I will throw her and those who commit adultery with her into a bed of great oppression, unless they repent of her works]]. [[v23|I will kill her children with Death, and all the assemblies will know that I am he who searches the minds and hearts. I will give to each one of you according to your deeds]]. Christ's patience is evident; He gave her time to repent. But persistent refusal brings judgment. "Her children" likely means her followers rather than literal children. The judgment will show all the churches that Christ sees the inner life and judges justly. Even here, the door of repentance remains open: "unless they repent."

[[v24|But to you I say, to the rest who are in Thyatira—as many as don’t have this teaching, who don’t know what some call ‘the deep things of Satan’—to you I say, I am not putting any other burden on you]]. [[v25|Nevertheless, hold that which you have firmly until I come]]. Some false teachers apparently claimed access to "deep things," perhaps teaching that believers could experience evil without being harmed by it. Christ calls their teaching what it is: "the deep things of Satan." For the faithful, He gives a simple command: hold on until He comes.

[[v26|He who overcomes, and he who keeps my works to the end, to him I will give authority over the nations]]. [[v27|He will rule them with a rod of iron, shattering them like clay pots, as I also have received of my Father;]] [[v28|and I will give him the morning star]]. These promises draw on Psalm 2:8–9, a messianic psalm. Christ promises that His faithful people will share in His reign. The "morning star" is later identified as Christ Himself (Revelation 22:16). The greatest reward is Christ.

## Important Verses Explained

### Revelation 2:4–5 — Return to Your First Love

verse: Revelation 2:4-5

#### Simple Explanation

After commending the Ephesian church for its hard work and faithfulness to truth, Christ adds a serious concern: "But I have this against you, that you left your first love." The church had not lost its doctrine or its energy. But it had lost the warm, wholehearted love it had at the beginning.

This "first love" may refer to love for Christ, love for one another, or both. The two are closely connected. When love for Christ cools, love for others often cools too.

In verse 5, Christ gives three steps for restoration: "Remember therefore from where you have fallen, and repent and do the first works." Remember what it was like when love was fresh. Repent by turning back. And do again the works that flowed from that early love.

Then comes a serious warning: "or else I am coming to you swiftly, and will move your lamp stand out of its place, unless you repent." The lampstand represents the church itself (1:20). If the church does not return to love, it will lose its place as a light-bearer for Christ.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This message teaches that sound doctrine and hard work are not enough. A church can be orthodox, disciplined, and busy, yet lack love. Paul made the same point: [[1 Corinthians 13:2|If I have the gift of prophecy, and know all mysteries and all knowledge, and if I have all faith, so as to remove mountains, but don’t have love, I am nothing]].

The Ephesian church may have become so focused on guarding against error that it grew suspicious, harsh, or cold. Vigilance for truth is good, and Christ commended it. But truth without love becomes lifeless. Jesus said that love would be the mark of His disciples (John 13:35).

The warning about removing the lampstand shows how seriously Christ takes this. A church without love cannot fulfill its purpose of shining His light. Yet the warning comes with an invitation. Christ does not simply condemn; He calls the church back. Repentance is always the path to renewal.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers and churches can drift into routine faith, doing the right things without the love that once moved them. This message invites them to remember what their love for Christ was like when it was fresh, and to return to Him.

Restoration begins with honest reflection. Believers can recall the joy and devotion they once had, recognize where love has cooled, and turn back to Christ in repentance. Then they can return to the practices that once nourished their love: time with God in prayer and Scripture, worship, generous service, and care for others.

Churches can also examine whether their commitment to truth is matched by love. Faithfulness to sound doctrine should lead to warmth, compassion, and kindness, not coldness or suspicion.

### Revelation 2:10 — Be Faithful to Death

verse: Revelation 2:10

#### Simple Explanation

To the suffering church in Smyrna, Christ gives no rebuke, only encouragement. He begins: "Don’t be afraid of the things which you are about to suffer." He does not promise that they will escape suffering. Instead, He tells them in advance what is coming and urges them not to fear.

He explains: "Behold, the devil is about to throw some of you into prison, that you may be tested." Behind human persecution stands a spiritual enemy. But even this testing is under God's control and has a limit: "you will have oppression for ten days." "Ten days" likely symbolizes a short, limited period, not an endless trial.

Then comes the command and promise: "Be faithful to death, and I will give you the crown of life." Some believers in Smyrna would face death for their faith. Christ calls them to remain faithful even if it costs their lives, and He promises them eternal life as their reward.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Smyrna was known for its loyalty to Rome and for a ring of beautiful buildings on the hill above the city, sometimes called "the crown of Smyrna." Athletes who won contests received crowns, or wreaths, as prizes. Christ promises a far greater crown: the crown of life, eternal life with Him.

This verse shows that suffering does not mean Christ has abandoned His people. The church in Smyrna was one of only two churches (with Philadelphia) that received no rebuke. Poverty and persecution are not signs of God's displeasure. Often they accompany faithfulness.

The limit of "ten days" reminds believers that their suffering is bounded by God's sovereignty. The devil can test, but only as far as God allows (Job 1–2). And no suffering on earth can compare with the glory to come (Romans 8:18).

Early Christian tradition records that Polycarp, a bishop of Smyrna in the second century, was martyred for refusing to deny Christ. Asked to curse Christ, he is reported to have replied that he had served Him for eighty-six years and that Christ had done him no wrong. His faithfulness is a living example of this verse.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many believers around the world today face persecution, imprisonment, and even death for their faith. This verse speaks directly to them, and it calls other believers to pray for and support them.

For those who do not face violent persecution, the verse still applies. Faithfulness may mean enduring mockery, loss of opportunity, rejection by family, or quiet hardship. Christ's word is the same: do not be afraid, and remain faithful.

This verse also reframes how believers view poverty and suffering. Those who are poor in the world's eyes may be rich in God's sight. Believers can find hope in knowing that their faithfulness, even in hidden struggles, is seen by Christ and will be rewarded with life.

### Revelation 2:17 — Hidden Manna and a New Name

verse: Revelation 2:17

#### Simple Explanation

To the overcomers in Pergamum, Christ makes three promises. First, "I will give of the hidden manna." Manna was the bread from heaven that fed Israel in the wilderness (Exodus 16). A jar of manna was kept in the ark of the covenant (Exodus 16:33–34; Hebrews 9:4). The believers in Pergamum were tempted to eat food sacrificed to idols at pagan feasts. Christ promises them a far better food: heavenly nourishment from Him.

Second, "I will give him a white stone." In the ancient world, white stones were used in several ways: as tokens of acquittal in a trial, as tickets of admission to special feasts, and as signs of victory. Any of these meanings would fit: the believer is declared innocent, welcomed to God's banquet, and honored as a victor.

Third, on the stone is "a new name written which no one knows but he who receives it." This new name represents a new identity given by Christ, a personal and intimate relationship known fully only to the one who receives it.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Jesus called Himself [[John 6:35|the bread of life]]. The hidden manna points to Christ Himself as the true nourishment for the soul. Believers who refuse the world's tempting feasts will be satisfied with something far greater.

The white stone and new name speak of acceptance and identity. Believers in Pergamum faced pressure to compromise in order to belong, to keep their place in society and their trades. Christ promises them a deeper belonging. Even if the world rejects them, He welcomes them and gives them a new name.

In Scripture, a new name often marks a new identity and calling, as when Abram became Abraham and Jacob became Israel. The new name given by Christ is personal. It suggests that each believer's relationship with Him is unique and precious, known intimately by the Lord who calls each one by name.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers today face many pressures to compromise in order to fit in, at work, in school, in social circles, or in public life. This promise reminds them that what Christ offers is far better than what compromise can give.

When believers feel rejected or overlooked for staying faithful, they can remember that Christ accepts them, honors them, and knows them by name. Their identity rests not on the approval of others but on His love.

This passage also invites believers to find their nourishment in Christ. Through His word, prayer, and fellowship with Him, they receive the hidden manna that truly satisfies the soul.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Revelation 2 shows that Christ knows His churches intimately. To each one He says, "I know your works." He sees their faithfulness and their failures, their suffering and their compromise. Nothing is hidden from Him, and His words are both searching and loving.

The message to Ephesus reminds believers that faithfulness to truth must be joined with love. A church or a believer can work hard and guard doctrine but still lose the love that once filled their hearts. Christ calls such believers to remember, repent, and return to their first love.

The message to Smyrna encourages those who suffer. Poverty and persecution do not mean God has abandoned His people. Christ calls them not to fear, but to be faithful even to death, and He promises them the crown of life.

The messages to Pergamum and Thyatira warn against compromise. Both churches had remarkable strengths: faithfulness under pressure and growing love and service. But both tolerated teaching that led believers into idolatry and immorality. Christ calls His people to reject such compromise, even when the pressure to fit in is strong. He promises hidden manna, a new name, authority with Him, and Himself as the morning star.

Each message ends with an invitation to listen: "He who has an ear, let him hear what the Spirit says to the assemblies." Believers today can listen, allowing Christ's words to encourage, correct, and strengthen them, so that they too may overcome.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, You walk among Your churches and know our works. Search my heart, and show me where my love for You has grown cold. Help me to remember, repent, and return to my first love. Thank You for those who suffer for Your name; give them courage not to be afraid and strength to remain faithful, even to death. Guard me from compromise with the world, from the pressure to fit in at the cost of faithfulness, and from any teaching that leads away from You. Nourish me with Your hidden manna, and let me rest in the new name You give. Let my love, faith, service, and endurance grow, and help me to hold fast to what I have until You come. Give me ears to hear what the Spirit says to the churches. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
