# 2 Peter 3
theme: The Promise of His Coming
main: In this second letter, Peter again stirs believers to remember the words of the prophets and the commandment of the Lord given through the apostles. He warns that in the last days scoffers will come, following their own desires and mocking, "Where is the promise of his coming?" claiming everything continues as it always has. But they deliberately forget that God created the heavens and earth by His word and that the world was once destroyed by the flood; by the same word, the present heavens and earth are reserved for fire and judgment. With the Lord, one day is like a thousand years and a thousand years like one day. He is not slow in keeping His promise but patient, not wanting anyone to perish but all to come to repentance. The day of the Lord will come like a thief, when the heavens will pass away and the earth and its works will be exposed. Since everything will be dissolved, believers should live holy and godly lives as they wait for and hasten the day of God, looking for new heavens and a new earth where righteousness dwells. They should strive to be found at peace, spotless and blameless, regarding the Lord's patience as salvation, as Paul also wrote, though some twist his letters as they twist other Scriptures. Peter closes by warning them to guard against error and to grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ.

## Chapter Background and Story

The false teachers described in chapter 2 apparently mocked the idea that Christ would return. Perhaps they argued that since the first generation of believers was dying and Christ had not come, the promise must be false. If there was no return, there would be no judgment, and so there would be no reason to live a holy life. Their denial of the second coming supported their immoral lifestyle.

Peter answers this challenge directly. He shows that God has acted in history before and will act again. He explains that God's timing is different from human timing and that His apparent delay is actually an expression of His patience and mercy. And he describes the glorious future God has promised: a new heavens and a new earth where righteousness is at home.

This chapter is Peter's final word. It ends not with fear but with a call to growth and a burst of praise to Jesus Christ.

### Remember the Words (3:1–2)

[[v1|This is now, beloved, the second letter that I have written to you; and in both of them I stir up your sincere mind by reminding you]] [[v2|that you should remember the words which were spoken before by the holy prophets and the commandment of us, the apostles of the Lord and Savior]]. The "second letter" suggests 1 Peter was the first. Peter wants his readers to remember two sources of truth: the Old Testament prophets and the teaching of the apostles, which carries the authority of the Lord Jesus. Together, these form the foundation of the faith (Ephesians 2:20).

The same word that created and judged the world in the past has reserved the present world for a future judgment. ## Important Verses Explained

### 2 Peter 3:3–4 — Where Is the Promise of His Coming?

verse: 2 Peter 3:3-4

#### Simple Explanation

"Knowing this first, that in the last days mockers will come." "The last days" refers to the whole period between Christ's first and second coming. Peter warns that scoffers will appear, people who ridicule God's promises.

"Walking after their own lusts." Their mockery is connected to their lifestyle. They want to live according to their own desires, and denying Christ's return removes any sense of accountability.

Verse 4: "and saying, 'Where is the promise of his coming?'" They mock the promise of Christ's return, pointing out that it has not happened yet.

"For, from the day that the fathers fell asleep, all things continue as they were from the beginning of the creation." "The fathers" may refer to the first generation of Christians who had died, or to the patriarchs of Israel. The scoffers argued that the world goes on as it always has; there is no sign of divine intervention, so there is no reason to expect it in the future.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The scoffers' argument is essentially that the world is a closed system where nothing changes and God does not intervene. Peter shows that this assumption ignores history. God created the world, and God judged the world in the flood. He has acted decisively before, and He will act again.

There is also a moral dimension. Peter notes that the scoffers follow "their own lusts." People who do not want to be accountable to God often find reasons to deny His involvement in the world. Unbelief is sometimes driven not by evidence but by desire.

Scoffing at Christ's return has continued throughout history, and it remains common today. Many people dismiss the idea that Jesus will return. Peter's words help believers understand that such mockery was predicted and does not disprove God's promise.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

When believers encounter skepticism or mockery about Christ's return, they need not be shaken. Peter warned that scoffers would come. Their presence actually confirms Scripture's reliability.

Believers can also examine their own hearts. Do they live as though Christ will return? Or have they, perhaps unconsciously, begun to assume that life will simply go on as it always has? Remembering Christ's promised return can shape how they live.

Finally, believers can respond to skeptics with gentleness and respect, explaining the hope they have (1 Peter 3:15), rather than with defensiveness or anger.

### 2 Peter 3:8–9 — The Lord Is Patient

verse: 2 Peter 3:8-9

#### Simple Explanation

"But don’t forget this one thing, beloved, that one day is with the Lord as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day." Peter draws on Psalm 90:4. God does not experience time as humans do. He is eternal. What seems like a long delay to people is not a delay to Him. A thousand years is like a single day in His sight.

Verse 9: "The Lord is not slow concerning his promise, as some count slowness." God is not late. He is not failing to keep His word. People may think He is slow, but they are measuring Him by human standards.

"But he is patient with us." The real reason for the apparent delay is God's patience.

"Not wishing that anyone should perish, but that all should come to repentance." God is giving people time to turn to Him. He does not want anyone to be destroyed. He wants everyone to repent and be saved.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses transform how believers understand the waiting period before Christ's return. What looks like delay is actually mercy. Every day that passes is another opportunity for people to repent and come to God. This echoes 1 Timothy 2:4, which says God desires all people to be saved, and Ezekiel 33:11, where God says He takes no pleasure in the death of the wicked.

God's patience has a purpose, but it is not unlimited. The day of the Lord will come (v10). God's patience is an invitation to respond now, not a reason to delay.

As discussed at 1 Timothy 2:4, Christians understand God's desire for all to be saved in relation to His sovereignty in different ways. But all agree that God's heart is merciful, that His invitation is genuine, and that His patience gives people real opportunity to repent.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers who wonder why Christ has not yet returned can find the answer here: God is patient, giving people time to repent. This perspective can transform impatience into gratitude and mission.

Believers can use this time well, sharing the gospel with those who do not yet know Christ, so that more people will come to repentance.

For those who have not yet turned to God, these verses are an invitation. God's patience is giving them time, but that time will not last forever. Today is the day to respond.

### 2 Peter 3:10–13 — New Heavens and a New Earth

verse: 2 Peter 3:10-13

#### Simple Explanation

"But the day of the Lord will come as a thief in the night." The day of the Lord, the day of Christ's return and God's final judgment, will come suddenly and unexpectedly, as Jesus Himself said (Matthew 24:43–44; 1 Thessalonians 5:2).

"In which the heavens will pass away with a great noise, and the elements will be dissolved with fervent heat; and the earth and the works that are in it will be burned up." Peter describes cosmic upheaval. "The elements" may mean the basic components of the physical world, or the heavenly bodies. Some ancient manuscripts read "the earth and the works that are in it will be found," or "laid bare," meaning exposed before God's judgment.

Verse 11: "Therefore, since all these things will be destroyed like this, what kind of people ought you to be in holy living and godliness." The prospect of this day should shape how believers live now. Since this world is temporary, believers should live holy and godly lives.

Verse 12: "looking for and earnestly desiring the coming of the day of God." Believers eagerly anticipate this day. The word translated "earnestly desiring" can also mean "hastening." Believers may in some way hasten Christ's return through holy living, prayer, and spreading the gospel (Matthew 24:14).

Verse 13: "But, according to his promise, we look for new heavens and a new earth, in which righteousness dwells." The goal is not destruction for its own sake, but renewal. God has promised new heavens and a new earth (Isaiah 65:17; 66:22; Revelation 21:1). In this new creation, righteousness will be at home. Evil, injustice, and suffering will be gone.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Christians have understood these verses in different ways. Some believe the present universe will be completely destroyed and replaced with an entirely new creation. Others believe the fire describes a purifying judgment that will cleanse and renew the existing creation, much as the flood judged the world without annihilating the earth. They point to Romans 8:19–22, which speaks of creation itself being set free from decay, and to the reading "laid bare" in verse 10. Both views agree that the present world, as it is now, marred by sin, will come to an end, and that God will bring a new creation where righteousness dwells.

The phrase "in which righteousness dwells" describes the heart of the new creation. It will be a world where everything is right: relationships with God, with one another, and with creation itself. This is the fulfillment of every longing for justice and peace.

The connection between future hope and present living is central. Because believers know where history is heading, they are called to live holy lives now. Hope is not escapism; it is motivation.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can hold the things of this world loosely, knowing that they are temporary. Possessions, achievements, and status will not last. What lasts is a life of holiness and godliness.

Believers can also look forward with hope to the new creation. Every injustice, sorrow, and suffering will be made right. This hope can sustain them through hard times.

Finally, believers can live in a way that "hastens" Christ's return: praying for His kingdom to come, sharing the gospel, and living holy lives that reflect the righteousness of the world to come.

### 2 Peter 3:18 — Grow in Grace and Knowledge

verse: 2 Peter 3:18

#### Simple Explanation

Peter's final instruction is simple and profound. "But grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ."

"Grow." The Christian life is not static. Believers are meant to keep growing, maturing, and deepening.

"In the grace." Believers grow in their experience and understanding of God's undeserved favor. They grow in receiving grace and in extending it to others.

"And knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ." They grow in personal knowledge of Jesus, knowing Him more deeply, not just knowing about Him.

"To him be the glory both now and forever. Amen." Peter ends with praise to Jesus Christ. Glory belongs to Him, now and in eternity.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse is the antidote to false teaching. The false teachers twisted grace into an excuse for sin and claimed a false knowledge. Peter calls believers to grow in true grace and true knowledge. Growth in grace and knowledge protects believers from being carried away by error (v17).

Notice that Peter ends his letter, and perhaps his life's writing, with praise to Jesus. Glory is given to Christ "both now and forever," literally "to the day of eternity." This doxology, directed to Christ, shows that Peter worshiped Jesus as God. The fisherman who once denied Jesus now gives Him eternal glory.

This verse also echoes the beginning of the letter, which spoke of "grace" and "knowledge" (2 Peter 1:2–3). The whole letter is framed by this call: know Christ more, and grow in His grace.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can ask whether they are growing. Are they knowing Christ more deeply? Are they experiencing and extending His grace more fully? Growth comes through Scripture, prayer, worship, fellowship, service, and obedience.

Growth is a lifelong process. No matter how long believers have followed Christ, there is always more of His grace to experience and more of Him to know.

Finally, believers can make Peter's closing words their own, giving glory to Jesus Christ now and forever. Worship is both the goal and the source of growth.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Second Peter 3 addresses those who mock the promise of Christ's return. Scoffers claim that nothing ever changes, so there is no reason to expect God to intervene. But they forget that God created the world by His word and judged it through the flood. The same word has reserved the present world for judgment. Believers need not be shaken by such mockery; Peter predicted it.

The chapter explains why Christ has not yet returned. With the Lord, a day is like a thousand years. He is not slow but patient, not wanting anyone to perish but all to come to repentance. Every day is an expression of His mercy and an opportunity for people to turn to Him. Believers can use this time to share the gospel, and those who have not yet turned to God can respond today.

The day of the Lord will come suddenly, like a thief. The present world will come to an end, and God will bring new heavens and a new earth where righteousness dwells. Christians understand the details in different ways, but all share this hope. And this hope shapes how believers live: in holiness and godliness, at peace, without spot or blame, eagerly looking forward to the day of God.

Finally, Peter warns believers to guard against error and to grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. The best protection against false teaching is growing in the true knowledge of Christ. And Peter's last word is praise: to Jesus be the glory, now and forever.
