# 1 Corinthians 1
theme: The Foolishness of the Cross and the Folly of Division
main: Paul greets the church in Corinth as people made holy in Christ, thanks God for His grace and gifts among them, and assures them that the faithful God will keep them to the end. Then he confronts the quarrels that have split the church into factions around favorite leaders, asking whether Christ is divided. He explains that the message of the cross looks foolish to the world but is the power of God for those being saved. God deliberately chose the weak and lowly to shame the proud, so that no one may boast before Him, and Christ Himself has become their wisdom, righteousness, holiness, and redemption.

## Chapter Background and Story

Corinth was a proud city. It had been destroyed by the Romans in 146 B.C. and rebuilt by Julius Caesar in 44 B.C. as a Roman colony. By Paul's time, it was booming, filled with people seeking wealth, status, and advancement. Corinthians admired eloquent speakers, philosophers, and people of influence. This culture of status-seeking had seeped into the church. Believers were dividing into factions, each claiming a favorite teacher, and some were proud of their spiritual gifts and knowledge.

Paul writes to correct these attitudes. His central answer is the cross of Christ. The cross overturns the world's values: it shows God's power in weakness, His wisdom in what seems foolish, and His choice of the lowly rather than the proud. If believers understood the cross, they would stop boasting and stop dividing.

### Greeting and Thanksgiving (1:1–9)

Paul writes as [[v1|called to be an apostle of Jesus Christ through the will of God]], together with [[v1|our brother Sosthenes]], who may be the synagogue leader mentioned in Acts 18:17. He addresses the church as [[v2|those who are sanctified in Christ Jesus, called saints]]. Despite their many problems, the Corinthian believers were "saints," people set apart for God, because of their union with Christ. Holiness is first God's gift, then a calling to live out.

Paul thanks God for the grace given to them. [[v5|that in everything you were enriched in him, in all speech and all knowledge]]. They were richly gifted. [[v7|so that you come behind in no gift, waiting for the revelation of our Lord Jesus Christ]]. The Corinthians had many spiritual gifts, which Paul will discuss in chapters 12–14. But these gifts were meant to be used while waiting for Christ's return, not as grounds for pride. Paul's confidence in God's faithfulness is studied below.

### Divisions in the Church (1:10–17)

Paul's appeal for unity is studied below. He had heard [[v11|by those who are from Chloe’s household, that there are contentions among you]]. Chloe was probably a woman with a household that included servants or family members who traveled between Corinth and Ephesus.

The divisions: [[v12|“I follow Paul,” “I follow Apollos,” “I follow Cephas,” and, “I follow Christ.”]] Some were loyal to Paul, who founded the church. Some preferred Apollos, the eloquent teacher from Alexandria (Acts 18:24–28). Some followed Cephas (Peter), perhaps Jewish believers who valued his connection to Jesus and the Jerusalem church. And some claimed to follow "Christ" alone, perhaps in a proud way that looked down on all human leaders.

Paul responds with sharp questions: [[v13|Is Christ divided? Was Paul crucified for you? Or were you baptized into the name of Paul?]] Christ cannot be divided into pieces. No human leader died for them. They were baptized into Christ's name, not anyone else's.

Paul is glad he baptized only a few of them, such as Crispus, Gaius, and the household of Stephanas, so that no one could claim to have been baptized into Paul's name. [[v17|For Christ sent me not to baptize, but to preach the Good News—not in wisdom of words, so that the cross of Christ wouldn’t be made void]]. Paul does not belittle baptism; he simply emphasizes that his main calling was to preach. And he refused to rely on clever rhetoric, because that would distract from the power of the cross.

### The Message of the Cross (1:18–25)

Paul's statement about the power of the cross is studied below. He quotes Isaiah 29:14: [[v19|I will destroy the wisdom of the wise. I will bring the discernment of the discerning to nothing]]. [[v20|Where is the wise? Where is the scribe? Where is the debater of this age? Hasn’t God made foolish the wisdom of this world?]] The great thinkers of the world, with all their wisdom, could not find God. [[v21|For seeing that in the wisdom of God, the world through its wisdom didn’t know God, it was God’s good pleasure through the foolishness of the preaching to save those who believe]].

[[v22|For Jews ask for signs, Greeks seek after wisdom]], [[v23|but we preach Christ crucified, a stumbling block to Jews and foolishness to Greeks]]. Many Jews wanted miraculous proof of God's power; a crucified Messiah seemed to contradict everything they expected, since Scripture said that anyone hanged on a tree was cursed (Deuteronomy 21:23). Many Greeks valued philosophy and eloquence; the idea that God would save the world through a crucified Jewish carpenter seemed absurd. Yet [[v24|but to those who are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ is the power of God and the wisdom of God]]. [[v25|because the foolishness of God is wiser than men, and the weakness of God is stronger than men]]. What looks foolish and weak in God's ways is far wiser and stronger than the best of human wisdom and power.

### God Chose the Lowly (1:26–31)

Paul's reminder of their calling is studied below. He concludes: [[v30|Because of him, you are in Christ Jesus, who was made to us wisdom from God, and righteousness and sanctification, and redemption]]. Everything believers need is found in Christ. He is their "wisdom," the true wisdom that comes from God. He is their "righteousness," the right standing they have before God. He is their "sanctification," the holiness they are growing into. And He is their "redemption," their full and final deliverance. [[v31|that, as it is written, “He who boasts, let him boast in the Lord.”]] Paul quotes Jeremiah 9:24. Since everything comes from God, the only proper boasting is in Him.

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 Corinthians 1:9 — God Is Faithful

verse: 1 Corinthians 1:9

#### Simple Explanation

Paul began his letter to the Corinthians with thanksgiving. Despite all the problems he was about to address, including divisions, immorality, lawsuits, and confusion in worship, he thanked God for the grace He had given them. They had been enriched with spiritual gifts. And Paul expressed confidence that Christ [[v8|will also confirm you until the end, blameless in the day of our Lord Jesus Christ]].

How could Paul be so confident about such a troubled church? His answer: [[v9|God is faithful]]. Paul's hope for the Corinthians did not rest on their performance, which was often poor. It rested on God's faithfulness. God keeps His promises. He finishes what He starts.

[[v9|through whom you were called into the fellowship of his Son, Jesus Christ our Lord]]. God had called them into "fellowship" with His Son. The Greek word koinonia means sharing, partnership, or communion. Believers share life with Christ. They are united with Him, partners in His life, His sufferings, and His glory.

This fellowship was God's initiative. He "called" them. And the God who called them would be faithful to keep them. Paul writes elsewhere, [[1 Thessalonians 5:24|He who calls you is faithful, who will also do it]].

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that the security of believers rests on God's character, not their own. The Corinthian church was deeply flawed, yet Paul was confident that God would keep them to the end. God's faithfulness is greater than human failure. Paul writes, [[2 Timothy 2:13|If we are faithless, he remains faithful; for he can’t deny himself]].

It also shows that salvation is relational. Believers are called into fellowship with Jesus Christ. The Christian life is not merely believing correct ideas or following rules; it is a shared life with the Son of God.

Finally, the verse shows Paul's pastoral wisdom. Before correcting the Corinthians, he reminded them of God's grace and faithfulness. Correction is easier to receive when it comes in the context of hope and affirmation.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse brings assurance to believers who feel weak or who have failed. Their hope does not rest on their own strength but on God's faithfulness. He will keep them to the end.

It also invites believers to enjoy fellowship with Christ. Believers have been called into a shared life with Jesus. They can spend time with Him in prayer, Scripture, and worship, and they can share in His life throughout the day.

Finally, the verse shows a healthy way to approach difficult situations in the church. Before addressing problems, believers can remember and give thanks for God's grace at work in others. This sets correction within the framework of hope.

### 1 Corinthians 1:10 — No Divisions Among You

verse: 1 Corinthians 1:10

#### Simple Explanation

After his opening thanksgiving, Paul turns to the first major problem in the Corinthian church: divisions. He had heard from members of Chloe's household that the believers were quarreling, splitting into groups around different leaders.

[[v10|Now I beg you, brothers, through the name of our Lord, Jesus Christ]]. Paul makes his appeal with great seriousness, "through the name of our Lord." The name of Christ, in which all believers were baptized and which unites them, is the basis of his plea. He calls them "brothers," reminding them that they are one family.

[[v10|that you all speak the same thing]]. Paul wants them to agree, to speak with one voice rather than shouting competing slogans.

[[v10|and that there be no divisions among you]]. The word "divisions" (schismata) describes a tear in a garment. The church was being torn apart. Paul wants this tearing to stop.

[[v10|but that you be perfected together in the same mind and in the same judgment]]. "Perfected together" was used of mending torn nets or setting broken bones. Paul wants the church to be repaired, restored to wholeness, sharing the same mind and purpose.

This does not mean believers must agree on every opinion. In Romans 14 and 1 Corinthians 8–10, Paul allows for differences in matters of conscience. But they should be united in their commitment to Christ, in their understanding of the gospel, and in their love for one another.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that unity is essential to the church. Jesus prayed that His followers would be one (John 17:21). Paul writes, [[Ephesians 4:3|being eager to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace]]. Division damages the church's witness and grieves the heart of Christ.

It also shows that divisions often come from pride. The Corinthians were exalting human leaders, each group claiming to be better than the others. Paul's answer throughout this chapter is the cross, which humbles all human pride.

Finally, the verse shows that unity is something believers must work toward. Paul uses the image of mending. Unity is not automatic; it requires effort, humility, and a shared focus on Christ.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse challenges believers to examine whether they contribute to division in their churches. Believers can be tempted to form groups around favorite pastors, teachers, styles, or opinions. Paul calls them to focus on Christ, who unites them.

It also encourages believers to work for reconciliation. When relationships in the church are torn, believers can seek to mend them through humility, forgiveness, and honest conversation.

Finally, the verse reminds believers that unity is rooted in Christ. Believers do not create unity by agreeing on everything; they preserve unity by remaining centered on Jesus and His gospel.

### 1 Corinthians 1:18 — The Word of the Cross

verse: 1 Corinthians 1:18

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had just said that Christ sent him to preach the gospel, "not in wisdom of words, so that the cross of Christ wouldn't be made void." The Corinthians admired eloquent speakers and impressive philosophy. Paul refused to rely on such things because he did not want to distract from the cross.

[[v18|For the word of the cross is foolishness to those who are dying]]. "The word of the cross" is the message that Jesus Christ was crucified for sinners. To many people, this message seems foolish. In the Roman world, crucifixion was the most shameful form of execution, reserved for slaves, rebels, and criminals. It was considered so horrible that polite Romans avoided even mentioning it. The idea that a crucified man could be the Savior of the world, or that God would save people through such a death, seemed absurd.

Notice the phrase "those who are dying." People who reject the message of the cross are on the path to destruction. They see the cross as foolishness because they do not understand their need for it.

[[v18|but to us who are being saved it is the power of God]]. To those who believe, the cross is not foolish but powerful. Through the cross, God saves people, forgives sins, defeats evil, and brings new life. Notice the present tense, "being saved." Salvation is a reality believers have now and are continuing to experience until its completion.

The same message produces opposite responses. To some it is foolishness; to others it is God's power. The difference is not in the message but in how people receive it.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that the cross is central to the Christian faith. Paul writes later in this letter, [[1 Corinthians 2:2|For I determined not to know anything among you, except Jesus Christ and him crucified]]. The cross is not a side issue; it is the heart of the gospel.

It also shows that God's ways often contradict human expectations. Humans expect power to look like strength, victory to look like triumph, and wisdom to look like cleverness. God revealed His power through weakness and His wisdom through what seemed foolish.

Finally, the verse shows that the gospel's power is not in human presentation. Paul did not rely on eloquence or clever arguments. The power is in the message itself, because it is God's power.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to keep the cross at the center of their faith and witness. In a world that values success, strength, and self-sufficiency, the message of a crucified Savior may seem foolish. Believers can proclaim it with confidence, knowing that it is God's power.

It also helps believers understand why some people reject the gospel. To those who are perishing, the cross seems foolish. Believers can pray that God would open their eyes to see its power.

Finally, the verse encourages believers to rely on the gospel's power rather than their own cleverness. Believers do not need to be eloquent or persuasive to share their faith. God works through the simple message of Christ crucified.

### 1 Corinthians 1:27–29 — God Chose the Foolish and the Weak

verse: 1 Corinthians 1:27-29

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been explaining that the message of the cross seems foolish to the world but is the power of God. Now he asks the Corinthians to look at themselves as evidence of this truth.

[[v26|For you see your calling, brothers, that not many are wise according to the flesh, not many mighty, and not many noble]]. When God called the Corinthian believers, He did not mainly choose the educated, powerful, or aristocratic. Some believers in Corinth did have status, such as Crispus the synagogue ruler and Erastus the city treasurer. But "not many" did. Most were ordinary people, and many were slaves or freed slaves.

[[v27|but God chose the foolish things of the world that he might put to shame those who are wise. God chose the weak things of the world that he might put to shame the things that are strong]]. God deliberately chose those whom the world considered foolish and weak. Why? To shame the wise and the strong, to show that their wisdom and strength cannot save them.

[[v28|God chose the lowly things of the world, and the things that are despised, and the things that don’t exist, that he might bring to nothing the things that exist]]. God chose those who were looked down on, ignored, even regarded as nothing, in order to overturn the world's values.

[[v29|that no flesh should boast before God]]. This is the purpose: so that no one can boast. If God had chosen only the wise, powerful, and noble, they might think they deserved salvation. By choosing the lowly, God shows that salvation is entirely His gift.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show God's pattern throughout Scripture. He chose Abraham, an elderly man without children; Jacob, the younger son; Joseph, a slave; Moses, a man who could not speak well; David, the youngest shepherd boy; Mary, a young woman from an unimportant town. Jesus Himself was born in a stable and grew up in Nazareth. God consistently works through the unlikely.

They also show that the gospel humbles human pride. No one can say, "God chose me because I was smart, strong, or important." Salvation is grace from beginning to end. Paul writes, [[Ephesians 2:9|not of works, that no one would boast]].

Finally, these verses bring dignity to the lowly. The world may despise the poor, the uneducated, and the powerless. But God chooses them and uses them for His glory. In God's kingdom, the last are first.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers who feel insignificant. Many believers think they have little to offer because they lack education, wealth, or status. God delights to use such people. Their weakness is not a barrier but an opportunity for God's power.

They also challenge believers who are proud of their achievements. Education, wealth, and influence are not grounds for boasting before God. Believers with these gifts can use them humbly, recognizing that everything they have comes from Him.

Finally, these verses call believers to value people the world overlooks. Churches can honor the poor, the uneducated, and the marginalized, recognizing that God chooses and uses them. Believers can resist the temptation to favor the wealthy and influential.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

1 Corinthians 1 reminds believers that their security rests on God's faithfulness. Despite the Corinthians' many problems, Paul was confident that God would keep them to the end, because God is faithful. Believers who feel weak or have failed can rest in the faithfulness of the God who called them into fellowship with His Son.

The chapter also confronts division in the church. The Corinthians were splitting into factions around favorite leaders. Paul asks, "Is Christ divided?" Believers today can examine whether they are contributing to division by elevating leaders, preferences, or groups above Christ, and they can work to mend broken relationships.

1 Corinthians 1 places the cross at the center of the Christian faith. The message of Christ crucified seems foolish to the world, but it is the power of God for those being saved. Believers can proclaim the cross with confidence, relying not on their own eloquence but on the power of God's message.

Finally, the chapter shows that God chooses the lowly to shame the proud, so that no one can boast before Him. Christ Himself is believers' wisdom, righteousness, holiness, and redemption. Believers can lay aside all pride, find their worth in Christ alone, honor those the world overlooks, and boast only in the Lord.

## Chapter Prayer

Faithful God, thank You for calling me into fellowship with Your Son, Jesus Christ. Thank You that my hope does not rest on my own strength but on Your faithfulness, and that You will keep me to the end. Forgive me for the times I have contributed to division in Your church by exalting people, preferences, or groups above Christ. Make me a peacemaker who helps mend what is torn. Thank You for the message of the cross, which seems foolish to the world but is Your power to save. Help me never to be ashamed of it. Thank You that You chose the weak and lowly, and that You chose me, not because of anything in me, but because of Your grace. Lord Jesus, You are my wisdom, righteousness, holiness, and redemption. Let me boast only in You. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
