# John 15
theme: The True Vine: Remaining in Christ, Loving as Friends, Enduring the World's Hatred
main: Jesus describes Himself as the true vine, His Father as the farmer, and His disciples as branches. Fruitfulness comes only by remaining in Him, and the Father prunes fruitful branches so that they bear more. Jesus calls His disciples friends, commands them to love one another with the self-giving love He is about to show on the cross, and prepares them for the hatred of a world that hated Him first, promising that the Spirit of truth will testify about Him through them.

## Chapter Background and Story

John 14 ended with Jesus saying, "Arise, let's go from here" (14:31). Chapters 15–17 may have been spoken as Jesus and the disciples walked from the upper room through Jerusalem toward the Kidron Valley and the Mount of Olives, perhaps passing vineyards on the slopes, or the great golden vine that decorated the entrance to the temple sanctuary, a symbol of Israel. Or Jesus may have continued speaking before they left the room. Either way, the image of the vine would have been vivid to the disciples.

The chapter moves through three relationships: the disciples' relationship with Jesus (abiding in the vine), their relationship with one another (love as friends), and their relationship with the world (hatred and witness).

### The Vine and the Branches (15:1–8)

Jesus said, [[v1|I am the true vine, and my Father is the farmer]]. This is the seventh and final "I am" saying in John's Gospel. In the Old Testament, Israel was often pictured as God's vine or vineyard (Psalm 80:8–16; Isaiah 5:1–7; Jeremiah 2:21). God planted it with care and looked for good fruit, but too often it produced wild grapes. Jesus calls Himself the "true" vine. He is the faithful Israel, the One who perfectly bears the fruit God desires, and all who are joined to Him become part of God's true people. The Father's care for the vine is studied below.

[[v3|You are already pruned clean because of the word which I have spoken to you]]. The Greek word for "pruned" and "clean" is the same word. Through Jesus' teaching and their relationship with Him, the disciples had already been cleansed.

Jesus then called them to remain in Him, studied below. He warned, [[v6|If a man doesn’t remain in me, he is thrown out as a branch and is withered; and they gather them, throw them into the fire, and they are burned]]. Vine wood is useless for building or carving (Ezekiel 15:2–5). A branch that is cut off is good only for burning. This sobering warning shows that a mere outward connection to Jesus, without true union with Him, ends in judgment. Judas, who had walked with Jesus and then left, is a sobering example.

Jesus promised, [[v7|If you remain in me, and my words remain in you, you will ask whatever you desire, and it will be done for you]]. Those who remain in Him, and in whom His words remain, will desire what He desires. Their prayers will be shaped by His will. [[v8|In this my Father is glorified, that you bear much fruit; and so you will be my disciples]]. Fruitfulness is the evidence of true discipleship.

### Remaining in His Love (15:9–17)

Jesus spoke of the depth of His love: [[v9|Even as the Father has loved me, I also have loved you. Remain in my love]]. The love Jesus has for His disciples is the same love the Father has for Him. They are invited to remain, to live, in that love.

How do they remain in His love? [[v10|If you keep my commandments, you will remain in my love, even as I have kept my Father’s commandments and remain in his love]]. Obedience is not the way to earn Jesus' love; it is the way to live within it. Jesus Himself lived in obedience to the Father, and that obedience was an expression of their loving relationship.

[[v11|I have spoken these things to you, that my joy may remain in you, and that your joy may be made full]]. Jesus' commands are not meant to burden His disciples but to fill them with His joy.

He repeated the command He had given in John 13:34: [[v12|This is my commandment, that you love one another, even as I have loved you]]. Then He spoke of His friendship with them, studied below.

[[v16|You didn’t choose me, but I chose you and appointed you, that you should go and bear fruit, and that your fruit should remain]]. In Jesus' day, disciples usually chose which rabbi to follow. Jesus reversed this. He chose them, and He appointed them for a purpose: to go and bear lasting fruit. The disciples' mission flowed from His initiative.

### The World's Hatred (15:18–25)

Jesus then prepared the disciples for opposition, studied below. He reminded them of what He had said after washing their feet: [[v20|A servant is not greater than his lord]]. If the world persecuted Jesus, it would persecute them. If it kept His word, it would keep theirs.

The world's hatred of Jesus was not caused by ignorance alone. Jesus said that if He had not come and spoken to them and done works no one else had done, they would not be guilty of this sin. But now they had seen and hated both Him and His Father. [[v23|He who hates me, hates my Father also]]. This fulfilled the Scripture, [[v25|They hated me without a cause]], words found in Psalms 35:19 and 69:4, where David described the unjust hostility of his enemies. Jesus, the Son of David, experienced the same.

### The Witness of the Spirit and the Disciples (15:26–27)

Jesus closed this section with a promise. The Counselor, the Spirit of truth whom He would send from the Father, [[v26|will testify about me]]. The disciples too would testify, [[v27|because you have been with me from the beginning]]. The Spirit would bear witness to Jesus through the disciples' witness. They were not left to defend Jesus in their own strength. The Spirit would work with them and through them.

## Important Verses Explained

### John 15:1–2 — The Father Prunes the Branches

verse: John 15:1-2

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus was speaking to His disciples on the night before His crucifixion. He used an image they all understood: a vineyard. Vineyards covered the hillsides of Israel, and grapes were one of the most important crops. Every Israelite knew how much care a vineyard required.

[[v1|I am the true vine, and my Father is the farmer]]. Jesus is the vine, the main stem through which life flows. The Father is the farmer, the vinedresser who tends the vine with skill and care.

[[v2|Every branch in me that doesn’t bear fruit, he takes away]]. A vinedresser removes branches that are dead or unfruitful. Some interpreters understand "takes away" as describing those who seem to be connected to Jesus but have no real life in Him, like Judas. Others note that the Greek word can also mean "lifts up," and suggest that the vinedresser lifts fallen branches off the ground so that they can receive sunlight and become fruitful. Both readings show the farmer's active care for the vine.

[[v2|Every branch that bears fruit, he prunes, that it may bear more fruit]]. Pruning involves cutting back healthy branches, removing shoots that would drain energy without producing fruit. A pruned vine may look bare and wounded for a time, but it produces a much better harvest. Skilled vinedressers prune heavily because they know what the vine can become.

This verse shows that the Father is deeply involved in the lives of believers. He is not a distant observer but an attentive vinedresser, working to make His people more fruitful.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The image of pruning helps believers understand some of the painful experiences of life. God sometimes uses difficulties, discipline, and loss to remove what hinders growth. Hebrews teaches that God disciplines His children [[Hebrews 12:10|for our profit, that we may be partakers of his holiness]]. Pruning is not punishment but loving care with a purpose.

It also shows that fruitful believers are not exempt from pruning. In fact, it is the fruitful branches that are pruned. God's work in a believer's life is never finished. Even those who are growing will experience His shaping, so that they bear more fruit.

Finally, this image reveals God's goal: fruit. Throughout Scripture, "fruit" refers to the evidence of a life connected to God, including Christlike character (Galatians 5:22–23), good works (Colossians 1:10), and the influence that brings others to faith. The Father's work in His people is directed toward a life that reflects His goodness.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse helps believers interpret seasons of difficulty. Not every hardship is God's pruning; some suffering comes from the brokenness of the world, from the wrongdoing of others, or from evil that God hates. But God can use painful experiences to shape His people. When believers face loss, disappointment, or difficult change, they can ask how God might be using it to make them more fruitful.

It also encourages believers to trust the Vinedresser. Pruning is painful, and it is not always clear why it is happening. But the Father is skilled and loving. He knows what He is doing and has a good purpose.

Finally, this verse invites believers to welcome God's shaping. Sometimes believers cling to habits, attitudes, or priorities that drain their energy without producing fruit. They can invite God to remove what hinders their growth, trusting that His pruning leads to a fuller life.

### John 15:5 — Apart from Me You Can Do Nothing

verse: John 15:5

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus continued the image of the vine and branches. He had already told the disciples to remain in Him, as a branch remains in the vine (15:4). Now He made the picture explicit: [[v5|I am the vine. You are the branches]].

A branch draws everything it needs from the vine: water, nutrients, and life. A branch does not produce fruit by trying hard; it bears fruit by staying connected. The fruit is the natural result of the vine's life flowing through it.

[[v5|He who remains in me and I in him bears much fruit]]. The word "remains" (sometimes translated "abides") is a key word in this chapter, used many times. To remain in Jesus means to stay connected to Him in a continuing relationship of trust, love, and obedience. It is not a one-time decision but an ongoing life. Notice the mutual relationship: "remains in me and I in him." The believer lives in Christ, and Christ lives in the believer.

The promise is "much fruit." Jesus does not promise a small or barely-there result. Those who remain in Him will be fruitful.

[[v5|for apart from me you can do nothing]]. This is a strong statement. A branch cut off from the vine cannot produce even one grape. In the same way, believers cannot produce spiritual fruit apart from Christ. They may accomplish many things in human strength, but nothing of eternal value comes without Him.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse reveals the secret of the Christian life: dependence on Christ. The Christian life is not primarily about trying harder but about staying closer. Paul expressed the same truth: [[Galatians 2:20|it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me]]. The life of Christ flows into believers through their union with Him.

It also humbles human pride. People like to think of themselves as self-sufficient. Jesus says that apart from Him, His disciples can do nothing. This is not an insult but a liberating truth. Believers do not have to produce fruit by their own strength. They need to stay connected to the One who does.

Finally, the verse shows that union with Christ is personal and mutual. Believers are "in Christ," and Christ is "in" them. This union is made real by the Holy Spirit, who lives in believers and joins them to Christ.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to prioritize their relationship with Jesus. Remaining in Him involves practices such as prayer, reading and meditating on Scripture, worship, obedience, and fellowship with other believers. These are not ways to earn His love but ways to stay connected to the source of life.

It also brings relief to weary believers. Many Christians feel pressure to produce results through their own effort. Jesus' words remind them that fruit comes from Him. When believers feel empty, the answer is not simply to work harder but to return to Christ and draw on His life.

Finally, this verse challenges self-reliance. Believers may be tempted to rely on their own talents, plans, or strength in ministry, work, or family life. Jesus calls them to depend on Him in everything. Even the smallest act of faithful service bears fruit when it flows from union with Him.

### John 15:13–15 — Greater Love and True Friendship

verse: John 15:13-15

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus had just repeated His command that His disciples love one another as He had loved them. Then He described the greatest form of love: [[v13|Greater love has no one than this, that someone lay down his life for his friends]]. There is no greater proof of love than giving one's life for another.

Within hours, Jesus would demonstrate this love on the cross. He was not speaking theoretically. He was describing what He was about to do for these very disciples.

[[v14|You are my friends if you do whatever I command you]]. Friendship with Jesus shows itself in obedience. This is not a condition for earning friendship but a description of what His friends are like. Those who are truly His friends follow His teaching.

[[v15|No longer do I call you servants, for the servant doesn’t know what his lord does]]. A servant receives orders without explanation. He does his master's will without being told the master's plans.

[[v15|But I have called you friends, for everything that I heard from my Father, I have made known to you]]. Jesus had shared His heart and His Father's purposes with them. He had brought them into His confidence. They were not merely servants but friends.

In the Old Testament, only a few were called God's friends, such as Abraham (Isaiah 41:8; James 2:23) and Moses, with whom God spoke [[Exodus 33:11|face to face, as a man speaks to his friend]]. Jesus extends this privilege to all His disciples.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal the heart of the gospel. Jesus laid down His life for His friends. Paul goes even further, noting that Christ died for us while we were still sinners and even enemies (Romans 5:8, 10). Christ's love made enemies into friends. His death was the greatest act of love the world has ever known.

They also show the intimacy of the relationship between Jesus and His followers. Believers are still His servants in the sense that they belong to Him and obey Him; the apostles often called themselves servants of Christ. But they are more than servants. They are friends, invited to know His heart and share His purposes.

Finally, these verses show that love and obedience go together. Jesus' friends do what He commands. This obedience is not cold duty but the natural response of friends who trust and love Him.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses invite believers to see themselves as friends of Jesus. Many Christians relate to God mainly as a distant master or judge. Jesus offers friendship. Believers can speak to Him openly in prayer, trust His heart, and enjoy His company.

They also set the standard for Christian love. Jesus' love was sacrificial; He laid down His life. Believers may not be called to die for others, but they are called to lay down their lives in smaller ways every day: giving time, setting aside preferences, serving, forgiving, and caring for others' needs.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to listen to Jesus. Friends share what is on their hearts. Jesus has made known to His disciples what He heard from the Father. Believers can grow in friendship with Jesus by listening to His words in Scripture and responding with trust and obedience.

### John 15:18–19 — If the World Hates You

verse: John 15:18-19

#### Simple Explanation

After speaking about love within the community of disciples, Jesus turned to the opposition they would face. He wanted to prepare them so that they would not be shocked or discouraged.

[[v18|If the world hates you, you know that it has hated me before it hated you]]. In John's Gospel, "the world" often refers to humanity organized in rebellion against God. Jesus had experienced this hatred. The religious leaders plotted to kill Him, the crowds turned against Him, and soon He would be crucified. His disciples should expect similar treatment.

The words "it has hated me before it hated you" offer comfort. The disciples would not be alone in their suffering. Jesus had walked the path first.

[[v19|If you were of the world, the world would love its own]]. If the disciples shared the world's values and lived like the world, they would be accepted. People tend to welcome those who are like them.

[[v19|But because you are not of the world, since I chose you out of the world, therefore the world hates you]]. Jesus had chosen them out of the world. They now belonged to Him. Their new identity, values, and loyalty would set them apart and provoke opposition.

This does not mean that Christians should try to provoke hatred or should assume that every criticism is persecution. Sometimes believers are disliked because of their own unkindness or foolishness. Peter later warned believers not to suffer as wrongdoers, but if they suffer for being Christians, not to be ashamed (1 Peter 4:15–16).

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal the cost of belonging to Christ. Following Jesus means being identified with Him, and that includes sharing in His rejection. Paul writes that [[2 Timothy 3:12|all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution]]. This is not a sign that something has gone wrong; it is part of what it means to follow a rejected Lord.

They also show the believer's new identity. Jesus told His disciples, [[v19|I chose you out of the world]]. They still live in the world and are sent into it (John 17:18), but they no longer belong to it. Their identity is rooted in Christ.

Finally, these verses give perspective on suffering for Christ. Jesus said earlier, [[Matthew 5:11|Blessed are you when people reproach you, persecute you, and say all kinds of evil against you falsely, for my sake]]. Suffering for His name connects believers with Christ and with the faithful throughout history.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses prepare believers to expect opposition. Many Christians are surprised or discouraged when they face rejection, ridicule, or hostility because of their faith. Jesus' words remind them that this is not unusual. He experienced it first.

They also encourage believers to examine their lives. If believers are never different from the world around them, it may be because they have absorbed the world's values. Following Jesus involves living by a different standard, even when that brings misunderstanding.

Finally, these verses call believers to respond to hatred with love. Jesus did not respond to the world's hatred with hatred. He prayed for those who crucified Him. Believers are called to bless those who persecute them and to continue bearing witness to Christ with gentleness and courage. Around the world today, many Christians face severe persecution; believers can remember them in prayer and stand with them.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

John 15 teaches that the Christian life is a life of union with Christ. Jesus is the vine, and believers are the branches. Fruitfulness does not come from trying harder in human strength but from remaining in Him. Believers can build their lives around staying connected to Jesus through prayer, Scripture, worship, obedience, and fellowship, trusting that His life will flow through them.

The chapter also helps believers understand God's work in their lives. The Father is a skilled and loving vinedresser who prunes fruitful branches so they bear more fruit. Seasons of difficulty and loss can become times of growth when believers trust Him. Not every hardship is pruning, but God is able to use even painful experiences for good.

John 15 calls believers to love one another with the self-giving love of Jesus, who laid down His life for His friends. It also offers believers the astonishing privilege of being Jesus' friends, invited to know His heart and share His purposes. Love for others and friendship with Christ are bound together.

Finally, the chapter prepares believers for opposition. The world hated Jesus, and it may also reject those who follow Him. Believers do not need to be shocked or afraid. They are not alone; the Spirit of truth testifies about Jesus through them. They are called to remain faithful, to love even those who oppose them, and to bear witness to Christ with courage and grace.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, You are the true vine, and I am a branch. Apart from You, I can do nothing. Keep me close to You, and let Your life flow through me so that I bear fruit that glorifies the Father. Father, You are the vinedresser. When You prune me, help me to trust Your skill and Your love, even when it is painful. Thank You, Lord Jesus, for laying down Your life for Your friends, and for calling me Your friend. Teach me to remain in Your love by keeping Your commandments, and fill me with Your joy. Help me to love my brothers and sisters as You have loved me. When the world rejects me because I belong to You, give me courage and keep me from bitterness. Let Your Spirit testify about You through my words and my life. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
