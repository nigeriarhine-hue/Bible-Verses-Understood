# John 17
theme: The Prayer of Jesus for Himself, His Disciples, and All Who Will Believe
main: Before going to the cross, Jesus lifts His eyes to heaven and prays. He asks the Father to glorify the Son so that the Son may glorify Him, and He defines eternal life as knowing the only true God and Jesus Christ whom He sent. He prays for His disciples to be kept from the evil one and made holy by God's word of truth, and He prays for all future believers to be one, as the Father and the Son are one, so that the world may believe. He ends by asking that His people may be with Him and see His glory.

## Chapter Background and Story

John 17 is the longest recorded prayer of Jesus. It is often called His High Priestly Prayer, because in it Jesus intercedes for His people as a priest stands before God on behalf of others. Just as the high priest in Israel carried the names of the twelve tribes on his breastplate into God's presence (Exodus 28:29), Jesus brings His disciples, and all who will believe through them, before the Father.

The prayer comes at the end of Jesus' farewell teaching, after He told His disciples, "I have overcome the world" (16:33). Within hours He would be arrested. Yet His prayer is not filled with fear. It is filled with confidence in the Father, love for His disciples, and concern for the glory of God. The disciples, and through John's Gospel all readers, are allowed to listen as the Son speaks to the Father.

The prayer has three movements: Jesus prays for Himself (17:1–5), for His disciples (17:6–19), and for all future believers (17:20–26).

### Jesus Prays for Himself (17:1–5)

[[v1|lifting up his eyes to heaven]], a common posture of prayer, Jesus said, [[v1|Father, the time has come. Glorify your Son, that your Son may also glorify you]]. Throughout John's Gospel, Jesus' "hour" or "time" referred to His death, resurrection, and return to the Father. Now it had arrived. Jesus asked to be glorified, not for selfish reasons, but so that He could glorify the Father. His glorification would come through the cross, where God's love, justice, and saving power would be most fully revealed.

The Father had given the Son [[v2|authority over all flesh]], so that He could give eternal life to all whom the Father had given Him. Jesus then defined eternal life, studied below.

[[v4|I glorified you on the earth. I have accomplished the work which you have given me to do]]. Jesus spoke as though His work were already complete, because He was about to finish it on the cross, where He would say, "It is finished" (19:30). Then He asked, [[v5|Now, Father, glorify me with your own self with the glory which I had with you before the world existed]]. This is a clear statement of Jesus' eternal existence. Before creation, the Son shared the Father's glory. In becoming human, He set aside the visible display of that glory. Now He would return to it.

### Jesus Prays for His Disciples (17:6–19)

Jesus prayed for the disciples whom the Father had given Him. He said that He had revealed the Father's name to them, meaning that He had made known who God is. They had received His words and believed that the Father sent Him. Their faith was still weak, and that very night they would scatter, but Jesus spoke of them with confidence and affection.

[[v9|I pray for them. I don’t pray for the world, but for those whom you have given me]]. This does not mean Jesus did not care for the world; He came to save the world (3:16–17), and in verse 21 He prays that the world may believe. But at this moment, He prayed specifically for His disciples, who would face the world without His physical presence.

[[v11|Holy Father, keep them through your name which you have given me, that they may be one, even as we are]]. Jesus asked the Father to protect them and to keep them united. While He was with them, He had kept them, and none was lost [[v12|except the son of destruction]], referring to Judas, whose betrayal fulfilled Scripture (Psalm 41:9).

Jesus prayed that His disciples would have His joy made full in them. He had given them the Father's word, and the world hated them because they did not belong to it. His requests for their protection and holiness are studied below.

[[v18|As you sent me into the world, even so I have sent them into the world]]. The disciples were not to escape the world but to be sent into it, as Jesus had been. [[v19|For their sakes I sanctify myself, that they themselves also may be sanctified in truth]]. Jesus "sanctified" or set Himself apart for His sacrificial death, so that His disciples could be set apart for God.

### Jesus Prays for All Believers (17:20–26)

Then Jesus' prayer reached across the centuries: [[v20|Not for these only do I pray, but for those also who will believe in me through their word]]. Every believer who has come to faith through the apostles' message, preserved in the New Testament, was included in this prayer. His prayer for unity is studied below.

[[v22|The glory which you have given me, I have given to them, that they may be one, even as we are one]]. Jesus shares His glory with His people, the glory of being loved by the Father and of reflecting God's character. The goal again is unity and witness: [[v23|that the world may know that you sent me and loved them, even as you loved me]]. The Father loves believers with the same love with which He loves His Son.

Jesus' final requests are studied below. He concluded, [[v25|Righteous Father, the world hasn’t known you, but I knew you; and these knew that you sent me]]. [[v26|I made known to them your name, and will make it known; that the love with which you loved me may be in them, and I in them]]. The prayer ends where all of God's purposes lead: the love of the Father dwelling in His people, and Christ Himself living in them.

## Important Verses Explained

### John 17:3 — This Is Eternal Life

verse: John 17:3

#### Simple Explanation

At the beginning of His prayer, Jesus asked the Father to glorify the Son. He explained that the Father had given Him authority to give eternal life to all whom the Father had given Him. Then He defined what eternal life is.

[[v3|This is eternal life, that they should know you, the only true God, and him whom you sent, Jesus Christ]]. Many people think of eternal life mainly as living forever, an endless length of time after death. Jesus defines it differently. Eternal life is first of all a relationship: knowing God.

The word "know" in Scripture means more than knowing facts. It describes personal, intimate relationship. In the Old Testament, "knowing" God involved trust, love, and covenant faithfulness. To know God is to be in relationship with Him, to experience His love, and to live in fellowship with Him.

Jesus speaks of knowing [[v3|the only true God]]. In a world filled with many gods and idols, Israel confessed that there is one God (Deuteronomy 6:4). Jesus affirms this. He then adds, [[v3|and him whom you sent, Jesus Christ]]. Knowing God is inseparable from knowing Jesus. The Father is known through the Son He sent.

Because eternal life is knowing God, it begins now, not only after death. Those who know God through Jesus already possess eternal life (John 5:24). And because God is eternal, the relationship continues forever.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse reveals that the heart of salvation is relationship with God. Forgiveness, adoption, and eternal life all serve this purpose: to bring people into knowing God. Through Jeremiah, God promised that in the new covenant, [[Jeremiah 31:34|they will all know me, from their least to their greatest]]. Jesus fulfills this promise.

It also shows the unity and distinction of the Father and the Son. Jesus speaks of "the only true God" and of Himself as the One sent. Yet knowing God involves knowing both. Elsewhere in John, Jesus says that whoever has seen Him has seen the Father (14:9). The Father and the Son are distinct, yet one.

Finally, the verse reveals the quality of eternal life. It is not merely endless existence, which could be a burden, but life filled with the presence and love of God. Eternal life is the life of God shared with His people.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites believers to see their faith as a relationship, not merely a set of beliefs or rules. Christianity is about knowing God through Jesus Christ. Believers can grow in this relationship through prayer, Scripture, worship, obedience, and fellowship with other believers.

It also encourages believers to enjoy eternal life now. Eternal life is not only a future hope but a present reality. Every day, believers can experience the presence, love, and guidance of God.

Finally, the verse gives purpose to all spiritual activity. Bible study, church attendance, and service can become routine. Their true purpose is to know God more deeply. Believers can approach each practice with the desire to know the Father and the Son.

### John 17:15–17 — Kept from the Evil One, Sanctified by the Truth

verse: John 17:15-17

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus was praying for His disciples, who would soon face the world without His physical presence. He had told them that the world hated them because they did not belong to it. One might expect Him to ask the Father to take them out of the world to protect them. Instead, He prayed, [[v15|I pray not that you would take them from the world, but that you would keep them from the evil one]].

Jesus did not want His disciples to escape the world. They had a mission there. Instead, He asked that they would be protected from Satan, the evil one, who would try to destroy their faith, divide them, and draw them away from God.

[[v16|They are not of the world, even as I am not of the world]]. The disciples lived in the world but no longer belonged to it. Their identity, values, and loyalty were now rooted in God.

[[v17|Sanctify them in your truth. Your word is truth]]. To "sanctify" means to set apart for God, to make holy. Jesus asked the Father to make His disciples holy, and He named the means: God's truth, which is His word.

God's word is not merely true; it is truth itself, the reliable revelation of reality. Through His word, God shapes His people's minds, hearts, and lives, making them more like Him.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses describe the Christian's position in the world. Believers are "in the world" but "not of the world." They are not to withdraw from the world in isolation, nor to be absorbed into it. They are to live in the world as people who belong to God, bringing His light into dark places.

They also reveal the means of spiritual growth. God sanctifies His people through His word. Paul writes that all Scripture is inspired by God and is profitable for teaching, reproof, correction, and instruction in righteousness (2 Timothy 3:16–17). As believers receive and obey God's word, they are transformed.

Finally, these verses show the reality of spiritual warfare. Jesus prayed for protection from the evil one because the enemy is real and active. But believers are not left defenseless. Jesus Himself prays for them, and the Father keeps them.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to engage with the world rather than retreating from it. Christians are called to live faithfully in their workplaces, schools, neighborhoods, and communities, bringing the presence of Christ into every area of life.

They also call believers to stay rooted in God's word. Living in the world without being shaped by it requires a steady diet of truth. Believers can make Scripture a regular part of their lives, allowing it to shape their thinking and choices.

Finally, these verses offer comfort in the face of spiritual attack. Believers may feel the pull of temptation, discouragement, or doubt. They can take heart that Jesus prayed for their protection from the evil one, and that He continues to intercede for them (Hebrews 7:25).

### John 17:20–21 — That They May All Be One

verse: John 17:20-21

#### Simple Explanation

After praying for His disciples, Jesus extended His prayer to include all who would believe in Him in the future: [[v20|Not for these only do I pray, but for those also who will believe in me through their word]]. The apostles would preach and write about Jesus, and through their message, people in every generation would come to faith. Jesus prayed for all of them, including believers today.

His central request for them was this: [[v21|that they may all be one]]. Jesus prayed for the unity of His followers. He knew that the church would include people of many nations, cultures, and backgrounds. He prayed that they would be united.

He described the kind of unity He meant: [[v21|even as you, Father, are in me, and I in you, that they also may be one in us]]. The model for Christian unity is the relationship between the Father and the Son: a relationship of love, mutual indwelling, and shared purpose. This is not a mere organizational unity but a deep spiritual oneness rooted in God Himself.

Then He gave the purpose: [[v21|that the world may believe that you sent me]]. The unity of believers is a witness to the world. When people see Christians from different backgrounds loving one another and living in harmony, it points to the truth of the gospel.

This unity does not mean that all believers must be identical. The Father and the Son are distinct Persons, yet perfectly one. Christian unity includes diversity.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This prayer reveals how deeply Jesus cares about the unity of His people. On the night before His death, one of His central prayers was that His followers would be one. Divisions among Christians grieve the heart of Christ.

It also shows that Christian unity is a gift and a calling. It is a gift because believers are already united in Christ through the Spirit. Paul urges believers to [[Ephesians 4:3|keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace]]. Believers do not create unity; they preserve and express what God has given.

Finally, the prayer connects unity with mission. The world's belief in Jesus is linked to the unity of His followers. Division weakens the church's witness; unity strengthens it.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This prayer challenges believers to pursue unity with other Christians. Churches and believers often divide over secondary issues, preferences, or personalities. Jesus calls His people to love one another, to seek reconciliation, and to work together for the gospel.

It also encourages humility. Believers in different traditions may disagree about important matters, and the prayer does not require pretending that truth does not matter, since Jesus has just prayed for His people to be sanctified in the truth. But it does call believers to recognize one another as members of the same family, to speak of one another with charity, and to cooperate where they can.

Finally, the prayer reminds believers that their relationships with one another are a witness to the world. The way Christians treat each other in families, churches, and communities can either draw people to Christ or push them away.

### John 17:24 — That They May Be with Me and See My Glory

verse: John 17:24

#### Simple Explanation

Near the end of His prayer, Jesus expressed His deepest desire for His people: [[v24|Father, I desire that they also whom you have given me be with me where I am]]. Jesus wanted His followers to be with Him. This echoes His promise in John 14:3, where He said He would come again and take them to Himself so that where He is, they may be also.

[[v24|that they may see my glory which you have given me]]. Jesus wanted His followers to see His glory. During His earthly ministry, His glory was partly hidden. The disciples saw glimpses of it in His miracles, His transfiguration, and His love. But Jesus wanted them to see His glory fully, in heaven.

[[v24|for you loved me before the foundation of the world]]. The glory Jesus has is rooted in the eternal love of the Father for the Son. Before the world was created, the Father loved the Son. That love is the source of Jesus' glory.

Notice the tone of this request. Jesus does not say, "I would like," but "I desire." It is a strong expression of His will. Jesus truly wants His people with Him.

This verse reveals that heaven is not merely a place of rest or reward. Its greatest joy is being with Jesus and beholding His glory.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse reveals the heart of Jesus for His people. He does not merely tolerate them; He desires them to be with Him forever. The Bible ends with the same vision: [[Revelation 21:3|Behold, God’s dwelling is with people]]. The goal of salvation is eternal fellowship with God.

It also shows the eternal love within God. The Father loved the Son "before the foundation of the world." Love did not begin with creation; it has always existed within the Triune God. Believers are invited to share in this eternal love (17:26).

Finally, the verse shapes the hope of believers. Paul writes that to depart and be with Christ is [[Philippians 1:23|far better]]. The hope of seeing Christ's glory gives believers courage to face suffering and death.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse gives believers assurance of Jesus' love. He wants them to be with Him. Believers who struggle with feelings of unworthiness can rest in the fact that Jesus Himself prayed for them to be with Him forever.

It also shapes how believers think about heaven. The center of heaven is not mansions or streets of gold but Jesus Himself. Believers can cultivate a longing for Him now by spending time in His presence through prayer and worship.

Finally, the verse brings comfort in grief. When believers lose loved ones who trusted in Christ, they can know that Jesus' prayer has been answered for them: they are with Him, seeing His glory.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

John 17 lets believers listen to Jesus pray for them. Before going to the cross, He prayed for Himself, His disciples, and all who would believe through their message. This prayer reveals the heart of Christ for His people. Believers can take great comfort in knowing that Jesus prayed for them then and continues to intercede for them now.

The chapter defines eternal life as knowing God through Jesus Christ. This invites believers to treat their faith as a living relationship rather than a set of routines. Prayer, Scripture, worship, and obedience are all means of knowing God more deeply. Eternal life begins now and continues forever.

John 17 also describes how believers are to live in the world: not removed from it, but not belonging to it either. They are sent into the world as Jesus was sent, kept by the Father from the evil one, and sanctified by God's word of truth. Staying rooted in Scripture helps believers live faithfully in a world that often pulls them in other directions.

Finally, the chapter reveals Jesus' deep desire for the unity of His people and for their eternal fellowship with Him. Christian unity, rooted in the love between the Father and the Son, is a powerful witness to the world. Believers are called to pursue love and harmony with other Christians, and to live with the hope of one day being with Jesus and seeing His glory.

## Chapter Prayer

Holy Father, thank You for the prayer Your Son prayed for His disciples and for all who would believe through their word, including me. Thank You that eternal life is knowing You, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom You sent. Help me to know You more deeply each day. Keep me from the evil one, and sanctify me by Your truth, for Your word is truth. As You sent Jesus into the world, send me into my everyday world to live for You. Make Your people one, as You and the Son are one, so that the world may believe. Forgive us for our divisions, and teach us to love one another. Lord Jesus, thank You for desiring that I be with You and see Your glory. Fill me with that hope, and let the Father's love for You dwell in me. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
