# Luke 24
theme: He Is Risen: Burning Hearts, Opened Minds, and a Mission to All Nations
main: Women find the tomb empty and hear angels ask why they seek the living among the dead. The risen Jesus walks unrecognized with two grieving disciples on the road to Emmaus, explains how all the Scriptures point to Him, and is recognized as He breaks bread. He appears to the gathered disciples, shows them His wounded hands and feet, eats with them, opens their minds to understand the Scriptures, commissions them to preach repentance and forgiveness to all nations, promises the Spirit's power, and ascends to heaven as they worship with great joy.

## Chapter Background and Story

Luke 24 is the triumphant conclusion of Luke's Gospel. Jesus had been crucified on Friday and buried in Joseph of Arimathea's tomb. The women who had followed Him from Galilee saw where He was laid, prepared spices, and rested on the Sabbath (Luke 23:55–56). Now, on the first day of the week, everything changes.

The entire chapter takes place on a single day, the day of resurrection, at least until the ascension at the end, which Acts 1:3 tells us happened forty days later; Luke compresses the time in his Gospel and describes it more fully in Acts. Luke's resurrection account has several distinctive emphases: the fulfillment of Scripture, the physical reality of Jesus' risen body, the gradual movement of the disciples from confusion and doubt to understanding and joy, and the mission that will continue in Luke's second volume, Acts.

### The Empty Tomb (24:1–12)

Very early on Sunday morning, the women came to the tomb with the spices they had prepared. They found the stone rolled away. When they went in, they did not find the body of the Lord Jesus. While they were puzzled, two men in dazzling clothing appeared beside them. The women were terrified and bowed with their faces to the ground. The men said, [[v5|Why do you seek the living among the dead?]] This question, with the announcement that follows, is studied below.

The angels reminded the women of what Jesus had told them in Galilee: the Son of Man must be handed over to sinful men, be crucified, and rise on the third day. [[v8|They remembered his words]]. They returned from the tomb and told everything to the eleven and the others. Luke names them: [[v10|Mary Magdalene, Joanna, and Mary the mother of James]], and other women. These are the same women who had supported Jesus' ministry (Luke 8:2–3) and stayed near the cross (Luke 23:49).

But the apostles thought their words were [[v11|nonsense]], and they did not believe them. In that culture, women's testimony was often given less weight. The disciples' unbelief shows that they were not expecting a resurrection; they were not inventing a story they wanted to believe. Peter, however, got up and ran to the tomb. He looked in and saw the linen cloths lying by themselves, and he went home wondering what had happened.

### The Road to Emmaus (24:13–35)

That same day, two of Jesus' followers were walking to a village called Emmaus, about seven miles from Jerusalem (sixty stadia). They were discussing everything that had happened. As they talked, Jesus Himself came near and walked with them, [[v16|But their eyes were kept from recognizing him]].

Jesus asked what they were discussing. They stopped, their faces downcast. One of them, named Cleopas, asked whether He was the only visitor in Jerusalem who did not know what had happened. Jesus asked, [[v19|What things?]] They told Him about Jesus of Nazareth, [[v19|a prophet mighty in deed and word before God and all the people]], whom the chief priests and rulers had handed over to be crucified. Then they said, [[v21|But we were hoping that it was he who would redeem Israel]]. Their hope had died with Jesus. They also told Him about the women's report of an empty tomb and angels saying He was alive, and that some had gone to the tomb and found it empty, but had not seen Him.

Jesus said, [[v25|Foolish people, and slow of heart to believe in all that the prophets have spoken!]] He asked, [[v26|Didn’t the Christ have to suffer these things and to enter into his glory?]] Then, [[v27|Beginning from Moses and from all the prophets, he explained to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning himself]]. Jesus showed them that the whole Old Testament pointed to a Messiah who would suffer and then be glorified. Their problem was not that the cross contradicted Scripture, but that they had read Scripture selectively, expecting only a triumphant king.

As they approached the village, Jesus acted as if He would go farther. They urged Him to stay, because it was evening. He went in with them. When He was at the table with them, [[v30|he took the bread and gave thanks. Breaking it, he gave it to them]]. These actions echo the feeding of the five thousand (Luke 9:16) and the Last Supper (Luke 22:19). [[v31|Their eyes were opened and they recognized him; then he vanished out of their sight]].

They said to each other, [[v32|Weren’t our hearts burning within us while he spoke to us along the way, and while he opened the Scriptures to us?]] This verse is studied below. They got up immediately and returned to Jerusalem, a seven-mile walk at night. They found the Eleven and others gathered together, who told them, [[v34|The Lord is risen indeed, and has appeared to Simon!]] Paul also mentions this appearance to Peter (1 Corinthians 15:5). Then the two told what had happened on the road and how Jesus was recognized by them [[v35|in the breaking of the bread]].

### Jesus Appears to the Disciples (24:36–43)

While they were still talking, Jesus Himself stood among them and said, [[v36|Peace be to you]]. They were startled and frightened, thinking they were seeing a ghost. Jesus asked why they were troubled and why doubts arose in their hearts. He invited them to look at His hands and feet and to touch Him, showing that He was not a spirit. This is studied below.

Luke adds, [[v41|While they still didn’t believe for joy, and wondered]]. Their joy was so great that it seemed too good to be true. Then Jesus asked, [[v41|Do you have anything here to eat?]] They gave Him a piece of broiled fish, and He ate it in front of them, further proving that He was physically alive.

### Opening Their Minds and Commissioning Them (24:44–49)

Jesus reminded them of what He had told them while He was still with them: [[v44|that all things which are written in the law of Moses, the prophets, and the psalms concerning me must be fulfilled]]. These three groups covered the whole Hebrew Bible. [[v45|Then he opened their minds, that they might understand the Scriptures]]. Throughout Luke, the disciples had not understood Jesus' predictions of His death (Luke 9:45; 18:34). Now Jesus opened their minds.

He told them that the Scriptures taught that the Messiah would suffer and rise on the third day, and that repentance and forgiveness of sins would be proclaimed in His name to all nations, beginning at Jerusalem. This is studied below. He said, [[v48|You are witnesses of these things]]. They had seen His life, death, and resurrection, and they would testify about Him.

Then He gave a promise: [[v49|Behold, I send out the promise of my Father on you. But wait in the city of Jerusalem until you are clothed with power from on high]]. "The promise of my Father" is the Holy Spirit, whom the prophets had promised (Joel 2:28). Acts 2 records the fulfillment of this promise on the day of Pentecost.

### The Ascension (24:50–53)

Jesus led them out as far as Bethany, on the Mount of Olives. He lifted up His hands and blessed them, the posture of a priest pronouncing a blessing (Leviticus 9:22). [[v51|While he blessed them, he withdrew from them and was carried up into heaven]]. Jesus' last action on earth was to bless His followers.

[[v52|They worshiped him and returned to Jerusalem with great joy]]. This is the first time in Luke that the disciples are said to worship Jesus, recognizing Him as Lord and God. They were not sad that He had left; they were filled with joy. [[v53|and were continually in the temple, praising and blessing God]]. Luke's Gospel began in the temple, with Zechariah offering incense (Luke 1:8–9), and it ends in the temple, with Jesus' followers praising God. The story continues in Acts.

## Important Verses Explained

### Luke 24:5–6 — "Why Do You Seek the Living Among the Dead?"

verse: Luke 24:5-6

#### Simple Explanation

These verses record the angels' message to the women at Jesus' tomb. Early on Sunday morning, the first day of the week, the women who had followed Jesus from Galilee came to the tomb with spices to anoint His body. They had watched Him die on Friday and had seen where He was buried. They came expecting to care for a corpse.

But they found the stone rolled away, and when they entered, Jesus' body was not there. While they stood perplexed, two men in dazzling clothing stood beside them. Luke later identifies them as angels (v. 23). The women were terrified and bowed their faces to the ground.

The angels asked, [[v5|Why do you seek the living among the dead?]] Tombs were for the dead. The women had come to a place of death looking for someone who was alive. The question gently corrects their expectations: they were looking in the wrong place, because Jesus was no longer dead.

[[v6|He isn’t here, but is risen]]. This is the central announcement of the Christian faith. The word "risen" is a passive form meaning "has been raised," emphasizing that God raised Him from the dead.

[[v6|Remember what he told you when he was still in Galilee]]. The angels reminded the women that Jesus had predicted His death and resurrection. In Luke, Jesus predicted this several times (Luke 9:22, 44; 18:31–33). The women had heard His words but had not understood them. Now the angels called them to remember.

Verse 8 records their response: [[v8|They remembered his words]]. Remembering Jesus' words transformed their confusion into understanding. They went back and told the apostles everything.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses announce that Jesus is alive. He is not a dead teacher whose memory people honor, but a living Lord. Christianity is not primarily a set of moral teachings or a remembrance of a great person; it is a relationship with the risen Jesus, who is alive today.

They also show the importance of remembering Jesus' words. The women's confusion turned to understanding when they remembered what Jesus had said. Throughout Luke 24, understanding comes as people recall and understand Jesus' words and the Scriptures.

Finally, the angels' question challenges people who look for Jesus in the wrong places. Some look for Him only in history, as a figure of the past. Others look for Him in religious rituals without a living relationship. The angels remind everyone that Jesus is alive and must be sought as the living Lord.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses invite believers to relate to Jesus as alive, not merely as a historical figure. They can pray to Him, trust Him, and follow Him, knowing that He is present and active.

They also encourage believers to remember Jesus' words, especially in times of confusion or grief. Like the women at the tomb, believers can find clarity and hope when they recall what Jesus has said in Scripture.

Finally, these verses bring hope to those who grieve. The tomb could not hold Jesus. His resurrection is the promise that death will not have the final word for those who belong to Him. Believers can face loss with confidence that the living Lord will raise His people.

### Luke 24:32 — "Weren't Our Hearts Burning Within Us?"

verse: Luke 24:32

#### Simple Explanation

This verse records the words of two disciples after they recognized the risen Jesus. Earlier that day, they had been walking from Jerusalem to the village of Emmaus, discouraged and confused. Their hopes that Jesus would redeem Israel had been crushed by His crucifixion. They had heard reports from the women that the tomb was empty and that angels said He was alive, but they did not know what to make of it.

As they walked, Jesus joined them, but they did not recognize Him. When they explained their sadness, Jesus called them slow to believe the prophets, and then He explained to them, beginning with Moses and all the prophets, everything in the Scriptures about Himself. When they reached Emmaus, they invited Him to stay. At the table, He took bread, gave thanks, broke it, and gave it to them. Their eyes were opened, they recognized Him, and He disappeared.

Then they said to each other, [[v32|Weren’t our hearts burning within us while he spoke to us along the way, and while he opened the Scriptures to us?]]

"Our hearts burning within us" describes an inner warmth, excitement, and conviction. Even before they recognized Jesus, something stirred in them as He explained the Scriptures. Jeremiah described God's word as [[Jeremiah 20:9|a burning fire shut up in my bones]]. The disciples' hearts were set on fire by the truth.

[[v32|while he opened the Scriptures to us]]. The word "opened" means explained or unlocked the meaning. They had known the Scriptures, but Jesus showed them how the whole Old Testament pointed to Him, especially to a Messiah who would suffer before entering His glory.

The disciples' response was immediate. They got up that very hour and walked seven miles back to Jerusalem in the dark to tell the other disciples.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse reveals that Jesus is the key to understanding Scripture. The whole Bible, Old and New Testaments, points to Him. When people understand the Scriptures in light of Jesus, they come alive. Jesus later said the same thing to all the disciples (v. 44).

It also shows that Scripture and Jesus' presence work together. The disciples' hearts burned as He explained the Scriptures, and their eyes were opened when He broke bread. In the church's life, Jesus continues to make Himself known through the Word and through the shared life of believers, including the Lord's Supper.

Finally, this verse shows that encountering the risen Jesus transforms people. The disciples went from discouragement to joy, from walking away from Jerusalem to running back to share the news.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to read Scripture with Jesus at the center. When they read the Old Testament, they can look for how it points to Christ; when they read the New Testament, they can see how He fulfills God's promises. This kind of reading can set hearts on fire.

It also invites believers to ask Jesus to open the Scriptures to them. Before reading the Bible, they can pray for understanding, trusting that the risen Lord, through His Spirit, can make His Word come alive.

Finally, this verse offers hope to those who are discouraged. Like the two disciples on the road, believers may feel that their hopes have been crushed. Jesus walks with them, even when they do not recognize Him, and He can turn their sorrow into joy.

### Luke 24:39 — "See My Hands and My Feet"

verse: Luke 24:39

#### Simple Explanation

This verse records Jesus' words when He appeared to the gathered disciples on the evening of His resurrection. The two disciples from Emmaus had just returned and were telling the others how they had recognized Jesus. While they were still speaking, Jesus suddenly stood among them and said, "Peace be to you." They were startled and terrified, thinking they were seeing a spirit or ghost.

Jesus asked them why they were troubled and why doubts were arising in their hearts. Then He said, [[v39|See my hands and my feet, that it is truly me]]. His hands and feet bore the marks of the nails from the crucifixion. These wounds proved that the risen Jesus was the same person who had been crucified. John's Gospel records Jesus showing His wounds to Thomas as well (John 20:27).

[[v39|Touch me and see, for a spirit doesn’t have flesh and bones, as you see that I have]]. Jesus invited them to touch Him and confirm that He had a real, physical body. He was not a ghost, a vision, or a spirit. He had been raised bodily.

Luke then records that Jesus showed them His hands and feet. Even then, [[v41|While they still didn’t believe for joy, and wondered]], He asked for something to eat. They gave Him broiled fish, and He ate it in their presence. Eating provided further proof of His physical resurrection.

Luke, the physician and careful historian, emphasizes this bodily reality. Some people in the Greek world believed that the body was unimportant or even evil, and that only the soul mattered. Others might have thought the disciples saw a vision. Luke shows clearly that Jesus' resurrection was physical.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse teaches that Jesus was raised bodily. His resurrection was not merely spiritual survival but the transformation of His physical body. This is essential to the Christian faith. Paul writes that if Christ has not been raised, faith is useless (1 Corinthians 15:14–17). Jesus' bodily resurrection is the guarantee that believers will also be raised bodily (1 Corinthians 15:20–23).

It also shows that the risen Jesus still bears the marks of the cross. His wounds were not erased. They became signs of His victory and His love. In Revelation 5:6, Jesus is seen as a Lamb that appears to have been slain. The crucified one is the risen one.

Finally, the verse shows Jesus' patience with doubt. The disciples were frightened and doubtful, yet He did not rebuke them harshly. He gave them evidence: His wounds, His touch, and His eating. Jesus meets honest doubt with patient reassurance.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse gives believers confidence in the reality of the resurrection. Their faith rests on a real event: Jesus was raised bodily from the dead. This truth can strengthen faith in times of doubt.

It also gives hope for the future. Because Jesus was raised bodily, believers can look forward to their own bodily resurrection. God cares about the whole person, body and soul, and will one day restore all things.

Finally, this verse encourages believers who struggle with doubt. Jesus does not reject those who doubt. He invites them to look and see. Believers can bring their questions to Him and examine the evidence, trusting that He will strengthen their faith.

### Luke 24:46–47 — Repentance and Forgiveness Proclaimed to All Nations

verse: Luke 24:46-47

#### Simple Explanation

These verses contain Jesus' summary of the message His followers would proclaim. He had appeared to the gathered disciples, shown them His hands and feet, and eaten with them. Then He reminded them of what He had told them before His death: everything written about Him in the Law of Moses, the Prophets, and the Psalms must be fulfilled. [[v45|Then he opened their minds, that they might understand the Scriptures]].

Jesus summarized what the Scriptures taught: [[v46|Thus it is written, and thus it was necessary for the Christ to suffer and to rise from the dead the third day]]. The suffering and resurrection of the Messiah were not accidents or failures; they were God's plan, revealed in Scripture. Passages like Isaiah 53, Psalm 22, and Psalm 16 point to a Messiah who would suffer and be raised.

Then Jesus described what would follow: [[v47|and that repentance and remission of sins should be preached in his name to all the nations, beginning at Jerusalem]].

[[v47|repentance and remission of sins]]. This is the heart of the message. "Repentance" means turning away from sin and turning to God. "Remission" means forgiveness, the cancellation of the debt of sin. Because of Jesus' death and resurrection, forgiveness is available to all who repent.

[[v47|preached in his name]]. The message would be proclaimed by Jesus' authority and centered on Him.

[[v47|to all the nations]]. The message is for everyone, Jews and Gentiles alike. This fulfills Simeon's prophecy that Jesus would be a light for the Gentiles (Luke 2:32) and the promise that all flesh would see God's salvation (Luke 3:6).

[[v47|beginning at Jerusalem]]. The mission would start in Jerusalem, the city where Jesus was crucified. The book of Acts follows exactly this pattern: the gospel spreads from Jerusalem to Judea, Samaria, and the ends of the earth (Acts 1:8).

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses summarize the gospel: the Messiah suffered, died, and rose again, and through Him forgiveness is offered to all who repent. This message is not a human invention but the fulfillment of God's plan revealed throughout Scripture.

They also reveal the mission of the church. Jesus' followers are called to proclaim this message to all nations. This mission continues today. The phrase "beginning at Jerusalem" is significant: the message started in the very city that rejected Jesus. God's grace was offered first to those who had crucified Him.

Finally, these verses show that the message of forgiveness requires the response of repentance. Forgiveness is freely offered, but it is received by turning to God. Jesus' message is both an invitation and a call to change.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses invite believers to receive the message of repentance and forgiveness personally. Whatever sins they have committed, forgiveness is available in Jesus' name to all who turn to Him.

They also call believers to participate in proclaiming this message. Every believer is a witness to what Jesus has done. They can share the good news of forgiveness with family, friends, neighbors, and people of other nations, and support the work of spreading the gospel worldwide.

Finally, these verses remind believers that their message is rooted in Scripture. They can study how the whole Bible points to Christ, so that they can understand and share the good news with confidence.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Luke 24 announces the greatest news in history: Jesus is risen. The women found an empty tomb and heard the angels say, "Why do you seek the living among the dead?" Believers can relate to Jesus as their living Lord, present and active today, and they can face grief and death with the hope of resurrection.

The chapter shows how people move from confusion to faith. The women remembered Jesus' words. The disciples on the road to Emmaus had their hearts set on fire as Jesus explained the Scriptures. The gathered disciples saw His wounds and watched Him eat. In each case, Jesus patiently met people in their doubt and discouragement. Believers who struggle with doubt can bring their questions to Jesus, study the Scriptures, and trust that He will open their eyes.

The chapter also emphasizes that Jesus is the key to all Scripture. He opened the minds of His disciples to understand how the Law, the Prophets, and the Psalms pointed to Him. Believers can read the Bible with Christ at the center, and they can ask Him to make His Word come alive in their hearts.

Finally, Jesus commissioned His followers to proclaim repentance and forgiveness in His name to all nations, promising the power of the Holy Spirit. Believers are witnesses of these things. Like the first disciples, they can worship Jesus with great joy, praise God continually, and share the good news with others. The story that began in Luke continues in Acts, and it continues today through every believer who bears witness to the risen Lord.

## Chapter Prayer

Risen Lord Jesus, thank You that You are not among the dead, but alive forever. Thank You for conquering death and for keeping every promise You made. When I am discouraged like the disciples on the road to Emmaus, walk with me, open the Scriptures to me, and set my heart on fire with Your truth.

Thank You for showing Your hands and feet, the marks of Your love, and for proving that You are truly risen. Help me to trust You when I doubt. Open my mind to understand the Scriptures and to see You at the center of them. Thank You that repentance and forgiveness of sins are offered in Your name to all nations. Make me a faithful witness, fill me with the power of Your Spirit, and let my life overflow with joy and praise to God. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
