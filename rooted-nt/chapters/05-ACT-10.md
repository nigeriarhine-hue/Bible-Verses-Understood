# Acts 10
theme: God Shows No Favoritism: The Gospel Comes to the Gentiles
main: God prepares two men at the same time. Cornelius, a devout Roman centurion, is told by an angel to send for Peter, while Peter, praying on a rooftop in Joppa, sees a vision of unclean animals and hears, "What God has cleansed, you must not call unclean." Peter goes to Cornelius's house, the home of a Gentile, and declares that God shows no favoritism. As he proclaims Jesus' life, death, resurrection, and the forgiveness of sins for all who believe, the Holy Spirit falls on the Gentile listeners, and Peter has them baptized in the name of Jesus Christ.

## Chapter Background and Story

Acts 10 is one of the great turning points of the New Testament. Until now, the church was made up of Jews and of Samaritans, who shared much of Israel's heritage. The Ethiopian official was probably a God-fearer, a Gentile who worshiped Israel's God. But the question of whether Gentiles could become full members of God's people without first becoming Jews had not been faced directly. In this chapter, God answers that question, and Luke considers it so important that Peter retells the story in chapter 11 and refers to it again at the Jerusalem Council in chapter 15.

For Jews of Peter's day, the separation between Jews and Gentiles was deep. The Law of Moses distinguished between clean and unclean foods (Leviticus 11), and these distinctions marked Israel as a people set apart. Over time, many Jews avoided entering Gentile homes or eating with Gentiles, to avoid being made ritually unclean. This made table fellowship between Jews and Gentiles extremely difficult. God was about to break down this wall.

### Cornelius's Vision (10:1–8)

Cornelius lived in Caesarea, the Roman capital of the province of Judea, on the Mediterranean coast. He was [[v1|a centurion of what was called the Italian Regiment]], a Roman officer commanding about a hundred soldiers. He was [[v2|a devout man, and one who feared God with all his house, who gave gifts for the needy generously to the people, and always prayed to God]]. Cornelius was a "God-fearer," a Gentile who believed in the God of Israel, prayed, and gave to the poor, but who had not become a full convert to Judaism.

At about three in the afternoon, the time of evening prayer, an angel appeared to him in a vision: [[v4|Your prayers and your gifts to the needy have gone up for a memorial before God]]. The angel told him to send for Simon Peter in Joppa, who was staying with Simon the tanner by the sea. Cornelius immediately sent two servants and a devout soldier.

### Peter's Vision (10:9–23)

The next day, as Cornelius's messengers approached Joppa, Peter went up on the flat roof of the house to pray at noon. He became hungry, and while food was being prepared, he fell into a trance. He saw heaven opened and something like a great sheet being lowered by its four corners. In it were all kinds of animals, reptiles, and birds, including those the Law declared unclean.

A voice said, [[v13|Rise, Peter, kill and eat!]] Peter protested: [[v14|Not so, Lord; for I have never eaten anything that is common or unclean]]. Peter's reply shows his lifelong faithfulness to the food laws. The voice answered, studied below. This happened three times, and the sheet was taken back up to heaven. The threefold repetition may remind readers of Peter's three denials and Jesus' three questions in John 21; God was again patiently teaching Peter.

While Peter was puzzling over the vision, Cornelius's men arrived at the gate. The Spirit told Peter, [[v20|arise, get down, and go with them, doubting nothing; for I have sent them]]. The timing was perfect. God had prepared both men.

The men explained that Cornelius, [[v22|a righteous man and one who fears God, and well spoken of by all the nation of the Jews]], had been directed by an angel to invite Peter. Then Peter [[v23|called them in and provided a place to stay]]. Already, Peter was beginning to understand. He welcomed Gentiles as guests.

### Peter in Cornelius's House (10:24–33)

The next day, Peter traveled to Caesarea with some believers from Joppa. Cornelius was waiting, having gathered his relatives and close friends. When Peter entered, Cornelius fell at his feet in reverence. Peter lifted him up: [[v26|Stand up! I myself am also a man]]. Peter refused to accept worship.

Then Peter said, [[v28|You yourselves know how it is an unlawful thing for a man who is a Jew to join himself or come to one of another nation, but God has shown me that I shouldn’t call any man unholy or unclean]]. Peter had understood the vision. It was not ultimately about food but about people. God was teaching him that no person should be considered unclean or beyond the reach of His grace.

Cornelius explained his vision and said, [[v33|Now therefore we are all here present in the sight of God to hear all things that have been commanded you by God]]. Cornelius and his household were eager to hear God's word.

### Peter's Sermon (10:34–43)

Peter began, [[v34|Truly I perceive that God doesn’t show favoritism]]. This opening is studied below. Peter then summarized the story of Jesus, studied in part below: His ministry beginning in Galilee after John's baptism, His anointing with the Spirit and power, His healing of all who were oppressed by the devil, His death [[v39|hanging him on a tree]], and His resurrection on the third day. God made Him known to chosen witnesses [[v41|who ate and drank with him after he rose from the dead]]. Jesus commanded them to preach and to testify [[v42|that this is he who is appointed by God as the Judge of the living and the dead]]. Peter's conclusion and what followed are studied below.

### The Spirit Poured Out on the Gentiles (10:44–48)

While Peter was still speaking, the Holy Spirit fell on all who were listening. The Jewish believers who had come with Peter [[v45|were amazed]], [[v45|because the gift of the Holy Spirit was also poured out on the Gentiles]]. They heard the Gentiles [[v46|speaking in other languages and magnifying God]], just as the disciples had at Pentecost. This is sometimes called "the Gentile Pentecost."

Peter asked, [[v47|Can anyone forbid these people from being baptized with water? They have received the Holy Spirit just like us]]. God had already accepted these Gentiles by giving them His Spirit. The church could not refuse them baptism. Peter commanded them to be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ, and they asked him to stay with them for several days. Peter, the Jew who had never eaten anything unclean, now stayed in a Gentile home, sharing meals and fellowship.

## Important Verses Explained

### Acts 10:15 — What God Has Cleansed

verse: Acts 10:15

#### Simple Explanation

Peter was on the roof of a house in Joppa, praying at noon. He became hungry, and while waiting for food, he fell into a trance. He saw a large sheet lowered from heaven, filled with all kinds of animals, including those that the Law of Moses called unclean, such as pigs, reptiles, and certain birds. Jews were forbidden to eat these animals (Leviticus 11).

A voice told Peter to kill and eat. Peter refused, saying he had never eaten anything unclean. He had kept these food laws all his life, as faithful Jews did.

Then the voice said, [[v15|What God has cleansed, you must not call unclean]]. God was declaring that the old distinctions between clean and unclean foods were no longer in force. Jesus had already pointed in this direction when He taught that what goes into a person's mouth does not make them unclean, and Mark notes that by this Jesus declared [[Mark 7:19|making all foods clean]]. Now God made it clear to Peter in a vision.

But the vision was about more than food. While Peter was wondering what it meant, Gentile visitors arrived at the door. Peter soon understood: [[v28|God has shown me that I shouldn’t call any man unholy or unclean]]. The food laws had symbolized Israel's separation from the nations. Now that God was welcoming the nations into His people through Christ, that separation was ending.

The vision was repeated three times, emphasizing its importance and helping Peter accept a truth that went against everything he had been taught.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse reveals that God Himself has broken down the barriers between peoples. Through Christ, Gentiles are no longer outsiders. Paul writes that Christ [[Ephesians 2:15|abolished in his flesh the hostility, the law of commandments contained in ordinances]], so that He might create one new people from Jews and Gentiles.

It also shows that God's cleansing work is complete. What God has cleansed is truly clean. People cannot declare unclean those whom God has accepted. This applies to all who come to Christ: their sins are washed away, and they are welcomed fully into God's family.

Finally, the verse shows that God sometimes has to challenge His people's deeply held assumptions. Peter's refusal came from sincere faithfulness to what he had been taught. God patiently showed him something new, not by contradicting His purposes, but by revealing how they were fulfilled in Christ.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse challenges believers to examine their attitudes toward others. Many believers, consciously or unconsciously, view certain groups as less worthy of God's love, perhaps people of a different ethnicity, culture, social class, or background. God's word to Peter applies: no one should be called unclean whom God has cleansed.

It also encourages believers who feel unworthy. Some people believe they are too sinful or too different to be accepted by God. God declares clean all who come to Him through Christ. Their past does not define them.

Finally, the verse invites believers to be open to God's correction. Peter's convictions were sincere, but God corrected him. Believers can ask God to show them where their attitudes need to change, and they can be humble enough to accept His correction through His word.

### Acts 10:34–35 — God Doesn't Show Favoritism

verse: Acts 10:34-35

#### Simple Explanation

Peter stood in the home of Cornelius, a Roman centurion, surrounded by his Gentile family and friends. Only days before, he would never have entered such a house. Now, having seen God's vision and heard Cornelius's account, he began to speak.

[[v34|Truly I perceive that God doesn’t show favoritism]]. The word "perceive" suggests that Peter was coming to a new understanding. The phrase "show favoritism" literally means "receive faces," judging people by their outward appearance, nationality, or status. Peter realized that God does not accept or reject people based on their ethnicity or background.

[[v35|but in every nation he who fears him and works righteousness is acceptable to him]]. God welcomes people from every nation who revere Him and seek to do what is right. Cornelius was an example: he feared God, prayed, and gave generously to the poor.

This verse does not mean that people are saved by their good works or that Jesus is unnecessary. Cornelius still needed to hear about Jesus, and the angel told him to send for Peter so that he could hear the message by which he would be saved (Acts 11:14). The point is that God is open to people of every nation and that ethnicity is no barrier to coming to Him. Those whose hearts are turned toward God, like Cornelius, are welcomed to receive the gospel and the salvation it brings through faith in Jesus (10:43).

Peter's realization fulfilled what the Old Testament had already taught: God [[Deuteronomy 10:17|doesn’t respect persons or take bribes]].

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal the impartiality of God. God created all peoples and loves all peoples. His covenant with Israel was never meant to exclude the nations forever; it was meant to bring blessing to all nations (Genesis 12:3). In Christ, that purpose is fulfilled.

They also show that God's salvation is for everyone. Paul writes that there is no distinction between Jew and Greek, [[Romans 10:12|for the same Lord is Lord of all, and is rich to all who call on him]]. The gospel is not a message for one culture or nation but for the whole world.

Finally, the verses challenge every form of prejudice within the church. James warns believers not to show favoritism, treating the rich better than the poor (James 2:1–9). If God does not show favoritism, His people must not either.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses call believers to reject prejudice and discrimination. Every person, of whatever nationality, ethnicity, class, or background, is equally precious to God. Churches are called to welcome all people and to treat them with equal respect and love.

They also encourage believers to share the gospel with everyone. Some believers unconsciously assume that certain people are unlikely to be interested in Jesus or unworthy of their effort. Peter's experience shows that God may be preparing hearts in unexpected places.

Finally, these verses give hope to people who feel like outsiders. Many people feel that faith is for others, not for people like them. God shows no favoritism. Anyone from any nation can come to Him through Jesus.

### Acts 10:38 — He Went About Doing Good

verse: Acts 10:38

#### Simple Explanation

In his sermon at Cornelius's house, Peter summarized the life and ministry of Jesus. His Gentile listeners had probably heard something about Jesus, since His ministry had been well known in Judea and Galilee. Peter explained who He was and what He had done.

[[v38|how God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and with power]]. This anointing took place at Jesus' baptism, when the Spirit descended on Him like a dove (Luke 3:21–22). The title "Christ" means "Anointed One." Jesus was anointed by God for His mission.

[[v38|who went about doing good and healing all who were oppressed by the devil]]. This is a beautiful summary of Jesus' life. He "went about doing good." Wherever He went, He brought blessing: healing the sick, feeding the hungry, welcoming outcasts, teaching truth, and setting people free. His healing ministry was also a battle against evil. He freed people from the power of the devil.

[[v38|for God was with him]]. The source of Jesus' power was God's presence with Him. Jesus Himself said that He did nothing on His own but only what He saw the Father doing (John 5:19).

Peter then went on to describe Jesus' death and resurrection. But he began with Jesus' life of goodness. The One who died on the cross had spent His life doing good.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that Jesus' ministry was a demonstration of God's kingdom. When Jesus healed and delivered people, He was showing that God's rule had come to overthrow the power of evil. He said, [[Luke 11:20|But if I by God’s finger cast out demons, then God’s Kingdom has come to you]].

It also reveals the character of Jesus. He was not a distant teacher or a political revolutionary. He was full of compassion, moving toward people in need. His goodness was not passive; He "went about" doing good, actively seeking out those who needed help.

Finally, the verse shows that Jesus' life is a pattern for His followers. Believers are called to imitate Christ, filled with the same Spirit, doing good to all people (Galatians 6:10) and opposing the works of evil.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites believers to follow Jesus' example of doing good. Believers can look for opportunities to help others in practical ways: caring for the sick, supporting the poor, encouraging the discouraged, and standing up for the oppressed.

It also reminds believers that their power comes from God's presence. Jesus did good because God was with Him and He was anointed with the Spirit. Believers can rely on the same Spirit to empower them for good works.

Finally, the verse offers comfort to those who are oppressed, whether by spiritual forces, illness, or injustice. Jesus came to heal and set people free. Believers can bring their burdens to Him, trusting His power to help.

### Acts 10:43–44 — Everyone Who Believes Receives Forgiveness

verse: Acts 10:43-44

#### Simple Explanation

Peter was concluding his sermon to Cornelius and his household. He had described Jesus' ministry, death, and resurrection, and had said that Jesus was appointed by God as the Judge of the living and the dead. Then he offered the good news.

[[v43|All the prophets testify about him]]. The Old Testament prophets pointed to Jesus. Isaiah, Jeremiah, and others had spoken of a coming Savior who would bring forgiveness. Peter's message was the fulfillment of God's long-promised plan.

[[v43|that through his name everyone who believes in him will receive remission of sins]]. "Remission" means forgiveness or release. This is the heart of the gospel: forgiveness of sins comes through Jesus, and it is available to "everyone who believes in him." Not only Jews, but Gentiles. Not only the religious, but anyone. The only condition is believing in Jesus.

Then something remarkable happened: [[v44|While Peter was still speaking these words, the Holy Spirit fell on all those who heard the word]]. Peter had not finished his sermon. He had not given an invitation or laid hands on anyone. As soon as the listeners heard the message of forgiveness through faith in Jesus, God poured out His Spirit on them.

The Jewish believers with Peter were astonished. They heard these Gentiles speaking in tongues and praising God, just as the disciples had at Pentecost. God had given the Gentiles the same gift He had given the Jewish believers.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that salvation comes through faith in Jesus, not through ethnic identity or religious ritual. The Gentiles received the Spirit before they were circumcised or baptized, showing that God accepted them on the basis of faith alone. Peter later argued at the Jerusalem Council that God [[Acts 15:9|made no distinction between us and them, cleansing their hearts by faith]].

They also show that God takes the initiative. The Spirit fell on the listeners while Peter was still speaking. God did not wait for the church to approve the Gentiles. He acted first, and the church followed by baptizing them.

Finally, the verses show the unity of the church. The same Spirit given to Jewish believers at Pentecost was given to Gentile believers in Caesarea. Paul writes that in one Spirit believers were all baptized into one body, [[1 Corinthians 12:13|whether Jews or Greeks]].

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to share the simple message of forgiveness through faith in Jesus. The gospel is good news for everyone. Believers can share it with confidence, trusting that God's Spirit works through His word.

They also give assurance to anyone who believes. Forgiveness is not earned by good works or religious rituals. It is received by faith in Jesus. Believers can rest in this assurance, knowing that their sins are forgiven.

Finally, these verses challenge believers to welcome those whom God has accepted. The Jewish believers were amazed that God had poured out His Spirit on the Gentiles. Believers today may also be surprised at whom God chooses to save. When God works in someone's life, the church is called to welcome them fully.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Acts 10 shows that God shows no favoritism. He welcomes people from every nation who turn to Him through Jesus. Believers are called to reject every form of prejudice and to welcome all people as equally loved by God. Churches can reflect this by being places where people of every background feel at home.

The chapter also shows how God prepares hearts and directs His people. He spoke to Cornelius and to Peter at the same time, bringing them together for His purposes. Believers can trust that God is at work in the lives of people around them, and they can be attentive to His leading as they share their faith.

Acts 10 reminds believers that God sometimes challenges their assumptions. Peter had to unlearn deeply held beliefs about who was clean and unclean. Believers can be open to God's correction through His word, allowing Him to expand their understanding of His love and grace.

Finally, the chapter celebrates the heart of the gospel: through Jesus, everyone who believes receives forgiveness of sins. Jesus went about doing good, died, rose again, and now offers salvation to all. Believers can share this message with everyone, trusting that God's Spirit can fall on anyone who hears and believes.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord God, thank You that You show no favoritism, and that in every nation You welcome those who turn to You. Forgive me for the times I have looked at others as less worthy of Your love. Teach me not to call anyone unclean whom You have cleansed. Thank You for preparing Cornelius's heart and Peter's heart at the same time; prepare my heart and lead me to those who are seeking You. Lord Jesus, thank You that You went about doing good and healing all who were oppressed. Fill me with Your Spirit so that I may do good as You did. Thank You for the forgiveness of sins that comes to everyone who believes in You. Make Your church a place where people of every nation are welcomed and loved. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
