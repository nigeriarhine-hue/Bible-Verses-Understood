# Acts 5
theme: Holiness in the Church and Joy in Suffering for Jesus' Name
main: Ananias and Sapphira lie to the Holy Spirit about a gift they claim to have given in full, and both fall dead, filling the church with holy fear. The apostles perform many signs, multitudes believe, and when the jealous high priest jails them, an angel sets them free to keep preaching. Brought before the council again, Peter declares that God has exalted the crucified Jesus as Prince and Savior. The Pharisee Gamaliel warns the council that if this movement is from God, they cannot stop it. The apostles are flogged and leave rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer for Jesus' name.

## Chapter Background and Story

Acts 4 ended with a picture of a generous, united church, highlighted by Barnabas, who sold a field and gave the money to the apostles. Acts 5 opens with a shocking contrast: a couple who pretend to be as generous as Barnabas while secretly holding back. The chapter then returns to the growing conflict between the apostles and the Jerusalem authorities. Throughout, Luke shows that the church faces danger from within, through hypocrisy, and from without, through persecution, and that God protects His church in both.

### Ananias and Sapphira (5:1–11)

Ananias and his wife Sapphira sold a piece of property. They kept back part of the money and brought the rest to the apostles, presenting it as if it were the full amount. Sapphira knew about the plan. Peter's response is studied below.

When Peter confronted Ananias, he fell down and died. [[v5|Great fear came on all who heard these things]]. The young men wrapped his body and buried him. About three hours later, Sapphira came in, unaware of what had happened. Peter gave her an opportunity to tell the truth: [[v8|Tell me whether you sold the land for so much]]. She answered, [[v8|Yes, for so much]], repeating the lie. Peter said, [[v9|How is it that you have agreed together to tempt the Spirit of the Lord?]] She too fell down and died.

This is one of the most sobering stories in the New Testament. Many readers find it troubling. Several things help explain it. First, the sin was not keeping part of the money, which was entirely their right, but lying to God and to the church to gain a reputation for generosity they did not deserve. Second, this happened at the very beginning of the church's life, when God's presence was being powerfully revealed, much as Nadab and Abihu were struck down at the beginning of Israel's worship in the tabernacle (Leviticus 10:1–2), and Achan was judged at the beginning of Israel's conquest of the land (Joshua 7). Third, God's judgment here was unusual, not His normal way of dealing with believers' sins; elsewhere in Acts and the letters, sin in the church is addressed through confrontation, discipline, and restoration. The story shows how seriously God takes hypocrisy and deception among His people.

[[v11|Great fear came on the whole assembly, and on all who heard these things]]. This is the first time Luke uses the word "assembly" (often translated "church") in Acts. The holy fear that came on the church was not terror of a cruel God, but a deep reverence for His holiness.

### Signs and Growth (5:12–16)

The apostles performed many signs and wonders. The believers met together [[v12|with one accord in Solomon’s porch]]. Luke notes that [[v13|None of the rest dared to join them; however, the people honored them]]. After what happened to Ananias and Sapphira, no one joined the church casually. Yet [[v14|More believers were added to the Lord, multitudes of both men and women]].

People brought the sick into the streets, hoping that even Peter's shadow would fall on them. Crowds came from towns around Jerusalem, bringing the sick and those tormented by unclean spirits, [[v16|and they were all healed]]. This recalls the ministry of Jesus, when people sought to touch even the fringe of His garment (Mark 6:56). The same power that worked through Jesus now worked through His apostles.

### Arrest and Angelic Release (5:17–26)

The high priest and the Sadducees [[v18|laid hands on the apostles, then put them in public custody]]. Luke says they were [[v17|filled with jealousy]]. The apostles' popularity threatened their authority.

But [[v19|an angel of the Lord opened the prison doors by night, and brought them out]]. The irony is striking: the Sadducees did not believe in angels (Acts 23:8), yet an angel released their prisoners. The angel commanded, [[v20|Go stand and speak in the temple to the people all the words of this life]]. The gospel is a message of life.

At daybreak, the apostles were back in the temple teaching. When the council met and sent for the prisoners, the officers reported, [[v23|We found the prison shut and locked, and the guards standing before the doors, but when we opened them, we found no one inside!]] The leaders were baffled. Then someone reported that the men were standing in the temple teaching the people. The captain brought them back, but without force, because the officers feared the people might stone them.

### Before the Council Again (5:27–33)

The high priest said, [[v28|Didn’t we strictly command you not to teach in this name? Behold, you have filled Jerusalem with your teaching, and intend to bring this man’s blood on us]]. His words were an unintentional compliment: the apostles had filled Jerusalem with their teaching. And the leaders, who had once said of Jesus, [[Matthew 27:25|May his blood be on us and on our children!]], now resented being blamed for His death.

Peter and the apostles answered, [[v29|We must obey God rather than men]], restating the principle they had expressed before (4:19–20). Then they proclaimed the gospel again, studied below. The council was furious and wanted to kill them.

### Gamaliel's Advice (5:34–40)

A Pharisee named Gamaliel, [[v34|a teacher of the law, honored by all the people]], stood up. Gamaliel was one of the most respected rabbis of his time, and the apostle Paul had studied under him (Acts 22:3). He had the apostles removed and gave the council advice, studied below.

The council accepted his advice. But they still had the apostles flogged, probably with the thirty-nine lashes prescribed by Jewish law (Deuteronomy 25:3; 2 Corinthians 11:24), and ordered them again not to speak in Jesus' name. The apostles' response is studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### Acts 5:3–4 — You Have Not Lied to Men, but to God

verse: Acts 5:3-4

#### Simple Explanation

The early church was marked by remarkable generosity. Believers sold property and gave the money to the apostles so that no one would be in need. Barnabas had just been praised for selling a field and giving the money (4:36–37). Ananias and his wife Sapphira also sold a piece of property, but they kept part of the money for themselves while pretending to give it all.

Peter, apparently given insight by the Holy Spirit, confronted Ananias: [[v3|Ananias, why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit and to keep back part of the price of the land?]] Peter identified the source of the deception: Satan had influenced Ananias's heart. Yet Ananias was still responsible, as Peter's next words show.

[[v4|While you kept it, didn’t it remain your own? After it was sold, wasn’t it in your power?]] Peter made it clear that Ananias was under no obligation to sell the land or to give away the money. The property was his. The giving in the early church was voluntary. The problem was not that he kept some of the money.

[[v4|How is it that you have conceived this thing in your heart? You haven’t lied to men, but to God]]. The sin was deception. Ananias wanted the reputation of being generous without paying the full cost. He lied to the apostles and the church, but ultimately he lied to God.

Notice that Peter speaks of lying "to the Holy Spirit" in verse 3 and lying "to God" in verse 4. This passage is one of the places in Scripture that shows the Holy Spirit is God.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage reveals how seriously God views hypocrisy. Jesus repeatedly warned against hypocrisy, especially in religious matters. He criticized those who practiced their righteousness to be seen by others (Matthew 6:1–4). Ananias and Sapphira wanted to appear more generous than they were. Their sin was a performance of holiness without its reality.

It also shows that God sees the heart. Ananias could deceive people, but not God. The psalmist writes, [[Psalm 139:4|For there is not a word on my tongue, but, behold, LORD, you know it altogether]]. Nothing is hidden from God's sight (Hebrews 4:13).

Finally, the passage shows that the church is God's holy community. The Holy Spirit dwells in the church (1 Corinthians 3:16–17). To lie within the church is to lie in the presence of God. This calls believers to integrity and honesty in their life together.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage challenges believers to examine their motives. It is possible to do good things, such as giving, serving, or praying, in order to be seen and praised by others. God calls His people to serve with sincere hearts, not to build a reputation.

It also calls believers to honesty. Ananias and Sapphira lied about money, but the principle applies to all areas of life. Believers are called to be truthful with God, with the church, and with others, even when the truth is less impressive than a lie.

Finally, the passage reminds believers that giving is voluntary and should be done freely. Peter affirmed that Ananias had the right to keep his property. God does not want gifts given under pressure or for show. Paul writes, [[2 Corinthians 9:7|Let each man give according as he has determined in his heart, not grudgingly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver]].

### Acts 5:30–32 — Prince and Savior

verse: Acts 5:30-32

#### Simple Explanation

The apostles stood before the Sanhedrin for the second time. They had been arrested for preaching about Jesus, miraculously released from prison by an angel, and then found teaching in the temple again. The high priest accused them of disobeying the council's orders. Peter answered that they must obey God rather than men. Then he proclaimed the gospel to the council.

[[v30|The God of our fathers raised up Jesus, whom you killed, hanging him on a tree]]. Peter connected Jesus with the God of Israel's ancestors. The phrase "hanging him on a tree" recalls Deuteronomy 21:23, which says that anyone hanged on a tree is under God's curse. The leaders thought that crucifixion proved Jesus was cursed. But God raised Him. Paul later explains that Christ became a curse for us to redeem us from the curse of the law (Galatians 3:13).

[[v31|God exalted him with his right hand to be a Prince and a Savior]]. "Prince" means leader or ruler. "Savior" means rescuer. The One the council rejected had been exalted by God to the highest position of authority and given the role of saving His people.

[[v31|to give repentance to Israel, and remission of sins]]. Notice that repentance itself is described as a gift. Jesus gives both the ability to turn from sin and the forgiveness that follows. Even the council members who had condemned Jesus could receive this gift.

[[v32|We are his witnesses of these things; and so also is the Holy Spirit, whom God has given to those who obey him]]. The apostles were eyewitnesses of Jesus' resurrection. The Holy Spirit also bore witness through the miracles and through the transformed lives of believers. The Spirit is given to those who obey God, which in this context means those who respond to the gospel with faith.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses summarize the core of the apostles' preaching: Jesus was killed, God raised Him, exalted Him, and now offers repentance and forgiveness through Him. This pattern appears throughout Acts. It shows that the resurrection and exaltation of Jesus are the foundation of the gospel.

They also show that repentance is a gift of God's grace. People cannot turn from sin by their own strength alone. God grants repentance (Acts 11:18; 2 Timothy 2:25), enabling people to turn to Him. This gives hope even for those who seem far from God.

Finally, the verses reveal the double witness to Jesus: human witnesses and the Holy Spirit. Jesus had promised that the Spirit would testify about Him and that the disciples would also testify (John 15:26–27). The Christian witness is never merely human; the Spirit works through it.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses remind believers of the heart of the gospel message. When believers share their faith, they can focus on what God has done in Jesus: His death, His resurrection, and His offer of forgiveness.

They also encourage believers to pray for those who seem resistant to the gospel. The council members were hostile, yet Peter offered them repentance and forgiveness. God can give repentance to anyone. Believers can pray that God would soften hearts and grant repentance to those they love.

Finally, these verses invite believers to rely on the Holy Spirit's witness. Believers are not alone when they share Christ. The Spirit works alongside them, confirming the truth and drawing people to Jesus.

### Acts 5:38–39 — If It Is of God

verse: Acts 5:38-39

#### Simple Explanation

The council was so enraged by the apostles' testimony that they wanted to kill them. Then Gamaliel, a highly respected Pharisee and teacher of the law, stood up and urged caution. He had the apostles taken outside and spoke to the council.

He reminded them of two earlier movements. A man named Theudas had gathered about four hundred followers, but he was killed and his followers scattered. Then Judas of Galilee had led a revolt at the time of the census, but he too was killed and his followers dispersed. (The Jewish historian Josephus also mentions a Judas the Galilean who led a revolt against Roman taxation around A.D. 6.)

From these examples, Gamaliel drew his conclusion: [[v38|Now I tell you, withdraw from these men and leave them alone. For if this counsel or this work is of men, it will be overthrown]]. If the movement was merely human, it would collapse on its own, like the movements of Theudas and Judas.

[[v39|But if it is of God, you will not be able to overthrow it, and you would be found even to be fighting against God!]] If the movement truly came from God, no human power could stop it, and opposing it would mean opposing God Himself.

The council accepted his advice. They did not kill the apostles, though they still had them beaten.

Gamaliel's advice was wise in some ways. It was cautious, fair, and recognized God's sovereignty. But it was also incomplete. He did not take the further step of examining whether Jesus was truly the Messiah. He chose to wait and see rather than to believe.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Gamaliel's words, though spoken by someone who was not a follower of Jesus, express a deep truth: God's work cannot be stopped by human opposition. The rest of Acts proves it. Despite persecution, imprisonment, beatings, and killings, the gospel spread from Jerusalem to Rome. Jesus had promised, [[Matthew 16:18|I will build my assembly, and the gates of Hades will not prevail against it]].

The passage also shows how God protects His people, sometimes through unexpected means. God used a respected Pharisee to save the apostles' lives. God can use people who do not share believers' faith to accomplish His purposes and protect His church.

Finally, Gamaliel's warning about "fighting against God" applies to everyone. One of Gamaliel's own students, Saul of Tarsus, would soon fight against the church, until Jesus confronted him on the road to Damascus and asked, [[Acts 9:4|why do you persecute me?]].

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage gives believers confidence. When the church faces opposition, believers can remember that God's work cannot be overthrown. Churches, movements, and individuals may struggle, but God's purposes will prevail.

It also encourages humility in judging new movements or ideas. Gamaliel's caution can be a helpful principle: time often reveals whether something is from God. At the same time, believers are called to test everything by Scripture, not merely to wait (1 Thessalonians 5:21; 1 John 4:1).

Finally, the passage challenges anyone who is neutral about Jesus. Gamaliel recognized that the apostles' movement might be from God, but he did not commit himself. Waiting to see is not the same as believing. Each person must decide how to respond to Jesus.

### Acts 5:41–42 — Rejoicing to Suffer for His Name

verse: Acts 5:41-42

#### Simple Explanation

After Gamaliel's advice, the council decided not to kill the apostles. But they did not let them go unpunished. They had them flogged. Jewish flogging was severe; it often consisted of thirty-nine lashes, which could leave the back torn and bleeding. Then the council commanded them again not to speak in the name of Jesus and released them.

How did the apostles respond? [[v41|They therefore departed from the presence of the council, rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer dishonor for Jesus’ name]]. They rejoiced. They did not consider their suffering a disgrace but an honor. They were "counted worthy" to suffer for the One who had suffered for them.

This was not a strange reaction for the followers of Jesus. He had taught, [[Luke 6:22|Blessed are you when men hate you, and when they exclude and mock you, and throw out your name as evil, for the Son of Man’s sake]]. He had said that they should [[Luke 6:23|Rejoice in that day and leap for joy]].

[[v42|Every day, in the temple and at home, they never stopped teaching and preaching Jesus, the Christ]]. The beating did not silence them. They continued teaching publicly in the temple and privately in homes. Their message was simple: Jesus is the Christ.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal the transforming power of the gospel. The apostles had once fled in fear when Jesus was arrested. Now they rejoiced after being flogged for Him. This change was the work of the risen Christ and the Holy Spirit.

They also show that suffering for Christ is a privilege. Paul writes, [[Philippians 1:29|it has been granted to you on behalf of Christ, not only to believe in him, but also to suffer on his behalf]]. To suffer for Jesus is to share in His sufferings and to be identified with Him. Peter, who was among those beaten, later wrote to believers, [[1 Peter 4:13|But because you are partakers of Christ’s sufferings, rejoice]].

Finally, the verses show the importance of both public and private ministry. The apostles taught in the temple, the public center of Jewish life, and in homes, where believers gathered. The gospel spread through both settings.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers who suffer for their faith. Many Christians around the world face persecution, including imprisonment, beatings, and loss of property. Others face rejection or ridicule. The apostles' example shows that suffering for Christ can be met with joy, not because suffering is pleasant, but because it is an honor to be identified with Him.

They also challenge believers in places of relative safety. If the apostles rejoiced to suffer for Jesus, believers today can examine whether they are willing to accept even small costs for following Him, such as social awkwardness, criticism, or missed opportunities.

Finally, these verses call believers to persistence. The apostles never stopped teaching and preaching Jesus. Believers can continue to share Christ in public and in private, in their communities and in their homes, without giving up when it becomes difficult.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Acts 5 shows that God takes the holiness of His church seriously. Ananias and Sapphira wanted the reputation of generosity without its reality, and their deception was judged. Believers are called to integrity, honesty, and sincerity in their life together. God sees the heart, and He desires truth in the inner being.

The chapter also shows that God's work cannot be stopped. The apostles were arrested, but an angel released them. The council threatened them, but they kept preaching. Even a respected Pharisee recognized that if this movement was from God, no one could overthrow it. Believers can take courage when the church faces opposition, knowing that God's purposes will prevail.

Acts 5 proclaims the heart of the gospel: Jesus, whom people rejected and killed, has been raised and exalted as Prince and Savior, offering repentance and forgiveness. This message is for everyone, even those who have resisted God. Believers can share it with confidence and pray that God would grant repentance to those they love.

Finally, the chapter shows the joy of suffering for Jesus' name. The apostles rejoiced after being flogged, considering it an honor to suffer for Him. They never stopped teaching and preaching Jesus. Believers today can follow their example, accepting the costs of discipleship with joy and continuing faithfully in their witness, both publicly and at home.

## Chapter Prayer

Holy God, You see the heart, and nothing is hidden from You. Forgive me for the times I have tried to look better than I am, or have served to be seen by others. Make me honest and sincere before You and Your people. Thank You for the power of Your Spirit at work in the early church, and thank You that Your work cannot be overthrown by any human opposition. Lord Jesus, thank You that You were raised and exalted as Prince and Savior, and that You give repentance and forgiveness. Grant repentance to those I love who do not yet know You. Give me the joy of the apostles, who rejoiced to suffer for Your name. Help me never to stop speaking of You, in public and in my home. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
