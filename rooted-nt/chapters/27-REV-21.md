# Revelation 21
theme: A New Heaven, a New Earth, and the Holy City
main: John sees a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and earth have passed away, and there is no longer any sea. He sees the holy city, the new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, prepared as a bride beautifully dressed for her husband. A loud voice from the throne announces that God's dwelling place is now among His people: He will live with them, they will be His people, and God Himself will be with them as their God. He will wipe every tear from their eyes, and there will be no more death, mourning, crying, or pain, for the old order of things has passed away. The One on the throne says, I am making everything new, and tells John to write, for these words are trustworthy and true. He is the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End, and He will give water without cost from the spring of the water of life to the thirsty. Those who overcome will inherit all this; He will be their God and they will be His children. But the cowardly, unbelieving, vile, murderers, sexually immoral, those who practice magic arts, idolaters, and all liars will have their place in the lake of fire, the second death. One of the seven angels shows John the bride, the wife of the Lamb, carrying him in the Spirit to a great, high mountain to see the holy city, Jerusalem, coming down from God and shining with His glory. It has a great high wall with twelve gates bearing the names of the twelve tribes of Israel, guarded by twelve angels, and twelve foundations bearing the names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb. The angel measures the city with a golden rod: it is a cube, about 12,000 stadia in length, width, and height, and its wall is 144 cubits thick. The city is pure gold, like clear glass; its foundations are adorned with every kind of precious stone; its twelve gates are twelve pearls; and its great street is pure gold, like transparent glass. John sees no temple in the city, because the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are its temple. It needs no sun or moon, for the glory of God gives it light and the Lamb is its lamp. The nations will walk by its light, and the kings of the earth will bring their splendor into it. Its gates will never be shut, for there will be no night there. Nothing impure will ever enter it, but only those whose names are written in the Lamb's book of life.

## Chapter Background and Story

Revelation 21 brings the story of the Bible to its glorious goal. After the judgments, the battles, and the final defeat of evil, John sees what God has been working toward all along: a new heaven and a new earth, where God dwells with His people forever. This is not an escape from creation, but its renewal. The Bible begins with God creating the heavens and the earth (Genesis 1:1) and ends with God making all things new.

This vision answers every longing of the human heart and every promise of Scripture. The garden of Eden, where God walked with humanity, is restored and surpassed. The tabernacle and temple, where God's presence dwelt among Israel, find their fulfillment in a city where God dwells with all His people. Jerusalem, the city of God, is made new. And the curse that began in Genesis 3 is finally removed.

The vision draws heavily on the prophets, especially Isaiah 65–66, which promised new heavens and a new earth, and Ezekiel 40–48, which described a renewed temple and city. It uses rich, symbolic imagery to describe a reality beyond human imagination. As with the rest of Revelation, the images communicate truth about the beauty, security, and glory of God's eternal home.

### All Things New (21:1–8)

[[v1|I saw a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and the first earth have passed away, and the sea is no more]]. Christians have understood the "new heaven and new earth" in two main ways. Some believe the present creation will be completely destroyed and replaced by a new one. Many others believe that the present creation will be purified and renewed, transformed rather than discarded, just as believers' bodies will be raised and transformed rather than replaced (Romans 8:19–23; 1 Corinthians 15:42–44). Both views affirm that the new creation will be free from sin, death, and every effect of the curse.

"The sea is no more" does not mean that God dislikes oceans. In Revelation and the Old Testament, the sea often symbolizes chaos, danger, and evil. The beast rose out of the sea (13:1). For John, exiled on the island of Patmos, the sea was also what separated him from his churches. In the new creation, everything the sea represented, chaos, threat, and separation, will be gone.

[[v2|I saw the holy city, New Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, prepared like a bride adorned for her husband]]. The holy city "comes down" from heaven. God's people do not climb up to heaven; God brings heaven down to earth. The city is described as a bride, connecting it with the wedding of the Lamb in chapter 19. The city is not merely a place where God's people live; in a sense, it is God's people, the bride of Christ, in their perfected state. Its beauty contrasts with Babylon, the prostitute city.

God's dwelling with His people and His promise to make all things new are studied below.

[[v7|He who overcomes, I will give him these things. I will be his God, and he will be my son]]. The promises to the overcomers in the seven messages (chapters 2–3) are all fulfilled here. The overcomer inherits everything, and the greatest inheritance is a relationship with God as His child. This echoes God's covenant promise to David concerning his son (2 Samuel 7:14), now extended to all God's people.

[[v8|But for the cowardly, unbelieving, sinners, abominable, murderers, sexually immoral, sorcerers, idolaters, and all liars, their part is in the lake that burns with fire and sulfur, which is the second death.”]] This sobering verse reminds readers that the new creation is holy. Those who persist in rejecting God and clinging to evil have no place in it. "The cowardly" likely refers to those who denied Christ under pressure, refusing to stand with Him, in contrast to the overcomers. The list describes the character of those who followed the beast. This verse is not a list to make believers despair, since many believers were once such people and have been washed and made new (1 Corinthians 6:9–11). It is a warning to those who refuse God's invitation, and a call to everyone to turn to Him while there is time.

### The Holy City (21:9–21)

[[v9|One of the seven angels who had the seven bowls which were loaded with the seven last plagues came, and he spoke with me, saying, “Come here. I will show you the bride, the Lamb’s wife.”]] [[v10|He carried me away in the Spirit to a great and high mountain, and showed me the holy city, Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God,]] The same kind of angel who showed John the prostitute (17:1) now shows him the bride. The parallel is deliberate. John was carried into a wilderness to see Babylon; now he is carried to a great, high mountain to see the new Jerusalem, recalling Ezekiel 40:2.

[[v11|having the glory of God. Her light was like a most precious stone, like a jasper stone, clear as crystal;]] The city shines with God's own glory. The jasper stone recalls the appearance of God on His throne (4:3).

[[v12|having a great and high wall with twelve gates, and at the gates twelve angels, and names written on them, which are the names of the twelve tribes of the children of Israel]]. [[v13|On the east were three gates, and on the north three gates, and on the south three gates, and on the west three gates]]. [[v14|The wall of the city had twelve foundations, and on them twelve names of the twelve Apostles of the Lamb]]. The gates bear the names of the twelve tribes of Israel, and the foundations bear the names of the twelve apostles. The city unites the people of God from the old covenant and the new. God's people are one people, built on the foundation of Israel's story and the apostles' witness to Christ (Ephesians 2:19–22). The gates face every direction, open to people from every part of the world.

[[v15|He who spoke with me had for a measure a golden reed to measure the city, its gates, and its walls]]. [[v16|The city is square. Its length is as great as its width. He measured the city with the reed: twelve thousand twelve stadia. Its length, width, and height are equal]]. [[v17|Its wall is one hundred forty-four cubits, by the measure of a man, that is, of an angel]]. Twelve thousand stadia is about 1,400 miles (2,200 kilometers), roughly the size of the known Mediterranean world. The city is a perfect cube. The only other perfect cube in Scripture is the Most Holy Place in Solomon's temple (1 Kings 6:20), where God's presence dwelt. The whole city is now the Most Holy Place. God's presence fills everything. The numbers, twelve thousand and 144 (twelve times twelve), symbolize the completeness of God's people.

[[v18|The construction of its wall was jasper. The city was pure gold, like pure glass]]. [[v19|The foundations of the city’s wall were adorned with all kinds of precious stones. The first foundation was jasper, the second sapphire, the third chalcedony, the fourth emerald,]] [[v20|the fifth sardonyx, the sixth sardius, the seventh chrysolite, the eighth beryl, the ninth topaz, the tenth chrysoprase, the eleventh jacinth, and the twelfth amethyst]]. [[v21|The twelve gates were twelve pearls. Each one of the gates was made of one pearl. The street of the city was pure gold, like transparent glass]]. The materials are dazzling: gold, precious stones, and pearls. The twelve stones resemble the stones on the breastplate of Israel's high priest, which bore the names of the twelve tribes (Exodus 28:17–21). Isaiah also promised that God would rebuild Jerusalem with precious stones (Isaiah 54:11–12). Gold "like transparent glass" describes a purity and beauty beyond anything on earth. These images express the incomparable beauty and value of God's eternal home. What the world treasures most becomes the pavement of God's city.

### The Glory of the City (21:22–27)

The absence of a temple and the light of God's glory are studied below.

[[v24|The nations will walk in its light. The kings of the earth bring the glory and honor of the nations into it]]. [[v25|Its gates will in no way be shut by day (for there will be no night there),]] [[v26|and they shall bring the glory and the honor of the nations into it so that they may enter]]. This is a beautiful picture of the redeemed nations. The kings of the earth, who once served the beast and mourned Babylon, are now seen bringing their glory into God's city. The best of human culture, redeemed and purified, is brought as an offering to God (Isaiah 60:3–11). The gates are never shut, a sign of perfect security; in ancient cities gates were closed at night for protection, but there is no night and no danger.

[[v27|There will in no way enter into it anything profane, or one who causes an abomination or a lie, but only those who are written in the Lamb’s book of life]]. The city is perfectly holy. Nothing impure can enter. Only those whose names are in the Lamb's book of life, those redeemed by His blood, will live there.

## Important Verses Explained

### Revelation 21:3–4 — God Will Dwell with His People

verse: Revelation 21:3-4

#### Simple Explanation

A loud voice from the throne announces: "Behold, God’s dwelling is with people." The word for "dwelling" is related to the word for "tabernacle" or "tent." God will pitch His tent among His people.

The voice continues: "and he will dwell with them, and they will be his people, and God himself will be with them as their God." This is the heart of the new creation: God and His people living together in perfect relationship.

Verse 4 describes what this means: "He will wipe away every tear from their eyes. Death will be no more; neither will there be mourning, nor crying, nor pain any more. The first things have passed away." All the sorrows of this present life will be gone forever.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse fulfills the central promise of the whole Bible. God's covenant promise was always, [[Leviticus 26:12|I will walk among you, and will be your God, and you will be my people]] (see also Jeremiah 31:33; Ezekiel 37:27). In the tabernacle and temple, God dwelt among Israel, but His presence was hidden behind a curtain. In Jesus, [[John 1:14|The Word became flesh, and lived among us]], literally "tabernacled" among us. Through the Holy Spirit, God dwells in His people now. But in the new creation, God's presence will be fully and openly with His people forever.

The tender image of God wiping away every tear is deeply personal. God does not merely end suffering from a distance. He personally comforts each of His children. This promise echoes Isaiah 25:8: [[Isaiah 25:8|He has swallowed up death forever! The Lord GOD will wipe away tears from off all faces]].

The list of things that will be no more, death, mourning, crying, and pain, includes everything that makes this world so hard. These are the effects of sin and the curse. In the new creation, they will be completely removed. "The first things have passed away." The old order of sin and suffering will be gone forever.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage offers deep comfort to all who suffer. Those who grieve the loss of loved ones, those who live with chronic pain, those who weep over injustice, and those who struggle with sorrow can hold on to the promise that God will wipe away every tear. Their suffering is real, but it is not permanent.

This hope does not make present suffering insignificant, and it does not mean believers should hide their grief. Jesus wept at the tomb of Lazarus. But it means that grief is not the end of the story. Believers can grieve with hope.

This passage also reminds believers that the greatest gift of the new creation is God Himself. Heaven is not mainly about streets of gold or the absence of pain, wonderful as those are. It is about being with God. Believers can begin to enjoy His presence now, through prayer, worship, and the indwelling Spirit, as a foretaste of what is to come.

### Revelation 21:5–6 — I Am Making All Things New

verse: Revelation 21:5-6

#### Simple Explanation

For the first time in Revelation, God Himself speaks directly from the throne: "Behold, I am making all things new." God is the one who renews creation. He is not making new things, but making all things new, restoring and transforming what He created.

He tells John: "Write, for these words of God are faithful and true." These promises are completely reliable.

In verse 6, God declares: "I am the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End." He is the source of all things and the goal of all things.

Then He makes an invitation: "I will give freely to him who is thirsty from the spring of the water of life." Life with God is a free gift for all who thirst for it.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

"I am making all things new" echoes Isaiah 43:19 and 65:17, where God promises to do a new thing and create new heavens and a new earth. Paul declared that this renewal has already begun in those who belong to Christ: [[2 Corinthians 5:17|Therefore if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old things have passed away. Behold, all things have become new]]. The new creation that will be fully revealed at the end has already begun in the lives of believers.

The present tense, "I am making," suggests that God's work of renewal is ongoing. He is at work now, and He will complete His work at the end.

The title "the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End" appears at the start of Revelation (1:8) and here near its close. God was there at the beginning of creation, and He will be there at its completion. History is not random. It begins and ends with God.

The offer of the water of life "freely" is an echo of Isaiah 55:1, where God invites everyone who thirsts to come and drink without money. It also echoes Jesus' words to the Samaritan woman and His invitation at the feast in Jerusalem (John 4:10–14; 7:37–38). Eternal life is a gift of grace, not something earned. All that is required is thirst, a recognition of one's need, and coming to God to receive.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can find hope in knowing that God is making all things new. Whatever is broken in their lives, relationships, bodies, or world, God's renewing work is underway and will be completed.

This passage also invites believers to live as part of God's new creation now. Those who are in Christ are already new creations. They can live in the newness of life He gives, leaving behind old patterns of sin and embracing the life of the Spirit.

Finally, the offer of the water of life is for everyone who thirsts. Believers can come to God again and again to receive His life. And they can share this invitation with others, telling them that God's gift is free to all who come.

### Revelation 21:22–23 — No Temple, No Sun

verse: Revelation 21:22-23

#### Simple Explanation

John notices something surprising about the holy city: "I saw no temple in it." In the ancient world, every great city had temples, and Jerusalem's identity was centered on its temple. But the new Jerusalem has none.

The reason is given: "for the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb are its temple." God's presence is not confined to one building. He fills the whole city. The entire city is His dwelling.

Verse 23 adds another surprise: "The city has no need for the sun or moon to shine, for the very glory of God illuminated it and its lamp is the Lamb." God's own glory is the city's light. The Lamb is its lamp.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The temple in Israel was where God's presence dwelt among His people, but access was limited. Only priests could enter the holy place, and only the high priest could enter the Most Holy Place, once a year. The temple was a sign of God's presence, but also of the barrier between a holy God and sinful people.

In the new creation, that barrier is completely gone. There is no need for a temple, because God and the Lamb are present everywhere. Every part of the city is holy, and every person has direct access to God. This fulfills what Jesus began when He said that true worshipers would worship the Father in spirit and truth, not in one location (John 4:21–24), and what was signified when the temple curtain was torn at His death (Matthew 27:51).

Notice that "the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb" are named together as the temple. The Lamb shares fully in God's presence and glory. This is one of many places in Revelation where Jesus is honored as fully divine.

The absence of sun and moon recalls Isaiah 60:19–20: [[Isaiah 60:19|The sun will be no more your light by day, nor will the brightness of the moon give light to you, but the LORD will be your everlasting light, and your God will be your glory]]. In Genesis 1, God created light before He created the sun. He is the true source of light. In the new creation, His glory shines directly. There is no darkness at all.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage reveals the goal of all worship: direct, unhindered fellowship with God. Believers can look forward to a day when nothing stands between them and God. Even now, through Christ, they can draw near to Him with confidence (Hebrews 10:19–22).

The image of God's glory as the city's light invites believers to let God be their light now. In a dark world, they can walk in the light of His presence and reflect His light to others. Jesus said, [[John 8:12|I am the light of the world. He who follows me will not walk in the darkness, but will have the light of life]].

Finally, this passage reminds believers that the center of eternity is God and the Lamb. All of heaven's beauty points to them. Believers can center their lives on God now, making Him their greatest joy, as He will be forever.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Revelation 21 shows believers the goal toward which all of history is moving: a new heaven and a new earth where God dwells with His people forever. This vision is the ultimate hope of every believer. Whatever they face now, this is their future.

The heart of this hope is God's presence. He will dwell with His people, and they will be His people. He will wipe away every tear. Death, mourning, crying, and pain will be no more. Believers can hold on to these promises in times of suffering and grief, knowing that their sorrow is real but temporary.

God is making all things new. This renewal has already begun in those who are in Christ, and it will be completed at the end. Believers can live as part of His new creation now, and they can come to Him again and again for the water of life, which He gives freely to all who thirst.

The holy city reveals the beauty, security, and holiness of God's eternal home. It unites God's people from Israel and the church, welcomes the redeemed from every nation, and is filled with God's glory. There is no temple, because God and the Lamb are present everywhere, and no need for sun, because God's glory is its light.

Finally, the chapter reminds believers that the city is holy. Only those whose names are written in the Lamb's book of life will enter. This calls believers to rest in Christ's grace, to live in holiness, and to extend God's invitation to others, so that they too may drink from the water of life and enter the city of God.

## Chapter Prayer

Almighty God, thank You for the hope of a new heaven and a new earth, where You will dwell with Your people forever. Thank You that You will wipe away every tear, and that death, mourning, crying, and pain will be no more. Comfort all who are grieving and suffering today with this promise. Thank You that You are making all things new, and that in Christ I am already part of Your new creation. When I am thirsty, help me to come to You for the water of life, freely given. Let Your glory be my light, and let the Lamb be my lamp. Make me holy, and help me to invite others into Your city. I long for the day when I will see You face to face and live in Your presence forever. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
