# John 20
theme: The Risen Lord Calls, Sends, and Is Confessed as "My Lord and My God"
main: Early on the first day of the week, Mary Magdalene finds the tomb empty, and Peter and the beloved disciple see the grave clothes lying in place. Weeping outside the tomb, Mary meets the risen Jesus, who calls her by name and sends her to tell the disciples. That evening Jesus appears to the frightened disciples behind locked doors, gives them peace, breathes on them the Holy Spirit, and sends them as the Father sent Him. A week later, the doubting Thomas sees His wounds and confesses, "My Lord and my God!" John then tells his readers why he wrote: so that they may believe and have life in Jesus' name.

## Chapter Background and Story

John 20 tells of the resurrection of Jesus, the event on which the Christian faith stands or falls. Paul writes, [[1 Corinthians 15:17|If Christ has not been raised, your faith is vain; you are still in your sins]]. Each Gospel tells of the empty tomb and of Jesus' appearances, focusing on different witnesses and details. John's account is deeply personal. He describes four people coming to faith in the risen Jesus in different ways: the beloved disciple, who believes when he sees the grave clothes; Mary Magdalene, who recognizes Jesus when He speaks her name; the disciples, who rejoice when they see His hands and side; and Thomas, who moves from doubt to the highest confession in the Gospel.

The resurrection is also studied at Matthew 28, Mark 16, and Luke 24.

### The Empty Tomb (20:1–10)

[[v1|on the first day of the week, Mary Magdalene went early, while it was still dark, to the tomb]]. The first day of the week is Sunday. Jesus had been buried on Friday before sunset, and the Sabbath had passed. Mary Magdalene had been delivered by Jesus from seven demons (Luke 8:2) and had stood at the cross (19:25). Other women came with her, as the other Gospels report, and John hints at this when Mary says "we don't know" (20:2).

She saw that the stone had been removed from the entrance. Assuming that someone had taken the body, she ran to Peter and the beloved disciple: [[v2|They have taken away the Lord out of the tomb, and we don’t know where they have laid him!]] Grave robbery was common enough that, a few years later, a Roman emperor's decree ordered the death penalty for disturbing tombs.

Peter and the other disciple ran to the tomb. The other disciple arrived first, looked in, and saw the linen cloths lying there, but did not enter. Peter, characteristically, went straight in. He saw the linen cloths lying, [[v7|and the cloth that had been on his head, not lying with the linen cloths, but rolled up in a place by itself]]. The grave clothes were not scattered, as they would have been if thieves had hurriedly unwrapped the body. And thieves would hardly have taken the time to unwrap the body and leave behind about seventy-five pounds of costly spices bound into the linen (19:39–40). The scene suggested that Jesus' body had passed out of the wrappings, leaving them in place.

[[v8|So then the other disciple who came first to the tomb also entered in, and he saw and believed]]. He did not yet see Jesus, but he believed that He had risen. John adds honestly, [[v9|For as yet they didn’t know the Scripture, that he must rise from the dead]]. They had not yet understood passages like Psalm 16:10 and Isaiah 53:10–12 that pointed to the resurrection. Their belief came first from the evidence of the tomb; understanding of the Scriptures came later.

### Jesus Appears to Mary Magdalene (20:11–18)

Mary stayed outside the tomb, weeping. When she looked in, she saw two angels in white sitting where Jesus' body had been, one at the head and one at the feet. They asked, [[v13|Woman, why are you weeping?]] She answered that someone had taken her Lord.

Then she turned and saw Jesus standing there, but did not recognize Him. He asked, [[v15|Woman, why are you weeping? Who are you looking for?]] She thought He was the gardener and asked Him to tell her where He had put the body. This moment, and what happened next, is studied below.

Jesus said, [[v17|Don’t hold me, for I haven’t yet ascended to my Father]]. Mary was clinging to Him, perhaps afraid of losing Him again. Jesus gently told her that their relationship would not return to what it had been before. He would ascend to the Father, and a new kind of relationship would begin through the Spirit. He gave her a mission: [[v17|go to my brothers and tell them, ‘I am ascending to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God.’]] For the first time in John, Jesus calls His disciples "my brothers." Through His death and resurrection, His Father has become their Father.

Mary went and announced to the disciples, [[v18|she had seen the Lord]]. In a culture where women's testimony was often considered unreliable in court, the risen Jesus chose a woman to be the first witness of His resurrection. This detail is one reason many historians consider the accounts credible; it is not the kind of detail people would invent if they were creating a story to persuade others.

### Jesus Appears to the Disciples (20:19–23)

That evening, the disciples were gathered behind locked doors, [[v19|for fear of the Jews]], that is, the authorities who had arranged Jesus' death. Suddenly Jesus stood among them and said, [[v19|Peace be to you]]. This was the usual Jewish greeting, shalom, but on His lips it carried new meaning. These disciples had abandoned Him. He did not rebuke them. He gave them peace.

[[v20|he showed them his hands and his side]]. The marks of the nails and spear proved that it was truly Jesus, the same One who had been crucified. His resurrection body was real and physical, yet transformed, able to appear in a locked room. [[v20|The disciples therefore were glad when they saw the Lord]], just as Jesus had promised that their sorrow would turn to joy (16:20–22). Then Jesus commissioned them and breathed on them, studied below.

Jesus also said, [[v23|If you forgive anyone’s sins, they have been forgiven them. If you retain anyone’s sins, they have been retained]]. Christians have understood these words in different ways. Roman Catholic and Orthodox traditions see in them the authority given to the church's ministers to declare forgiveness in the sacrament of confession. Many Protestant traditions understand them as the authority given to the church to proclaim the gospel: when the church announces forgiveness through Christ, those who believe are truly forgiven, and those who reject Christ remain in their sins. All agree that only God can forgive sins (Mark 2:7), and that the church's role is to declare and administer the forgiveness that God gives through Christ.

### Thomas Sees and Believes (20:24–29)

Thomas, one of the Twelve, was not with them when Jesus came. The others told him, [[v25|We have seen the Lord!]] But Thomas would not believe without seeing and touching the wounds himself. A week later, Jesus appeared again, with Thomas present. This scene is studied below.

### The Purpose of the Gospel (20:30–31)

John concludes this chapter by explaining why he wrote his Gospel. This is studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### John 20:15–16 — "Mary"

verse: John 20:15-16

#### Simple Explanation

Mary Magdalene had come to the tomb before dawn. She found it empty and assumed someone had stolen Jesus' body. After Peter and the other disciple went home, she stayed outside the tomb, weeping. She had seen Jesus die. Now it seemed that even His body had been taken from her.

She turned around and saw a man standing there. [[v15|Jesus said to her, “Woman, why are you weeping? Who are you looking for?”]] His question gently invited her to express her grief. Notice the words "Who are you looking for?" Jesus asked the same question at the beginning of His ministry to His first disciples (1:38) and at His arrest (18:4).

Mary did not recognize Him. Perhaps her eyes were blurred with tears, or it was still dim, or Jesus' resurrection body was not immediately recognizable, as was true for the disciples on the road to Emmaus (Luke 24:16). [[v15|She, supposing him to be the gardener]], asked Him to tell her where He had put the body so she could take it away.

Then [[v16|Jesus said to her, “Mary.”]] One word, her name, spoken in the voice she knew. Instantly, she recognized Him. [[v16|She turned and said to him, “Rabboni!” which is to say, “Teacher!”]] "Rabboni" is an Aramaic word meaning "my teacher," a warm and personal form of address.

Mary went from deepest grief to overwhelming joy in a single moment, because Jesus called her by name.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This scene beautifully fulfills Jesus' words about the Good Shepherd: [[John 10:3|He calls his own sheep by name and leads them out]]. Mary recognized the Shepherd when He spoke her name. Faith is not only accepting facts about Jesus; it is recognizing His voice and responding to Him personally.

It also shows the tenderness of the risen Lord. His first recorded appearance in John was not to a powerful leader or to the apostles, but to a grieving woman who had been delivered from much (Luke 8:2). He came to the one who loved Him and stayed when others went home.

The setting in a garden is also significant. Humanity's story began in a garden, where sin brought death. Now, in a garden, the risen Jesus appears, the beginning of new creation. Mary's mistake in thinking He was the gardener holds a deeper truth: He is the One who will restore God's creation.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage brings comfort to those who grieve. Mary's tears were real, and Jesus did not dismiss them. He came to her in her grief and asked about her sorrow. Believers who are weeping can know that the risen Lord sees them and comes near.

It also reminds believers that Jesus knows each of them personally. He calls His sheep by name. Believers are not anonymous faces in a crowd. The Lord knows their names, their stories, and their needs.

Finally, the passage encourages believers to listen for Jesus' voice. Sometimes believers, like Mary, are so overwhelmed by grief or confusion that they do not recognize Jesus' presence. He speaks through His word, His Spirit, and His people. When believers hear His voice, they can turn to Him in faith and joy.

### John 20:21–22 — "As the Father Has Sent Me, Even So I Send You"

verse: John 20:21-22

#### Simple Explanation

It was the evening of the first Easter. The disciples were gathered behind locked doors, afraid of the authorities. Jesus suddenly stood among them, said "Peace be to you," and showed them the wounds in His hands and side. The disciples were filled with joy.

Then Jesus said again, [[v21|Peace be to you. As the Father has sent me, even so I send you]]. Throughout John's Gospel, Jesus described Himself as the One sent by the Father. He came into the world to reveal the Father, to do His will, and to give His life for the world's salvation. Now He sends His disciples into the world in the same way. They would continue His mission.

The pattern of Jesus' sending shapes the disciples' sending. He was sent in love, in humility, in obedience, and in dependence on the Father. The disciples would go in the same spirit. Jesus had already prayed, [[John 17:18|As you sent me into the world, even so I have sent them into the world]].

[[v22|When he had said this, he breathed on them, and said to them, “Receive the Holy Spirit!”]] The act of breathing recalls the creation of the first man, when God [[Genesis 2:7|breathed into his nostrils the breath of life]]. It also recalls Ezekiel's vision of the valley of dry bones, where God's breath brought the dead to life (Ezekiel 37:9–10). Jesus was giving new life and the power for mission through the Spirit.

Some see this as a first giving of the Spirit, followed by the fuller outpouring at Pentecost (Acts 2). Others see it as a symbolic act pointing forward to Pentecost. In either case, Jesus links the mission of the disciples with the gift of the Spirit.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage is John's version of the Great Commission (compare Matthew 28:19–20). Its emphasis is on the pattern of mission: as the Father sent the Son, so the Son sends His people. Christian mission is a continuation of the mission of Jesus, carried out in His way and by His Spirit.

It also shows that mission flows from peace. Twice Jesus said, "Peace be to you." The disciples were not sent out from fear or guilt, but from the peace that Jesus had won for them through His death and resurrection. Those who have peace with God are sent to share it.

Finally, the breathing of the Spirit points to new creation. The resurrection of Jesus begins God's renewal of all things. The Spirit makes believers new creations (2 Corinthians 5:17) and empowers them to bring Christ's life to the world.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage reminds believers that they are sent people. Every follower of Jesus is called to continue His mission in their own setting, whether at home, at work, at school, or across the world. Mission is not only for a few specialists; it is the calling of the whole church.

It also shapes how believers carry out their mission. Jesus was sent in humility, love, and obedience. Believers are called to serve others, speak truth with grace, and depend on the Father, just as Jesus did.

Finally, the passage encourages believers to rely on the Holy Spirit. The disciples were afraid and hiding behind locked doors. Jesus gave them peace and the Spirit. Believers who feel fearful or inadequate can trust that the same Spirit empowers them for the task to which they are sent.

### John 20:27–29 — "My Lord and My God!"

verse: John 20:27-29

#### Simple Explanation

Thomas had not been with the other disciples when Jesus first appeared to them. When they told him, "We have seen the Lord!" he refused to believe. He said that unless he saw the nail marks in Jesus' hands, put his finger into them, and put his hand into His side, he would not believe.

Thomas is often called "Doubting Thomas," but he was not unusually skeptical. The other disciples had not believed the women's report either (Luke 24:11). Thomas wanted the same evidence the others had received. Earlier in the Gospel, he had shown courage, saying, "Let's also go, that we may die with him" (11:16), and honesty, asking, "How can we know the way?" (14:5).

A week later, the disciples were together again, and Thomas was with them. Jesus came, though the doors were locked, and stood among them. He turned to Thomas: [[v27|Reach here your finger, and see my hands. Reach here your hand, and put it into my side. Don’t be unbelieving, but believing]]. Jesus knew exactly what Thomas had said. He offered him the very evidence he had asked for. He did not reject Thomas for his doubts; He met him in them.

[[v28|Thomas answered him, “My Lord and my God!”]] John does not say whether Thomas actually touched Jesus' wounds. What he records is Thomas's confession, the greatest confession of faith in the Gospel. Thomas called Jesus not only "Lord" but "God."

Jesus responded, [[v29|Because you have seen me, you have believed. Blessed are those who have not seen and have believed]]. Jesus pronounced a special blessing on all future believers who would trust Him without seeing Him physically.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Thomas's confession forms the climax of John's Gospel. The Gospel began with the declaration, [[John 1:1|the Word was God]]. Now a disciple confesses that same truth to Jesus' face: "My Lord and my God!" Jesus accepted this worship without correction, confirming His divine identity.

It also shows Jesus' patience with honest doubt. Thomas's doubt was not hardened unbelief but a struggle to believe something extraordinary. Jesus met him with gentleness and evidence. Doubt can become the doorway to deeper faith when it is brought honestly to Jesus.

Finally, the blessing on those who have not seen speaks to every reader of John's Gospel. Peter later wrote to believers, [[1 Peter 1:8|whom, not having known, you love. In him, though now you don’t see him, yet believing, you rejoice greatly with joy that is unspeakable and full of glory]]. Faith that rests on the testimony of eyewitnesses is not second-class; it is blessed by Jesus Himself.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage encourages believers who struggle with doubt. Many Christians experience seasons of questioning. Thomas's story shows that Jesus does not reject those who doubt. He meets them where they are. Believers can bring their questions honestly to Him, and to trusted fellow believers, and seek Him with an open heart.

It also invites every reader to make Thomas's confession their own. It is not enough to know that Jesus rose from the dead as a historical fact. Thomas's words are personal: "My Lord and my God." Faith involves surrendering to Jesus as Lord and worshiping Him as God.

Finally, Jesus' blessing gives believers confidence. Though they have not seen Jesus with their eyes, their faith is blessed. They believe through the testimony of those who saw, preserved in Scripture, and confirmed by the Spirit in their hearts.

### John 20:31 — That You May Believe and Have Life

verse: John 20:31

#### Simple Explanation

After recording Thomas's confession and Jesus' blessing on those who believe without seeing, John explained why he wrote his Gospel. He first acknowledged that he had not recorded everything: [[v30|Therefore Jesus did many other signs in the presence of his disciples, which are not written in this book]]. John selected certain signs and teachings for a specific purpose.

[[v31|but these are written that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God]]. John's purpose was to lead readers to faith. He wanted them to believe two things about Jesus. First, that He is "the Christ," the promised Messiah of Israel, the anointed King. Second, that He is "the Son of God," sharing the Father's nature and revealing Him.

Some ancient manuscripts have a verb form that could mean "that you may continue to believe." This suggests that John may have written both to bring unbelievers to faith and to strengthen believers in their faith. Both purposes are fulfilled through his Gospel.

[[v31|and that believing you may have life in his name]]. The goal of believing is life, eternal life, the life of God shared with His people. This life is found "in his name," meaning through Jesus Himself and all that He is and has done.

This verse is the key to understanding the whole Gospel. Every sign, conversation, and teaching John recorded was chosen to help readers believe in Jesus and receive life.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse reveals that the Bible is written with a purpose. John did not write simply to record history or to satisfy curiosity. He wrote so that readers would encounter Jesus and believe. Scripture is God's means of bringing people to faith. Paul writes that [[Romans 10:17|faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God]].

It also shows that belief and life are connected. In John's Gospel, believing is not merely agreeing with information. It is trusting Jesus personally and receiving Him. Those who believe receive the life He gives.

Finally, the verse summarizes the identity of Jesus as the whole Gospel presents it. Through seven signs, seven "I am" sayings, many conversations, and His death and resurrection, John has shown that Jesus is the Christ and the Son of God.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites every reader of John's Gospel to respond. John has presented the evidence. The question is whether each reader will believe. Those who have not yet trusted Jesus are invited to believe in Him and receive life.

It also encourages believers to read the Gospel of John as a means of strengthening their faith. Returning to John's account of Jesus' signs and words can deepen trust, renew love, and refresh the soul.

Finally, the verse encourages believers to share the Gospel with others. John wrote so that others would believe. Believers can share the story of Jesus, invite friends to read the Gospels, and trust that God uses His word to bring people to life.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

John 20 proclaims that Jesus is risen. This changes everything. Death is not the end. Sin has been defeated. The One who was crucified is alive and reigns. Believers can live with hope, courage, and joy because the tomb is empty and Jesus lives.

The chapter shows that the risen Jesus meets people personally. He called Mary by name in her grief, gave peace to frightened disciples behind locked doors, and offered Thomas the evidence he needed for his doubts. Believers who are grieving, afraid, or struggling with doubt can know that Jesus comes to them as well, meeting them in their particular need.

John 20 also reminds believers that they are sent. As the Father sent Jesus, Jesus sends His followers into the world, empowered by the Holy Spirit. The mission of Jesus continues through His people as they share His peace, proclaim forgiveness, and live out His love in every part of life.

Finally, the chapter calls each reader to make Thomas's confession their own: "My Lord and my God!" John wrote his Gospel so that readers would believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and have life in His name. Believers are blessed when they trust Him without seeing, and they can keep returning to His word to deepen that faith and share it with others.

## Chapter Prayer

Risen Lord Jesus, thank You that the tomb is empty and that You are alive. Thank You for calling Mary by name in her grief, and for knowing my name too. When I weep, help me to hear Your voice and turn to You. Thank You for coming to Your frightened disciples with peace, and for showing them Your wounds. Breathe Your Holy Spirit on me, and send me into the world as the Father sent You. When I struggle with doubt, meet me as You met Thomas, and lead me to deeper faith. I confess with Thomas that You are my Lord and my God. Thank You for the blessing You give to those who have not seen and yet believe. Strengthen my faith through Your word, and help me to share it so that others may believe and have life in Your name. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
