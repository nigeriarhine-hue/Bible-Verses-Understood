# Romans 13
theme: Honoring Authorities, Owing Love, and Waking Up to the Day
main: Paul teaches that governing authorities exist under God's ordering and serve as His servants for the good of society, rewarding what is good and restraining evil, so believers should submit to them, pay taxes, and give respect and honor. The only debt believers should always carry is the debt of love, because love does no harm to a neighbor and fulfills the whole Law. Paul then urges believers to wake up, because the night is almost over and the day of salvation is near. They are to cast off the works of darkness, put on the armor of light, and clothe themselves with the Lord Jesus Christ.

## Chapter Background and Story

Romans 13 continues Paul's practical teaching about how believers should live in response to God's mercy. In chapter 12, he described life within the church and how to respond to those who wrong us. Now he turns to life in society: relationships with government, financial obligations, love for neighbors, and living in light of Christ's return.

When Paul wrote this letter, the Roman emperor was Nero. In the early years of his reign (A.D. 54–59), Nero's government was considered relatively stable and fair. His persecution of Christians came later, after the fire of Rome in A.D. 64. Paul himself benefited from Roman law at times, appealing to his citizenship for protection. Yet Paul also knew that rulers could be unjust; he had been beaten by Roman magistrates in Philippi.

Romans 13:1–7 has been one of the most debated passages in Christian history regarding the relationship between believers and government. It must be read alongside the whole of Scripture, including passages where God's people rightly refused to obey rulers who commanded sin (Exodus 1:17; Daniel 3; 6; Acts 4:19–20; 5:29), and Revelation 13, which portrays a government that has become beastly and persecutes God's people.

### Submission to Governing Authorities (13:1–7)

Paul's teaching on authority is studied below. He continues: [[v2|Therefore he who resists the authority withstands the ordinance of God; and those who withstand will receive to themselves judgment]]. Rebelling against legitimate authority in general is resisting God's order.

[[v3|For rulers are not a terror to the good work, but to the evil]]. Paul describes the purpose of government: to encourage good and restrain evil. [[v4|for he is a servant of God to you for good. But if you do that which is evil, be afraid, for he doesn’t bear the sword in vain; for he is a servant of God, an avenger for wrath to him who does evil]]. The "sword" represents the government's authority to punish wrongdoing. In chapter 12, Paul told believers not to take personal revenge but to leave it to God's wrath (12:19). Here, he shows that one way God exercises justice in the present is through governing authorities.

[[v5|Therefore you need to be in subjection, not only because of the wrath, but also for conscience’ sake]]. Believers submit not only to avoid punishment, but because their conscience recognizes that God has established government.

[[v6|For this reason you also pay taxes, for they are servants of God’s service, continually doing this very thing]]. Paying taxes is part of honoring God's order. Jesus said, [[Matthew 22:21|Give therefore to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's]]. [[v7|Therefore give everyone what you owe: if you owe taxes, pay taxes; if customs, then customs; if respect, then respect; if honor, then honor]].

### The Debt of Love (13:8–10)

Paul then turns from what believers owe to the government to what they owe everyone. This is studied below.

### Wake Up! The Day Is Near (13:11–14)

Paul's final appeal is studied below in two parts. In between, he describes the kind of behavior that belongs to the night: [[v13|Let’s walk properly, as in the day; not in reveling and drunkenness, not in sexual promiscuity and lustful acts, and not in strife and jealousy]]. Notice that Paul places "strife and jealousy" alongside drunkenness and sexual immorality. Sins of attitude and relationship are as serious as sins of the body.

This passage has a famous place in church history. In A.D. 386, a young man named Augustine was struggling with his sinful lifestyle and his inability to change. Sitting in a garden in Milan, he heard a child's voice chanting, "Take up and read." He opened a copy of Paul's letters and read Romans 13:13–14. He later wrote that he needed to read no further, because a light of peace seemed to flood his heart and the darkness of his doubt vanished. Augustine became one of the most influential Christian thinkers in history.

## Important Verses Explained

### Romans 13:1 — No Authority Except from God

verse: Romans 13:1

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been describing how believers should live: offering themselves to God, loving one another, and responding to evil with good. Now he addresses how believers should relate to the government.

[[v1|Let every soul be in subjection to the higher authorities]]. "Every soul" means every person, including believers. "Higher authorities" refers to governing officials, those with civil authority. To be "in subjection" means to recognize their authority and cooperate with them.

[[v1|for there is no authority except from God]]. Paul explains why. All authority ultimately comes from God. Governments exist because God has ordained that human society should have order. Even rulers who do not know God hold their positions under His sovereign permission. Jesus told Pilate, [[John 19:11|You would have no power at all against me, unless it were given to you from above]].

[[v1|and those who exist are ordained by God]]. The existing authorities have been put in place by God's ordering. This does not mean that God approves of everything rulers do. It means that the institution of government is part of God's design for human society, and that God is sovereign over who holds power.

This teaching would have been important for the believers in Rome. Some might have thought that because they belonged to Christ's kingdom, they had no obligations to earthly rulers. Others might have been tempted to join the Jewish revolutionary movements that were growing at the time. Paul teaches that believers should be good citizens.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that God is sovereign over all human authority. Daniel declared that God [[Daniel 2:21|removes kings, and sets up kings]]. Earthly rulers are not ultimate; they are accountable to God.

It also shows that government has a God-given purpose: to promote good and restrain evil (13:3–4). When governments fulfill this purpose, they serve God, whether they know it or not.

Finally, the verse must be read in the context of all Scripture. Submission to authorities is the general rule, but it has limits. When rulers command what God forbids, believers must obey God rather than human authorities (Acts 5:29). The Hebrew midwives disobeyed Pharaoh's command to kill babies (Exodus 1:17), Daniel continued to pray despite the king's decree (Daniel 6), and the apostles kept preaching when ordered to stop (Acts 4:19–20). Believers throughout history have rightly resisted governments that committed grave injustice. Even then, believers are called to act with respect and without hatred, accepting the consequences when necessary. Christians also hold different views on how far believers may go in resisting unjust governments, and on matters such as participation in military or political action.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to be good citizens. Believers can obey laws, pay taxes, respect officials, and contribute to the common good. Their faith should make them better members of society.

It also encourages believers to pray for those in authority. Paul writes, [[1 Timothy 2:1|I exhort therefore, first of all, that petitions, prayers, intercessions, and givings of thanks be made for all men]], [[1 Timothy 2:2|for kings and all who are in high places, that we may lead a tranquil and quiet life in all godliness and reverence]]. Believers can pray for leaders regardless of their political views.

Finally, the verse reminds believers that their ultimate allegiance is to God. Earthly authorities are under God's authority. When human commands conflict with God's commands, believers must follow God. This calls for wisdom, courage, and humility.

### Romans 13:8–10 — Love Fulfills the Law

verse: Romans 13:8-10

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had just told believers to give everyone what they owe: taxes, revenue, respect, and honor. Now he speaks of one debt that can never be fully paid.

[[v8|Owe no one anything, except to love one another]]. Believers should pay their debts and not leave obligations unpaid. But there is one debt they will always owe: love. No matter how much they love others, they can never say, "I have loved enough." Love is an ongoing debt.

[[v8|for he who loves his neighbor has fulfilled the law]]. Love fulfills what the Law requires. The purpose of God's commands is love.

[[v9|For the commandments, “You shall not commit adultery,” “You shall not murder,” “You shall not steal,” “You shall not covet,” and whatever other commandments there are, are all summed up in this saying, namely, “You shall love your neighbor as yourself.”]] Paul quotes several of the Ten Commandments that deal with relationships with others. All of them are summed up in the command from Leviticus 19:18 to love one's neighbor as oneself. Jesus taught the same: the whole Law hangs on love for God and love for neighbor (Matthew 22:37–40).

[[v10|Love doesn’t harm a neighbor. Love therefore is the fulfillment of the law]]. Love never does wrong to another person. Someone who truly loves will not commit adultery, murder, steal, or covet. Love naturally fulfills these commands, not because it is following rules, but because it seeks the good of others.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that love is the heart of God's Law. The commandments are not arbitrary rules; they describe what love looks like. When believers love, they fulfill God's purposes. Paul writes, [[Galatians 5:14|For the whole law is fulfilled in one word, in this: “You shall love your neighbor as yourself.”]]

They also show that love is more than a feeling. Love is defined by action: it does no harm to a neighbor. It keeps promises, respects life, honors property, and is content rather than covetous.

Finally, these verses show how believers fulfill the Law without being "under the Law." Believers are not saved by keeping the Law (3:28), but through the Spirit, they fulfill the Law's righteous requirement (8:4) by loving others. Love, produced by the Spirit, accomplishes what rule-keeping alone never could.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses remind believers that they always owe love. Believers can ask each day how they can show love to those around them, in their families, workplaces, churches, and communities.

They also encourage believers to pay their financial debts responsibly. Paul's words "owe no one anything" encourage believers to avoid unpaid obligations and to be honest and faithful in financial matters.

Finally, these verses give believers a simple test for their actions. Before acting, believers can ask whether their choice will harm their neighbor. Love does no harm. This principle can guide decisions in many areas of life.

### Romans 13:11–12 — The Night Is Far Gone

verse: Romans 13:11-12

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been describing how believers should live: submitting to authorities, paying what they owe, and loving their neighbors. Now he gives an urgent reason to live this way.

[[v11|Do this, knowing the time]]. Believers should live with awareness of the time in which they live, the time between Christ's first coming and His return.

[[v11|that it is already time for you to awaken out of sleep]]. Paul uses the image of sleep for spiritual carelessness or laziness. It is time to wake up, to be alert and active.

[[v11|for salvation is now nearer to us than when we first believed]]. "Salvation" here refers to the final completion of salvation at Christ's return, when believers will be fully redeemed. Every day brings that day closer.

[[v12|The night is far gone, and the day is near]]. The present age is like night, a time of spiritual darkness. But the night is almost over. The day, the age to come, when Christ returns and God's kingdom comes in fullness, is about to dawn.

[[v12|Let’s therefore throw off the deeds of darkness, and let’s put on the armor of light]]. Just as people take off their nightclothes and put on their daytime clothes in the morning, believers are to throw off sinful behaviors that belong to the darkness and put on "the armor of light." Notice the word "armor." Living in the light involves spiritual battle. Believers need protection. Paul describes this armor more fully in Ephesians 6:10–18 and 1 Thessalonians 5:8.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that the hope of Christ's return should shape how believers live now. Jesus taught His disciples to be watchful and ready for His return (Matthew 24:42–44). John writes, [[1 John 3:3|Everyone who has this hope set on him purifies himself, even as he is pure]].

They also show that believers belong to the day, even though they live in the night. Paul writes, [[1 Thessalonians 5:5|You are all children of light, and children of the day]]. Believers already live by the light of the coming age, even though the darkness has not fully passed.

Finally, these verses show the urgency of the Christian life. Paul was not speculating about dates. He was calling believers to live with readiness and seriousness, knowing that the Lord's return could come at any time.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses call believers to wake up spiritually. It is possible for believers to become spiritually drowsy, going through the motions without real devotion. Paul's words call them to alertness, prayer, and active faithfulness.

They also encourage believers to examine their lives for "deeds of darkness." Believers can ask God to show them behaviors, habits, or attitudes that belong to the night, and to help them throw these off.

Finally, these verses give believers hope. The night is far gone, and the day is near. Whatever darkness believers face, it will not last forever. Christ is coming, and with Him the fullness of salvation.

### Romans 13:14 — Put On the Lord Jesus Christ

verse: Romans 13:14

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had urged believers to throw off the works of darkness and put on the armor of light. He had listed behaviors that belong to the night: wild parties, drunkenness, sexual immorality, sensuality, quarreling, and jealousy. Now he gives the positive alternative.

[[v14|But put on the Lord Jesus Christ]]. Instead of clothing themselves with sinful behavior, believers are to clothe themselves with Christ. This is a powerful image. Just as people put on clothes that cover them and shape how they appear, believers are to "put on" Christ so that His character covers and shapes their lives.

In one sense, believers have already put on Christ. Paul writes, [[Galatians 3:27|For as many of you as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ]]. But here Paul commands believers to put on Christ, showing that this is also an ongoing, daily action. Believers continually choose to live out their identity in Christ.

Notice the full title: "the Lord Jesus Christ." He is Lord, the one who rules; Jesus, the Savior; and Christ, the Messiah. Putting on Christ means submitting to Him as Lord and reflecting His character.

[[v14|and make no provision for the flesh, for its lusts]]. "Make no provision" means do not plan for or make room for the desires of the sinful nature. Believers should not set up situations that make it easy to sin. Instead of thinking about how to satisfy sinful desires, they should avoid giving those desires opportunity.

This is the verse Augustine read in the garden in Milan, which led to his conversion.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that the Christian life is not only about avoiding sin but about becoming like Christ. Holiness is not mainly a list of things not to do; it is a positive likeness to Jesus. Paul writes elsewhere, [[Colossians 3:12|Put on therefore, as God’s chosen ones, holy and beloved, a heart of compassion, kindness, lowliness, humility, and perseverance]].

It also shows practical wisdom in fighting sin. "Make no provision for the flesh" means being proactive. Believers should avoid situations, habits, and influences that feed sinful desires. Jesus spoke of radical measures to avoid sin (Matthew 5:29–30).

Finally, the verse shows the transforming power of God's word. One verse changed Augustine's life and, through him, influenced the whole history of Christian thought. God's word has power to change hearts.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites believers to "put on" Christ each day. Believers can begin each morning by consciously choosing to live as Christ would, in their thoughts, words, and actions, asking for His help to reflect His character.

It also encourages believers to be wise about temptation. Believers can identify situations, places, or habits that make sin easier, and take steps to avoid them. This might mean changing routines, setting boundaries on media use, or avoiding certain environments.

Finally, the verse brings hope to those who feel trapped in sin. Augustine had struggled for years to change. One verse, through the power of God's Spirit, set him free. Believers who struggle can turn to God's word and trust Him to transform them.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Romans 13 teaches believers to honor governing authorities as part of God's ordering of human society. Governments exist to promote good and restrain evil. Believers can be good citizens who obey laws, pay taxes, and show respect, while praying for those in authority. At the same time, believers recognize that their ultimate allegiance is to God, and that when human commands conflict with God's commands, they must obey God.

The chapter also teaches that love is the one debt believers can never finish paying. Love fulfills the whole Law, because love does no harm to a neighbor. Believers can let love guide their actions in every relationship, asking how they can seek the good of others.

Romans 13 calls believers to wake up. The night is far gone, and the day of Christ's return is near. Believers can live with alertness and urgency, throwing off the deeds of darkness and putting on the armor of light. Sins of attitude, such as quarreling and jealousy, are as serious as sins of the body.

Finally, the chapter calls believers to put on the Lord Jesus Christ and make no provision for sinful desires. The Christian life is about becoming like Christ, clothed with His character. Believers can begin each day by choosing to live as Christ would, avoiding situations that feed temptation, and trusting God's word and Spirit to transform them, as He transformed Augustine.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord God, You are sovereign over all authorities, and every ruler is accountable to You. Help me to be a good citizen, honoring those in authority, obeying just laws, and paying what I owe. I pray for the leaders of my nation and my community; give them wisdom and justice. Give me courage to obey You first whenever human commands conflict with Yours. Thank You that love fulfills the Law. Help me to pay the debt of love every day, doing no harm to my neighbor. Wake me from spiritual drowsiness, for the night is far gone and the day is near. Help me to throw off the deeds of darkness and to put on the armor of light. Today I put on the Lord Jesus Christ. Help me to make no provision for sinful desires, and transform me by Your word and Spirit. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
