# Titus 2
theme: The Grace That Trains Us to Live Godly Lives
main: Paul instructs Titus to teach what fits sound doctrine, giving practical guidance for each group in the church. Older men are to be sober, dignified, self-controlled, and sound in faith, love, and endurance. Older women are to be reverent, not slanderers or slaves to wine, but teachers of what is good, training younger women to love their husbands and children and to live self-controlled, pure, kind lives, so that God's word is not maligned. Younger men are to be self-controlled, and Titus himself must be an example of good works and sound, dignified teaching. Servants are to be trustworthy so that they adorn the teaching about God our Savior. All of this flows from the grace of God, which has appeared bringing salvation to all people and training believers to renounce ungodliness and worldly passions and to live self-controlled, upright, and godly lives while waiting for the blessed hope, the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ, who gave Himself to redeem and purify a people for His own possession, eager to do good works.

## Chapter Background and Story

Chapter 1 contrasted godly leaders with false teachers whose lives denied the God they professed to know. Chapter 2 turns to the whole church. Paul's concern is that every believer, young and old, men and women, free and enslaved, would live in a way that fits sound doctrine.

The repeated purpose in this chapter is the church's witness. Paul wants believers to live so "that God's word may not be blasphemed" (v5), so that opponents "may be ashamed, having no evil thing to say about us" (v8), and so that believers "may adorn the doctrine of God, our Savior, in all things" (v10). In a culture with a poor moral reputation, transformed lives would make the gospel attractive.

The instructions reflect the household structures of the first century. Christians today apply them in different ways to modern families and societies, as explained below. But the theological heart of the chapter, verses 11–14, is a timeless summary of how God's grace saves and transforms His people.

### Older Men (2:1–2)

[[v1|But say the things which fit sound doctrine]], [[v2|that older men should be temperate, sensible, sober minded, sound in faith, in love, and in perseverance]]. In contrast to the false teachers, Titus is to teach what fits healthy doctrine. Older men are to be clear-headed, dignified, self-controlled, and spiritually healthy in faith, love, and endurance. These are the marks of mature believers who can be examples to others.

### Older and Younger Women (2:3–5)

The instructions for older and younger women are studied below.

### Younger Men and Titus's Example (2:6–8)

[[v6|Likewise, exhort the younger men to be sober minded]]. Younger men are given one instruction: self-control. This word appears again and again in Titus (1:8; 2:2, 5, 6, 12). It means having a sound, disciplined mind and mastering one's impulses, an especially important quality for young people. Titus's own example is studied below.

### Servants (2:9–10)

[[v9|Exhort servants to be in subjection to their own masters and to be well-pleasing in all things, not contradicting]], [[v10|not stealing, but showing all good fidelity, that they may adorn the doctrine of God, our Savior, in all things]]. As in Ephesians 6, Colossians 3, and 1 Timothy 6, Paul addresses believers living within the Roman system of slavery. His instructions are pastoral guidance for people in a system they could not change, not an endorsement of slavery, which the gospel's teaching about the equal dignity of all in Christ eventually helped to overturn (see the discussion at Ephesians 6:5–9). Petty theft by slaves was common in the ancient world. By being honest and trustworthy, believing servants could "adorn" the gospel, making it beautiful and attractive, like jewelry that enhances its wearer. This is a remarkable thought: those with the lowest social status could display the beauty of God's teaching. Today, the principle applies to all who work: honesty and faithfulness at work can make the gospel attractive to others.

### The Grace of God and the Blessed Hope (2:11–14)

The theological heart of the chapter is studied below in two parts.

### Teach with Authority (2:15)

[[v15|Say these things and exhort and reprove with all authority. Let no one despise you]]. Titus is to teach these truths, encourage believers, and correct those who need it, with the authority that comes from God's word. Like Timothy (1 Timothy 4:12), he should not let anyone dismiss him.

## Important Verses Explained

### Titus 2:3–5 — Older Women Teaching What Is Good

verse: Titus 2:3-5

#### Simple Explanation

Paul gives instructions for older women. "Older women likewise be reverent in behavior." Their manner of life should reflect devotion to God, like those who serve in a holy place.

"Not slanderers": they should not spread gossip or speak evil of others. "Nor enslaved to much wine": heavy drinking was a common problem, and older women, like older men, were to be self-controlled.

"Teachers of that which is good." Older women have a vital teaching ministry. They are to model and teach goodness, especially to younger women.

Verse 4: "that they may train the young wives to love their husbands, to love their children." Older women are to mentor younger women in the practical art of loving their families. Love can be learned and strengthened through example and encouragement.

Verse 5: "to be sober minded, chaste, workers at home, kind, being in subjection to their own husbands." Younger women are encouraged to be self-controlled, pure, diligent in caring for their households, kind, and respectful toward their husbands.

"That God’s word may not be blasphemed." The purpose is the reputation of the gospel. In a culture that would judge Christianity by how its followers lived, faithful family life would honor God's word.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage shows the value of mentoring relationships between generations. Older believers have wisdom and experience that younger believers need. The church is meant to be a community where the older help the younger grow, not only through formal teaching but through friendship and example.

Christians apply the specific instructions in different ways. Many Christians understand "workers at home" and "being in subjection to their own husbands" as describing a lasting pattern for marriage and family, with the wife's primary sphere in the home and a loving, voluntary respect for her husband's leadership. Others understand these instructions as applying timeless principles, such as diligence, faithfulness, kindness, and respect, within the household structures of the first century, and believe they can be expressed in various ways in modern families where both spouses may work outside the home and share leadership. Both views affirm the dignity and value of women and of homemaking, and both agree that the purpose is a faithful witness to the gospel.

What this passage never teaches is that women must accept mistreatment. "Being in subjection to their own husbands" is set within the broader New Testament teaching that husbands must love their wives as Christ loved the church (Ephesians 5:25) and never be harsh with them (Colossians 3:19). A wife is never required to submit to abuse, to sin, or to danger. Abuse breaks the very purpose of marriage, and those facing it should seek safety and help.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Churches can encourage intentional mentoring between generations. Older women and men can invest in younger believers, sharing wisdom about faith, marriage, parenting, work, and life. Younger believers can seek out older mentors and learn from their experience.

The call to avoid slander and excessive drinking applies to everyone. Gossip and addiction harm individuals and communities. Believers can guard their speech and practice self-control.

Families can reflect on how their homes display the gospel, through love, kindness, faithfulness, and mutual respect. The way believers treat their spouses and children can make God's word attractive to a watching world.

### Titus 2:7–8 — An Example in Everything

verse: Titus 2:7-8

#### Simple Explanation

Paul now speaks directly to Titus. "In all things show yourself an example of good works." Titus is to be a model, not only in what he teaches but in how he lives. His good works should be visible to all.

"In your teaching, show integrity." His teaching should be pure, not corrupted by false ideas or wrong motives. "Seriousness": dignified, treating God's truth with respect. "Incorruptibility": genuine and sincere.

Verse 8: "and soundness of speech that can’t be condemned." His words should be healthy and beyond reproach, so that no one can legitimately criticize them.

"That he who opposes you may be ashamed, having no evil thing to say about us." The goal is that even opponents of the faith would have no grounds for accusation. If they look for something wrong, they will find nothing, and they will be embarrassed.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage emphasizes that leaders must lead by example. Jesus criticized religious leaders who said but did not do (Matthew 23:3). Paul repeatedly urged believers to follow his example as he followed Christ (1 Corinthians 11:1; Philippians 3:17). Example is one of the most powerful forms of teaching.

The concern for how outsiders view the church runs throughout this chapter and the New Testament. Peter wrote that believers should live such good lives among unbelievers that, even if accused of wrongdoing, their good deeds would be seen and God glorified (1 Peter 2:12). The church's integrity is part of its witness.

This does not mean believers can avoid all criticism. Jesus was criticized despite His perfect life. But believers should ensure that any criticism they receive is not justified by their own wrongdoing.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Everyone who teaches or leads, whether parents, pastors, small group leaders, or mentors, can examine whether their life matches their words. Integrity is more persuasive than eloquence.

Believers can also consider how their conduct appears to those outside the church. Are they honest, kind, and fair? Do their words build up rather than tear down? A consistent, gracious life can disarm critics and open hearts to the gospel.

Finally, this passage encourages believers to speak with care, choosing words that are sound, wise, and beyond reproach, especially when discussing faith or responding to critics.

### Titus 2:11–12 — The Grace That Trains Us

verse: Titus 2:11-12

#### Simple Explanation

Paul now gives the reason behind all his practical instructions. "For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation to all men." God's grace, His undeserved favor, has appeared in history in the person of Jesus Christ. Like the sun rising over the horizon, grace has shone into the world. And it brings salvation to "all men," to people of every kind: old and young, men and women, free and enslaved, Jew and Gentile.

Verse 12 describes what grace does. It is "instructing us." The word means training, like a parent training a child or a coach training an athlete. Grace does not only save; it also teaches.

Grace trains believers to do two things. First, "denying ungodliness and worldly lusts": saying no to everything that dishonors God and to selfish desires shaped by a world that ignores God. Second, to "live soberly, righteously, and godly in this present age": to live with self-control (in relation to oneself), with righteousness (in relation to others), and with godliness (in relation to God).

"In this present age" means now, in the midst of everyday life, not just in the future.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage shows that grace is not only the starting point of the Christian life but also its ongoing power. Some people think of grace as merely forgiveness, while holy living depends on human effort. Paul teaches that grace itself trains believers to live godly lives. Grace is not a license to sin (Romans 6:1–2); it is the power to live free from sin.

The three-part description of the godly life, sober, righteous, and godly, covers every relationship. Sobriety governs a person's inner life; righteousness governs their relationships with others; godliness governs their relationship with God. Grace transforms all three.

The phrase "bringing salvation to all men" has been understood in different ways, as discussed at 1 Timothy 2:4. At minimum, it means that God's grace is offered to people of every kind and background, without distinction.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can approach spiritual growth not as a grim struggle to earn God's approval but as a response to grace. God has already shown them favor. That grace now teaches them to say no to sin and yes to godliness.

When facing temptation, believers can remember that grace instructs them, [[v12|denying ungodliness and worldly lusts]], to live godly lives. Grace gives both the motivation and the power to resist.

Believers can also look at every area of life: personal habits, relationships, and devotion to God, asking how grace is shaping them to live self-controlled, upright, and godly lives.

### Titus 2:13–14 — Our Great God and Savior, Jesus Christ

verse: Titus 2:13-14

#### Simple Explanation

Paul continues: believers live godly lives while "looking for the blessed hope and appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior, Jesus Christ."

"The blessed hope" is the return of Christ. It is "blessed" because it brings joy and completes salvation. Believers wait for it with eager expectation.

"Appearing of the glory" describes Christ's return in splendor. The first appearing was of grace (v11); the second will be of glory.

"Our great God and Savior, Jesus Christ." Most scholars and translations understand this phrase as referring to one person: Jesus Christ is "our great God and Savior." This is one of the clearest statements in the New Testament that Jesus is God.

Verse 14 describes what Christ did: "who gave himself for us." He gave His own life. The purpose was twofold: "that he might redeem us from all iniquity," setting us free from the power and guilt of sin, "and purify for himself a people for his own possession." Christ is creating a people who belong to Him, cleansed and set apart.

"Zealous for good works." This people is marked by enthusiasm for doing good.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses connect Christ's first and second comings. His first coming brought grace and salvation; His second will bring glory. Believers live between these two appearings, trained by grace and looking forward in hope.

The title "our great God and Savior" is significant. In the Roman world, emperors were sometimes called "god" and "savior." Paul declares that the true God and Savior is Jesus Christ. This reflects the church's conviction that Jesus is fully divine, a conviction expressed in John 1:1, Romans 9:5, Hebrews 1:8, and 2 Peter 1:1.

The phrase "a people for his own possession" echoes God's words to Israel at Mount Sinai: [[Exodus 19:5|you shall be my own possession from among all peoples]]. Peter applies similar language to the church (1 Peter 2:9). Through Christ, God is gathering a treasured people from every nation.

The goal is a people "zealous for good works." Salvation is not by works (Titus 3:5), but it produces a passion for doing good. Grace saves, trains, and fills believers with zeal for good deeds.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

The hope of Christ's return can shape how believers live now. Knowing that He will appear in glory gives motivation to live godly lives, endurance in hardship, and joy in waiting.

The truth that Jesus is "our great God and Savior" invites worship. Believers can honor Him not only as a teacher or example but as God Himself, who gave Himself for them.

Finally, being "a people for his own possession" gives believers a deep sense of belonging. They are treasured by Christ, purchased by His sacrifice. This identity can inspire them to be "zealous for good works," eagerly looking for ways to serve others and reflect His love.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Titus 2 shows that sound doctrine must lead to godly living in every part of life. Paul gives practical instructions for older men, older women, younger women, younger men, and servants, each designed to help believers live in a way that honors God and makes the gospel attractive. Christians apply the specific household instructions in different ways today, but the underlying principles are clear: self-control, faithfulness, kindness, integrity, and love.

The chapter also highlights the value of mentoring across generations. Older believers can teach and model what is good, helping younger believers grow in faith and in loving their families. Leaders, like Titus, are called to be examples in everything, with teaching and lives beyond reproach.

A recurring purpose runs through the chapter: the reputation of the gospel. When believers live faithfully, even those with the lowest status in society can "adorn" the teaching about God, making it beautiful to others. The way believers live at home and at work is part of their witness.

At the heart of the chapter is the grace of God. Grace has appeared in Jesus Christ, bringing salvation to all people. That same grace trains believers to say no to ungodliness and to live self-controlled, upright, and godly lives now. And it fills them with hope as they wait for the appearing of the glory of their great God and Savior, Jesus Christ, who gave Himself to redeem them and make them His own people, eager to do good. Grace saves, grace trains, and grace gives hope.

## Chapter Prayer

Gracious God, thank You that Your grace has appeared in Jesus Christ, bringing salvation to people of every kind. Let Your grace train me to say no to ungodliness and worldly desires and to live a self-controlled, upright, and godly life in this present age. Help me to be an example of good works and to speak with integrity. Bless the relationships between generations in Your church; help the older to teach what is good and the younger to learn with humility. Let my home and my work adorn the teaching about You, our Savior. Protect every home from harm and fill families with love and respect. I wait with joy for the blessed hope, the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior, Jesus Christ. Thank You, Lord Jesus, for giving Yourself to redeem me and make me Your own. Make me zealous for good works. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
