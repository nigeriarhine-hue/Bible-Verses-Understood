# James 4
theme: Humble Yourselves Before the Lord
main: James asks where the fights and quarrels among believers come from, and answers that they come from desires battling within them. They want what they do not have, so they quarrel and fight; they do not have because they do not ask God, and when they ask, they ask with wrong motives, to spend on their own pleasures. Friendship with the world, adopting its values, is hostility toward God, like spiritual adultery. Yet God gives more grace, opposing the proud but giving grace to the humble. So believers must submit to God, resist the devil, draw near to God, cleanse their hands and purify their hearts, grieve over their sin, and humble themselves before the Lord, who will lift them up. They must not speak evil against or judge one another, because there is only one Lawgiver and Judge. James then rebukes those who confidently plan their business ventures as if they controlled the future, reminding them that life is a mist that appears briefly and vanishes; they should say instead, [[v15|If the Lord wills, we will both live, and do this or that]]. Finally, anyone who knows the right thing to do and fails to do it commits sin.

## Chapter Background and Story

James 3 ended by contrasting earthly wisdom, which produces envy, selfish ambition, and disorder, with heavenly wisdom, which produces peace. Chapter 4 shows what earthly wisdom looks like in practice: fights, quarrels, envy, and pride. The community James addressed was apparently troubled by conflict, perhaps over status, resources, or influence.

James diagnoses the root of these conflicts: selfish desires. He then calls believers to repentance, using some of the strongest language in his letter. But his call is full of hope. God gives more grace. Those who humble themselves will be lifted up. Those who draw near to God will find Him drawing near to them.

The final section addresses a different but related form of pride: the arrogance of planning life as though one were in control. James was likely speaking to merchants who traveled from city to city to trade. Their problem was not planning itself, but planning without reference to God.

### The Source of Conflict (4:1–3)

The source of fights and the problem of wrong motives in prayer are studied below.

### Friendship with the World (4:4–6)

[[v4|You adulterers and adulteresses, don’t you know that friendship with the world is hostility toward God? Whoever therefore wants to be a friend of the world makes himself an enemy of God]]. James uses the image of adultery, which the Old Testament prophets often used for Israel's unfaithfulness to God (Hosea 1–3; Jeremiah 3:20). God's people are like a bride pledged to Him. When they give their love to the world, its values, priorities, and pleasures, they are being unfaithful. "The world" here does not mean creation or people, whom God loves (John 3:16), but the system of values and desires opposed to God (1 John 2:15–17).

[[v5|Or do you think that the Scripture says in vain, “The Spirit who lives in us yearns jealously”?]] This verse is difficult. James does not quote a specific Old Testament verse, but summarizes a biblical theme. Interpreters understand it in two main ways. It may mean that God jealously longs for the spirit He placed in human beings, wanting their undivided devotion, as a faithful husband longs for his wife's love (Exodus 20:5; 34:14). Or it may mean that the human spirit, left to itself, tends toward envy. The first reading fits the context of spiritual adultery well: God's love is a jealous love that will not share His people with rivals. The call to humility and drawing near is studied below.

### Do Not Judge One Another (4:11–12)

[[v11|Don’t speak against one another, brothers. He who speaks against a brother and judges his brother, speaks against the law and judges the law. But if you judge the law, you are not a doer of the law but a judge]]. Slander and harsh judgment of fellow believers break the royal law of love (James 2:8). Those who judge others in this way place themselves above the law, acting as if they were the lawgiver rather than those under the law.

[[v12|Only one is the lawgiver, who is able to save and to destroy. But who are you to judge another?]] God alone is the Lawgiver and Judge. He alone has the authority and knowledge to judge rightly. This does not forbid all discernment or correction; Scripture calls believers to recognize sin and to restore those who fall (Galatians 6:1; Matthew 18:15). It forbids condemning, slandering, and setting oneself up as judge over others' hearts. Jesus taught, [[Matthew 7:1|Don't judge, so that you won't be judged]].

## Important Verses Explained

### James 4:1–3 — The Source of Quarrels

verse: James 4:1-3

#### Simple Explanation

"Where do wars and fightings among you come from?" James asks a direct question about the conflicts in the community. "Wars and fightings" describe ongoing quarrels and disputes.

"Don’t they come from your pleasures that war in your members?" The conflicts come from within. "Pleasures" means desires for gratification. These desires are at war within people, and they spill out into conflicts with others.

Verse 2: "You lust, and don’t have." People desire things they do not have. "You murder and covet, and can’t obtain." James may be speaking literally of violence that arises from desire, or he may use "murder" in the sense Jesus did, of hatred and anger in the heart (Matthew 5:21–22). Coveting, wanting what belongs to others, leads to frustration. "You fight and make war." The inner war becomes an outer war.

"You don't have, because you don't ask." Instead of bringing their needs to God, people try to grab what they want through conflict.

Verse 3: "You ask, and don’t receive, because you ask with wrong motives, so that you may spend it on your pleasures." Even when they pray, their prayers are selfish. They want God to fulfill their desires for their own pleasure, not for His purposes.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses give a profound analysis of human conflict. Whether in families, churches, workplaces, or nations, conflicts often arise from unsatisfied desires: for possessions, power, status, recognition, or control. When people do not get what they want, they fight.

The remedy is not to suppress all desire but to bring desires to God. Prayer is the alternative to grasping and fighting. Jesus taught His followers to ask the Father for their needs (Matthew 7:7–11).

But prayer itself can be corrupted. When people pray only for their own pleasure, treating God as a means to satisfy their desires, their prayers go unanswered. True prayer seeks God's will and glory, as Jesus taught: [[Matthew 6:10|Your will be done]]. First John 5:14 says that if we ask anything according to His will, He hears us.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

When believers find themselves in conflict, they can look inward and ask what desires are driving it. Is it a desire for control, recognition, comfort, or getting their own way? Recognizing the root can open the door to resolution.

Believers can also bring their desires to God in prayer instead of trying to seize what they want. Honest prayer about desires, disappointments, and needs is healthy.

Finally, believers can examine their motives in prayer. Are they seeking God's will or only their own pleasure? Prayer that aligns with God's purposes, seeking His kingdom first, is prayer that He delights to answer.

### James 4:6–8 — Grace for the Humble

verse: James 4:6-8

#### Simple Explanation

After the strong rebuke of verses 1–5, James offers hope. "But he gives more grace." However great the problem of worldliness and pride, God's grace is greater. He gives grace upon grace.

"Therefore it says, 'God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble.'" James quotes Proverbs 3:34 (in the Greek version). Peter quotes the same verse (1 Peter 5:5). God opposes those who exalt themselves, but He pours out grace on those who humble themselves.

Verse 7: "Be subject therefore to God." Humility means submitting to God, placing oneself under His authority.

"Resist the devil, and he will flee from you." Submitting to God and resisting the devil go together. The devil is real and active, but he is not all-powerful. When believers stand firm against him in God's strength, he flees.

Verse 8: "Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you." This is a wonderful promise. When believers come close to God, He comes close to them. He is not distant or reluctant.

"Cleanse your hands, you sinners. Purify your hearts, you double-minded." Drawing near to God involves turning from sin. "Hands" represent actions; "hearts" represent inner desires and motives. Both need cleansing. "Double-minded" describes those whose loyalty is divided between God and the world (James 1:8).

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses describe the path of repentance and restoration. It begins with God's grace, which is greater than any sin. It continues with humility, submission to God, and resistance to the devil. It leads to intimacy with God, as believers draw near and He draws near to them. And it involves genuine change, the cleansing of actions and the purifying of hearts.

Verses 9–10 continue: "Lament, mourn, and weep... Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and he will exalt you." James calls for genuine sorrow over sin, not a shallow apology. This sorrow is not meant to last forever; it leads to God's lifting up. Jesus said that those who humble themselves will be exalted (Luke 14:11).

The promise "draw near to God, and he will draw near to you" reveals God's eagerness for fellowship. Like the father in the parable of the prodigal son, who ran to meet his returning son (Luke 15:20), God responds to every step toward Him.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

When believers feel distant from God, they can take a step toward Him, through prayer, confession, Scripture, or worship, trusting His promise to draw near.

Humility is the doorway to grace. Believers can practice humility by acknowledging their dependence on God, confessing their sins honestly, and submitting their plans and desires to Him.

When believers face temptation or spiritual attack, they can resist the devil, standing firm in God's strength, refusing to give in, and trusting that he will flee.

### James 4:13–15 — If the Lord Wills

verse: James 4:13-15

#### Simple Explanation

"Come now, you who say, 'Today or tomorrow let’s go into this city and spend a year there, trade, and make a profit.'" James addresses merchants who made confident plans: where they would go, how long they would stay, what they would do, and what they would gain. Their plans left God out completely.

Verse 14: "Yet you don’t know what your life will be like tomorrow." No one knows the future. Illness, accident, economic change, or death can overturn the best-laid plans.

"For what is your life? For you are a vapor that appears for a little time and then vanishes away." Life is like morning mist or a puff of breath on a cold day. It appears briefly and then disappears. Human life is fragile and short.

Verse 15: "For you ought to say, 'If the Lord wills, we will both live, and do this or that.'" Instead of presumptuous planning, believers should plan with humility, acknowledging that their lives and plans depend on God's will.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

James is not condemning planning. Scripture commends wise planning (Proverbs 21:5). Paul made travel plans, often adding [[1 Corinthians 16:7|if the Lord permits]] (see also Acts 18:21). The problem is arrogance: planning as if one controls the future and does not need God.

Jesus told a parable about a rich man who planned to build bigger barns and enjoy his wealth for many years, but God said, [[Luke 12:20|You foolish one, tonight your soul is required of you]] (Luke 12:16–21). The man's plans left God out, and his life ended suddenly.

The image of life as a vapor appears throughout Scripture. Psalm 39:5 says that every person is but a breath. Ecclesiastes repeatedly calls life "vanity," a word that means vapor or breath. This is not meant to make believers despair, but to make them wise, valuing each day and depending on God.

Saying "If the Lord wills" is not meant to be a superstitious formula, but an expression of a heart that submits all plans to God.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can make plans for their careers, families, finances, and futures, but they can hold those plans with humility, praying about them and remaining open to God's direction.

Remembering that life is short can help believers prioritize what truly matters: relationships, faithfulness, love, and eternal things. It can also free them from anxiety about the future, since the future is in God's hands.

When plans change unexpectedly, through illness, loss, or disappointment, believers can trust that God is still in control. His will is good, even when it differs from their plans.

### James 4:17 — Knowing to Do Good

verse: James 4:17

#### Simple Explanation

James ends the chapter with a short but powerful statement. "To him therefore who knows to do good and doesn’t do it, to him it is sin."

Sin is not only doing wrong things. It is also failing to do right things. When a person knows what is good and has the opportunity to do it but chooses not to, that failure is sin.

This verse may connect to the previous section. Those who know they should depend on God and acknowledge His will, but continue in arrogant self-reliance, are sinning. But it also states a general principle.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Christians sometimes distinguish between "sins of commission," doing what is wrong, and "sins of omission," failing to do what is right. James emphasizes that both are real sins.

Jesus' parables highlight sins of omission. In the parable of the good Samaritan, the priest and Levite did no active harm to the wounded man, but they passed by without helping (Luke 10:30–37). In the parable of the sheep and the goats, those condemned are not accused of doing evil but of failing to feed the hungry, welcome the stranger, clothe the naked, and visit the sick and imprisoned (Matthew 25:41–46).

This verse also connects with James's emphasis on being doers of the word (James 1:22) and on faith that works (James 2:14–26). Knowledge without action is not enough. Knowing God's will brings responsibility to do it.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can ask whether there are good things they know they should do but have been putting off: a kindness to show, a person to forgive, a need to meet, a conversation to have, or a step of obedience to take.

This verse is not meant to produce guilt over every possible good deed left undone. No one can meet every need. But it calls believers to respond to the specific opportunities God places before them.

Believers can pray for sensitivity to God's leading and courage to act on what they know is right, trusting that God will give them the strength to obey.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

James 4 exposes the roots of conflict. Fights and quarrels come from desires warring within people. When they want what they do not have, they fight instead of asking God, or they pray with selfish motives. Believers can look beneath their conflicts to the desires driving them, and bring those desires honestly to God.

James warns that friendship with the world, embracing its values and priorities, is like spiritual adultery against God, who jealously desires His people's undivided love. But God gives more grace. He opposes the proud and gives grace to the humble. The path back to God is humility: submitting to Him, resisting the devil, drawing near to God, cleansing actions and purifying hearts, grieving over sin, and humbling oneself before the Lord, who will lift up the humble. God promises to draw near to those who draw near to Him.

The chapter also warns against speaking evil of and judging one another. God alone is the Lawgiver and Judge. Believers can practice discernment and loving correction without slander or condemnation.

Finally, James warns against arrogant planning that leaves God out. Life is a vapor that appears briefly and vanishes. Believers can plan wisely while saying, "If the Lord wills," holding their futures in humble dependence on God. And they are reminded that knowing the good and failing to do it is sin. Faith acts on what it knows.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord, search my heart and show me the desires that cause conflict in my relationships. Forgive me for fighting to get what I want instead of bringing my needs to You, and for praying with selfish motives. Keep me from friendship with the world and give me an undivided love for You. Thank You that You give more grace, and that You give grace to the humble. I submit myself to You. Help me to resist the devil. I draw near to You; thank You for drawing near to me. Cleanse my hands and purify my heart. Keep me from slandering or judging others. Help me to hold my plans humbly, remembering that my life is a vapor and that my future is in Your hands. Give me courage to do the good that I know I should do. I humble myself before You, trusting You to lift me up. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
