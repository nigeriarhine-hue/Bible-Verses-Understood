# Ephesians 5
theme: Walking in Love, Light, and Wisdom
main: Paul calls believers to imitate God as beloved children and to walk in love as Christ loved them and gave Himself for them. Because they were once darkness but are now light in the Lord, they must turn from sexual immorality, impurity, and greed, and live as children of light, exposing the deeds of darkness. They are to live wisely, making the most of their time, and to be filled with the Spirit, which shows itself in worship, thanksgiving, and mutual submission out of reverence for Christ. Paul then describes Christian marriage as a living picture of Christ and the church: wives are to submit to their husbands as to the Lord, and husbands are to love their wives as Christ loved the church and gave Himself up for her.

## Chapter Background and Story

Ephesians 5 continues the practical teaching that began in chapter 4. Paul has called believers to put off the old self and put on the new. Now he describes what that new life looks like using three images: walking in love (5:1–6), walking in light (5:7–14), and walking in wisdom (5:15–21). He then begins a section on Christian households (5:22–6:9), starting with marriage.

The city of Ephesus, like most Greek and Roman cities, was marked by widespread sexual immorality, often connected with pagan religion. Feasts in honor of gods could include drunkenness and sexual excess. Greed and exploitation were common in commerce. Against this background, Paul's call to purity, generosity, and Spirit-filled worship was strikingly countercultural.

His teaching on marriage was also shaped by its setting. In the Roman world, husbands held enormous legal power over their wives. Moral philosophers wrote "household codes" instructing wives, children, and slaves to obey, but they rarely placed demands on the husband. Paul's household instructions take that familiar form and transform it. He addresses wives directly as moral agents, places all relationships under Christ, and gives husbands the demanding command to love their wives with the self-sacrificing love of Christ.

### Imitators of God, Walking in Love (5:1–6)

The call to imitate God and walk in love is studied below.

[[v3|But sexual immorality, and all uncleanness or covetousness, let it not even be mentioned among you, as becomes saints]]. Paul sets a high standard. These sins should not even be associated with God's people. "Covetousness" is greed, the restless desire for more. It is listed alongside sexual sins because both are forms of selfish grasping.

[[v4|nor filthiness, nor foolish talking, nor jesting, which are not appropriate, but rather giving of thanks]]. "Jesting" here refers to crude or vulgar joking, not to healthy humor. Instead of speech that degrades, believers are to be people of thanksgiving.

[[v5|Know this for sure, that no sexually immoral person, nor unclean person, nor covetous man (who is an idolater), has any inheritance in the Kingdom of Christ and God]]. Paul calls greed a form of idolatry, because it makes possessions into a god. He is describing people whose lives are characterized by these sins, not believers who struggle and repent. [[v6|Let no one deceive you with empty words, for because of these things the wrath of God comes on the children of disobedience]]. Some were apparently teaching that such sins did not matter. Paul warns against being misled.

### Children of Light (5:7–14)

[[v7|Therefore don’t be partakers with them]]. [[v8|For you were once darkness, but are now light in the Lord. Walk as children of light]]. Paul does not say believers were once "in" darkness but that they "were" darkness. Now they "are" light. Their identity has changed completely.

[[v9|for the fruit of the Spirit is in all goodness and righteousness and truth]], [[v10|proving what is well pleasing to the Lord]]. Light produces fruit: goodness, righteousness, and truth. Believers are to discern and test what pleases the Lord.

[[v11|Have no fellowship with the unfruitful deeds of darkness, but rather even reprove them]]. Believers should not participate in evil but should expose it. Light naturally reveals what darkness hides. [[v13|But all things, when they are reproved, are revealed by the light, for everything that reveals is light]].

This principle is important for the life of the church. Hiding wrongdoing, especially abuse, in order to protect a reputation is a deed of darkness. Bringing such things into the light, with care for those who have been harmed and through proper channels and authorities, is part of walking as children of light.

[[v14|Therefore he says, “Awake, you who sleep, and arise from the dead, and Christ will shine on you.”]] This may be a line from an early Christian hymn, perhaps sung at baptisms, drawing on themes from Isaiah 60:1. It calls people to wake from spiritual sleep and receive the light of Christ.

### Walking in Wisdom and Filled with the Spirit (5:15–21)

Paul's call to live wisely and use time well is studied below. [[v17|Therefore, don’t be foolish, but understand what the will of the Lord is]]. Wisdom means understanding God's will and living by it. The call to be filled with the Spirit, and what that produces, is studied below.

### Marriage: A Picture of Christ and the Church (5:22–33)

Paul's teaching on wives and husbands is studied below. [[v31|“For this cause a man will leave his father and mother and will be joined to his wife. Then the two will become one flesh.”]] Paul quotes Genesis 2:24, the foundational text on marriage, which Jesus also quoted (Matthew 19:5). Marriage is a covenant union so close that husband and wife become one.

[[v32|This mystery is great, but I speak concerning Christ and the assembly]]. Paul reveals that marriage, from the beginning, was designed to point beyond itself to the relationship between Christ and His people. Human marriage is a living parable of Christ's love for the church. [[v33|Nevertheless each of you must also love his own wife even as himself; and let the wife see that she respects her husband]]. Paul returns to the practical. Love and respect are the twin pillars of a Christian marriage.

## Important Verses Explained

### Ephesians 5:1–2 — Imitators of God, Walking in Love

verse: Ephesians 5:1-2

#### Simple Explanation

This verse follows directly from the end of chapter 4, where Paul urged believers to forgive one another as God in Christ forgave them. Now he says, "Be therefore imitators of God, as beloved children."

Children naturally imitate their parents. They copy their words, gestures, and habits. Paul says believers, as God's beloved children, should imitate their heavenly Father. What does that look like? Verse 2 explains: "Walk in love."

The model of this love is Christ: "even as Christ also loved us and gave himself up for us." Christ's love was not merely a feeling. It was self-giving. He gave His very life.

Paul describes Christ's self-giving as "an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweet-smelling fragrance." In the Old Testament, sacrifices that pleased God were described as a pleasing aroma (Genesis 8:21; Leviticus 1:9). Christ's sacrifice on the cross was the perfect offering, fully pleasing to God.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

It is remarkable that Paul calls believers to imitate God. In one sense, God is utterly beyond human imitation. But God has revealed Himself in Jesus Christ, so believers can see what God's love looks like in human form. Jesus said, [[John 13:34|just as I have loved you, you also love one another]].

The phrase "beloved children" is important. Believers do not imitate God in order to become His children; they imitate Him because they already are His beloved children. The motivation is not fear but love. A child who knows they are loved naturally wants to be like the parent who loves them.

Christ's sacrifice is the measure of love. It is not merely kindness when it is convenient but costly self-giving for the good of others. This is the love believers are called to walk in, a love that forgives, serves, and puts others first.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse provides a simple guide for daily life: imitate God by walking in love. In each relationship and situation, believers can ask how Christ's self-giving love would act here. That might mean patience with a difficult family member, generosity toward someone in need, or forgiveness toward someone who has caused hurt.

It also reminds believers of their identity. They are God's beloved children. Living from that security frees them to love others without needing to earn approval or protect themselves at all costs.

### Ephesians 5:15–16 — Redeeming the Time

verse: Ephesians 5:15-16

#### Simple Explanation

"Therefore watch carefully how you walk, not as unwise, but as wise." Paul calls believers to pay careful attention to how they live. Life should not be lived carelessly or thoughtlessly. Wisdom means understanding God's ways and applying them to daily choices.

"Redeeming the time." The word "redeem" means to buy back or buy up, like a merchant seizing a good opportunity in the market. "Time" here refers to opportunities, moments that can be used for good. Believers are to make the most of every opportunity God gives them.

"Because the days are evil." Paul recognized that the world is full of evil influences and that time is limited. In such a world, opportunities to do good, to share the gospel, and to grow in faith should not be wasted.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The Bible often speaks of life's brevity. Psalm 90:12 prays, [[Psalms 90:12|So teach us to count our days, that we may gain a heart of wisdom]]. James 4:14 reminds readers that life is a vapor that appears for a little while and then vanishes. Wisdom includes recognizing that time is a gift to be stewarded for God's purposes.

"The days are evil" does not mean believers should withdraw from the world in fear. Rather, it means that in a world darkened by sin, the light of believers is especially needed. Every opportunity to love, serve, and witness matters.

This wisdom is not anxious busyness. Jesus Himself rested, withdrew to pray, and enjoyed meals with friends. Redeeming the time means using it well for God's purposes, which includes rest, worship, and relationships, not just productivity.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

In an age of endless distractions, these verses invite believers to examine how they use their time. Hours can slip away through mindless scrolling, entertainment, or busyness that does not matter. Redeeming the time means being intentional about what fills each day.

This might mean setting aside regular time for prayer and Scripture, prioritizing relationships with family and friends, looking for opportunities to serve, or saying no to activities that crowd out what matters most. Wisdom also means recognizing God-given opportunities when they appear, such as a conversation with someone in need or an open door to share faith, and seizing them.

### Ephesians 5:18–21 — Be Filled with the Spirit

verse: Ephesians 5:18-21

#### Simple Explanation

Paul contrasts two kinds of influence. "Don’t be drunken with wine, in which is dissipation." Drunkenness leads to "dissipation," a wasteful, reckless, out-of-control life. In the pagan festivals of the time, drunkenness was often part of worship, believed to bring people into contact with the gods.

"But be filled with the Spirit." Instead of being controlled by wine, believers are to be filled and influenced by the Holy Spirit. In Greek, this command is in the present tense, meaning "keep on being filled." It is a continual experience, not a one-time event.

Paul then describes what a Spirit-filled life looks like through four results.

First, "speaking to one another in psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs." The Spirit produces worship that builds up the community.

Second, "singing and making melody in your heart to the Lord." Worship is not only outward but from the heart.

Third, "giving thanks always concerning all things in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ to God, even the Father." The Spirit produces a grateful heart.

Fourth, "subjecting yourselves to one another in the fear of Christ." The Spirit produces humility and mutual submission, a willingness to put others before oneself out of reverence for Christ.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Being filled with the Spirit is not mainly about dramatic experiences, though the Spirit may sometimes work in powerful ways. According to Paul, the evidence of being Spirit-filled is worship, gratitude, and humble relationships. These are the marks of a community in which the Spirit is at work.

The phrase "giving thanks always concerning all things" does not mean thanking God for evil itself, such as abuse, injustice, or tragedy. It means giving thanks in every circumstance, trusting that God is present and working even in hard times (1 Thessalonians 5:18). Gratitude flows from confidence in God's goodness.

Verse 21 is a bridge to the household teaching that follows. In Greek, verse 22 does not even have its own verb; it borrows "subjecting" from verse 21. Mutual submission is the umbrella over all the relationships Paul goes on to describe. Every believer is called to humble, other-centered living, out of reverence for Christ.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many people turn to alcohol, entertainment, or other substances to escape stress or find joy. Paul points to a better source: the Holy Spirit. Believers can ask regularly to be filled with the Spirit and can make room for Him through prayer, worship, and Scripture.

The fruit of the Spirit-filled life can be cultivated. Singing to God, alone and together, nurtures the heart. Practicing gratitude, perhaps by naming specific blessings each day, shapes perspective. Mutual submission shows up in everyday humility: listening, serving, deferring to others, and refusing to insist on one's own way.

### Ephesians 5:22–25 — Marriage Shaped by Christ's Love

verse: Ephesians 5:22-25

#### Simple Explanation

Paul now applies the principle of mutual submission (v21) to marriage. He addresses wives first: "Wives, be subject to your own husbands, as to the Lord." The word "subject" means to arrange oneself under, to yield or defer. Paul grounds this in the wife's relationship with Christ: it is "as to the Lord," an expression of her devotion to Him.

"For the husband is the head of the wife, as Christ also is the head of the assembly, being himself the savior of the body." Paul compares the husband's role to Christ's role as head of the church. Importantly, Christ is described as "savior," the one who gives His life for the body.

Then Paul addresses husbands, and this is where his teaching most sharply differs from the culture around him. "Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the assembly and gave himself up for her." The standard for husbands is Christ's self-sacrificing love. Christ did not use His position to dominate the church; He died for her. Husbands are called to the same kind of costly, self-giving love. In the verses that follow, Paul adds that husbands should love their wives as their own bodies, nourishing and cherishing them (vv28–29).

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Christians have understood this passage in different ways, and both views are held by sincere believers who take Scripture seriously. Many, often called complementarians, understand "headship" to mean that husbands have a God-given responsibility of loving, servant leadership in the home, and that wives are called to respond to that leadership with respect and support. Others, often called egalitarians, emphasize that verse 21 calls all believers to mutual submission, understand "head" here primarily in terms of source, care, and self-giving rather than authority, and see Paul's instructions as working within the structures of his time while planting the seeds of full partnership. Both views agree that husbands and wives are equal in dignity and worth before God (Galatians 3:28; 1 Peter 3:7), and that the model for the husband is Christ's self-giving love.

What this passage never teaches is that a husband may control, demean, or harm his wife. A husband who uses this passage to justify domination or abuse has completely reversed its meaning. Christ's headship is expressed in sacrifice, not in force. Likewise, a wife's submission is "as to the Lord," which means it can never require her to sin, to accept abuse, or to place herself or her children in danger. Abuse in any form is a grievous sin that breaks the marriage covenant's purpose. A spouse who is being abused should seek safety and help, and churches must protect and support victims rather than urging them to stay in harm's way.

The deepest point of this passage is that marriage is a picture of the gospel (v32). Christ's love for His people, self-giving, faithful, purifying, and nourishing, is the pattern for marriage. The church's trusting response to Christ is also part of the picture. A marriage shaped by these realities becomes a living testimony to God's love.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

For married believers, this passage invites reflection on how their marriage can reflect Christ's love. For husbands, the question is whether their love is self-giving: do they put their wife's good above their own comfort, listen to her, cherish her, and serve her? For wives, the question is whether they honor and support their husband as an expression of their devotion to Christ. For both, the call to mutual submission (v21) means humble, other-centered love.

Couples who hold different views on headship and roles can still agree on the heart of the passage: marriage is to be marked by Christlike love, respect, faithfulness, and mutual care. Wise counsel, honest communication, and prayer together can help couples work out these principles in their particular situation.

For those whose marriages are marked by control, fear, or abuse, this passage offers no support for that harm. God's design is a relationship of love and safety. Seeking help from trusted pastors, counselors, domestic violence services, and, when necessary, legal authorities is a right and wise step.

For those who are single, widowed, or divorced, this passage still speaks. It reveals the love Christ has for every member of His church. Every believer is cherished, nourished, and loved by Christ, who gave Himself for them.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Ephesians 5 calls believers to a life that reflects the character of God. As His beloved children, they are to imitate Him by walking in love, the self-giving love Christ showed when He gave Himself for them. This love shapes every relationship and every choice.

Believers are also called to walk as children of light. Once they were darkness; now they are light in the Lord. This means turning from sexual immorality, impurity, and greed, which damage both the self and others, and choosing goodness, righteousness, and truth. Children of light also expose the deeds of darkness rather than covering them up, which includes refusing to hide abuse or wrongdoing within the church.

The chapter calls for wisdom in how believers use their time. In a world full of distraction and evil, every opportunity to love, serve, and witness is precious. Believers can live intentionally, making the most of each day.

At the heart of the chapter is the call to be continually filled with the Spirit. The evidence is not mainly dramatic experiences but worship, gratitude, and mutual submission out of reverence for Christ. This Spirit-filled life shapes even the most intimate relationship, marriage. Husbands are called to love their wives with Christ's self-sacrificing love, and wives are called to honor their husbands as an expression of their devotion to Christ. Marriage becomes a living picture of Christ's love for His church. It is never a license for control or harm, but a covenant of love, safety, and faithfulness that points to the greatest love of all.

## Chapter Prayer

Father, I am Your beloved child. Help me to imitate You by walking in love, just as Christ loved me and gave Himself for me. Thank You that I was once darkness but am now light in the Lord. Keep me from sexual immorality, impurity, and greed, and help me to live in goodness, righteousness, and truth. Give me courage to bring hidden wrongs into the light and compassion for those who have been harmed. Teach me to live wisely and to make the most of the time You give me. Fill me continually with Your Holy Spirit, so that my life overflows with worship, thanksgiving, and humble love for others. Bless marriages with Christlike love, respect, and faithfulness, and protect all who are in danger in their homes. Thank You, Lord Jesus, for loving Your church and giving Yourself for her. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
