# 2 John 1
theme: Walking in Truth and Love
main: The elder writes to "the chosen lady and her children," whom he loves in truth, along with all who know the truth. He rejoices that some of her children are walking in truth, as the Father commanded, and he asks them to keep the commandment they have had from the beginning: to love one another. Love means walking according to God's commandments. He warns that many deceivers have gone out into the world who do not confess that Jesus Christ came in the flesh; such a person is the deceiver and the antichrist. Believers must watch themselves so that they do not lose what they have worked for, but receive a full reward. Anyone who goes beyond the teaching of Christ and does not remain in it does not have God, but whoever remains in the teaching has both the Father and the Son. If someone comes bringing a different teaching, the church should not receive that person into the house or give a welcome, because doing so shares in their evil work. The elder has much more to say, but he hopes to visit and speak face to face so that their joy may be complete.

## Chapter Background and Story

Second John is a short pastoral letter written to protect a church that the elder loved. In the first century, traveling teachers and missionaries moved from town to town, relying on the hospitality of believers. Churches met in homes, so welcoming a traveling teacher often meant giving that person a place to stay, a meal, and an opportunity to teach the gathered church. This was a beautiful practice that helped spread the gospel. But it also created an opening for false teachers to gain influence.

The same deceivers described in 1 John, those who denied that Jesus Christ came in the flesh, were apparently traveling among the churches. The elder writes to warn this congregation before they arrive. His letter weaves together two words again and again: truth and love. He wants the church to love one another deeply, and he wants that love to be guarded by the truth about Jesus.

### Greeting in Truth and Love (vv1–3)

[[v1|The elder, to the chosen lady and her children, whom I love in truth, and not I only, but also all those who know the truth]]. The elder's love for this church is not mere affection. It is love "in truth," rooted in their shared faith in Jesus. And it is not his love alone. All who know the truth share this bond. The gospel creates a family of love that reaches across cities and congregations.

[[v2|for the truth’s sake, which remains in us, and it will be with us forever:]] The truth is not just a set of ideas but a living reality that "remains" in believers. In John's writings, truth is closely connected with Jesus Himself, who said, [[John 14:6|I am the way, the truth, and the life]]. Because the truth remains in believers, their fellowship has a permanent foundation.

[[v3|Grace, mercy, and peace will be with us, from God the Father and from the Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of the Father, in truth and love]]. Most New Testament greetings express a wish or prayer for grace and peace. Here the elder states it as a confident promise: grace, mercy, and peace "will be with us." These gifts come from both the Father and the Son, and they are experienced "in truth and love," the two themes that will shape the entire letter.

### The Commandment of Love (vv4–6)

The elder's joy over believers walking in truth and his appeal to love one another are studied below.

### Watch Out for Deceivers (vv7–11)

The warning about deceivers, the call to remain in Christ's teaching, and the instruction about hospitality are studied below.

### Hope to Meet Face to Face (vv12–13)

[[v12|Having many things to write to you, I don’t want to do so with paper and ink, but I hope to come to you and to speak face to face, that our joy may be made full]]. The elder has more to say, but he prefers to say it in person. Letters are valuable, but nothing replaces being together. Christian fellowship is meant to be personal and embodied, and the elder expects that their joy will be complete when they meet.

[[v13|The children of your chosen sister greet you]]. The letter closes with greetings from "the children of your chosen sister," likely the members of the elder's own church. Two congregations, joined in Christ, send love to one another like sisters in one family.

## Important Verses Explained

### 2 John 1:4–6 — Walking in Truth, Walking in Love

verse: 2 John 1:4-6

#### Simple Explanation

The elder begins with joy: "I rejoice greatly that I have found some of your children walking in truth." He has heard that members of the church are living out the truth of the gospel. "Walking" is a biblical picture of daily conduct. Walking in truth means living in a way that matches what one believes about Jesus.

He notes that this is "even as we have been commanded by the Father." Walking in truth is not just a good idea; it is what God the Father asks of His children.

In verse 5, the elder makes a gentle appeal: "Now I beg you, dear lady, not as though I wrote to you a new commandment, but that which we had from the beginning, that we love one another." There is nothing novel about this command. It goes back to Jesus Himself, who told His disciples to love one another as He had loved them (John 13:34).

Verse 6 explains what this love looks like: "This is love, that we should walk according to his commandments." Love is not a vague feeling. It is shown by living the way God directs. And God's central command is to walk in love.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that truth and love are inseparable. Some people emphasize truth and become cold, harsh, or proud. Others emphasize love and become willing to set truth aside. The elder holds both together. The church that walks in truth must also walk in love, and genuine love walks according to God's commands.

There is a beautiful circle in verse 6. Love means obeying God's commands, and God's command is that we love. Love and obedience define each other. This reflects Jesus' teaching that the whole law hangs on loving God and loving neighbor (Matthew 22:37–40).

The elder's appeal to what was "from the beginning" is also significant. The false teachers likely claimed new insights that went beyond the original message. The elder points back to what the church first received from Jesus and the apostles. True spiritual growth does not leave the original gospel behind; it goes deeper into it.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers today can take encouragement that faithful living brings joy to others. Those who invest in a church, a family, or the faith of others rejoice when they see people walking in truth.

This passage also invites believers to keep truth and love together in every relationship. Speaking truth without love can wound. Offering love without truth can mislead. In families, friendships, and church life, believers are called to be both truthful and loving, as Jesus was full of grace and truth (John 1:14).

Love, as the elder defines it, is practical. It shows itself in obedience to God: patience, kindness, honesty, forgiveness, faithfulness, and care. These are the everyday ways believers walk in love.

### 2 John 1:7–9 — Remain in the Teaching of Christ

verse: 2 John 1:7-9

#### Simple Explanation

The elder now explains why he has stressed truth: "For many deceivers have gone out into the world, those who don’t confess that Jesus Christ came in the flesh." These deceivers denied that Jesus truly became human. They may have taught that He only seemed to have a body or that the divine Christ was separate from the man Jesus. The elder calls such a person "the deceiver and the Antichrist," meaning one who opposes the true Christ.

In verse 8 he warns: "Watch yourselves, that we don’t lose the things which we have accomplished, but that we receive a full reward." The church has labored in faith, love, and service. If they are led away by false teaching, they could lose the fruit of that labor. He wants them to receive a full reward.

Verse 9 gives the test: "Whoever transgresses and doesn’t remain in the teaching of Christ doesn’t have God." The word "transgresses" can also be understood as "goes ahead" or "goes beyond." The false teachers claimed to have advanced beyond the basic message about Jesus. But going beyond Christ's teaching means leaving God behind. In contrast, "He who remains in the teaching has both the Father and the Son."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The incarnation, the truth that the eternal Son of God became a real human being, is at the heart of the Christian faith. If Jesus did not truly come in the flesh, He could not truly die for sins or rise bodily from the dead. The salvation of human beings depends on Jesus being both fully God and fully human. That is why the elder takes this denial so seriously.

"The teaching of Christ" may mean the teaching about Christ, or the teaching Christ Himself gave. Both are true. The apostles passed on what Jesus taught and what He revealed about Himself. To remain in this teaching is to remain in relationship with the Father and the Son. Truth is not merely information; it is the way of knowing God personally.

The warning against "going beyond" is a needed caution. Christians should grow in understanding, but growth that departs from the apostolic witness about Jesus is not progress. The safest path forward in faith is always deeper into Christ, not beyond Him.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many voices today offer new ideas about Jesus. Some reduce Him to a wise teacher, others to a symbol or a spiritual force. Believers can test such ideas by Scripture: Does this teaching affirm that Jesus is the Son of God who truly became human, died, and rose again?

The call to "watch yourselves" is also personal. Every believer needs to guard their heart and mind, staying rooted in Scripture and in a community that faithfully teaches it. This is not fearfulness, but wisdom.

Believers can also take comfort in the promise of verse 9. Those who remain in the teaching of Christ have both the Father and the Son. There is no higher spiritual knowledge to chase. In Jesus, believers already have everything they need for life and godliness.

### 2 John 1:10–11 — Discernment in Welcoming Teachers

verse: 2 John 1:10-11

#### Simple Explanation

The elder now gives a practical instruction: "If anyone comes to you and doesn’t bring this teaching, don’t receive him into your house, and don’t welcome him." This refers to traveling teachers who came to churches expecting hospitality and an opportunity to teach. The elder tells the church not to give such teachers a home base or a platform.

Verse 11 explains why: "for he who welcomes him participates in his evil deeds." Supporting a false teacher, giving them lodging, resources, and official approval, would make the church a partner in spreading what destroys faith.

The phrase "don’t welcome him" refers to a formal greeting of acceptance that signaled endorsement and fellowship, not ordinary politeness.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This instruction must be read carefully in its setting. In the first century, churches met in homes. Receiving a teacher "into your house" meant inviting them into the gathered church and sponsoring their ministry. The elder is not forbidding believers from showing basic kindness, courtesy, or compassion to people who disagree with them. Elsewhere Scripture calls believers to love their enemies, to do good to all people, and to show hospitality to strangers (Luke 6:27; Galatians 6:10; Hebrews 13:2).

What the elder forbids is giving active support and endorsement to those who are spreading a message that denies Christ. Love for the church requires protecting it from teaching that would lead people away from Jesus. Shepherds protect sheep from wolves (Acts 20:28–30).

This passage also shows that hospitality, though a precious Christian virtue, must be practiced with discernment. Truth guides love. The same letter that urges believers to love one another also urges them not to lend their homes and resources to the spread of falsehood.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers and churches today can apply this passage by being thoughtful about whom they support and promote. Inviting someone to teach, sharing their materials, funding their work, or giving them a platform implies endorsement. Churches have a responsibility to be discerning about what they help spread.

This passage does not justify rudeness, hostility, or refusing friendship to people with different beliefs. Believers are called to be gracious and kind to everyone, including those who do not share their faith. Many people come to Christ because believers showed them patient love and welcome.

The balance the elder teaches is wise: warm love for people and firm loyalty to the truth about Jesus. Believers can show kindness to all while refusing to become partners in spreading what denies Christ.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Second John is brief, but its message is rich. It shows that truth and love belong together. The elder loves this church "in truth," rejoices that its members are walking in truth, and calls them to keep the commandment they have had from the beginning: to love one another. Love, in turn, means walking according to God's commands. Neither truth nor love can be set aside without losing the other.

The letter also calls believers to be watchful. Deceivers were spreading a false message about Jesus, denying that He truly came in the flesh. Today, too, believers meet many ideas about who Jesus is. They are called to remain in the teaching of Christ, testing every message by the witness of Scripture. Those who remain in His teaching have both the Father and the Son.

This watchfulness shapes how believers use their influence and resources. Hospitality and generosity are good gifts, but they should not be used to promote teaching that leads people away from Jesus. At the same time, believers can show genuine kindness to all people, holding truth firmly while extending grace freely.

Finally, the letter ends with a longing for personal fellowship. The elder wants to see his friends face to face so that their joy may be complete. Believers today can value the gift of being together, investing in real relationships within the body of Christ, where truth and love are lived out side by side.

## Chapter Prayer

Father, thank You for the truth that remains in us and will be with us forever, and for the grace, mercy, and peace that come from You and from Your Son, Jesus Christ. Help me to walk in truth and to keep the commandment I have heard from the beginning, to love others as You have loved me. Teach me that love means walking according to Your commands. Guard my heart and mind from every deception about Jesus, and keep me rooted in the teaching of Christ, so that I may have both the Father and the Son. Give me wisdom and discernment in what I support and share, and give me a gracious and kind heart toward all people. Thank You for the joy of fellowship with Your people, and draw us together in truth and love. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
