# 1 Corinthians 8
theme: Knowledge Puffs Up, but Love Builds Up
main: Answering a question about food sacrificed to idols, Paul agrees that idols are nothing and that there is only one God, the Father, and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom all things exist. But he warns that knowledge alone can make people proud, while love builds others up. Not every believer has this knowledge; some, who once worshiped idols, would feel their consciences defiled by eating such food. If believers with knowledge eat in idol temples and lead weaker believers to violate their consciences, they sin against those for whom Christ died, and against Christ Himself. Paul declares that he would rather never eat meat again than cause a brother to stumble.

## Chapter Background and Story

1 Corinthians 8 begins a three-chapter discussion (chapters 8–10) of a question the Corinthians had asked: Can believers eat food that has been sacrificed to idols?

This was a practical issue in Corinth. Much of the meat sold in the marketplace came from animals sacrificed in pagan temples. Part of the animal was burned on the altar, part went to the priests, and the rest was often sold in the market or eaten at feasts in the temple dining rooms. Social life in Corinth often revolved around such meals. Trade guilds, family celebrations, and civic events were frequently held in temple banquet halls. Refusing to attend could mean social isolation or economic loss.

Some Corinthian believers argued that since idols are not real gods, eating food offered to them was meaningless; they could eat freely, even in temple dining halls. Others, especially those who had recently left idol worship, felt that eating such food would draw them back into idolatry and violate their consciences.

Paul's answer is nuanced. In chapter 8, he agrees that idols are nothing, but he emphasizes that love must govern the use of knowledge and freedom. In chapter 9, he gives his own example of setting aside his rights for the sake of others. In chapter 10, he warns against participating in idol worship and gives practical guidelines about eating.

### Knowledge and Love (8:1–3)

Paul's opening principle is studied below.

### One God and One Lord (8:4–6)

[[v4|Therefore concerning the eating of things sacrificed to idols, we know that no idol is anything in the world, and that there is no other God but one]]. Paul agrees with the Corinthians who have knowledge: idols are not real gods. There is only one God. This reflects the great confession of Israel, the Shema: [[Deuteronomy 6:4|Hear, Israel: The LORD is our God. The LORD is one]].

[[v5|For though there are things that are called “gods”, whether in the heavens or on earth—as there are many “gods” and many “lords”—]]. In the Roman world, people worshiped many gods and called many rulers "lord." Paul acknowledges that these so-called gods exist in people's minds and practices, and later he warns that demons stand behind idol worship (10:20). Paul's great confession is studied below.

### Concern for the Weak (8:7–13)

[[v7|However, that knowledge isn’t in all men. But some, with consciousness of an idol until now, eat as of a thing sacrificed to an idol, and their conscience, being weak, is defiled]]. Not every believer has fully grasped that idols are nothing. Some who had worshiped idols for years still associated the food with idol worship. If they ate it, they felt that they were participating in idolatry, and their consciences were troubled.

[[v8|But food will not commend us to God. For neither, if we don’t eat are we the worse, nor if we eat are we the better]]. Food itself does not make anyone more or less acceptable to God. Eating or not eating is spiritually neutral.

[[v9|But be careful that by no means does this liberty of yours become a stumbling block to the weak]]. However, freedom must be exercised carefully. [[v10|For if a man sees you who have knowledge sitting in an idol’s temple, won’t his conscience, if he is weak, be emboldened to eat things sacrificed to idols?]] If a weaker believer saw a stronger believer eating in a pagan temple, they might be encouraged to do the same, even though their conscience told them it was wrong. The consequences, and Paul's own resolution, are studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 Corinthians 8:1–3 — Knowledge Puffs Up, Love Builds Up

verse: 1 Corinthians 8:1-3

#### Simple Explanation

The Corinthians had asked Paul about eating food sacrificed to idols. Some of them believed they had special knowledge on this subject. They knew that idols were not real gods, so they felt free to eat such food without concern.

[[v1|Now concerning things sacrificed to idols: We know that we all have knowledge]]. The phrase "we all have knowledge" may be a slogan from the Corinthians' letter. Paul agrees that they have knowledge, but he immediately qualifies it.

[[v1|Knowledge puffs up, but love builds up]]. Knowledge alone can make people proud. The word "puffs up" pictures something inflated, like a balloon, full of air but empty inside. The Corinthians were proud of their knowledge, and it was making them arrogant toward those who did not share it. Love, on the other hand, "builds up." It strengthens others, like a builder constructing a house. Love uses knowledge to help others, not to feel superior.

[[v2|But if anyone thinks that he knows anything, he doesn’t yet know as he ought to know]]. People who think they have everything figured out do not truly understand. True knowledge includes humility, recognizing how much one does not know.

[[v3|But anyone who loves God is known by him]]. Paul shifts the focus. What matters most is not what people know, but whether they love God. And those who love God are known by Him. Being known by God, having a relationship with Him, is far more important than having knowledge about Him.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that love is greater than knowledge. Paul later writes in this same letter, [[1 Corinthians 13:2|If I have the gift of prophecy, and know all mysteries and all knowledge]], [[1 Corinthians 13:2|but don’t have love, I am nothing]]. Knowledge is valuable, but without love, it becomes a source of pride and division.

They also show that true knowledge leads to humility. The more people truly understand, the more they recognize the limits of their understanding. Pride in knowledge is a sign of shallow understanding.

Finally, these verses show that being known by God is the heart of faith. Paul writes, [[Galatians 4:9|But now that you have come to know God, or rather to be known by God]]. Salvation is not merely knowing facts about God; it is being in a relationship with Him in which He knows and loves His people.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses challenge believers who are knowledgeable about the Bible or theology. Knowledge is good, but it must be joined with love. Believers can ask whether their knowledge makes them humble and loving, or proud and critical.

They also encourage believers to use their knowledge to build others up. Instead of using knowledge to win arguments or feel superior, believers can use it to help others grow in faith.

Finally, these verses remind believers that what matters most is loving God and being known by Him. Believers can focus on deepening their relationship with God, not merely accumulating information about Him.

### 1 Corinthians 8:6 — One God, the Father, and One Lord, Jesus Christ

verse: 1 Corinthians 8:6

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had agreed with the Corinthians that idols are nothing and that there is only one God. He acknowledged that people in the Roman world worshiped many so-called gods and lords. Now he gives a powerful confession of Christian faith.

[[v6|yet to us there is one God, the Father, of whom are all things, and we for him]]. For believers, there is one God, the Father. All things come "from" Him; He is the Creator and source of everything. And believers exist "for" Him; He is their purpose and goal.

[[v6|and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom are all things, and we live through him]]. There is also one Lord, Jesus Christ. All things came into being "through" Him; He was the agent of creation. And believers live "through" Him; He is the means of their salvation and new life.

This verse is remarkable. Paul is adapting the Shema, the central confession of Israel's faith: [[Deuteronomy 6:4|Hear, Israel: The LORD is our God. The LORD is one]]. In the Greek translation of the Old Testament, the word for "LORD" is kyrios. Paul takes this confession and expands it to include Jesus. "One God" is the Father; "one Lord" is Jesus Christ. Paul includes Jesus within the identity of the one God of Israel, while distinguishing Him from the Father.

This is one of the earliest and clearest statements of what Christians later called the doctrine of the Trinity. There is one God, yet the Father and the Son are distinct Persons, both sharing the divine identity.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that Jesus is fully divine. He shares in the work of creation, which belongs to God alone. John writes, [[John 1:3|All things were made through him. Without him, nothing was made that has been made]]. Paul writes, [[Colossians 1:16|For by him all things were created]].

It also shows that Christian faith is monotheistic. Christians do not worship many gods. They worship one God, revealed as Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. This confession set early Christians apart in a world of many gods.

Finally, the verse shows the purpose of human life. All things are from God and for God. Believers live through Christ. Their lives have meaning and direction because they belong to the one true God.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to worship Jesus as Lord. He is not merely a great teacher or prophet. He is the one Lord through whom all things were made. Believers can give Him the honor and trust that belong to God.

It also encourages believers to reject modern idols. People today may not worship statues, but they often give ultimate allegiance to money, success, pleasure, power, or human leaders. Believers confess that there is one God and one Lord, and they can give their highest loyalty to Him alone.

Finally, the verse gives believers a sense of purpose. All things are from God and for Him, and believers live through Christ. Every part of life, including work, relationships, and leisure, can be lived for God's glory.

### 1 Corinthians 8:11–12 — Sinning Against Christ

verse: 1 Corinthians 8:11-12

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had explained that some believers had the knowledge that idols are nothing, while others did not. If a believer with knowledge ate in an idol's temple, a weaker believer who saw them might be encouraged to eat too, even though their conscience told them it was wrong.

[[v11|And through your knowledge, he who is weak perishes, the brother for whose sake Christ died]]. Paul describes the serious consequences. The weaker believer could be "destroyed" or "ruined." This does not necessarily mean losing salvation, but it describes serious spiritual harm. Acting against their conscience could draw them back into idolatry, damage their faith, or lead them away from Christ.

Notice how Paul describes this weaker believer: "the brother for whose sake Christ died." Christ valued this person so much that He gave His life for them. How could a stronger believer harm someone so precious to Christ over something as unimportant as food?

[[v12|Thus, sinning against the brothers, and wounding their conscience when it is weak, you sin against Christ]]. Using freedom in a way that harms a fellow believer is not neutral; it is sin. And it is not only sin against the brother, but sin against Christ Himself. Jesus identifies with His people. When believers harm one another, they harm Christ.

This echoes what Jesus said to Saul on the road to Damascus: [[Acts 9:4|why do you persecute me?]]. Jesus is so united with His people that what is done to them is done to Him.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show the value of every believer. Each one is a person for whom Christ died. This gives every believer immense worth, regardless of how mature, knowledgeable, or strong they are.

They also show the seriousness of causing others to stumble. Jesus warned, [[Mark 9:42|Whoever will cause one of these little ones who believe in me to stumble, it would be better for him if he were thrown into the sea with a millstone hung around his neck]]. Believers have a responsibility to care for one another's spiritual welfare.

Finally, these verses show the union between Christ and His people. Christ is so identified with believers that sinning against them is sinning against Him. This should lead believers to treat one another with the same care and reverence they would show to Christ.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to consider how their actions affect others. Believers may have the freedom to do certain things, but they should ask whether their actions might harm a weaker believer.

They also call believers to value every fellow believer. Every person in the church is someone for whom Christ died. Believers can treat one another with care and respect, especially those who are new or struggling in their faith.

Finally, these verses remind believers that how they treat others matters to Christ. Believers can examine their relationships, asking whether they have harmed anyone through careless words or actions, and seeking to make things right.

### 1 Corinthians 8:13 — I Will Eat No Meat

verse: 1 Corinthians 8:13

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had explained that believers with knowledge should be careful not to cause weaker believers to stumble. Now he gives his personal resolution.

[[v13|Therefore, if food causes my brother to stumble, I will eat no meat forever more, that I don’t cause my brother to stumble]]. Paul says that if eating meat would cause a fellow believer to fall into sin, he would never eat meat again. This is a remarkable statement. Paul knew that he had the freedom to eat. He agreed that idols are nothing and food does not affect a person's standing with God. Yet he was willing to give up his freedom entirely for the sake of a brother.

Paul's statement is a strong expression of love. He was not speaking about all situations; he would eat meat when it did not cause harm. But when his freedom threatened another person's faith, love took priority.

This verse prepares for chapter 9, where Paul explains how he gave up many rights for the sake of the gospel and others. He writes there, [[1 Corinthians 9:19|For though I was free from all, I brought myself under bondage to all, that I might gain the more]].

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that love is willing to sacrifice. Christian freedom is real, but it is not the highest value. Love for others is greater. Jesus Himself set aside His rights and privileges for the sake of others (Philippians 2:5–8).

It also shows that Christian maturity is measured by love, not by freedom. The Corinthians who had knowledge thought they were mature because they felt free to eat. Paul shows that true maturity is shown in willingness to give up freedom for the sake of others.

Finally, the verse shows Paul's example as a leader. He did not ask others to do what he was unwilling to do. He modeled the self-giving love he taught.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse challenges believers to consider whether there are freedoms they should give up for the sake of others. This might involve choices about alcohol, entertainment, or other activities. If a believer's freedom might lead someone else into sin or harm their faith, love may call them to set it aside, at least in certain settings.

It also encourages believers to prioritize relationships over rights. Believers can ask not only "Am I allowed to do this?" but "Will this help or harm others?"

Finally, the verse encourages believers to follow Paul's example. Leaders, parents, and mature believers can model self-giving love, showing others that following Christ means putting others first.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

1 Corinthians 8 teaches that knowledge without love leads to pride, while love builds others up. Believers can value knowledge and grow in understanding, but they must always use their knowledge in love, seeking to strengthen others rather than to feel superior. What matters most is loving God and being known by Him.

The chapter also contains a powerful confession: there is one God, the Father, and one Lord, Jesus Christ. Believers can worship Jesus as the Lord through whom all things were made, and reject every idol, ancient or modern, that competes for their allegiance.

1 Corinthians 8 calls believers to care for those whose consciences are weaker. Every believer is someone for whom Christ died. Using freedom in a way that harms a fellow believer is sin against Christ Himself. Believers can consider how their actions affect others and be willing to limit their freedom out of love.

Finally, the chapter shows Paul's example of self-giving love. He was willing to give up meat forever rather than cause a brother to stumble. Believers can follow his example, prioritizing love over rights and modeling for others what it means to follow Christ, who gave up everything for us.
