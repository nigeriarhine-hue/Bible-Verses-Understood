# 1 John 2
theme: Knowing God, Loving One Another, and Remaining in Him
main: John writes so that his dear children will not sin, but if anyone does sin, they have an Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous, who is the atoning sacrifice for their sins and for the sins of the whole world. People show they know God by keeping His commands; whoever claims to know Him but disobeys is a liar, while those who obey have God's love made complete in them and should walk as Jesus walked. The old command that is also new is to love one another; whoever claims to be in the light but hates a brother or sister is still in darkness, but whoever loves remains in the light. John encourages children, fathers, and young people in their forgiveness, knowledge of God, and victory over the evil one. He warns believers not to love the world or its desires of the flesh, desires of the eyes, and pride in possessions, because the world is passing away, but whoever does God's will lives forever. Many antichrists have already come, some of whom left the fellowship, showing they never truly belonged; the liar is the one who denies that Jesus is the Christ, denying the Father and the Son. Believers have an anointing from the Holy One and know the truth; they should let what they heard from the beginning remain in them, so they will remain in the Son and the Father and have confidence, not shame, when He appears.

## Chapter Background and Story

In chapter 1, John declared that God is light and that believers must walk in the light, confessing their sins. Chapter 2 continues these themes and introduces several of the "tests" that run through the letter. How can believers know they truly know God? John gives three answers: they obey God's commands, they love one another, and they believe the truth about Jesus.

The false teachers who had left the community claimed to know God, but their lives and beliefs showed otherwise. They did not obey God's commands, they did not love the believers, and they denied that Jesus is the Christ. John wants to reassure the faithful believers who remained that they truly belong to God, while warning them not to be led astray.

Throughout the chapter, John speaks with tender affection, calling his readers "my little children" and "beloved." He writes as an elderly apostle, a spiritual father to the churches.

### Our Advocate (2:1–2)

Jesus as Advocate and atoning sacrifice is studied below.

### Knowing Him by Obedience (2:3–6)

[[v3|This is how we know that we know him: if we keep his commandments]]. Obedience is evidence of genuine relationship with God. [[v4|One who says, “I know him,” and doesn’t keep his commandments, is a liar, and the truth isn’t in him]]. Claiming to know God while living in disobedience is self-deception. [[v5|But God’s love has most certainly been perfected in whoever keeps his word. This is how we know that we are in him]]: "God's love" may mean love for God or God's love working in a person; obedience shows that God's love has reached its goal in them. The call to walk as Jesus walked is studied below.

### The Old and New Commandment (2:7–11)

[[v7|Brothers, I write no new commandment to you, but an old commandment which you had from the beginning. The old commandment is the word which you heard from the beginning]]. The command to love is not new; the readers had heard it from the beginning of their Christian lives, and it was already in the Law (Leviticus 19:18).

[[v8|Again, I write a new commandment to you, which is true in him and in you, because the darkness is passing away and the true light already shines]]. Yet it is also new, because Jesus gave it new meaning: [[John 13:34|Just as I have loved you, you also love one another]]. It is fulfilled in Christ and now being lived out in His people. The new age has dawned; darkness is fading. The contrast between love and hatred is studied below.

### Words to Every Stage of Faith (2:12–14)

[[v12|I write to you, little children, because your sins are forgiven you for his name’s sake]]. [[v13|I write to you, fathers, because you know him who is from the beginning. I write to you, young men, because you have overcome the evil one. I write to you, little children, because you know the Father]]. [[v14|I have written to you, fathers, because you know him who is from the beginning. I have written to you, young men, because you are strong, and the word of God remains in you, and you have overcome the evil one]]. John addresses believers at different stages. "Little children" may refer to all believers or to new believers; their sins are forgiven and they know the Father. "Fathers" are mature believers who have known Christ deeply over time. "Young men" are those in the vigor of faith, strong in God's word and victorious over the evil one. These are words of affirmation and encouragement. John writes not because he doubts them but because he is confident in God's work in them. Though the WEB uses "men," the encouragement applies to believers of every sex and age.

### Do Not Love the World (2:15–17)

The warning against loving the world is studied below.

### Antichrists (2:18–27)

[[v18|Little children, these are the end times, and as you heard that the Antichrist is coming, even now many antichrists have arisen. By this we know that it is the final hour]]. "The final hour" refers to the last age, the period between Christ's first and second comings. John's readers had heard that "the Antichrist" would come, a figure of opposition to Christ (compare the "man of sin" in 2 Thessalonians 2:3–4). John says that "many antichrists" have already appeared. Christians understand this in different ways: some believe a single future Antichrist will appear before Christ's return, preceded by many lesser "antichrists"; others see "antichrist" mainly as a spirit or pattern of opposition present throughout history. John's main concern is practical: anyone who denies the truth about Jesus is acting in the spirit of antichrist.

[[v19|They went out from us, but they didn’t belong to us; for if they had belonged to us, they would have continued with us. But they left, that they might be revealed that none of them belong to us]]. The false teachers had left the community. Their departure revealed that they had never truly belonged.

[[v20|You have an anointing from the Holy One, and you all have knowledge]]. The "anointing" is the Holy Spirit, given by Christ, "the Holy One." The false teachers may have claimed special knowledge for a select few; John insists that all believers have the Spirit and know the truth. [[v21|I have not written to you because you don’t know the truth, but because you know it, and because no lie is of the truth]].

[[v22|Who is the liar but he who denies that Jesus is the Christ? This is the Antichrist, he who denies the Father and the Son]]. [[v23|Whoever denies the Son doesn’t have the Father. He who confesses the Son has the Father also]]. The false teachers denied that Jesus is the Christ, perhaps separating the human Jesus from the divine Christ. To deny the Son is to lose the Father too, because the Father is known only through the Son (John 14:6–9).

[[v24|Therefore, as for you, let that remain in you which you heard from the beginning. If that which you heard from the beginning remains in you, you also will remain in the Son, and in the Father]]. [[v25|This is the promise which he promised us, the eternal life]]. Believers do not need new teachings. They need to hold on to the gospel they first received.

[[v26|These things I have written to you concerning those who would lead you astray]]. [[v27|As for you, the anointing which you received from him remains in you, and you don’t need for anyone to teach you. But as his anointing teaches you concerning all things, and is true, and is no lie, and even as it taught you, you will remain in him]]. John does not mean believers need no teachers at all; he himself is teaching them. He means they do not need the false teachers' supposed new revelations. The Spirit, who taught them the truth of the gospel, continues to guide them in it.

### Remain in Him (2:28–29)

[[v28|Now, little children, remain in him, that when he appears, we may have boldness and not be ashamed before him at his coming]]. "Remain," or "abide," is a key word in John's writings (John 15:4–10). Believers are to stay connected to Christ, so that when He returns, they will meet Him with confidence rather than shame.

[[v29|If you know that he is righteous, you know that everyone who practices righteousness has been born of him]]. A family resemblance shows who belongs to God. Those born of God reflect His righteousness.

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 John 2:1–2 — Jesus Our Advocate and Atoning Sacrifice

verse: 1 John 2:1-2

#### Simple Explanation

"My little children, I write these things to you so that you may not sin." John has just said that everyone sins (1 John 1:8–10). But he does not want his readers to think sin is acceptable. His goal is that they would not sin.

"If anyone sins, we have a Counselor with the Father, Jesus Christ, the righteous." When believers do sin, they are not left without help. They have a "Counselor," an Advocate, like a defense lawyer who speaks on their behalf. Jesus stands before the Father representing them. He is "the righteous," perfectly sinless, and therefore able to plead for sinners.

Verse 2: "And he is the atoning sacrifice for our sins." Jesus is not only the Advocate but also the sacrifice. His death dealt with sin, satisfying God's justice and removing guilt. The word translated "atoning sacrifice" has been rendered "propitiation" (turning away God's righteous wrath against sin) or "expiation" (removing sin itself). Both ideas are present: through Christ's sacrifice, sin is removed and God's justice is satisfied.

"And not for ours only, but also for the whole world." Christ's sacrifice is not limited to one group. It is sufficient for the whole world. The gospel is for everyone.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses hold together two truths: the call not to sin and the provision for when we do. The gospel does not make sin acceptable, but it also does not leave believers without hope when they fail.

Jesus as Advocate is a beautiful image. When believers sin, they might fear that God will reject them. But Jesus stands with them before the Father. Hebrews 7:25 says He always lives to make intercession for them. Romans 8:34 asks, "Who is he who condemns? It is Christ who died... who also makes intercession for us."

The phrase "for the whole world" has been understood in different ways. Some understand it to mean that Christ died for every person without exception, making salvation available to all. Others understand it to mean that Christ died for people from every nation, not only Jews or one community. All agree that the gospel is to be offered freely to everyone and that no one is beyond the reach of Christ's sacrifice.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can aim not to sin, taking holiness seriously. But when they do sin, they need not despair. They can come to the Father through Jesus, their Advocate, confessing their sin and trusting His sacrifice.

This passage also gives confidence in sharing the gospel. Christ's sacrifice is for the whole world. There is no person to whom believers cannot say, "Christ died for sins, and you can be forgiven."

Finally, believers can find comfort in knowing that Jesus is on their side. He is not their accuser but their Advocate.

### 1 John 2:6 — Walk as He Walked

verse: 1 John 2:6

#### Simple Explanation

"He who says he remains in him." Many people claim to have a relationship with God, to "remain," or abide, in Him.

"Ought himself also to walk just like he walked." Those who make this claim should live as Jesus lived. "Walk" means to conduct one's life. Jesus is the model for how believers should live.

How did Jesus walk? He walked in obedience to the Father, in love for others, in humility, in compassion for the suffering, in truthfulness, and in self-giving sacrifice.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse sets a high standard: the life of Jesus Himself. Believers are not called merely to follow rules but to follow a person. Peter wrote that Christ left an example so that believers should follow in His steps (1 Peter 2:21). Paul urged believers to imitate him as he imitated Christ (1 Corinthians 11:1).

This standard is not achieved by human effort alone. Believers "remain in him," and from that connection, Christ's life flows through them. Jesus said, [[John 15:4|Remain in me, and I in you. As the branch can't bear fruit by itself unless it remains in the vine, so neither can you, unless you remain in me]]. Walking as Jesus walked is the fruit of abiding in Him.

The word "ought" indicates obligation, but it is an obligation that flows from relationship. Those who truly know Christ will want to become like Him.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can study the life of Jesus in the Gospels, observing how He treated people, how He responded to opposition, how He prayed, and how He obeyed the Father. These observations become a pattern for their own lives.

In daily situations, believers can ask, "How would Jesus respond here?" This question can shape how they treat difficult people, how they use their time and money, and how they respond to temptation.

Finally, believers can remember that walking as Jesus walked depends on remaining in Him. Prayer, Scripture, worship, and fellowship keep them connected to the source of His life.

### 1 John 2:9–10 — Love Remains in the Light

verse: 1 John 2:9-10

#### Simple Explanation

"He who says he is in the light and hates his brother is in the darkness even until now." Someone may claim to be in the light, to know God and live in His truth. But if they hate a fellow believer, they are still in darkness, no matter what they claim.

Verse 10: "He who loves his brother remains in the light, and there is no occasion for stumbling in him." In contrast, those who love their fellow believers live in the light. "No occasion for stumbling" may mean that they will not stumble themselves, or that they will not cause others to stumble. Love keeps the path clear.

Verse 11 adds: "But he who hates his brother is in the darkness, and walks in the darkness, and doesn't know where he is going, because the darkness has blinded his eyes." Hatred blinds people. They lose their way and cannot see clearly.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

John makes love a test of genuine faith. It is not enough to claim to be in the light; love proves it. Hatred, even when hidden behind religious claims, reveals darkness. John will return to this theme repeatedly (1 John 3:14–15; 4:20).

"Brother" refers to fellow believers, members of God's family, both men and women. John's focus is on love within the community, which was threatened by division. But Jesus also taught love for neighbors and enemies (Luke 6:27–36), so love in the church is meant to overflow to all.

The image of darkness blinding the eyes is striking. Hatred distorts perception. People filled with resentment or bitterness often misjudge others and make poor decisions. Love, by contrast, brings clarity and keeps people on the right path.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can examine their hearts for hatred, resentment, or bitterness toward fellow believers. These attitudes are incompatible with walking in the light.

When conflicts arise, believers can seek reconciliation, forgive offenses, and choose love, even when it is difficult. Love does not require pretending that wrongs did not happen or remaining in harmful situations, but it does require letting go of hatred.

Finally, believers can let love guide their perception. Seeing others through eyes of love brings clarity, compassion, and understanding.

### 1 John 2:15–17 — Do Not Love the World

verse: 1 John 2:15-17

#### Simple Explanation

"Don’t love the world or the things that are in the world." "The world" here does not mean God's good creation or the people He loves (John 3:16). It means the system of values, desires, and priorities that is opposed to God, human society organized without Him.

"If anyone loves the world, the Father’s love isn’t in him." Love for the world and love for God compete. A heart devoted to the world's values cannot be fully devoted to God.

Verse 16 describes what is in the world. "The lust of the flesh": desires for physical pleasure and gratification apart from God's design. "The lust of the eyes": desires stirred by what one sees, covetousness and greed. "And the pride of life": arrogance about one's possessions, achievements, or status, boasting in what one has.

"Isn't the Father's, but is the world's." These desires do not come from God.

Verse 17: "The world is passing away with its lusts, but he who does God’s will remains forever." The world and its desires are temporary. Those who do God's will have a life that lasts forever.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The three temptations John describes appear throughout Scripture. In the Garden of Eden, Eve saw that the fruit was good for food (desire of the flesh), pleasing to the eyes (desire of the eyes), and desirable to make one wise (pride) (Genesis 3:6). Jesus faced similar temptations in the wilderness: to turn stones into bread, to receive the world's kingdoms, and to make a spectacular display of His status (Matthew 4:1–11). Where Adam and Eve failed, Jesus triumphed.

The contrast between what passes away and what remains is crucial. People who invest their lives in temporary things will lose them. Those who do God's will invest in what lasts forever. Jesus taught the same: [[Mark 8:36|For what does it profit a man to gain the whole world, and forfeit his life?]].

This warning does not mean believers must reject all enjoyment of life. God gives good gifts to enjoy (1 Timothy 6:17). The problem is loving those gifts more than the Giver, or pursuing desires in ways contrary to His will.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can examine what captures their hearts. Are they driven by the pursuit of pleasure, possessions, or status? These are powerful forces in modern culture, amplified by advertising and social media.

Believers can resist these pressures by cultivating love for God, through worship, Scripture, prayer, and service, and by practicing contentment, generosity, and humility.

Finally, believers can live with eternity in view. The things of this world are passing away. Investing in God's will, through love, faithfulness, and service, brings rewards that last forever.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

First John 2 offers both comfort and challenge. The comfort is that believers who sin have an Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous, who is the atoning sacrifice for their sins and for the sins of the whole world. When they fail, they can come to the Father through Him.

The challenge is that genuine faith shows itself in a changed life. Those who know God keep His commands and walk as Jesus walked. Those who are in the light love their brothers and sisters; hatred reveals darkness, no matter what a person claims. Believers can examine their lives by these tests, not to produce anxiety, but to grow in assurance and faithfulness.

John encourages believers at every stage of faith, reminding them that their sins are forgiven, that they know the Father, and that they have overcome the evil one. He warns them not to love the world, with its desires of the flesh, desires of the eyes, and pride in possessions, because the world is passing away, while those who do God's will live forever.

Finally, John warns about those who deny that Jesus is the Christ, showing the spirit of antichrist. Believers have the anointing of the Holy Spirit and know the truth. They need not chase new teachings. They can remain in what they heard from the beginning, remaining in the Son and in the Father, so that when Christ appears, they will meet Him with confidence and not shame.

## Chapter Prayer

Father, thank You that when I sin, I have an Advocate with You, Jesus Christ the righteous, who is the atoning sacrifice for my sins and for the sins of the whole world. Help me not to sin, and help me to keep Your commands. Teach me to walk as Jesus walked, in love, humility, and obedience. Remove any hatred or bitterness from my heart, and help me to love my brothers and sisters, so that I may remain in the light. Thank You that my sins are forgiven and that I know You. Keep me from loving the world, its pleasures, its possessions, and its pride. Help me to do Your will, which lasts forever. Guard me from every teaching that denies the truth about Your Son. Thank You for the anointing of Your Spirit. Help me to remain in Christ, so that I may have confidence when He appears. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
