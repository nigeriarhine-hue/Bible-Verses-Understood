# 2 Timothy 1
theme: Not a Spirit of Fear
main: Writing from prison near the end of his life, Paul thanks God for Timothy, remembers his tears, and recalls the sincere faith that first lived in Timothy's grandmother Lois and mother Eunice. He urges Timothy to fan into flame the gift of God, for God has not given a spirit of fear but of power, love, and self-control. Timothy must not be ashamed of the testimony about the Lord or of Paul's chains, but share in suffering for the gospel by God's power. God saved and called His people by His own purpose and grace, given in Christ before time began and now revealed through Christ, who abolished death and brought life and immortality to light. Paul is not ashamed, because he knows whom he has believed and is convinced He is able to guard what has been entrusted to Him. He urges Timothy to hold to sound teaching and guard the good deposit, and he contrasts those who deserted him with Onesiphorus, who sought him out and was not ashamed of his chains.

## Chapter Background and Story

When Paul wrote this letter, he was in a cold Roman prison, chained and facing death. He knew his time was short (2 Timothy 4:6). Many of his companions had left him. He longed to see Timothy one more time.

Timothy, meanwhile, was still in Ephesus, dealing with false teachers and the pressures of leadership. He may have been tempted toward fear or discouragement. Christianity was increasingly dangerous. Nero's persecution had made it costly to be associated with the faith, and even more costly to be associated with a prisoner like Paul. Some believers had distanced themselves from Paul to protect themselves.

In this chapter, Paul writes with deep tenderness to encourage Timothy to be courageous. He reminds him of his spiritual heritage, of the gift God gave him, of the power of the gospel, and of the faithfulness of God. The chapter is a call to boldness rooted in grace.

### Thanksgiving and Remembrance (1:1–5)

[[v1|Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ through the will of God, according to the promise of the life which is in Christ Jesus]]. Even facing death, Paul speaks of "the promise of the life which is in Christ Jesus." Death would not end his life; it would bring him into the fullness of life with Christ.

[[v2|to Timothy, my beloved child: Grace, mercy, and peace, from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Lord]]. In 1 Timothy, Paul called him "my true child"; here, "my beloved child." The affection is deep.

[[v3|I thank God, whom I serve as my forefathers did, with a pure conscience. How unceasing is my memory of you in my petitions, night and day]] [[v4|longing to see you, remembering your tears, that I may be filled with joy]]. Paul's service to God continued the faith of his Jewish ancestors; he saw faith in Christ as the fulfillment of that heritage. He remembers Timothy's tears, perhaps from their last parting. He longs to see him again. Timothy's spiritual heritage is studied below.

### Fan into Flame the Gift (1:6–7)

Paul's call to stir up the gift, and his reminder about the spirit God gives, are studied below.

### Do Not Be Ashamed (1:8–12)

Paul's call to unashamed courage and his description of the gospel are studied below. [[v11|For this I was appointed as a preacher, an apostle, and a teacher of the Gentiles]]. Paul's whole life was devoted to proclaiming this gospel. Paul's confidence in the face of suffering is studied below.

### Guard the Good Deposit (1:13–14)

[[v13|Hold the pattern of sound words which you have heard from me, in faith and love which is in Christ Jesus]]. A "pattern" is a model or outline, like an architect's sketch. Timothy is to keep Paul's teaching as his standard. And he is to hold it "in faith and love," not coldly or harshly, but with trust in Christ and love for people.

[[v14|That good thing which was committed to you, guard through the Holy Spirit who dwells in us]]. The gospel is a precious deposit entrusted to Timothy. He cannot guard it in his own strength, but he can guard it through the Holy Spirit, who lives in him and in all believers.

### Deserters and a Faithful Friend (1:15–18)

[[v15|This you know, that all who are in Asia turned away from me, of whom are Phygelus and Hermogenes]]. "Asia" refers to the Roman province whose capital was Ephesus. Many believers there, perhaps out of fear, had distanced themselves from Paul. This does not necessarily mean they abandoned the faith, but they abandoned Paul in his hour of need. This must have been deeply painful.

[[v16|May the Lord grant mercy to the house of Onesiphorus, for he often refreshed me, and was not ashamed of my chain]], [[v17|but when he was in Rome, he sought me diligently and found me]] [[v18|(the Lord grant to him to find the Lord’s mercy in that day); and in how many things he served at Ephesus, you know very well]]. In contrast, Onesiphorus was a faithful friend. When he came to Rome, he searched hard for Paul, which may have been difficult and dangerous in a large city full of prisons, and when he found him, he refreshed him. He was not ashamed of Paul's chains. Some interpreters think Onesiphorus may have died, since Paul prays for his household and for mercy for him "in that day." Whatever the case, his loyalty stands as a beautiful example of courageous friendship.

## Important Verses Explained

### 2 Timothy 1:5 — A Sincere Faith Passed Down

verse: 2 Timothy 1:5

#### Simple Explanation

Paul remembers Timothy's faith. "Having been reminded of the sincere faith that is in you." "Sincere" means without hypocrisy, genuine, unmasked. Timothy's faith was real.

"Which lived first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice." Timothy's faith had been passed down through two generations of women. Acts 16:1 tells us that Timothy's mother was a Jewish believer and his father was Greek. His father may not have been a believer. Timothy's mother and grandmother had taught him the Scriptures from childhood (2 Timothy 3:15), and they had come to faith in Christ, probably during Paul's first visit to Lystra (Acts 14:6–20).

"And, I am persuaded, in you also." Paul is confident that the same genuine faith now lives in Timothy.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse highlights the powerful role of family in passing on faith. Lois and Eunice did not have public ministries recorded in Scripture, but their faithful influence shaped one of the most important leaders of the early church. They taught Timothy the Scriptures, modeled genuine faith, and prepared him to receive the gospel.

The Old Testament emphasizes the responsibility of parents to teach their children about God (Deuteronomy 6:6–7; Psalm 78:4–7). Lois and Eunice fulfilled that calling. Their example also shows that faith can thrive even in a home where not everyone shares it.

At the same time, faith cannot simply be inherited. Each generation must come to genuine faith for themselves. Paul says the faith "lived first" in Lois and Eunice and now lives in Timothy. It was the same faith, but Timothy had to make it his own.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Parents, grandparents, and other family members can have a profound influence on the faith of children. Teaching them Scripture, praying with them, living out a genuine faith, and creating a home where faith is natural and real can plant seeds that bear fruit for generations.

This verse also encourages those who are raising children in homes where not everyone shares their faith. Eunice's husband was likely not a believer, yet her faith shaped Timothy. Faithful influence matters.

For those who did not grow up in a home of faith, this verse is not a cause for discouragement. God can call anyone to faith. And they can become the first generation to pass faith on to those who come after them.

### 2 Timothy 1:6–7 — Power, Love, and Self-Control

verse: 2 Timothy 1:6-7

#### Simple Explanation

"For this cause, I remind you that you should stir up the gift of God which is in you through the laying on of my hands." "Stir up" means to rekindle, to fan into flame. The image is of a fire that has burned low and needs to be stirred so that it blazes again. Timothy had received a spiritual gift for ministry when Paul and the elders laid hands on him (1 Timothy 4:14). Paul urges him to keep it burning brightly.

Verse 7: "For God didn’t give us a spirit of fear, but of power, love, and self-control." Timothy may have been naturally timid, and the dangers of the time could make anyone afraid. Paul reminds him that the Spirit God gives is not a spirit of cowardice.

Instead, God gives a spirit "of power": the strength to serve, to speak, and to endure. "Of love": care for others that motivates courageous action. And "of self-control": a sound, disciplined, sensible mind, able to think clearly under pressure.

The word "spirit" may refer to the Holy Spirit or to the disposition God gives. Most interpreters see a reference to the Holy Spirit, who produces these qualities in believers.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Fear is a natural human response to danger, and Scripture does not condemn every feeling of fear. Even Jesus felt deep distress in Gethsemane (Mark 14:33–34). But fear must not control God's people or keep them from obeying God. The Bible repeatedly commands, "Don't be afraid," because God is with His people (Joshua 1:9; Isaiah 41:10).

The three qualities Paul names balance one another. Power without love can become harsh. Love without power can become weak. And both need self-control, a clear and disciplined mind. Together they describe a healthy, courageous, Christlike character.

The image of fanning a flame suggests that spiritual gifts and passion can grow dim through neglect, discouragement, or fear. They need to be stirred up through prayer, use, and reliance on the Spirit.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many believers struggle with fear: fear of failure, rejection, conflict, the future, or speaking about their faith. This verse reminds them that God's Spirit gives power, love, and a sound mind. They can ask Him to strengthen them in moments of fear.

Believers can also "stir up" their gifts by using them. Gifts grow through practice. Those who have let their passion for God or their sense of calling grow cold can ask God to rekindle it and take small steps of obedience.

For those who struggle with anxiety disorders or persistent fear, this verse is not a rebuke. God understands human frailty. His Spirit brings comfort and strength, and help from counselors, doctors, and trusted friends can also be part of how He brings peace.

### 2 Timothy 1:8–10 — Do Not Be Ashamed of the Gospel

verse: 2 Timothy 1:8-10

#### Simple Explanation

"Therefore don’t be ashamed of the testimony of our Lord, nor of me his prisoner." In the Roman world, being associated with a condemned prisoner was shameful and dangerous. Paul urges Timothy not to be ashamed of the gospel or of Paul himself. Notice that Paul calls himself "his prisoner," the Lord's prisoner, not Rome's.

"But endure hardship for the Good News according to the power of God." Rather than avoiding suffering, Timothy should share in it, relying on God's power.

Verse 9 describes God: "who saved us and called us with a holy calling, not according to our works, but according to his own purpose and grace." Salvation is entirely God's work. He saved believers and called them to a holy life, not because of anything they had done but because of His own purpose and grace.

"Which was given to us in Christ Jesus before times eternal." God's grace was planned and given in Christ before the world began.

Verse 10: "but has now been revealed by the appearing of our Savior, Christ Jesus, who abolished death, and brought life and immortality to light through the Good News." What God planned from eternity has now been revealed in history through Jesus' coming. Christ "abolished death," broke its power, through His death and resurrection. And He "brought life and immortality to light," revealing the eternal life God offers through the gospel.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses explain why believers need not be ashamed of the gospel, even when it brings suffering. The gospel is the message of God's eternal purpose, His grace given before time began, and Christ's victory over death. It is the most glorious news in the world.

Paul wrote in Romans 1:16, [[Romans 1:16|For I am not ashamed of the Good News of Christ, because it is the power of God for salvation for everyone who believes]]. Jesus also warned that whoever is ashamed of Him and His words, He will be ashamed of when He comes in glory (Mark 8:38).

The statement that Christ "abolished death" does not mean that believers will not die physically. It means that death has been defeated and no longer has the final word. Paul writes in 1 Corinthians 15:54–57 that death has been swallowed up in victory through Christ. For believers, death is no longer an enemy to be feared but a doorway to life.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

In many places today, being openly Christian can bring ridicule, social exclusion, or even persecution. These verses encourage believers not to be ashamed of the gospel or of fellow believers who suffer for it. Standing with persecuted Christians, through prayer, advocacy, and support, is a way of living out this command.

Believers can also take comfort that their salvation does not depend on their works. God saved them by His grace, planned before time began. This security gives courage to face hardship.

Finally, the truth that Christ has abolished death gives hope in the face of mortality. Believers can face illness, aging, and death with confidence, because Christ has brought life and immortality to light.

### 2 Timothy 1:12 — I Know Whom I Have Believed

verse: 2 Timothy 1:12

#### Simple Explanation

"For this cause I also suffer these things." Paul's imprisonment and suffering were the result of his faithfulness in preaching the gospel.

"Yet I am not ashamed." Despite chains, desertion, and approaching death, Paul is not ashamed.

"For I know him whom I have believed." Paul's confidence does not rest on an idea or a set of beliefs alone, but on a person: Jesus Christ. He knows Christ personally, and he trusts Him completely. Notice that Paul does not say "I know what I have believed," but "whom."

"And I am persuaded that he is able to guard that which I have committed to him against that day." Paul has entrusted something to Christ, his life, his soul, his ministry, his future. He is convinced that Christ is able to keep it safe until "that day," the day of Christ's return and judgment. Some interpreters understand the phrase as "what He has entrusted to me," referring to the gospel; either way, Paul's confidence is in Christ's ability to guard it.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse expresses the heart of Christian assurance. It is not rooted in one's own strength, performance, or circumstances, but in the character and power of Christ. Paul knew Christ. He had walked with Him for decades. He had experienced His faithfulness in shipwrecks, beatings, imprisonments, and betrayals. Now, at the end of his life, he could say with confidence that Christ would keep what he had entrusted to Him.

This truth echoes Jesus' promise in John 10:28–29 that no one can snatch His sheep out of His hand or the Father's hand. It also echoes Jude 1:24, which praises God as the one who is able to keep believers from stumbling and present them blameless before His glory.

The image of entrusting something to another's care is powerful. Believers can entrust their lives, their loved ones, their futures, and their deepest fears to Christ, knowing He is able to guard them.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

When believers face suffering, uncertainty, or fear, they can say with Paul, [[v12|I know him whom I have believed]]. Their confidence rests in Christ's faithfulness, not in their circumstances.

Knowing Christ personally is the foundation of this confidence. Believers can deepen that knowledge through prayer, Scripture, worship, and obedience, so that, like Paul, they have a history of walking with Him to draw on in hard times.

Believers can also practice entrusting things to Christ: placing their worries, plans, and loved ones in His hands in prayer, and trusting Him to guard them.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Second Timothy 1 is a call to courage rooted in grace. Paul, facing death in prison and abandoned by many, wrote to encourage Timothy not to give in to fear. His words speak to every believer who faces pressure, danger, or discouragement because of their faith.

The chapter begins by honoring the faith passed down through Timothy's grandmother and mother. Family influence can be a powerful channel of faith, and believers can invest in the spiritual lives of the next generation through teaching, prayer, and example.

Paul then urges Timothy to fan into flame the gift God has given him, reminding him that God's Spirit is not a spirit of fear but of power, love, and self-control. Believers can ask God to rekindle their passion and courage, and they can use their gifts faithfully.

Paul calls Timothy not to be ashamed of the gospel or of those who suffer for it, but to share in suffering by God's power. The gospel is worth it: it reveals God's eternal grace and Christ's victory over death. Believers can stand with persecuted Christians and live boldly, knowing that death has been defeated.

At the heart of the chapter is Paul's confession: "I know him whom I have believed, and I am persuaded that he is able to guard that which I have committed to him." Believers can rest their whole lives in Christ's faithful care. They are called to hold to sound teaching in faith and love, to guard the gospel through the Holy Spirit, and, like Onesiphorus, to be faithful friends who are not ashamed to stand with those who suffer.

## Chapter Prayer

Father, thank You for those who have passed on the faith to me, and help me to pass it on to others. Stir up the gifts You have given me, and rekindle my love for You and my courage to serve. Thank You that You have not given me a spirit of fear, but of power, love, and self-control. When I am afraid, fill me with Your Spirit. Help me never to be ashamed of the gospel or of those who suffer for it. Thank You that You saved me and called me by Your grace, not by my works, and that Christ Jesus has abolished death and brought life and immortality to light. Lord Jesus, I know whom I have believed, and I trust that You are able to guard all that I have entrusted to You until that day. Help me to guard the truth through Your Spirit, and make me a faithful friend to those who need encouragement. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
