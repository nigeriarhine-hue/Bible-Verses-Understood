# John 8
theme: The Light of the World, the Truth That Frees, and the Eternal "I Am"
main: Jesus refuses to condemn a woman caught in adultery, shaming her accusers with their own sin while calling her to leave her sin behind. In the temple He declares that He is the light of the world, and He tells those who believe that remaining in His word will make them true disciples who know the truth and are set free. The debate grows fierce as Jesus confronts slavery to sin and the refusal to hear Him, and it reaches its climax when He declares, "Before Abraham came into existence, I AM."

## Chapter Background and Story

John 8 continues the setting of the Feast of Booths in the temple courts. During the festival, four enormous golden lamps were lit in the Court of the Women, and it was said that their light shone over all Jerusalem. The celebration recalled the pillar of fire that guided Israel through the wilderness by night. Against that background, Jesus says, "I am the light of the world."

The chapter contains some of the sharpest exchanges in the Gospels. John often uses the phrase "the Jews" to refer to the religious authorities and others in Jerusalem who opposed Jesus, not to the Jewish people as a whole. Jesus Himself, His disciples, and the first believers were Jewish, and many Jews in this very chapter believe in Him (8:30). These harsh words were spoken within a family argument among Jews about the meaning of their own Scriptures and the identity of their Messiah. They must never be used to justify contempt for Jewish people, as tragically has happened in history.

### A Woman Caught in Adultery (7:53–8:11)

A note on the text is needed here. The story of the woman caught in adultery (7:53–8:11) is not found in the earliest and most reliable Greek manuscripts of John, and in some later manuscripts it appears in other places, such as after Luke 21:38. For this reason, most modern Bibles place it in brackets or with a note. Many scholars nevertheless believe that it records a genuine event from Jesus' life, preserved in the church's memory, because it fits His character and teaching so well. The World English Bible includes it in its traditional place, and this commentary treats it with that understanding.

Jesus spent the night on the Mount of Olives and returned to the temple at dawn, where crowds gathered to hear Him. The scribes and Pharisees brought a woman and set her in the middle of the crowd. [[v4|Teacher, we found this woman in adultery, in the very act]]. They reminded Jesus that the Law of Moses commanded stoning in such cases (Leviticus 20:10; Deuteronomy 22:22–24) and asked what He said.

John explains their motive: [[v6|They said this testing him, that they might have something to accuse him of]]. It was a trap. If Jesus said to release her, they could accuse Him of disregarding the Law. If He said to stone her, He would contradict His reputation for mercy and might also defy Roman authorities, who did not permit Jews to carry out executions on their own (John 18:31). The trap also revealed their injustice. The Law required both the man and the woman to be punished, yet only the woman was brought. She was being used as a tool in their plan.

Jesus stooped and wrote on the ground with His finger. No one knows what He wrote. Some suggest He wrote the sins of the accusers, others a verse such as Jeremiah 17:13, others that He was simply refusing to be rushed. When they kept pressing Him, He stood and said, [[v7|He who is without sin among you, let him throw the first stone at her]]. Then He stooped and wrote again.

One by one, beginning with the oldest, they left. Jesus was left alone with the woman. His words to her are studied below.

### The Light of the World (8:12–20)

Jesus then declared, [[v12|I am the light of the world]]. This is studied below. The Pharisees objected that He was testifying about Himself, so His testimony was invalid. Jesus answered that His testimony was true because He knew where He came from and where He was going. And He was not alone: the Father who sent Him also testified about Him. The Law required two witnesses (Deuteronomy 19:15), and Jesus had two, Himself and His Father.

They asked, [[v19|Where is your Father?]] Jesus replied, [[v19|You know neither me nor my Father. If you knew me, you would know my Father also]]. To know Jesus is to know the Father. He said these things in the treasury area of the temple, yet no one seized Him, [[v20|because his hour had not yet come]].

### "Unless You Believe That I Am He" (8:21–30)

Jesus warned, [[v21|I am going away, and you will seek me, and you will die in your sins]]. His listeners wondered if He meant to take His own life. Jesus explained that they were from below and He was from above. Then He said, [[v24|unless you believe that I am he, you will die in your sins]]. The word "he" is not in the Greek; Jesus literally says, "unless you believe that I am." This is another echo of God's name, preparing for the climax of the chapter.

They asked, [[v25|Who are you?]] Jesus said that He was what He had been telling them from the beginning. Then He spoke of the cross: [[v28|When you have lifted up the Son of Man, then you will know that I am he]]. His crucifixion, the moment of apparent defeat, would reveal who He truly is. He added, [[v29|The Father hasn’t left me alone, for I always do the things that are pleasing to him]]. As He spoke, [[v30|many believed in him]].

### The Truth That Sets Free (8:31–47)

Jesus spoke to those who had believed in Him about what true discipleship involves. This is studied below. Some listeners objected that they were Abraham's descendants and had never been slaves to anyone, which, considering Egypt, Babylon, and Rome, was a remarkable statement. Jesus answered, [[v34|everyone who commits sin is the bondservant of sin]]. A slave does not remain in the household forever, but a son does. [[v36|If therefore the Son makes you free, you will be free indeed]].

The conversation became heated. They claimed Abraham as their father, but Jesus said that if they were truly Abraham's children, they would act like Abraham, who welcomed God's word. They were seeking to kill Him, which Abraham never did. They answered, [[v41|We were not born of sexual immorality]], possibly hinting at rumors about Jesus' birth. They claimed God as their Father.

Jesus said, [[v42|If God were your father, you would love me, for I came out and have come from God]]. Then came His strongest words: [[v44|You are of your father the devil]]. Jesus explained that the devil was a murderer from the beginning and the father of lies. Their desire to kill Jesus and their refusal of the truth showed whose will they were following. These words were addressed to specific opponents who were plotting murder, not to Jewish people as a group; Jesus also told Peter, His own disciple, [[Matthew 16:23|Get behind me, Satan!]] when Peter opposed God's plan. The point is that anyone who rejects the truth and plots evil is following the deceiver rather than God.

Jesus challenged them: [[v46|Which of you convicts me of sin?]] No one could. He concluded, [[v47|He who is of God hears the words of God]].

### Before Abraham Was, I AM (8:48–59)

The opponents accused Jesus of being a Samaritan and demon-possessed. Jesus calmly denied it and said that He honored His Father. Then He made a promise: [[v51|if a person keeps my word, he will never see death]]. They were outraged. Abraham died, and so did the prophets. [[v53|Who do you make yourself out to be?]]

Jesus said that His Father glorified Him, and that [[v56|Your father Abraham rejoiced to see my day. He saw it and was glad]]. Abraham, through faith in God's promises, looked forward to the coming of the promised Seed through whom all nations would be blessed (Genesis 22:18). His listeners protested that Jesus was not even fifty years old. How could He have seen Abraham? Jesus' answer is studied below. The crowd picked up stones to throw at Him, but He slipped away from the temple.

## Important Verses Explained

### John 8:10–11 — Neither Do I Condemn You

verse: John 8:10-11

#### Simple Explanation

The woman had been dragged before a crowd in the temple courts early in the morning, caught in adultery. Her accusers made her stand in the middle of everyone, publicly shamed, while they tried to trap Jesus. Under the Law, adultery was punishable by death. Her life seemed to hang on Jesus' answer.

Jesus said that whoever was without sin should throw the first stone. One by one, the accusers left, beginning with the oldest. They were [[v9|convicted by their conscience]]. Perhaps the older ones were wise enough to recognize their own sins most quickly. At the end, only Jesus and the woman remained.

Jesus stood up and asked her, [[v10|Woman, where are your accusers? Did no one condemn you?]] She answered, [[v11|No one, Lord]]. Then Jesus spoke words of astonishing grace: [[v11|Neither do I condemn you]]. The only person present who was truly without sin, and who therefore had the right to throw a stone, chose not to condemn her.

But Jesus did not stop there: [[v11|Go your way. From now on, sin no more]]. He did not pretend that what she had done was acceptable. He called it sin. But He gave her freedom from condemnation first, and then called her to a new life. Mercy came before the command, and the command flowed from the mercy.

It is worth noticing what Jesus did not do. He did not shame her further, lecture her in front of the crowd, or demand that she prove herself before He would show mercy. He treated her with dignity, addressing her directly and with respect.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This scene shows how Jesus holds together grace and truth (John 1:14). Grace: "Neither do I condemn you." Truth: "From now on, sin no more." Some people emphasize grace in a way that excuses sin; others emphasize truth in a way that crushes sinners. Jesus does neither. He forgives freely and calls to holiness.

It also shows how Jesus could release her from condemnation without setting aside God's justice. Jesus Himself would soon bear the condemnation of sin on the cross. Paul writes, [[Romans 8:1|There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus]]. The mercy Jesus showed the woman was costly; it would be paid for at Calvary.

The scene also exposes the hypocrisy of self-righteous accusers. The scribes and Pharisees were eager to condemn someone else's sin while ignoring their own, and they showed no concern for justice toward the man involved. Jesus' words turned the spotlight on every heart. All have sinned, and none can stand as a sinless judge over others.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage offers deep comfort to anyone weighed down by shame. Some people carry the memory of sins that others know about, or that they fear others will discover. Jesus' words are for them: He does not condemn those who come to Him. His forgiveness is not grudging or partial.

It also calls forgiven people to live differently. Jesus' mercy is not permission to continue in sin but power to leave it behind. Gratitude for grace becomes the motivation for a changed life.

Finally, the passage challenges anyone who is quick to condemn others. Believers can easily focus on the visible sins of others while ignoring their own pride, harshness, or hidden failings. Jesus' words invite humility. Those who know they have been forgiven are called to treat others with the same mercy, while still speaking the truth in love. At the same time, mercy toward sinners never means ignoring harm done to others; Jesus' compassion for this woman included protecting her from violence and from those who were using her.

### John 8:12 — I Am the Light of the World

verse: John 8:12

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus spoke these words in the temple during or just after the Feast of Booths. During the feast, great golden lamps were lit in the temple courts, giving light to the whole city. These lamps reminded the people of the pillar of fire by which God led Israel through the wilderness at night (Exodus 13:21). Against this background, Jesus made a bold claim: [[v12|I am the light of the world]].

Jesus does not say that He is one light among many, or a light for Israel only. He says that He is the light of the whole world. In the Old Testament, light is often associated with God Himself. [[Psalm 27:1|The LORD is my light and my salvation]]. Isaiah prophesied that God's servant would be [[Isaiah 49:6|a light to the nations]].

[[v12|He who follows me will not walk in the darkness]]. Darkness in Scripture represents sin, ignorance, confusion, danger, and separation from God. Without Jesus, people walk in darkness, unable to see their way. Following Him means walking in His light.

[[v12|but will have the light of life]]. Jesus does not only show the way; He gives life. The light He gives is the light that produces and sustains true life. Just as plants need sunlight to grow, people need the light of Christ to truly live.

The word "follows" is important. Jesus does not say, "He who admires Me," but "He who follows Me." Like the Israelites following the pillar of fire through the wilderness, believers follow Jesus step by step.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This is the second of Jesus' "I am" sayings in John. It connects with the opening of the Gospel, where John wrote that in the Word [[John 1:4|was life, and the life was the light of men]], and that [[John 1:5|The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness hasn’t overcome it]]. Jesus is the eternal light who has entered the world's darkness.

The saying also points to Jesus' role in revealing the truth about God and about people. Light exposes what is hidden. When people come to Jesus, their sin is revealed, but so is God's grace. Those who love darkness avoid the light (John 3:19–20), but those who come to the light find life.

In the next chapter, Jesus demonstrates this claim by giving sight to a man born blind, saying again, [[John 9:5|I am the light of the world]]. His words and His works agree.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse offers guidance to people who feel lost or confused. Many people today are uncertain about the meaning of life, the right way to live, or the path ahead. Jesus offers Himself as the light. Following Him does not mean every question is answered at once, but it means walking with the One who sees the way clearly.

It also calls believers to follow Jesus daily. Light is given for walking. Believers walk in His light by listening to His words, obeying His teaching, and staying close to Him in prayer. When they wander from Him, they find themselves stumbling in darkness again.

Finally, the verse reminds believers of their calling. Jesus also told His disciples, [[Matthew 5:14|You are the light of the world]]. Believers reflect His light to others through lives of truth, goodness, and love.

### John 8:31–32 — The Truth Will Make You Free

verse: John 8:31-32

#### Simple Explanation

As Jesus spoke about His relationship with the Father, many people believed in Him (8:30). Jesus then addressed these new believers, explaining what genuine discipleship involves: [[v31|If you remain in my word, then you are truly my disciples]].

To "remain" in Jesus' word means to continue in it, to live in it, and to keep returning to it. It is not a one-time decision but a lasting commitment. Initial belief is the beginning, but true disciples continue in His teaching over time.

[[v32|You will know the truth]]. Those who remain in Jesus' word come to know the truth. This is not merely knowing facts but knowing reality as God sees it, and ultimately knowing Jesus Himself, who later says, [[John 14:6|I am the way, the truth, and the life]].

[[v32|and the truth will make you free]]. This verse is often quoted in schools, libraries, and public buildings, usually to mean that knowledge or education liberates people. But Jesus means something deeper. The freedom He speaks of is freedom from the slavery of sin.

The listeners objected that they had never been slaves. Jesus explained, [[v34|everyone who commits sin is the bondservant of sin]]. Sin is not merely a series of bad actions; it is a power that enslaves. Then He promised, [[v36|If therefore the Son makes you free, you will be free indeed]]. True freedom comes through Jesus Himself.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal that genuine faith shows itself in perseverance. Throughout John, some people "believed" in a shallow way and later turned away (John 2:23–25; 6:66). In this very chapter, some who believed soon became hostile. Jesus teaches that true disciples continue in His word. Perseverance does not earn salvation, but it is evidence of genuine faith.

The passage also redefines freedom. Many people think of freedom as doing whatever they want. Jesus teaches that doing whatever one wants can be slavery, because sinful desires control the heart. True freedom is being released from sin's power to live as God intended. Paul expresses the same idea: [[Galatians 5:1|Stand firm therefore in the liberty by which Christ has made us free]].

Finally, the passage shows that truth and freedom are connected to a Person. The truth that frees is not an abstract idea but Jesus Himself and His word. Freedom comes through relationship with the Son, who has the authority to release slaves and make them members of God's family.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to remain in Jesus' word. Discipleship grows as believers read, study, and obey His teaching over time. Christians are called not only to begin well but to keep continuing in His word through every season of life.

They also offer hope to people who feel trapped. Many struggle with habits, addictions, fears, or patterns of sin they cannot break. Jesus promises real freedom. This freedom may come gradually, through the work of His Spirit, His word, and His people, but His promise is sure: those whom the Son sets free are free indeed.

Finally, these verses challenge cultural ideas of freedom. Believers can show that true freedom is not found in having no limits but in living in the truth. Life with Christ brings freedom from guilt, from slavery to sin, and from the fear of death.

### John 8:58 — Before Abraham, I AM

verse: John 8:58

#### Simple Explanation

This verse comes at the end of a long, heated debate in the temple courts. Jesus had promised that whoever keeps His word will never see death. His opponents were outraged. Abraham, the father of the nation, had died, and so had the prophets. Was Jesus claiming to be greater than Abraham?

Jesus answered that Abraham rejoiced to see His day. The opponents responded, [[v57|You are not yet fifty years old! Have you seen Abraham?]] Jesus was in His early thirties. Abraham had lived about two thousand years earlier. The claim seemed absurd.

Then Jesus said, [[v58|Most certainly, I tell you, before Abraham came into existence, I AM]]. Notice the contrast in the verbs. Abraham "came into existence"; he had a beginning. But Jesus does not say, "Before Abraham was, I was." He says, "I AM." He speaks of His existence as timeless and continuous, without beginning.

The phrase "I AM" recalls God's revelation of His name to Moses at the burning bush: [[Exodus 3:14|God said to Moses, “I AM WHO I AM,” and he said, “You shall tell the children of Israel this: ‘I AM has sent me to you.’”]] Jesus applies God's own name to Himself.

The listeners understood what He meant. [[v59|Therefore they took up stones to throw at him]]. Stoning was the penalty for blasphemy (Leviticus 24:16). They recognized that Jesus was claiming to be God.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse is one of the clearest claims to deity in the Gospels. Jesus does not merely claim to be a prophet or a messenger from God. He claims eternal existence and identifies Himself with the God of Israel who revealed Himself to Moses. This fulfills John's opening words: [[John 1:1|In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God]].

It also shows that Jesus is the fulfillment of Abraham's hope. God promised Abraham that all nations would be blessed through his offspring. Abraham looked forward to that promise and rejoiced. Jesus is the One in whom the promise is fulfilled. He is greater than Abraham, not as a rival, but as the eternal Lord whom Abraham trusted.

Finally, this verse forces a decision. As many have observed, someone who claims to be God is either mistaken, deceptive, or telling the truth. The opponents concluded that Jesus was a blasphemer. John wrote his Gospel so that readers would conclude that He is the eternal Son of God (John 20:31).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites people to consider who Jesus really is. He cannot be reduced to a wise teacher, a moral example, or an inspiring figure. He claimed to be the eternal I AM. Each person must decide how to respond to that claim.

It also gives believers deep confidence. The Jesus they trust is not limited by time. He existed before Abraham, He is present now, and He will be forever. The One who promises that those who keep His word will never see death is the eternal Lord who has authority over life and death.

Finally, the verse calls believers to worship. If Jesus is the I AM, He deserves the worship that belongs to God alone. Believers can bring Him their praise, trust, and obedience, knowing that the Lord who spoke to Moses from the burning bush is the same Lord who walked among us and died for us.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

John 8 begins with a woman surrounded by accusers and ends with Jesus surrounded by people ready to stone Him. In between, Jesus reveals who He is and what He offers. To the woman, He offers freedom from condemnation and a call to a new life. Those who carry shame can come to Jesus and hear the same words: "Neither do I condemn you." Those who are quick to condemn others are reminded that no one is without sin, and that mercy is the way of Christ.

The chapter presents Jesus as the light of the world. In a world of confusion and moral darkness, He offers light for the path and light that brings life. Following Him daily, listening to His word, and staying close to Him allow believers to walk in His light and reflect it to others.

John 8 also teaches that true discipleship means remaining in Jesus' word. The freedom He gives is not simply the freedom to do what we want but freedom from the slavery of sin. Many people who feel trapped by patterns they cannot break can take hope in His promise that those whom the Son sets free are free indeed.

Finally, the chapter brings readers face to face with Jesus' claim to be the eternal I AM. This claim cannot be ignored or softened. It calls for worship from those who believe and a serious response from those who are still considering Him. And it reminds believers that the One who forgives, guides, and frees them is the eternal Lord, worthy of their whole trust.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, You are the eternal I AM, who existed before Abraham and who stands above all time. I worship You. Thank You that You did not condemn the woman who was brought before You in shame, and that You do not condemn those who come to You for mercy. Thank You for bearing the condemnation I deserved. Help me to leave behind my sin and to walk in the new life You give. You are the light of the world; lead me out of darkness and let me follow You each day. Keep me in Your word, so that I may truly be Your disciple and know the truth that sets me free. Where I am still bound by sin or fear, set me free indeed. Guard my heart from self-righteousness and from quick condemnation of others, and make me a person of grace and truth like You. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
