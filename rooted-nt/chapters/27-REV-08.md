# Revelation 8
theme: Silence in Heaven and the First Four Trumpets
main: When the Lamb opens the seventh seal, there is silence in heaven for about half an hour. John sees the seven angels who stand before God, and seven trumpets are given to them. Another angel with a golden censer stands at the altar and is given much incense to offer with the prayers of all God's people on the golden altar before the throne. The smoke of the incense rises with the prayers before God. Then the angel fills the censer with fire from the altar and throws it on the earth, and there are peals of thunder, rumblings, flashes of lightning, and an earthquake. The seven angels prepare to sound their trumpets. At the first trumpet, hail and fire mixed with blood are hurled to the earth, and a third of the earth, a third of the trees, and all the green grass are burned up. At the second, something like a great mountain burning with fire is thrown into the sea; a third of the sea becomes blood, a third of sea creatures die, and a third of the ships are destroyed. At the third, a great star named Wormwood falls from the sky, burning like a torch, onto a third of the rivers and springs, making the waters bitter so that many people die. At the fourth, a third of the sun, moon, and stars are struck, so that a third of the day and night are without light. Then John sees an eagle flying in midair and crying, Woe, woe, woe to those who live on the earth, because of the three trumpets yet to sound.

## Chapter Background and Story

Revelation 8 opens the second series of seven judgments: the trumpets. In the Old Testament, trumpets were sounded to announce God's presence (Exodus 19:16, 19), to call His people to assembly, to sound the alarm in war, and to herald the day of the Lord (Joel 2:1). The trumpet judgments are warnings that call the world to repentance before the final day.

The trumpets closely resemble the plagues God sent on Egypt before the exodus: hail and fire, water turned to blood, darkness, locusts. Just as those plagues revealed God's power over the false gods of Egypt and led to the liberation of His people, the trumpets reveal His power over a world in rebellion and point toward the final deliverance of His people.

### How the Trumpets Relate to the Seals

Interpreters have understood the relationship between the seals, trumpets, and bowls in different ways. Some see them as a single sequence of events, one following another in time, with the seventh seal containing the seven trumpets and the seventh trumpet containing the seven bowls. Others see them as overlapping cycles that describe the same period from different angles, each ending with a picture of the end (6:12–17; 11:15–19; 16:17–21) and each growing in intensity. A notable feature supports the second view: the seals affect a fourth of the earth (6:8), the trumpets a third, and the bowls the whole, showing an increasing intensity rather than simply a new set of events. Many readers combine these insights, seeing real progression toward the end while recognizing that the cycles also repeat and deepen the same themes.

### The Seventh Seal and the Prayers of the Saints (8:1–5)

The silence in heaven and the offering of the prayers of the saints are studied below.

[[v2|I saw the seven angels who stand before God, and seven trumpets were given to them]]. These seven angels appear to be a special group who stand in God's presence, ready to carry out His commands. Jewish tradition spoke of seven archangels, including Gabriel, who said to Zechariah, [[Luke 1:19|I am Gabriel, who stands in the presence of God]].

[[v6|The seven angels who had the seven trumpets prepared themselves to sound]]. The scene is set. The prayers of God's people have risen before Him, and now His response begins.

### The Third Woe Announced (8:13)

[[v13|I saw, and I heard an eagle, flying in mid heaven, saying with a loud voice, “Woe! Woe! Woe to those who dwell on the earth, because of the other blasts of the trumpets of the three angels, who are yet to sound!”]] An eagle flies high overhead where all can see and hear it, announcing three woes for the three remaining trumpets. The eagle may also call to mind a bird of prey circling, a sign of coming judgment (compare Matthew 24:28). "Those who dwell on the earth" is a phrase Revelation uses for people who are hostile to God and settled in the world's ways, as distinct from God's people (compare Revelation 6:10; 13:8). The warning shows that the coming judgments will be even more severe than the first four.

Yet even this cry of woe carries mercy within it. A warning is given before the blow falls. Throughout Scripture, God sends warnings so that people may turn and live (Ezekiel 33:11). The eagle's cry, like the trumpets themselves, gives the world one more opportunity to listen. The tragedy, as chapter 9 will show, is that many refuse.

## Important Verses Explained

### Revelation 8:1 — Silence in Heaven

verse: Revelation 8:1

#### Simple Explanation

After all the drama of the first six seals and the loud worship of chapter 7, the opening of the seventh seal brings something surprising: "there was silence in heaven for about half an hour."

Heaven, which has been filled with songs, cries, and thunder, falls completely silent. The silence lasts "about half an hour," a brief but noticeable pause.

This silence prepares for what follows. The seventh seal does not reveal a new disaster directly. Instead, it opens into the seven trumpets.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Interpreters have suggested several meanings for this silence, and they may all contain truth. First, silence is a fitting response to God's coming in judgment. The prophets called for silence before the Lord: [[Habakkuk 2:20|But the LORD is in his holy temple. Let all the earth be silent before him!]] (see also Zephaniah 1:7; Zechariah 2:13). The silence expresses awe at the holiness and seriousness of God's judgments.

Second, the silence may allow the prayers of the saints to be heard. In Jewish tradition, the temple worship included times of silence when incense was offered and the people prayed. The very next verses describe the prayers of God's people rising before Him. It is as though all heaven falls silent to listen to the prayers of the suffering saints.

Third, the silence creates a dramatic pause before the next series of judgments, much like the hush before a storm breaks. It heightens the sense that something momentous is about to happen.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

In a world full of noise, this passage invites believers to recover the practice of silence before God. Worship is not only singing and speaking; it also includes stillness, reverence, and awe. Believers can make space in their lives to be quiet before God, to listen, and to wonder at His holiness.

The connection between silence and prayer is also encouraging. If heaven falls silent to hear the prayers of God's people, then believers can be confident that their prayers matter to God. He listens.

Finally, this silence reminds believers to take God's judgments seriously. They are not to be treated lightly or as entertainment. They call for humility, reverence, and repentance.

### Revelation 8:3–5 — Prayers Rising Like Incense

verse: Revelation 8:3-5

#### Simple Explanation

John sees "another angel" with "a golden censer," a container for burning incense. He stands at the golden altar before the throne. In the temple in Jerusalem, a golden altar of incense stood before the curtain leading to the Most Holy Place, and incense was burned there every morning and evening.

The angel is given "much incense," which he adds "to the prayers of all the saints." Verse 4 describes the result: "The smoke of the incense, with the prayers of the saints, went up before God out of the angel’s hand." The prayers of God's people rise into His presence, mingled with fragrant incense.

Then in verse 5, the angel fills the censer "with the fire of the altar," and throws it on the earth. Immediately there are "thunders, sounds, lightnings, and an earthquake." The prayers that went up to God now come down to earth in the form of His powerful response.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage reveals the power of prayer in God's purposes. The prayers of the saints, including the cry of the martyrs in 6:10, "How long?", have risen before God's throne. Now God answers. The trumpet judgments that follow are, in part, God's response to the prayers of His people for justice and for His kingdom to come.

The image of prayer as incense appears elsewhere in Scripture. David prayed, [[Psalms 141:2|Let my prayer be set before you like incense]]. In Revelation 5:8, the elders hold golden bowls of incense, [[Revelation 5:8|which are the prayers of the saints]]. Prayers are precious to God, a fragrant offering that delights Him.

The added incense from the angel may suggest that the prayers of God's people are made acceptable and powerful before God through His grace. Believers do not pray in their own worthiness but through the mediation of Christ (Hebrews 7:25).

The fire thrown on the earth recalls Ezekiel 10:2, where burning coals from among the cherubim are scattered over Jerusalem in judgment. The thunder, lightning, and earthquake recall God's appearance at Sinai. Heaven's response to prayer shakes the earth.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage encourages believers to pray with confidence. Their prayers are not lost or ignored. They rise before God like incense, and He hears and answers. Even prayers that seem to go unanswered for a long time are gathered before His throne.

Believers can pray especially for God's kingdom to come and His justice to be done, as Jesus taught (Matthew 6:10). These prayers are part of God's plan to set the world right.

This passage also shows that prayer is not a small or passive activity. It is part of the way God works in the world. Ordinary believers, praying faithfully, take part in God's great purposes.

### Revelation 8:7–12 — The First Four Trumpets

verse: Revelation 8:7-12

#### Simple Explanation

The first four trumpets bring judgments on the natural world. At the first trumpet, "hail and fire, mixed with blood" fall on the earth, burning a third of the earth and trees and all the green grass. This recalls the seventh plague on Egypt, when hail and fire fell (Exodus 9:23–25).

At the second trumpet, "something like a great burning mountain was thrown into the sea." A third of the sea becomes blood, a third of sea creatures die, and a third of ships are destroyed. This recalls the first plague, when the Nile turned to blood (Exodus 7:20–21).

At the third trumpet, "a great star fell from the sky, burning like a torch," onto the rivers and springs. Its name is "Wormwood," a bitter plant. A third of the waters become bitter, and many people die.

At the fourth trumpet, a third of the sun, moon, and stars are darkened. This recalls the ninth plague, the darkness over Egypt (Exodus 10:21–23).

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The repeated phrase "one third" is significant. These judgments are severe but partial. They do not destroy everything. They are warnings, meant to call people to repentance before final judgment comes. Even in judgment, God shows patience.

The images have been interpreted in different ways. Some take them as literal future events: ecological disasters, perhaps involving meteors or volcanic eruptions. Some historicist interpreters have linked them to invasions that struck the Roman Empire. Many interpreters see them as symbols of the ways God's judgment affects every part of creation that sinful humanity depends on: land, sea, fresh water, and light. The burning mountain may echo Jeremiah 51:25, where Babylon is called a destroying mountain that God will make a burnt mountain. Wormwood recalls Jeremiah 9:15, where God warns that He will give His unfaithful people wormwood to eat and poisoned water to drink. Rejecting God turns life's sweetness to bitterness.

The connection with the plagues of Egypt is the key. In Exodus, the plagues showed that the gods of Egypt were powerless and that the Lord alone is God. They also preceded the deliverance of God's people. The trumpets carry the same message: the powers of this world cannot stand against God, and He will deliver His people.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can see in these judgments a reminder that the created world depends on God. Land, sea, water, and light are His gifts. When humanity turns from Him, the effects ripple through all of life. This truth encourages gratitude for God's gifts and responsible care for His creation.

The partial nature of these judgments reveals God's patience. He does not desire anyone to perish but wants all to come to repentance (2 Peter 3:9). Believers can share this message of patience and invitation with others.

Finally, this passage reminds believers that God is greater than all the powers of the world. Like Israel in Egypt, they can trust that He will deliver them, even when the world around them is shaken.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Revelation 8 begins with silence. Heaven pauses in awe before God's judgments, and in that silence the prayers of the saints rise before Him. Believers can learn to be still before God, to approach Him with reverence, and to trust that He listens to their prayers.

The chapter shows that prayer is powerful. The prayers of God's people, mingled with incense, rise before His throne, and God answers by acting on the earth. Believers can pray faithfully for God's kingdom to come and His justice to be done, knowing that their prayers are part of His purposes.

The first four trumpets reveal that God's judgments affect every part of creation. They echo the plagues of Egypt and show that the powers of this world cannot stand against Him. Yet these judgments are partial, a sign of God's patience and a call to repentance.

Finally, the eagle's cry of woe reminds believers that the time is serious. A world that rejects God faces increasing judgment. This calls believers to live faithfully, to pray for those who do not know God, and to share the hope of the gospel while there is still time.

Holy God, teach me to be still before You, in awe of Your holiness and power. Thank You that my prayers rise before You like incense, and that You hear every prayer of Your people. Help me to pray with faith for Your kingdom to come and Your will to be done on earth as it is in heaven. Thank You for Your patience, which gives time for repentance. Make me grateful for the gifts of Your creation and faithful in caring for them. When the world is shaken, help me to trust that You are greater than every power, and that You will deliver Your people. Give me a heart that longs for others to turn to You, and use me to share Your hope while there is time. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
