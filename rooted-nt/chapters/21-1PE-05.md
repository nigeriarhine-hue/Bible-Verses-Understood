# 1 Peter 5
theme: Humble Shepherds, Watchful Believers, and the God of All Grace
main: Peter, writing as a fellow elder and a witness of Christ's sufferings, urges the elders to shepherd God's flock willingly, eagerly, and not for dishonest gain, not lording it over those in their care but being examples to them, and promises that when the Chief Shepherd appears they will receive an unfading crown of glory. Younger people are to respect the elders, and all believers are to clothe themselves with humility toward one another, because God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble. Humbling themselves under God's mighty hand, they can cast all their anxiety on Him, because He cares for them. They must be alert and sober, because their enemy the devil prowls like a roaring lion looking for someone to devour; they are to resist him, firm in faith, knowing that believers throughout the world face the same sufferings. After they have suffered a little while, the God of all grace, who called them to His eternal glory in Christ, will Himself restore, confirm, strengthen, and establish them. Peter closes by noting that he wrote with Silvanus's help to testify that this is the true grace of God, and sends greetings from the church in "Babylon" and from Mark, his son in the faith.

## Chapter Background and Story

In the final chapter of his letter, Peter turns to the leaders of the churches. In times of suffering and pressure, good leadership is essential. Shepherds who care for the flock, protect it, and set a good example help believers stand firm. Poor leaders, who serve for selfish reasons or dominate others, cause great harm.

Peter writes with personal authority and humility. He calls himself a "fellow elder," placing himself alongside the leaders rather than above them. He was a "witness of the sufferings of Christ," having been present during Jesus' ministry and arrest, though he had failed Jesus at the crucial moment. After the resurrection, Jesus had commissioned Peter three times to [[John 21:17|Feed my sheep]] (John 21:15–17). Now Peter passes that charge on to other shepherds.

The chapter then gives final instructions to all believers about humility, trust, and spiritual watchfulness, before closing with a beautiful promise of God's restoring grace.

### Shepherding the Flock (5:1–4)

[[v1|Therefore I exhort the elders among you, as a fellow elder and a witness of the sufferings of Christ, and who will also share in the glory that will be revealed]]: Peter's appeal is grounded in his own experience and hope. He saw Christ suffer, and he will share in His glory. The charge to elders is studied below.

[[v4|When the chief Shepherd is revealed, you will receive the crown of glory that doesn’t fade away]]. Jesus is "the chief Shepherd." Elders are under-shepherds, serving under Him and accountable to Him. When He returns, faithful shepherds will receive a reward: a crown of glory that never fades. Athletes in ancient games received wreaths of leaves that soon withered; this crown lasts forever.

### Clothed with Humility (5:5–7)

[[v5|Likewise, you younger ones, be subject to the elder. Yes, all of you clothe yourselves with humility and subject yourselves to one another; for “God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble.”]] Younger believers are to respect the leaders. But humility is required of everyone. "Clothe yourselves" uses a word for tying on an apron, the garment a servant wore. Peter may have been remembering the night Jesus tied a towel around Himself and washed the disciples' feet, including Peter's (John 13:4–5). The quotation from Proverbs 3:34 is also found in James 4:6, where it is discussed more fully. The call to humble oneself and cast anxieties on God is studied below.

### Resisting the Devil (5:8–9)

The warning about the devil is studied below.

### The God of All Grace (5:10–11)

God's promise of restoration is studied below. [[v11|To him be the glory and the power forever and ever. Amen]]. The promise leads naturally to praise.

### Final Greetings (5:12–14)

[[v12|Through Silvanus, our faithful brother, as I consider him, I have written to you briefly, exhorting and testifying that this is the true grace of God in which you stand]]. Silvanus, also called Silas, was Paul's companion on his missionary journeys (Acts 15:40; 1 Thessalonians 1:1). He may have helped Peter write the letter or carried it to the churches. Peter summarizes his whole letter: it testifies to "the true grace of God." Believers are to stand firm in it.

[[v13|She who is in Babylon, chosen together with you, greets you. So does Mark, my son]]. "She who is in Babylon" most likely refers to the church in Rome. Babylon, the city that had exiled God's people in the Old Testament, became a symbol for Rome, the center of worldly power (Revelation 17–18). Believers in Rome were, like Peter's readers, exiles in a foreign land. "Mark, my son" refers to John Mark, author of the Gospel of Mark, whom Peter regarded as a spiritual son. Early tradition holds that Mark's Gospel was based on Peter's preaching.

[[v14|Greet one another with a kiss of love. Peace be to all of you who are in Christ Jesus. Amen]]. The letter ends with a call to show love to one another and a blessing of peace for all who are in Christ.

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 Peter 5:2–3 — Shepherd the Flock of God

verse: 1 Peter 5:2-3

#### Simple Explanation

"Shepherd the flock of God which is among you." Elders are shepherds. Their job is to feed, guide, protect, and care for God's people. Notice that it is "the flock of God," not the elders' own flock. The people belong to God, and elders care for them on His behalf.

"Exercising the oversight." Elders watch over the flock, paying attention to its needs and dangers.

Peter then gives three contrasts describing how elders should lead.

"Not under compulsion, but voluntarily." Elders should serve willingly, not because they feel forced or pressured. Some manuscripts add "according to God," meaning as God would have them serve.

"Not for dishonest gain, but willingly." Elders should not serve for money or personal advantage, but eagerly, with enthusiasm for the work.

Verse 3: "not as lording it over those entrusted to you, but making yourselves examples to the flock." Elders should not dominate, control, or bully those in their care. Instead, they should lead by example, living in a way others can follow.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses describe Christlike leadership. Jesus, the Good Shepherd, laid down His life for the sheep (John 10:11). He did not come to be served but to serve (Mark 10:45). He warned His disciples against leaders who "lord it over" others, saying it must not be so among them (Mark 10:42–43). Peter, who heard those words, now passes them on.

The three temptations Peter names, reluctance, greed, and domination, have afflicted church leaders throughout history. Some lead out of duty without love. Some seek financial gain or status. Some use spiritual authority to control and harm. Each distorts the shepherd's calling.

The final phrase, "making yourselves examples to the flock," shows that true leadership is about influence through character, not force through position. Paul similarly urged believers to imitate him as he imitated Christ (1 Corinthians 11:1).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Church leaders can examine their motives and methods. Are they serving willingly and eagerly? Are they free from the desire for personal gain? Are they leading by example rather than by control?

Churches can use this passage to evaluate their leaders and to establish structures of accountability. Leaders who dominate, manipulate, or abuse their authority contradict this passage, and those affected deserve protection and care.

All believers who lead in any capacity, whether as parents, managers, teachers, or ministry leaders, can apply these principles: serving willingly, avoiding selfish gain, and leading by example rather than domination.

### 1 Peter 5:6–7 — Cast All Your Anxiety on Him

verse: 1 Peter 5:6-7

#### Simple Explanation

"Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God." God's "mighty hand" is a common biblical image for His power, especially His power to deliver His people, as when He rescued Israel from Egypt (Exodus 3:19–20; Deuteronomy 26:8). Believers are to submit humbly to God's power, trusting His purposes even in suffering.

"That he may exalt you in due time." God will lift up the humble at the right time, His time. Believers may not see it immediately, but God will vindicate and honor those who trust Him.

Verse 7: "casting all your worries on him, because he cares for you." "Casting" means throwing, as one might throw a heavy load off one's shoulders. Believers are to throw all their anxieties, worries, and burdens onto God. The word "all" includes everything, large and small.

"Because he cares for you." The reason believers can do this is that God personally cares for them. He is not distant or indifferent. He is concerned about their lives.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses connect humility and trust. Anxiety often comes from trying to carry burdens that belong to God, from trying to control what cannot be controlled. Humility acknowledges that God is in control and that believers depend on Him. Casting anxieties on God is an act of humility.

This echoes Psalm 55:22: [[Psalm 55:22|Cast your burden on the LORD, and he will sustain you]]. Jesus taught His followers not to be anxious about their lives, because their heavenly Father knows what they need (Matthew 6:25–34). Paul wrote, [[Philippians 4:6|In nothing be anxious, but in everything, by prayer and petition with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God]].

"He cares for you" is one of the most comforting phrases in Scripture. For believers who felt rejected by society and threatened by persecution, it was a reminder that the God of the universe cared personally for each of them.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can practice casting their anxieties on God through prayer, naming their worries specifically and handing them over to Him. This may need to be done repeatedly, as anxieties return.

Humility can help reduce anxiety. Accepting that one cannot control everything and trusting God with the outcome brings freedom.

For those who struggle with ongoing anxiety, this passage offers comfort, not condemnation. God cares about their struggles. Alongside prayer, seeking help from trusted friends, counselors, or medical professionals can be part of how God provides care.

### 1 Peter 5:8–9 — Resist the Roaring Lion

verse: 1 Peter 5:8-9

#### Simple Explanation

"Be sober and self-controlled. Be watchful." Believers need to stay alert and clear-minded. Spiritual danger is real.

"Your adversary, the devil, walks around like a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour." The devil is believers' adversary, their enemy and accuser. Peter compares him to a lion that roars and prowls, looking for prey. Lions often hunt animals that are isolated, weak, or unaware. The devil seeks to destroy believers' faith, often through fear, discouragement, temptation, or division.

Verse 9: "Withstand him steadfast in your faith." Believers are to resist the devil, standing firm. The weapon is faith, trust in God. James gives a similar promise: [[James 4:7|Resist the devil, and he will flee from you]].

"Knowing that your brothers who are in the world are undergoing the same sufferings." Believers are not alone. Christians everywhere face similar struggles. This knowledge brings comfort and solidarity.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Peter knew from experience how the devil works. Jesus had warned him, [[Luke 22:31|Satan asked to have all of you, that he might sift you as wheat]], [[Luke 22:32|but I prayed for you, that your faith wouldn’t fail]]. That night, Peter fell asleep instead of watching and praying, and then denied Jesus three times. Peter learned the hard way the importance of being alert.

The image of a roaring lion suggests that the devil may use fear and intimidation. The persecution Peter's readers faced could feel like a lion's roar, meant to frighten them into abandoning their faith. But believers can stand firm, because Christ has already defeated the devil (Colossians 2:15; Hebrews 2:14).

The reminder that believers worldwide face similar sufferings counters one of the devil's tactics: isolation. When believers feel alone in their struggles, they are more vulnerable. Knowing that others share their experiences strengthens them.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can stay spiritually alert through prayer, Scripture, and fellowship. These practices help them recognize and resist temptation, discouragement, and deception.

When believers feel attacked by fear, doubt, or temptation, they can resist by holding firmly to their faith in God, reminding themselves of His promises, and refusing to give in.

Believers can also remember that they are part of a worldwide family. Connecting with other believers, sharing struggles, and praying for persecuted Christians around the world brings strength and solidarity.

### 1 Peter 5:10 — The God of All Grace Will Restore You

verse: 1 Peter 5:10

#### Simple Explanation

Peter ends his instructions with a magnificent promise. "But may the God of all grace." God is the source of every kind of grace believers need: saving grace, sustaining grace, strengthening grace.

"Who called you to his eternal glory by Christ Jesus." God's purpose for believers is glory, eternal and shared with Christ. Their present suffering is not the end of the story.

"After you have suffered a little while." Suffering is real, but it is temporary. Compared with eternal glory, it lasts only "a little while."

"Perfect, establish, strengthen, and settle you." God Himself will do four things for His suffering people. "Perfect" means restore, repair, or make complete, like mending a torn net or setting a broken bone. "Establish" means make firm and stable. "Strengthen" means give power and endurance. "Settle" means place on a solid foundation, so that nothing can shake them.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse summarizes the hope of the whole letter. Believers suffer, but their suffering is temporary. God is the God of all grace, who has called them to eternal glory. And He Himself will restore and strengthen them.

The four verbs are a powerful picture of God's work. He repairs what is broken. He makes firm what is shaky. He gives strength to the weak. He establishes believers on an unshakable foundation. This promise speaks to every kind of suffering: physical, emotional, relational, and spiritual.

For Peter, these words were deeply personal. Jesus had told him, [[Luke 22:32|when once you have turned again, establish your brothers]]. Peter had failed, but the God of all grace had restored and established him. Now Peter promises the same to his readers.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers who are suffering can hold onto this promise. Their suffering is real, but it is "a little while." God is the God of all grace, and He will restore, establish, strengthen, and settle them.

This verse also invites believers to look beyond their present circumstances to the eternal glory God has called them to. That perspective brings hope and endurance.

Finally, believers can trust that God Himself is at work in them. They do not need to restore themselves. The God of all grace will do it.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

First Peter 5 begins with a charge to leaders. Elders are to shepherd God's flock willingly, eagerly, and without greed, not domineering over those in their care but setting an example. Leaders serve under Jesus, the Chief Shepherd, who will reward faithfulness with an unfading crown of glory. Churches today can value and hold accountable leaders who serve in this way, and protect those harmed by leaders who do not.

All believers are called to clothe themselves with humility, serving one another as Jesus did when He washed His disciples' feet. God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble. Humbling themselves under God's mighty hand, believers can cast all their anxieties on Him, because He cares for them personally.

The chapter also warns of spiritual danger. The devil prowls like a roaring lion, seeking to devour. Believers must stay alert and sober, resisting him firm in their faith, remembering that believers around the world share the same struggles. They are not alone.

Finally, Peter gives a promise that summarizes the whole letter. After believers have suffered a little while, the God of all grace, who called them to eternal glory in Christ, will Himself restore, establish, strengthen, and settle them. This is the true grace of God, and believers are called to stand firm in it.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, You are the Chief Shepherd. Raise up shepherds for Your church who serve willingly and eagerly, without greed, not lording it over others but leading by example. Protect Your people from leaders who would harm them. Clothe me with humility, and help me to serve others as You served Your disciples. I humble myself under Your mighty hand. I cast all my anxieties on You, because You care for me. Keep me alert and self-controlled, and help me to resist the devil, standing firm in faith. Strengthen my brothers and sisters around the world who suffer for Your name. God of all grace, You have called me to Your eternal glory in Christ. After I have suffered a little while, restore me, establish me, strengthen me, and settle me. To You be the power forever and ever. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
