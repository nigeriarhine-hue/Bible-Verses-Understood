# John 2
theme: New Wine and a New Temple
main: At a wedding in Cana, Jesus turns water set aside for ritual washing into abundant, excellent wine, the first of His signs, revealing His glory so that His disciples believe. At Passover in Jerusalem, He drives the merchants out of the temple and declares that if this temple is destroyed He will raise it in three days, speaking of His own body. Jesus brings the joy of God's new age and is Himself the true meeting place between God and people.

## Chapter Background and Story

John 2 contains two events that introduce major themes of John's Gospel. John calls Jesus' miracles "signs," because they point beyond themselves to reveal who Jesus is. The first sign, at Cana, shows Jesus bringing abundant joy and transforming the old into the new. The second event, the cleansing of the temple, shows that Jesus Himself replaces the temple as the place where people meet God. Both events concern Jewish religious practices, ceremonial washing jars and the Jerusalem temple, and both show Jesus fulfilling and surpassing them.

### The Wedding at Cana (2:1–11)

[[v1|The third day]], counting from Jesus' conversation with Nathanael, there was a wedding in Cana of Galilee, a village a few miles north of Nazareth. Nathanael came from Cana (John 21:2). Jesus' mother was there, and Jesus and His disciples had also been invited.

Weddings in that culture were major community celebrations that could last a week. The groom's family was responsible for providing food and drink for the guests. Running out of wine would have been a serious embarrassment, a social disgrace that could be remembered for years, and in some cases could even lead to legal complaints from guests who had given gifts.

When the wine ran out, Jesus' mother told Him, [[v3|They have no wine]]. She seems to have expected Jesus to do something. Jesus replied, [[v4|Woman, what does that have to do with you and me? My hour has not yet come]]. Addressing His mother as "Woman" was not rude in that culture; it was a respectful but somewhat formal address. Jesus uses it again from the cross when He lovingly provides for her (John 19:26). His question suggests that His actions would be guided by His Father's timing, not by family requests. "My hour" is an important theme in John's Gospel. Jesus' "hour" refers to the time of His death, resurrection, and glorification (John 12:23; 13:1; 17:1).

His mother did not argue. She said to the servants, [[v5|Whatever he says to you, do it]]. This verse is studied below.

Six stone water jars stood nearby, used [[v6|after the Jews’ way of purifying]]. Jews washed their hands and utensils before meals for ritual purity (Mark 7:3–4). Stone jars were used because, according to Jewish law, stone did not become ritually unclean as pottery could. Each jar held twenty or thirty gallons, so together they held about 120 to 180 gallons.

Jesus told the servants to fill the jars with water, and they filled them to the brim. Then He told them to draw some out and take it to the master of the banquet, who was in charge of the feast. The master tasted the water that had become wine, without knowing where it came from, though the servants knew. He called the bridegroom and said, [[v10|Everyone serves the good wine first, and when the guests have drunk freely, then that which is worse. You have kept the good wine until now!]]

John concludes, [[v11|This beginning of his signs Jesus did in Cana of Galilee, and revealed his glory; and his disciples believed in him]]. This verse is studied below.

### Interlude in Capernaum (2:12)

After the wedding, Jesus went down to Capernaum with His mother, brothers, and disciples, and they stayed there a few days. Capernaum, on the shore of the Sea of Galilee, became Jesus' base for much of His Galilean ministry.

### Cleansing the Temple (2:13–22)

When the Jewish Passover was near, Jesus went up to Jerusalem. John's Gospel records Jesus attending several festivals in Jerusalem, showing that His ministry lasted about three years.

In the temple courts, Jesus found people selling oxen, sheep, and doves, and money changers sitting at their tables. Pilgrims needed animals that met the requirements for sacrifice, and they needed to exchange foreign currency for the Tyrian shekels used for the temple tax. These services were convenient, but they had turned the outer court, the only area where Gentiles could worship, into a noisy marketplace, and there was opportunity for exploitation.

Jesus made a whip of cords and drove them all out of the temple, with the sheep and oxen. He poured out the money changers' coins and overturned their tables. To those selling doves, He said, [[v16|Take these things out of here! Don’t make my Father’s house a marketplace!]] Jesus called the temple "my Father's house," claiming a unique relationship with God.

His disciples remembered Psalm 69:9: [[v17|Zeal for your house will eat me up]]. Jesus' passion for God's honor would consume Him, and ultimately it would lead to His death.

The Jewish authorities asked, [[v18|What sign do you show us, seeing that you do these things?]] They wanted proof of His authority to act this way. Jesus answered, [[v19|Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up]]. This statement is studied below. They misunderstood, thinking He meant the physical temple, which had been under construction for forty-six years. (Herod the Great began rebuilding it around 20 B.C., and work continued for decades.) But John explains, [[v21|But he spoke of the temple of his body]]. After Jesus was raised from the dead, His disciples remembered what He had said and believed the Scripture and His words.

John places this event early in Jesus' ministry, while Matthew, Mark, and Luke place a temple cleansing during His final week. Some scholars believe Jesus cleansed the temple twice, at the beginning and end of His ministry. Others think John placed the event early for theological reasons, to show from the start that Jesus replaces the temple. Either way, the meaning is the same: Jesus has authority over God's house, and He Himself is the new meeting place between God and people.

### Many Believe, But Jesus Knows Their Hearts (2:23–25)

While Jesus was in Jerusalem during the Passover festival, many people believed in His name when they saw the signs He was doing. But Jesus [[v24|didn’t entrust himself to them, because he knew everyone]]. He did not need anyone's testimony about people, [[v25|for he himself knew what was in man]]. There is a play on words in the Greek: many "believed" in Jesus, but Jesus did not "believe" (entrust) Himself to them. Their faith was based on miracles and may have been shallow. Jesus knew the human heart and knew that some who were impressed by signs would not truly follow Him. This prepares for the next chapter, where Nicodemus, one of these impressed observers, comes to Jesus at night.

## Important Verses Explained

### John 2:5 — "Whatever He Says to You, Do It"

verse: John 2:5

#### Simple Explanation

These words were spoken by Jesus' mother, Mary, to the servants at the wedding in Cana. The wedding celebration was in progress when the wine ran out, a serious embarrassment for the bridegroom's family, who were responsible for providing for the guests. Mary told Jesus, "They have no wine." She seems to have trusted that He could do something about it.

Jesus responded, "Woman, what does that have to do with you and me? My hour has not yet come." His answer showed that He would act according to His Father's timing and purposes, not simply because His mother asked. Yet Mary was not discouraged. She turned to the servants and said, [[v5|Whatever he says to you, do it]].

Mary's words show remarkable faith. She did not know exactly what Jesus would do, and she did not try to tell Him how to solve the problem. She simply trusted Him and instructed the servants to obey whatever He commanded.

Her words also echo Genesis 41:55, where Pharaoh told the starving Egyptians during a famine, [[Genesis 41:55|Go to Joseph. What he says to you, do]]. Joseph provided for the people's needs; Jesus would provide for this wedding, and ultimately for all who come to Him.

The servants obeyed. When Jesus told them to fill six large stone jars with water, they filled them to the brim. When He told them to draw some out and take it to the master of the banquet, they did. Their obedience was part of the miracle. They did not understand what Jesus was doing, but they followed His instructions, and the water became wine.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse expresses the heart of discipleship: doing whatever Jesus says. Mary, the mother of Jesus, points people not to herself but to her Son. Her words are a fitting summary of faith: trust Jesus and obey Him.

It also shows that faith often involves obeying without fully understanding. The servants filled the jars with water and drew some out to serve, which must have seemed strange. But their obedience made them witnesses to Jesus' first sign. Often, believers see God's work only after they obey.

Finally, the verse shows the value of bringing needs to Jesus and then trusting His response. Mary brought the need to Jesus, accepted His answer, and trusted Him to act in His own way. Believers can do the same in prayer.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites believers to commit to doing whatever Jesus says. His commands are found in Scripture: love God and neighbor, forgive, be generous, tell the truth, serve others, and follow Him. Believers can ask, "What is Jesus telling me to do?" and then obey, even when it is difficult or does not make sense to them.

It also encourages believers to bring their needs to Jesus and trust Him with the answer. Like Mary, they can tell Him honestly what is lacking, without dictating how He should respond, and then wait for His direction.

Finally, this verse reminds believers that obedience often comes before understanding. The servants did not know what Jesus would do until they obeyed. Believers may find that God reveals His work as they take steps of faithful obedience.

### John 2:11 — The First Sign Revealed His Glory

verse: John 2:11

#### Simple Explanation

This verse concludes the story of the wedding at Cana. Jesus had turned about 120 to 180 gallons of water into wine, and the master of the banquet, not knowing where it came from, praised the bridegroom for saving the best wine until last. Most hosts served the best wine first and cheaper wine later, when guests were less likely to notice. This wine was excellent and abundant.

John comments, [[v11|This beginning of his signs Jesus did in Cana of Galilee]]. John calls Jesus' miracles "signs," a word he uses deliberately. A sign points beyond itself to something greater. Jesus' miracles were not just displays of power; they revealed who He is. John's Gospel records seven major signs before Jesus' death and resurrection, and this is the first.

[[v11|and revealed his glory]]. "Glory" refers to the visible display of God's presence, character, and power. In John 1:14, John wrote, "We saw his glory." At Cana, Jesus' glory was revealed in His power over creation, His generosity, and His care for people in need.

[[v11|and his disciples believed in him]]. The sign led to faith. The disciples, who had just begun following Jesus, saw His glory and trusted Him more deeply.

What did this sign reveal? First, Jesus' power over creation: only the Creator can transform water into wine. Second, His abundant generosity: He provided far more than was needed, and of the highest quality. The Old Testament prophets pictured the age of the Messiah as a time of overflowing wine and joy (Amos 9:13–14; Isaiah 25:6). Third, the transformation of the old into the new: the water came from jars used for Jewish purification rituals, and Jesus turned it into the wine of celebration. He brings something better than the old ways of purification.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that Jesus' miracles are meant to reveal His identity and lead to faith. John wrote his Gospel so that readers would believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God (John 20:31). Each sign is an invitation to see Jesus' glory and trust Him.

It also shows that Jesus brings joy. His first sign was not at a funeral or in a synagogue, but at a wedding celebration. He saved a family from embarrassment and kept a celebration going. God's kingdom is often pictured as a wedding feast (Matthew 22:2; Revelation 19:7–9). Jesus brings the joy of that kingdom into ordinary life.

Finally, the sign points to the new covenant. The water for purification represents the old system of ritual cleansing. Jesus transformed it into wine, which later, at the Last Supper, would represent His blood of the new covenant. Jesus brings a deeper cleansing and a greater joy than the old rituals could provide.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites believers to look for Jesus' glory in His works. When they read the Gospels or reflect on how God has worked in their lives, they can ask what these things reveal about Jesus and allow them to deepen their faith.

It also reminds believers that Jesus cares about ordinary human needs and joys. A wedding running out of wine was not a life-or-death crisis, yet Jesus cared. Believers can bring everyday concerns to Him, trusting that He cares about their lives.

Finally, this verse encourages believers to expect Jesus' abundant generosity. He did not provide just enough wine; He provided an overflowing supply of the best wine. While God does not promise believers everything they want, He is generous, and His grace is more than sufficient.

### John 2:19–21 — The Temple of His Body

verse: John 2:19-21

#### Simple Explanation

These verses record Jesus' answer to the religious authorities after He cleansed the temple. During Passover, Jesus found merchants selling animals and money changers at their tables in the temple courts. He made a whip from cords, drove them out, overturned the tables, and said, "Don't make my Father's house a marketplace!"

The Jewish authorities challenged Him: "What sign do you show us, seeing that you do these things?" They wanted proof that He had authority to act this way.

Jesus answered, [[v19|Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up]]. He did not perform a miracle on the spot. Instead, He gave them a puzzling statement.

The authorities misunderstood: [[v20|It took forty-six years to build this temple! Will you raise it up in three days?]] Herod the Great had begun a massive rebuilding of the temple around 20 B.C., and construction was still ongoing. To them, Jesus' words seemed absurd.

But John explains, [[v21|But he spoke of the temple of his body]]. Jesus was speaking of His own body. The authorities would "destroy" it by having Him crucified, and He would raise it on the third day. His resurrection would be the sign that proved His authority.

Verse 22 adds that after Jesus was raised from the dead, His disciples remembered this statement and believed the Scripture and Jesus' words. At the time, they did not understand, but the resurrection made everything clear.

Jesus' words were later twisted and used against Him at His trial, where false witnesses claimed He had said He would destroy the temple (Mark 14:58), and they were thrown back at Him as He hung on the cross (Mark 15:29).

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal that Jesus Himself is the true temple. In the Old Testament, the temple was the place where God's presence dwelt among His people and where sacrifices were offered for sin. Jesus' body is now the place where God dwells with humanity (John 1:14) and where the final sacrifice for sin was made. The physical temple was destroyed by the Romans in A.D. 70, but Jesus, the true temple, was raised and lives forever.

They also point to the resurrection as the ultimate sign. The authorities demanded a sign; Jesus pointed to His death and resurrection. Throughout the Gospels, the resurrection is the supreme proof of Jesus' identity and authority (Romans 1:4).

Finally, these verses connect to the church. The New Testament teaches that believers, united to Christ, become God's temple, the dwelling place of His Spirit (1 Corinthians 3:16; 6:19; Ephesians 2:21–22). Through Jesus, God now dwells in His people.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses invite believers to meet God through Jesus. They do not need to travel to a particular building or perform certain rituals to come into God's presence. Jesus is the meeting place between God and humanity. Through Him, believers can approach God anywhere.

They also give believers confidence in the resurrection. Jesus predicted His resurrection, and it happened. This gives assurance that His other words are trustworthy.

Finally, these verses remind believers that they are now part of God's temple. Their bodies and their community are places where God's Spirit dwells. This calls them to honor God in how they live, both individually and together as the church.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

John 2 shows Jesus revealing His glory in two very different settings: a joyful wedding and a corrupted temple. At Cana, Jesus showed His care for ordinary human needs, His power over creation, and His abundant generosity. He transformed water used for purification into the best wine, showing that He brings the joy of God's new age. Believers can bring their everyday needs to Jesus, trust His care, and expect Him to bring joy and transformation.

Mary's words to the servants, "Whatever he says to you, do it," summarize the response Jesus desires. Believers can commit to obeying Jesus, even when His instructions do not make sense to them, trusting that obedience leads to seeing His glory.

The cleansing of the temple shows Jesus' zeal for God's honor and His authority over God's house. He would not allow worship to be turned into a marketplace. Believers can examine whether their worship has become cluttered with distractions or commercial concerns, and they can ask Jesus to cleanse their hearts.

Jesus' statement about the temple of His body points to His death and resurrection and reveals that He is the true meeting place between God and humanity. Believers can approach God through Him with confidence. Finally, the note that Jesus knew what was in every human heart calls believers to genuine faith that goes beyond being impressed by miracles, a faith that entrusts itself fully to Jesus.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for revealing Your glory at the wedding in Cana, caring for ordinary needs and bringing abundant joy. Help me to bring my needs to You and to trust You with the answer. Like Mary, I want to say, "Whatever You say to me, I will do." Give me faith to obey even when I do not understand.

Thank You for Your zeal for God's house. Cleanse my heart of anything that distracts from true worship. Thank You that You are the true temple, the place where I meet God, and that You were raised on the third day just as You said. You know what is in my heart. Make my faith genuine, not based only on signs, but on trusting You completely. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
