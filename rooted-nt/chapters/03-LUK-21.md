# Luke 21
theme: Endure, Look Up, and Stay Alert
main: After praising a poor widow's gift, Jesus predicts the destruction of the temple and prepares His followers for deception, wars, persecution, and the fall of Jerusalem. He promises them wisdom when they are put on trial and assures them that by endurance they will gain their lives. When the signs of the end appear, they are to lift up their heads because redemption is near, and meanwhile they must guard their hearts and pray, staying ready to stand before the Son of Man.

## Chapter Background and Story

Luke 21 concludes Jesus' public teaching in the temple during His final week in Jerusalem. It parallels Matthew 24 and Mark 13, the "Olivet Discourse," which are studied in depth in those commentaries (Matthew 24:4, 24:36, 24:45–46; Mark 13:11, 13:31, 13:37). Luke's version has several distinctive emphases: it clearly separates the destruction of Jerusalem from the final coming of the Son of Man, it gives specific words of encouragement for believers facing persecution, and it ends with a warning against being weighed down by the cares of life.

As with the parallel chapters, Christians interpret the details of this prophecy in different ways. Luke's wording makes it especially clear that part of Jesus' prophecy concerned the Roman siege and destruction of Jerusalem in A.D. 70, while other parts look forward to His return at the end of the age.

### The Widow's Offering (21:1–4)

The chapter opens in the temple, where Jesus watched rich people putting gifts into the treasury. He saw a poor widow put in two small copper coins. He said she had put in more than all the others, because they gave out of their abundance, but she, out of her poverty, put in [[v4|all that she had to live on]]. This scene follows directly after Jesus' warning about scribes who [[Luke 20:47|devour widows’ houses]]. (The widow's gift is studied at Mark 12:43–44.) Her total devotion stands in contrast to the temple's outward splendor, which is described next.

### The Temple Will Be Destroyed (21:5–7)

Some people were admiring the temple, [[v5|how it was decorated with beautiful stones and gifts]]. Herod the Great's rebuilt temple was magnificent, with gleaming white stone and gold. Wealthy donors had given gifts to adorn it, such as a golden vine with grape clusters described by the historian Josephus. Jesus said, [[v6|the days will come in which there will not be left here one stone on another that will not be thrown down]]. They asked when this would happen and what sign would show it was about to take place.

### Do Not Be Deceived or Terrified (21:8–11)

Jesus warned, [[v8|Watch out that you don’t get led astray]]. Many would come in His name, claiming, "I am he," and, [[v8|The time is at hand]]. Luke alone includes this second claim. False teachers would proclaim that the end was imminent. Jesus said, [[v8|Therefore don’t follow them]]. When they heard of wars and uprisings, they should not be terrified, because these things must happen first, [[v9|but the end won’t come immediately]]. Nations would fight, and there would be earthquakes, famines, plagues, and frightening signs in the sky.

### Persecution and Promised Help (21:12–19)

[[v12|But before all these things]], Jesus said, His followers would be arrested and persecuted, handed over to synagogues and prisons, and brought before kings and governors because of His name. The book of Acts, Luke's second volume, records this happening: Peter and John before the Sanhedrin (Acts 4), Paul before governors Felix and Festus and King Agrippa (Acts 24–26).

[[v13|It will turn out as a testimony for you]]. Their trials would become opportunities to bear witness to Jesus. They should resolve not to prepare their defense in advance, [[v15|for I will give you a mouth and wisdom which all your adversaries will not be able to withstand or to contradict]]. In Mark 13:11, this promise is connected to the Holy Spirit; here Jesus says He Himself will give them words and wisdom. In Acts 6:10, Stephen's opponents [[Acts 6:10|weren’t able to withstand the wisdom and the Spirit by which he spoke]].

Jesus warned that they would be betrayed even by parents, brothers, relatives, and friends, and some would be put to death. They would be hated by everyone because of His name. Then He gave a promise and a call to endurance, studied below.

### The Fall of Jerusalem (21:20–24)

Luke's version is especially specific about Jerusalem: [[v20|But when you see Jerusalem surrounded by armies, then know that its desolation is at hand]]. Where Matthew and Mark speak of "the abomination of desolation," Luke describes the siege directly. When they saw this, people in Judea should flee to the mountains, those in the city should get out, and those in the countryside should not enter the city.

[[v22|For these are days of vengeance, that all things which are written may be fulfilled]]. The destruction would be God's judgment, fulfilling prophetic warnings about the consequences of rejecting Him. Jesus expressed compassion for pregnant women and nursing mothers. There would be great distress and wrath on the people. They would fall by the sword and be taken as captives to all nations, and [[v24|Jerusalem will be trampled down by the Gentiles until the times of the Gentiles are fulfilled]].

This prophecy was fulfilled in A.D. 70, when Roman armies under Titus surrounded Jerusalem, besieged it, and destroyed the city and temple. Josephus records that over a million people died and nearly a hundred thousand were taken captive. Early church writers report that Christians in Jerusalem heeded Jesus' warning and fled before the final siege.

The phrase "until the times of the Gentiles are fulfilled" indicates a period during which Gentiles would dominate Jerusalem, but it also suggests that this period has a limit set by God. Christians have understood it in different ways. Some connect it with the period of the gospel going to the Gentiles (Romans 11:25), others with Gentile political control of Jerusalem until the end of the age.

### The Coming of the Son of Man (21:25–28)

Jesus then spoke of signs in the sun, moon, and stars, and on earth, nations in anguish and perplexity at the roaring of the sea and waves. People would faint from fear and foreboding of what was coming on the world, for the powers of the heavens would be shaken. [[v27|Then they will see the Son of Man coming in a cloud with power and great glory]], language from Daniel 7:13–14. Then came a word of encouragement for believers, studied below.

### The Fig Tree and Jesus' Words (21:29–33)

Jesus told them to look at the fig tree and all the trees. When they sprout leaves, people know summer is near. In the same way, when they saw these things happening, they would know that [[v31|God’s Kingdom is near]]. He said this generation would not pass away until all things had happened (see the discussion at Matthew 24:34), and declared, [[v33|Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will by no means pass away]]. (This verse is studied at Mark 13:31.)

### Watch and Pray (21:34–38)

Jesus ended with a warning unique to Luke, studied below: [[v34|So be careful, or your hearts will be loaded down with carousing, drunkenness, and cares of this life, and that day will come on you suddenly]]. That day would come on everyone on earth like a trap. Therefore they should always be watchful, praying for strength to escape what was coming and [[v36|to stand before the Son of Man]].

Luke concludes the section with a summary: every day Jesus taught in the temple, and every night He went out and spent the night on the Mount of Olives. All the people came early in the morning to hear Him in the temple. These peaceful days would soon end as the events of His betrayal and death began in chapter 22.

## Important Verses Explained

### Luke 21:18–19 — Not a Hair Will Perish; Endurance Wins Life

verse: Luke 21:18-19

#### Simple Explanation

These verses come at the end of Jesus' warnings about persecution. He had told His disciples that they would be arrested, imprisoned, and brought before kings and governors because of Him. They would be betrayed by parents, brothers, relatives, and friends. [[v16|They will cause some of you to be put to death]]. They would be hated by everyone because of His name.

Then Jesus said, [[v18|And not a hair of your head will perish]]. This seems puzzling, since He had just said that some of them would be killed. How can both be true?

The answer is that Jesus was speaking of ultimate safety, not physical safety in every case. Some of His followers would die, but nothing could destroy them in the deepest sense. Their lives were secure in God's hands. Earlier, Jesus had said, [[Luke 12:4|don’t be afraid of those who kill the body, and after that have no more that they can do]], and that even the hairs of their heads are counted (Luke 12:7). God's care for them would extend beyond death to the resurrection. Not even the smallest part of who they are would be lost to God.

[[v19|By your endurance you will win your lives]]. "Endurance" means patient perseverance, steadfastness under pressure, continuing to trust and follow Jesus through suffering. "Win your lives" (or "gain your souls") means that by remaining faithful, they would secure true and eternal life. Elsewhere Jesus says, [[Mark 13:13|he who endures to the end will be saved]].

This promise does not mean that people earn salvation by enduring. It means that genuine faith perseveres, and that those who belong to Christ will be kept through trials. Endurance is the way faith shows itself in hard times.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal that believers' security rests in God, not in circumstances. Persecution, betrayal, and even death cannot ultimately harm those who belong to Christ. Paul expresses the same confidence: nothing, not tribulation, persecution, danger, or death, can separate believers from the love of God in Christ Jesus (Romans 8:35–39).

They also show that endurance is essential to the Christian life. Faith is not only a one-time decision but a lifelong journey of trust. In the parable of the sower, the good soil represents those who [[Luke 8:15|produce fruit with perseverance]]. The early church faced intense persecution, and these words gave them courage to remain faithful.

Finally, the verses hold together honesty and hope. Jesus did not promise His followers an easy life or physical protection from every harm. He told them plainly that some would die. But He also promised that nothing could take away their true life.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers who face opposition, persecution, or suffering for their faith. In many parts of the world, Christians are imprisoned, rejected by family, or killed. Others face lesser but real hostility. Jesus' words assure them that their ultimate safety is secure in God's care.

They also call believers to endurance. When faith is tested by suffering, disappointment, or long waiting, believers can keep trusting and following Jesus. Perseverance is not about grim willpower but about holding onto Christ, who holds onto them.

Finally, these verses help believers face fear. Fear of loss, pain, or death can paralyze. Remembering that not a hair of their head will perish in God's ultimate care can give believers courage to live faithfully.

### Luke 21:28 — Look Up, Your Redemption Is Near

verse: Luke 21:28

#### Simple Explanation

This verse comes after Jesus described frightening events that would precede His return. He spoke of signs in the sun, moon, and stars; nations in anguish and confusion at the roaring of the sea; and people fainting from fear and foreboding of what was coming on the world. The powers of the heavens would be shaken. Then the Son of Man would come in a cloud with power and great glory.

While the world would respond to these events with terror, Jesus told His followers to respond differently: [[v28|But when these things begin to happen, look up and lift up your heads, because your redemption is near]].

[[v28|look up and lift up your heads]]. People who are afraid or ashamed often bow their heads and look down. Jesus told His followers to look up with confidence and hope. Lifting the head was a sign of joy and expectation.

[[v28|because your redemption is near]]. "Redemption" means release or deliverance, the act of being set free, like a slave being freed or a captive being ransomed. For believers, the return of the Son of Man means final deliverance from sin, suffering, death, and every evil. What terrifies the world brings hope to God's people.

This verse shows a striking contrast. The same events produce fear in some and hope in others. The difference is relationship with the coming King. For those who belong to Him, His coming is the moment they have longed for.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse reveals that the Christian hope is not escape from the world's troubles but redemption through them. Believers do not need to fear the end of the age, because it brings the completion of their salvation. Paul writes that believers are [[Romans 8:23|waiting for adoption, the redemption of our body]]. Jesus' return will bring that final redemption.

It also shows that believers can face frightening times with a different perspective. When the world seems to be shaking, believers can look beyond the chaos to the coming King. Their hope is not in the stability of the world but in the faithfulness of God.

Finally, the verse encourages believers to live with expectation. Like people waiting for a long-awaited guest, they can keep their eyes on the horizon, ready to welcome Christ.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse offers hope to believers in troubled times. Wars, disasters, pandemics, and social upheaval can produce fear and anxiety. Believers can acknowledge the reality of these troubles while lifting their heads in hope, knowing that God is working out His purposes and that their redemption is near.

It also invites believers to examine their response to world events. Do they react with the same fear and despair as the world, or with confident hope in Christ? Regular reflection on Christ's promised return can shape their outlook.

Finally, this verse encourages believers to share their hope with others. When people around them are fainting with fear, believers can point them to the One who brings redemption.

### Luke 21:34 — Guard Your Hearts

verse: Luke 21:34

#### Simple Explanation

This verse begins the final warning of Jesus' teaching about the end times, found only in Luke. Jesus had described the destruction of Jerusalem, cosmic signs, and the coming of the Son of Man. He had told His followers to lift up their heads because their redemption was near. Now He turned to how they should live while they wait.

[[v34|So be careful]]. Jesus called His followers to watchfulness over their own lives.

[[v34|or your hearts will be loaded down]]. The word means weighed down, burdened, or dulled. Jesus warned that their hearts could become so heavy that they would lose spiritual alertness.

Jesus named three things that could weigh down the heart. [[v34|carousing]] refers to excessive partying and indulgence, and the dullness that follows it. [[v34|drunkenness]] is self-explanatory. [[v34|and cares of this life]] refers to anxieties about daily needs, worries about money, health, and the future. Notice that Jesus put ordinary worries alongside drunkenness. Both indulgence and anxiety can dull spiritual awareness.

[[v34|and that day will come on you suddenly]]. "That day" is the day of the Son of Man's return. If their hearts were dulled, it would catch them by surprise. The next verse adds that it will come [[v35|like a snare]] on everyone on earth, a trap that springs without warning.

Then Jesus gave the remedy: [[v36|Therefore be watchful all the time, praying that you may be counted worthy to escape all these things that will happen, and to stand before the Son of Man]]. Watchfulness and prayer keep the heart alert.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse reveals that spiritual danger often comes not from dramatic persecution but from ordinary life. The disciples might face prisons and kings, but they might also simply drift into spiritual sleep through indulgence or worry. Many believers fall away not because of a single crisis but through slow distraction.

It also connects with the parable of the sower, where the seed among thorns is choked by [[Luke 8:14|cares, riches, and pleasures of life]]. Both pleasures and worries can choke spiritual life. Jesus warns against both.

Finally, the verse shows the goal of watchfulness: to stand before the Son of Man. Believers will one day stand before Christ. Living with that day in view shapes how they live now.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites believers to examine what weighs down their hearts. It may be indulgence, such as excessive entertainment, alcohol, or pleasure-seeking that dulls spiritual sensitivity. Or it may be anxiety about work, money, family, or the future. Believers can identify these weights and bring them to God.

It also encourages believers to practice watchfulness and prayer. Regular prayer, Scripture reading, worship, and fellowship help keep the heart alert. Prayer specifically for strength to remain faithful is a practical response to Jesus' warning.

Finally, this verse calls believers to live with eternity in view. They will stand before the Son of Man. This awareness is not meant to produce fear but focus, helping them live faithfully each day and resist the distractions that would draw them away from Christ.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Luke 21 prepares believers to live faithfully in a world of turmoil while waiting for Christ's return. The poor widow's offering reminds them that God values wholehearted devotion more than outward splendor. The magnificent temple would be destroyed, but the widow's gift was precious to God.

Jesus' warnings about deception call believers to discernment. Many will claim to speak for Christ or announce that the end is near. Believers can test such claims by Scripture and refuse to follow them. When they hear of wars and disasters, they need not be terrified.

The promises about persecution offer courage. Believers who face opposition for their faith can trust that Jesus will give them words and wisdom, that their trials can become opportunities for testimony, and that their ultimate safety is secure in God's care. By endurance they will gain their lives.

The fall of Jerusalem shows that Jesus' words come true, which gives confidence that His promises about the future will also be fulfilled. When the world shakes, believers can lift up their heads in hope, because their redemption is near.

Finally, Jesus' closing warning calls believers to guard their hearts against both indulgence and anxiety, and to stay watchful in prayer. Each day can be lived with the awareness that they will stand before the Son of Man, a hope that brings focus, courage, and joy.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for the poor widow's example of wholehearted devotion. Help me to give You my best and all that I have. Guard me from deception, and keep me from being terrified by wars and disasters. When I face opposition for my faith, give me words and wisdom, and help me to see my trials as opportunities to testify about You.

Thank You that not a hair of my head will perish outside Your care, and that by endurance I will gain my life. When the world is shaken, help me to lift up my head, because my redemption is near. Keep my heart from being weighed down by indulgence or worry. Help me to stay watchful and prayerful, so that I may be ready to stand before You when You come. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
