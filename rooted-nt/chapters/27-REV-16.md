# Revelation 16
theme: The Seven Bowls of God's Wrath
main: A loud voice from the temple commands the seven angels to pour out the seven bowls of God's wrath on the earth. The first bowl brings ugly, festering sores on those who bear the mark of the beast and worship its image. The second turns the sea into blood like that of a dead person, and every living thing in the sea dies. The third turns the rivers and springs into blood, and the angel in charge of the waters declares that God is just in these judgments, for those who shed the blood of His people and prophets have been given blood to drink; the altar responds that His judgments are true and just. The fourth bowl is poured on the sun, which scorches people with fire, yet they curse God and refuse to repent and glorify Him. The fifth is poured on the throne of the beast, plunging its kingdom into darkness; people gnaw their tongues in agony and curse God, but do not repent of their deeds. The sixth dries up the Euphrates River to prepare the way for the kings from the east. John sees three impure spirits like frogs coming out of the mouths of the dragon, the beast, and the false prophet; they are demonic spirits performing signs, gathering the kings of the whole world for battle on the great day of God Almighty. Jesus declares that He comes like a thief and blesses those who stay awake and keep their clothes. The kings are gathered to the place called Armageddon. The seventh bowl is poured into the air, and a loud voice from the throne says, It is done! There are lightning, thunder, and a severe earthquake unlike any before. The great city splits into three parts, the cities of the nations collapse, and God remembers Babylon the great and gives her the cup of His fury. Every island flees, the mountains disappear, and huge hailstones fall, yet people curse God because of the plague.

## Chapter Background and Story

Revelation 16 describes the seven bowls of God's wrath, the last of the three series of judgments. These bowls complete God's wrath (15:1). They are swift and severe, poured out one after another with no interlude, unlike the seals and trumpets. The bowls strike the whole earth rather than a portion of it.

The bowl judgments closely parallel both the trumpets and the plagues of Egypt. The first bowl brings sores, like the sixth plague (Exodus 9:8–12). The second and third turn water to blood, like the first plague (Exodus 7:17–21). The fifth brings darkness, like the ninth plague (Exodus 10:21–23). The seventh brings hail, like the seventh plague (Exodus 9:22–26). Frogs, the second plague of Egypt, appear as unclean spirits (Exodus 8:1–15). Just as the plagues on Egypt exposed the powerlessness of Pharaoh and his gods and led to Israel's deliverance, the bowls expose the powerlessness of the beast and lead to the final deliverance of God's people.

As with the seals and trumpets, interpreters understand the bowls in various ways: as literal future judgments in the last days, as judgments on Rome and the first-century world, as symbols of God's ongoing judgments in history, or as a combination. What all agree on is that the bowls portray God's righteous and complete judgment on a world that persistently refuses to repent.

### The First Four Bowls (16:1–9)

[[v1|I heard a loud voice out of the temple, saying to the seven angels, “Go and pour out the seven bowls of the wrath of God on the earth!”]] The command comes from the temple, from God Himself.

[[v2|The first went, and poured out his bowl into the earth, and it became a harmful and painful sore on the people who had the mark of the beast, and who worshiped his image]]. The first bowl falls specifically on the beast's followers. Those who received the beast's mark now receive painful sores. Their choice of allegiance brings its consequences.

[[v3|The second angel poured out his bowl into the sea, and it became blood as of a dead man. Every living thing in the sea died]]. Under the second trumpet, a third of the sea became blood (8:8–9). Now the judgment is complete: every living thing in the sea dies.

[[v4|The third poured out his bowl into the rivers and springs of water, and they became blood]]. Fresh water, essential for life, also becomes blood. The response of the angel of the waters and the altar is studied below.

[[v8|The fourth poured out his bowl on the sun, and it was given to him to scorch men with fire]]. [[v9|People were scorched with great heat, and people blasphemed the name of God who has the power over these plagues. They didn’t repent and give him glory]]. The sun, God's gift of light and warmth, becomes an instrument of judgment. Notice that people recognize God as the One who controls these plagues, yet instead of repenting, they curse Him. This tragic response is repeated three times in the chapter (vv9, 11, 21). It contrasts with the call of the eternal gospel in Revelation 14:7: [[Revelation 14:7|Fear the Lord, and give him glory]]. God's people are promised the opposite: [[Revelation 7:16|They will never be hungry or thirsty any more. The sun won’t beat on them, nor any heat]].

### The Fifth and Sixth Bowls (16:10–16)

[[v10|The fifth poured out his bowl on the throne of the beast, and his kingdom was darkened. They gnawed their tongues because of the pain,]] [[v11|and they blasphemed the God of heaven because of their pains and their sores. They still didn’t repent of their works]]. The fifth bowl strikes the very center of the beast's power, its throne. The beast's kingdom, which seemed so mighty, is plunged into darkness. Those who followed it are left in agony. Yet still they will not repent.

[[v12|The sixth poured out his bowl on the great river, the Euphrates. Its water was dried up, that the way might be prepared for the kings that come from the sunrise]]. The Euphrates was the eastern boundary of the Roman Empire, beyond which lay the feared Parthian armies. The drying of the river recalls the drying of the Red Sea and the Jordan for Israel, but here it opens the way for invading armies. It may also recall the way the Persian king Cyrus captured ancient Babylon by diverting the Euphrates. The gathering of the kings and the battle of Armageddon are studied below.

### The Seventh Bowl (16:17–21)

The declaration "It is done" is studied below.

[[v18|There were lightnings, sounds, and thunders; and there was a great earthquake such as has not happened since there were men on the earth—so great an earthquake and so mighty]]. The signs that accompanied the end of the seals (8:5) and the trumpets (11:19) appear again, now at their greatest intensity. This earthquake is the greatest in human history, shaking the foundations of the world.

[[v19|The great city was divided into three parts, and the cities of the nations fell. Babylon the great was remembered in the sight of God, to give to her the cup of the wine of the fierceness of his wrath]]. The great city, Babylon, is shattered. God has not forgotten her sins. Her judgment will be described in detail in chapters 17 and 18.

[[v20|Every island fled away, and the mountains were not found]]. [[v21|Great hailstones, about the weight of a talent, came down out of the sky on people. People blasphemed God because of the plague of the hail, for this plague was exceedingly severe]]. A talent weighed roughly 75 to 100 pounds (35 to 45 kilograms). The hailstones recall the seventh plague of Egypt and the hailstones God sent against Israel's enemies in Joshua 10:11. The world is shaken to its foundations, yet people still curse God. Their hearts are hardened beyond repentance.

## Important Verses Explained

### Revelation 16:5–7 — True and Righteous Are Your Judgments

verse: Revelation 16:5-7

#### Simple Explanation

After the waters are turned to blood, John hears "the angel of the waters," an angel responsible for the waters of the earth. Rather than protesting the judgment, the angel praises God: "You are righteous, who are and who were, O Holy One, because you have judged these things."

Verse 6 explains why the judgment is just: "For they poured out the blood of saints and prophets, and you have given them blood to drink. They deserve this." Those who shed the blood of God's people now receive blood to drink. The punishment fits the crime.

In verse 7, another voice responds from the altar: "Yes, Lord God, the Almighty, true and righteous are your judgments." The altar is where the souls of the martyrs cried out for justice (6:9–10) and where the prayers of the saints were offered (8:3–5). Now the altar affirms that God's justice has come.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses are crucial for understanding the bowl judgments. Without them, the judgments might seem harsh or arbitrary. But the angel and the altar declare that God's judgments are righteous. They are not random acts of anger but just responses to real evil, especially the persecution and murder of God's people.

Notice that God is called the One "who are and who were." As in 11:17, the phrase "who is to come" is missing. God has come in judgment. His promised intervention is happening.

The principle of fitting punishment appears throughout Scripture. Those who shed innocent blood are repaid with blood. This reflects God's justice: evil is not ignored, and those who cause suffering will answer for it. The martyrs' cry, "How long?" (6:10), is finally answered.

The response from the altar is moving. Those who suffered and died for their faith, whose prayers rose from the altar, now affirm that God's judgments are true and righteous. They are not bitter or vengeful. They simply confirm that God has done what is right.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses assure believers that God sees injustice and will set it right. Those who suffer at the hands of others, especially for their faith, can trust that God will not ignore their pain. Justice will come.

This passage also invites believers to trust God's character when His judgments seem hard to understand. The angel and the altar affirm that God's judgments are always true and righteous. Believers can hold on to this truth even when they do not understand everything.

Finally, this passage reminds believers that they do not need to take revenge. God Himself will judge rightly. Believers can leave justice in His hands, pray for those who harm them, and continue to bear witness with love.

### Revelation 16:13–16 — Gathering for Armageddon

verse: Revelation 16:13-16

#### Simple Explanation

John sees "three unclean spirits, something like frogs" coming out of the mouths of "the dragon," "the beast," and "the false prophet." This is the first time the second beast is called "the false prophet," which describes his role of deceiving people. The frogs come from their mouths, suggesting deceptive words and propaganda.

Verse 14 explains: "for they are spirits of demons, performing signs." They go out "to the kings of the whole inhabited earth, to gather them together for the war of that great day of God the Almighty." Through deception, the powers of evil gather the world's rulers for a final rebellion against God.

Verse 15 interrupts with a word from Jesus: "Behold, I come like a thief. Blessed is he who watches, and keeps his clothes, so that he doesn’t walk naked, and they see his shame." This is a call to readiness for believers.

Verse 16 names the gathering place: "He gathered them together into the place which is called in Hebrew, ‘Harmagedon’." This is often spelled "Armageddon."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The unclean spirits are a picture of deception that leads people into rebellion against God. Throughout Revelation, the dragon and the beasts work through lies and false signs. The final rebellion is driven by deception. People are led to believe they can fight against God and win.

"Harmagedon" likely means "mountain of Megiddo." Megiddo was a city overlooking the plain of Jezreel in northern Israel, the site of many famous battles in Israel's history (Judges 5:19; 2 Kings 23:29). There is no actual mountain called Megiddo, which has led many interpreters to see the name as symbolic: the place of the final decisive battle between God and the forces of evil. Some futurist interpreters expect a literal battle in that region of Israel in the last days. Others see Armageddon as a symbol of the final confrontation between God and all the powers that oppose Him, wherever and however it takes place. Some connect "mountain" with Mount Zion or Mount Carmel, where Elijah confronted the prophets of Baal.

Notably, the battle itself is not described here. When the confrontation is finally narrated (19:11–21; 20:7–10), there is no real battle at all. Christ simply defeats His enemies by the word of His mouth. The gathering of the world's armies is futile.

Jesus' words in verse 15 are the third blessing in Revelation. In the middle of this terrifying scene, Christ speaks directly to His people, urging them to stay awake and spiritually clothed. The image recalls His warning to Sardis (3:3) and Laodicea (3:18). The danger for believers is not the armies of Armageddon, but spiritual sleepiness and unpreparedness.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage warns believers about the power of deception. Lies, propaganda, and false signs can lead whole nations into rebellion against God. Believers are called to test what they hear, hold to the truth of Scripture, and resist voices that stir up hostility toward God and His people.

The blessing in verse 15 is the most practical part of this passage for believers. Rather than speculating about the details of Armageddon, believers are called to watchfulness. They can stay spiritually awake through prayer, Scripture, and faithful living, and they can remain clothed in Christ's righteousness, ready for His return.

Believers can also take comfort that the final confrontation between God and evil is not a close contest. The kings of the earth gather, but their rebellion is doomed. Christ will triumph.

### Revelation 16:17 — It Is Done

verse: Revelation 16:17

#### Simple Explanation

The seventh angel pours out his bowl "into the air." The air was sometimes thought of as the realm of evil spiritual powers (Ephesians 2:2). This final bowl affects the whole atmosphere surrounding the earth.

Then "A loud voice came out of the temple of heaven, from the throne, saying, ‘It is done!’" The voice comes from God Himself. With the seventh bowl, God's judgment on the world is complete.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The declaration "It is done!" echoes Jesus' cry from the cross: [[John 19:30|It is finished]]. At the cross, Jesus completed the work of salvation, bearing the judgment for sin. Here, God completes the work of judgment on those who have rejected that salvation. The two declarations belong together. Because of the cross, judgment and salvation are both brought to completion.

These words also anticipate the moment in Revelation 21, when God makes all things new and declares, [[Revelation 21:6|I am the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End]]. The completion of judgment opens the way for the new creation. Evil must be removed before God's renewed world can be fully established.

This verse shows that history is moving toward a goal. God's purposes are not endless cycles of judgment and suffering. There will be an end. God will finish what He has begun.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can take comfort in the certainty that God's purposes will be completed. The struggles of this life, the suffering of His people, and the apparent triumph of evil will not go on forever. One day God will say, "It is done."

This verse also points believers back to the cross. Jesus' cry, "It is finished," means that everything needed for their salvation has been accomplished. They do not need to add anything. They can rest in His finished work.

Finally, believers can live with hope, knowing that the end of judgment means the beginning of the new creation. The God who completes His judgment will also complete His promise to make all things new.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Revelation 16 portrays the final outpouring of God's judgment on a world in rebellion. The bowls strike the beast's followers, the sea, the waters, the sun, the beast's throne, the Euphrates, and the air. Like the plagues of Egypt, they expose the powerlessness of every false god and every proud power. Believers can take courage that no power on earth can stand against God.

The chapter also reveals the tragic hardness of the human heart. Three times people curse God and refuse to repent. Suffering alone does not lead people to God. This should move believers to pray earnestly for those who are hardened and to share the gospel with urgency and love while there is time.

The voices of the angel and the altar remind believers that God's judgments are true and righteous. God sees injustice, especially the suffering of His people, and He will set things right. Believers can trust His justice and leave vengeance in His hands.

In the middle of the chapter, Jesus speaks directly to His people: "Behold, I come like a thief. Blessed is he who watches." Rather than being consumed by fear or speculation about the end, believers are called to stay spiritually awake and ready. And they can rest in the promise that God will bring all things to completion. One day He will say, "It is done," and the way will be open for the new creation.
