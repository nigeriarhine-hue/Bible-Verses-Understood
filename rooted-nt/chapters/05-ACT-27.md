# Acts 27
theme: Faith in the Storm: "I Believe God"
main: Paul sails for Rome as a prisoner under a centurion named Julius. Against Paul's warning, the ship leaves harbor late in the season and is caught in a violent storm that rages for two weeks, until all hope of being saved is gone. Paul stands among the terrified crew and passengers and tells them to take courage, because an angel of God has promised that all 276 aboard will survive. He prevents the sailors from abandoning ship, urges everyone to eat, gives thanks to God before them all, and breaks bread. The ship runs aground and breaks apart, but the centurion spares the prisoners for Paul's sake, and everyone reaches land safely.

## Chapter Background and Story

Acts 27 is one of the most detailed accounts of a sea voyage that survives from the ancient world. Luke writes in the first person ("we"), showing that he was on board. His descriptions of the route, the winds, the ship's handling, and the nautical procedures are so precise that historians and sailors have studied this chapter to understand ancient navigation. One nineteenth-century sailor and scholar, James Smith, retraced the voyage and found Luke's details accurate down to the depth soundings near Malta.

But the chapter is more than an exciting travel story. It shows God fulfilling His promise that Paul would testify in Rome (23:11), and it shows how a believer can respond to danger with faith, courage, and practical wisdom. Paul, the prisoner, becomes the true leader on the ship.

### Setting Out for Italy (27:1–8)

Paul and other prisoners were handed over to [[v1|a centurion named Julius, of the Augustan band]]. They sailed from Caesarea on a ship from Adramyttium, a port on the northwest coast of Asia Minor. Aristarchus from Thessalonica, who had been with Paul in Ephesus (19:29), came along, as did Luke.

At Sidon, [[v3|Julius treated Paul kindly and gave him permission to go to his friends and refresh himself]]. Throughout the voyage, Julius showed respect for Paul. The winds were against them, so they sailed along the coast to Myra, in Lycia, where Julius found [[v6|a ship of Alexandria sailing for Italy]]. This was probably a grain ship; Egypt supplied much of Rome's grain, and the government contracted with private ships to carry it.

The sailing was slow and difficult. They reached Fair Havens, on the south coast of Crete.

### Paul's Warning Ignored (27:9–12)

[[v9|When much time had passed and the voyage was now dangerous because the Fast had now already gone by]]. "The Fast" was the Day of Atonement, which fell in late September or October. Sailing on the Mediterranean was considered dangerous from mid-September to early November and was generally avoided from November to March.

Paul warned, [[v10|Sirs, I perceive that the voyage will be with injury and much loss, not only of the cargo and the ship, but also of our lives]]. Paul was an experienced traveler; he had already been shipwrecked three times (2 Corinthians 11:25). But [[v11|the centurion gave more heed to the master and to the owner of the ship than to those things which were spoken by Paul]]. It was natural to trust the professionals. The harbor at Fair Havens was not suitable for spending the winter, so the majority decided to try to reach Phoenix, a better harbor farther west on Crete.

### The Storm (27:13–20)

A gentle south wind made them think they could make it. But soon [[v14|a stormy wind beat down from shore, which is called Euroclydon]], a violent northeasterly wind. The ship was caught and could not head into the wind, so they let it be driven along.

Behind a small island called Clauda (Cauda), they managed to secure the lifeboat, which was being towed behind the ship. They passed ropes under the ship to hold it together, a technique called "undergirding." Fearing they would run aground on the Syrtis, dangerous sandbars off the coast of North Africa, they lowered the sea anchor (or the sails) and let the ship be driven.

The storm continued. The next day they threw cargo overboard. The third day they threw out the ship's tackle with their own hands. Then came a moment of despair, studied below.

### Paul's Message of Hope (27:21–26)

After a long time without food, Paul stood up among them. He began by gently reminding them that they should have listened to him, not to say "I told you so," but to give credibility to what he was about to say. His message of hope is studied below.

### Approaching Land (27:27–38)

On the fourteenth night, as they were driven across the Adriatic Sea (the ancient name included the sea between Greece, Italy, and Africa), the sailors sensed they were nearing land, perhaps hearing breakers. They took soundings: twenty fathoms (about 120 feet), then fifteen fathoms (about 90 feet). Fearing they would hit rocks, they dropped four anchors from the stern and prayed for daylight.

The sailors' attempt to escape, and Paul's response, are studied below. As dawn approached, Paul urged everyone to eat, which is also studied below. Luke notes that there were [[v37|two hundred seventy-six souls on the ship]]. After eating, they lightened the ship further by throwing the grain into the sea.

### Shipwreck (27:39–44)

At daylight, they did not recognize the land, but they saw a bay with a beach. They cut the anchors, loosened the steering oars, raised the foresail, and headed for the beach. But [[v41|coming to a place where two seas met, they ran the vessel aground]], probably a sandbar between two channels. The bow stuck fast, and the stern began to break apart in the pounding waves.

The soldiers planned to kill the prisoners so that none would escape. Under Roman law, guards who allowed prisoners to escape could face the same penalty the prisoners would have received. [[v43|But the centurion, desiring to save Paul, stopped them from their purpose]]. Julius had come to respect Paul. Because of Paul, all the prisoners were spared. Those who could swim jumped overboard first; the rest followed on planks and pieces of the ship. [[v44|So they all escaped safely to the land]]. God's promise was fulfilled exactly: every life was saved, but the ship was lost.

## Important Verses Explained

### Acts 27:20 — All Hope Was Taken Away

verse: Acts 27:20

#### Simple Explanation

The ship carrying Paul had been caught in a violent storm off the coast of Crete. For days, it had been driven by the wind, completely out of control. The crew had tied ropes around the ship to keep it from breaking apart, thrown cargo overboard, and even thrown away the ship's equipment with their own hands. Nothing seemed to help.

[[v20|When neither sun nor stars shone on us for many days]]. Ancient sailors navigated by the sun during the day and the stars at night. Without them, the crew had no way of knowing where they were or which direction they were going. They were lost in the darkness and the storm.

[[v20|and no small storm pressed on us]]. The storm did not let up. It kept battering them day after day.

[[v20|all hope that we would be saved was now taken away]]. Luke, who was on the ship, includes himself in this despair. Everyone on board, the sailors, soldiers, prisoners, and passengers, had given up hope of survival. They expected to die.

This verse is honest about the reality of despair. Luke does not pretend that the believers on board felt no fear or hopelessness. Even people of faith can reach moments when circumstances seem completely hopeless.

Yet this moment of total despair was the moment God chose to speak hope through Paul.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that believers are not exempt from life's storms. Paul was on a mission from God, traveling to Rome as Jesus had promised. Yet he still faced a terrible storm. Following God's will does not guarantee calm seas. Jesus' disciples also faced a storm while obeying His command to cross the lake (Mark 4:35–41).

It also shows that despair is a real human experience. Paul himself once wrote of being [[2 Corinthians 1:8|weighed down exceedingly, beyond our power, so much that we despaired even of life]]. Scripture does not shame those who feel hopeless; it meets them there.

Finally, the verse shows that God often speaks hope at the darkest moment. When all hope was gone, God gave a message of rescue. Psalm 107 describes sailors in a storm who cried to the Lord: [[Psalm 107:29|He makes the storm a calm, so that its waves are still]].

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse speaks to anyone who feels that all hope is gone. Some people face illness, loss, financial ruin, broken relationships, or depression so deep that they cannot see any way forward. Luke's honesty shows that such feelings are part of human experience. Those who feel this way can bring their despair to God, and they should also seek help from others, including trusted believers, counselors, and doctors.

It also reminds believers that times of darkness do not mean God has abandoned them. The sun and stars were hidden, but they still existed. In the same way, God is still present and at work even when believers cannot see Him.

Finally, the verse encourages believers to be ready to bring hope to others. Paul was in the same storm, yet he became a source of hope for everyone on board. Believers who are suffering can still point others to God's promises.

### Acts 27:23–25 — "I Believe God"

verse: Acts 27:23-25

#### Simple Explanation

After many days in the storm, with everyone on board having given up hope, Paul stood up in the middle of the crew and passengers. He reminded them that they should have listened to his earlier warning. Then he said, [[v22|Now I exhort you to cheer up, for there will be no loss of life among you, but only of the ship]].

Paul explained why he could be so confident: [[v23|For there stood by me this night an angel, belonging to the God whose I am and whom I serve]]. Notice how Paul described God: "whose I am and whom I serve." Paul belonged to God, and he served God. In a ship full of people who worshiped many gods, Paul spoke of the one true God to whom he belonged.

The angel's message: [[v24|Don’t be afraid, Paul. You must stand before Caesar. Behold, God has granted you all those who sail with you.]] God's purpose for Paul had not changed. He "must" stand before Caesar. The storm could not prevent it. And God had "granted" to Paul the lives of everyone on the ship. This suggests that Paul had been praying for them, and God had answered.

Then Paul said, [[v25|Therefore, sirs, cheer up! For I believe God, that it will be just as it has been spoken to me]]. This is the heart of the passage. Paul's courage did not come from the weather, the ship, or the skill of the sailors. It came from trusting God's word. "I believe God."

Paul added, [[v26|But we must run aground on a certain island]]. God's promise did not mean the journey would be easy. The ship would be wrecked. But everyone would live.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show the nature of faith: trusting God's word even when circumstances seem to contradict it. The storm was still raging, and no one could see a way to survive. Yet Paul believed God. Abraham was described in the same way: [[Romans 4:20|Yet, looking to the promise of God, he didn’t waver through unbelief, but grew strong through faith, giving glory to God]].

They also show the identity of a believer. Paul belonged to God and served God. This gave him stability in the storm. Believers who know whose they are can face any circumstance with confidence.

Finally, these verses show God's mercy to unbelievers through His people. God spared everyone on the ship for Paul's sake. Throughout Scripture, God blesses others through His people, as He blessed the nations through Abraham (Genesis 12:3) and spared cities for the sake of the righteous (Genesis 18:26).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to anchor their faith in God's word. In times of trouble, believers can hold on to God's promises in Scripture. Like Paul, they can say, "I believe God, that it will be just as He has said."

They also encourage believers to remember whose they are. Believers belong to God and serve Him. This identity gives them security and purpose in every storm.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to be sources of hope for others. Paul's faith gave courage to everyone on board. Believers can share God's promises with those around them who are afraid, offering hope rooted in God's faithfulness.

### Acts 27:31 — [[Acts 27:31|Unless These Stay in the Ship]]

verse: Acts 27:31

#### Simple Explanation

On the fourteenth night of the storm, the sailors sensed they were near land. They took soundings and found the water getting shallower. Fearing they would be wrecked on rocks in the dark, they dropped four anchors from the back of the ship and waited for daylight.

Then the sailors secretly tried to escape. [[v30|As the sailors were trying to flee out of the ship and had lowered the boat into the sea, pretending that they would lay out anchors from the bow]]. They lowered the lifeboat into the water, pretending they were going to drop more anchors from the front of the ship. In reality, they planned to row to shore and leave everyone else behind.

Paul noticed what was happening. He said to the centurion and the soldiers, [[v31|Unless these stay in the ship, you can’t be saved]]. The sailors were needed. Without their skill, no one would be able to bring the ship to land safely.

[[v32|Then the soldiers cut away the ropes of the boat and let it fall off]]. The soldiers acted immediately. They cut the ropes, and the lifeboat drifted away. Now everyone had to stay together.

This verse shows an interesting balance. God had promised that everyone would survive. Yet Paul still said, "Unless these stay in the ship, you can't be saved." God's promise did not mean that human actions were unimportant. God would save them, but He would do it through the sailors' skill and everyone's cooperation.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows the relationship between God's sovereignty and human responsibility. God had promised to save everyone, but He would accomplish it through human means. Faith in God's promises does not mean doing nothing. It means acting wisely and faithfully, trusting God to work through those actions. Paul wrote, [[Philippians 2:12|work out your own salvation with fear and trembling]], [[Philippians 2:13|for it is God who works in you both to will and to work, for his good pleasure]].

It also shows the importance of community. Everyone on the ship needed one another. The sailors needed the others, and the others needed the sailors. No one could be saved by abandoning the group. The church is also a community in which believers need one another (1 Corinthians 12:21).

Finally, the verse shows Paul's practical wisdom. Paul was not only a man of prayer and faith; he was also alert and practical. Faith and common sense work together.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to act responsibly while trusting God. Trusting God's promises does not mean neglecting wise action. Believers can pray for healing and also see a doctor, pray for provision and also work, pray for safety and also take precautions.

It also encourages believers to stay committed to their communities. In difficult times, it can be tempting to abandon others and look out only for oneself. Paul's words remind believers that they are bound together, and that faithfulness includes standing with others.

Finally, the verse encourages believers to be alert. Paul noticed the sailors' deception and acted. Believers can pay attention to what is happening around them and speak up when others are in danger.

### Acts 27:35 — He Gave Thanks to God in the Presence of All

verse: Acts 27:35

#### Simple Explanation

It was just before dawn on the fourteenth day of the storm. Everyone on the ship had been too afraid, seasick, or busy to eat properly for two weeks. They were exhausted and weak. Soon they would need all their strength to reach the shore.

Paul urged them to eat: [[v34|Therefore I beg you to take some food, for this is for your safety; for not a hair will perish from any of your heads]]. Paul cared about their physical needs. He repeated God's promise that they would all be safe.

[[v35|When he had said this and had taken bread, he gave thanks to God in the presence of all; then he broke it and began to eat]]. Paul took bread, gave thanks to God, broke it, and ate. He did this publicly, in front of all 276 people, most of whom did not share his faith. In the middle of a storm, on a ship that was about to be wrecked, Paul thanked God.

Paul's actions echo the way Jesus took bread, gave thanks, and broke it (Luke 9:16; 22:19; 24:30). This was not the Lord's Supper, since most on board were not believers, but it was a meal marked by thanksgiving to God.

[[v36|Then they all cheered up, and they also took food]]. Paul's faith and example encouraged everyone. They began to eat, and their spirits lifted.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows the power of thanksgiving in difficult times. Paul thanked God before the rescue had happened. He trusted God's promise enough to give thanks in advance. Paul later wrote, [[1 Thessalonians 5:18|In everything give thanks, for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus toward you]].

It also shows that faith can be a public witness. Paul gave thanks "in the presence of all." He was not ashamed of his faith before soldiers, sailors, and prisoners. His simple act of thanksgiving pointed everyone to the God he trusted.

Finally, the verse shows that God cares for physical needs. Paul urged everyone to eat for their survival. Spiritual faith and practical care go together. God created human bodies and cares for their needs.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to give thanks to God even in hard times. Thanksgiving is not only for when life is going well. Believers can thank God for His presence, His promises, and the good gifts He provides, even in the middle of storms.

It also encourages believers to let their faith be seen. Paul gave thanks publicly. Believers can pray before meals, speak of God's goodness, and live in a way that shows their trust in Him, even among people who do not share their faith.

Finally, the verse encourages believers to care for others' practical needs. Paul made sure everyone ate. Believers can show God's love by meeting the physical needs of those around them, especially in times of crisis.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Acts 27 shows that believers are not exempt from life's storms. Paul was traveling according to God's plan, yet he faced a terrible storm, darkness, and despair. Believers today may also face trials that seem overwhelming. This chapter assures them that God is present in the storm and that His purposes cannot be defeated.

The chapter also shows the power of faith in God's word. When all hope was gone, Paul stood up and said, "I believe God, that it will be just as it has been spoken to me." Believers can anchor themselves in God's promises, remember whose they are and whom they serve, and offer that hope to others who are afraid.

Acts 27 teaches that faith and responsible action go together. God had promised to save everyone, yet Paul insisted that the sailors stay on the ship and that everyone eat. Believers can trust God while acting wisely, staying committed to their communities, and caring for practical needs.

Finally, the chapter shows the witness of thanksgiving. Paul gave thanks to God in front of everyone before the rescue had come, and his example gave courage to all. Believers can give thanks in every circumstance, letting their trust in God be visible, and becoming, as Paul did, a source of hope and steadiness for those around them.
