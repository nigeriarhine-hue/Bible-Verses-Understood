# Ephesians 3
theme: The Mystery Revealed and the Love That Surpasses Knowledge
main: Paul, a prisoner for the sake of the Gentiles, explains the mystery God revealed to him: through the gospel, Gentiles are fellow heirs with Israel, members of the same body, and sharers in the same promise in Christ. Though he considers himself the least of all God's people, he was given the grace of proclaiming the unsearchable riches of Christ, so that through the church God's wisdom would be displayed even to the powers in the heavenly places. Paul then kneels before the Father and prays that believers would be strengthened by the Spirit, that Christ would dwell in their hearts, and that, rooted and grounded in love, they would grasp the vast love of Christ and be filled with all the fullness of God. He closes with praise to the God who is able to do far more than we ask or imagine.

## Chapter Background and Story

Paul begins chapter 3 as if he is about to pray: "For this cause I, Paul..." But he interrupts himself to explain his own role in God's plan, and he does not return to the prayer until verse 14. This digression, from verse 2 to verse 13, explains the "mystery" of the gospel and Paul's calling to proclaim it.

Paul was writing as a prisoner, probably in Rome, where he was under house arrest awaiting trial (Acts 28:16, 30–31). He had been arrested in Jerusalem precisely because of his ministry to the Gentiles (Acts 21:27–36; 22:21–22). So when he calls himself "the prisoner of Christ Jesus on behalf of you Gentiles," he means it literally. His chains were the direct result of his commitment to bringing the gospel to people like the Ephesians.

The chapter ends with one of the most beautiful prayers in the Bible and a soaring doxology. Together they form the climax of the first half of Ephesians, before Paul turns to practical instructions in chapter 4.

### Paul's Stewardship of the Mystery (3:1–5)

[[v1|For this cause I, Paul, am the prisoner of Christ Jesus on behalf of you Gentiles]]. Paul does not see himself as Rome's prisoner but as Christ's. His imprisonment is part of Christ's purpose.

[[v2|if it is so that you have heard of the administration of that grace of God which was given me toward you]]. "Administration" translates a word meaning stewardship or management of a household. God had entrusted Paul with a special responsibility: to dispense God's grace to the Gentiles.

[[v3|how that by revelation the mystery was made known to me, as I wrote before in few words]]. Paul had already touched on this in chapters 1 and 2. [[v5|which in other generations was not made known to the children of men, as it has now been revealed to his holy apostles and prophets in the Spirit]]. The Old Testament did speak of Gentiles being blessed through Abraham and turning to the LORD (Genesis 12:3; Isaiah 49:6). What had not been fully revealed was the way it would happen: Gentiles joining God's people on equal terms, without becoming Jews, united in one body in Christ. The content of the mystery is studied below.

### The Least of All Saints (3:7–13)

[[v7|of which I was made a servant according to the gift of that grace of God which was given me according to the working of his power]]. Paul's ministry was a gift of grace and empowered by God. Paul's humble description of himself and his calling is studied below.

[[v9|and to make all men see what is the administration of the mystery which for ages has been hidden in God, who created all things through Jesus Christ]]. The God who created all things through Christ has now revealed His plan for all things in Christ.

[[v10|to the intent that now through the assembly the manifold wisdom of God might be made known to the principalities and the powers in the heavenly places]]. This is a breathtaking statement. The church, a community of reconciled Jews and Gentiles, is God's showcase to the spiritual powers of the universe. "Manifold" means many-colored or multifaceted, like a richly embroidered fabric. When angels and spiritual powers look at the church, they see God's wisdom in bringing together people who had been divided. [[v11|according to the eternal purpose which he accomplished in Christ Jesus our Lord]].

[[v12|In him we have boldness and access in confidence through our faith in him]]. Because of Christ, believers can approach God freely and confidently, without fear. [[v13|Therefore I ask that you may not lose heart at my troubles for you, which are your glory]]. Paul did not want his readers to be discouraged by his imprisonment. His sufferings were for their benefit and showed how much God valued them.

### Paul's Prayer (3:14–19)

[[v14|For this cause, I bow my knees to the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ]]. Jews usually stood to pray (Mark 11:25; Luke 18:11, 13). Kneeling suggested special earnestness and humility. [[v15|from whom every family in heaven and on earth is named]]. In Greek there is a wordplay: "Father" is *pater*, and "family" is *patria*. Every family takes its name and existence from God, the ultimate Father. The content of Paul's prayer is studied below.

### Doxology (3:20–21)

Paul's closing praise is studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### Ephesians 3:6 — Fellow Heirs, Fellow Members, Fellow Partakers

verse: Ephesians 3:6

#### Simple Explanation

This verse states the content of the mystery Paul has been describing. "That the Gentiles are fellow heirs and fellow members of the body, and fellow partakers of his promise in Christ Jesus through the Good News."

In Greek, Paul uses three words that all begin with the same prefix, meaning "together with." The Gentiles are "co-heirs," "co-members of the body," and "co-sharers of the promise." The repetition emphasizes complete equality and partnership.

"Fellow heirs" means Gentiles share the same inheritance as Jewish believers. They are not second-class heirs who receive a lesser portion.

"Fellow members of the body" means Gentiles are part of the same body, the church, with Christ as its head. They are not attached loosely from the outside; they are members of one organism.

"Fellow partakers of his promise" means the promises God made to Abraham and Israel now belong to Gentile believers as well.

All of this happens "in Christ Jesus" and "through the Good News." Union with Christ, received through the gospel, is what brings Gentiles into this full belonging.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse expresses the revolutionary nature of the gospel. For centuries, the distinction between Israel and the nations had been fundamental. Israel was God's chosen people, set apart by covenant, Law, and temple. Gentiles could join Israel by becoming proselytes, but they had to adopt the Law and its customs. The mystery revealed in Christ is that Gentiles become full members of God's people simply by faith in Christ, without becoming Jews.

This does not mean God has abandoned Israel or that Jewish identity is erased. Paul elsewhere insists that God's gifts and calling to Israel are irrevocable (Romans 11:29), and that Gentile believers have been grafted into Israel's olive tree (Romans 11:17–24). The church is not a replacement for Israel but the fulfillment of God's promise that through Abraham all nations would be blessed. Christians have different views on how the church and Israel relate in God's ongoing plan, but they share the conviction that in Christ, Jew and Gentile are one family.

This mystery reveals God's heart for all peoples. From the beginning, His plan was to gather a family from every nation, tribe, people, and language (Revelation 7:9).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many believers feel like outsiders in their churches, perhaps because of their background, culture, past, or social status. This verse declares that in Christ, every believer is a full heir, a full member, and a full partaker of God's promises. No one is a second-class Christian.

This verse also challenges churches to embody this equality. Do people of every background feel fully welcomed, included, and valued? Are some treated as more important than others? The gospel calls the church to reflect God's design of full and equal belonging in Christ.

### Ephesians 3:8 — The Unsearchable Riches of Christ

verse: Ephesians 3:8

#### Simple Explanation

Paul describes himself with remarkable humility: "the very least of all saints." In Greek, Paul creates a new word, something like "leaster" or "less than the least." He is not merely saying he is the least of the apostles (as in 1 Corinthians 15:9); he says he is less than the least of all God's people.

Why did Paul think of himself this way? He never forgot that he had once persecuted the church (1 Timothy 1:13–15). He was deeply aware that his calling was not something he deserved.

"Was this grace given." Paul's ministry was a gift. God chose the least likely person to carry the gospel to the Gentiles.

The purpose: "to preach to the Gentiles the unsearchable riches of Christ." "Unsearchable" means something so vast that it can never be fully explored or traced out, like footprints that cannot be tracked to their end. The riches of Christ, His grace, love, wisdom, and power, are inexhaustible.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows the connection between humility and grace. Paul's sense of his own unworthiness did not make him timid or ineffective. On the contrary, it made him all the more amazed at God's grace and all the more eager to share it. Those who know they have received grace freely are often the most passionate about offering it to others.

The phrase "unsearchable riches of Christ" reminds believers that no one ever exhausts the treasure of knowing Christ. Even the apostle Paul, after years of ministry and deep revelation, saw himself standing at the edge of an ocean. Every believer can keep discovering more of Christ throughout life and eternity.

Paul's humility is genuine, not false modesty. He does not deny God's work in his life; in other places he defends his apostleship vigorously. But he attributes everything to grace. As he said in 1 Corinthians 15:10, [[1 Corinthians 15:10|by the grace of God I am what I am]].

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Some believers feel unqualified to serve God or to share their faith because of their past or their weaknesses. Paul's example shows that God delights in using unlikely people. A sense of unworthiness, rightly held, can become a channel for grace rather than an obstacle.

This verse also invites believers to keep exploring the riches of Christ. Faith is not meant to become stale or routine. Through Scripture, prayer, worship, and fellowship, there is always more to discover of His love and wisdom.

### Ephesians 3:16–19 — Rooted and Grounded in Love

verse: Ephesians 3:16-19

#### Simple Explanation

Paul's prayer has several connected requests, each building on the last.

First, that God would grant them "according to the riches of his glory" to be "strengthened with power through his Spirit in the inner person." The "inner person" is the heart, the spiritual center of a person. Paul prays for inner strength that comes from the Holy Spirit, measured not by human limits but by the riches of God's glory.

Second, "that Christ may dwell in your hearts through faith." Christ already lives in believers, but "dwell" suggests settling down and being fully at home. Paul prays that Christ would be completely at home in every part of their lives.

Third, that they would be "rooted and grounded in love." Paul mixes two images: a tree with deep roots and a building with a solid foundation. Love is the soil in which believers grow and the foundation on which they stand.

Fourth, that they would "be strengthened to comprehend with all the saints what is the width and length and height and depth." Paul is speaking of the dimensions of Christ's love. It is wide enough to embrace all people, long enough to last forever, high enough to lift people to heaven, and deep enough to reach the lowest places of human sin and suffering. And this understanding comes "with all the saints." It is grasped in community, not alone.

Fifth, "to know Christ’s love which surpasses knowledge." This is a paradox. Believers are to know what is beyond knowing. They can experience Christ's love truly, even though they can never measure it fully.

Finally, "that you may be filled with all the fullness of God." This is the ultimate goal: to be filled with God's own life, love, and character.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This prayer reveals what matters most in the Christian life. Paul does not pray for his readers' circumstances, comfort, or success. He prays for inner strength, the indwelling of Christ, deep roots in love, and an ever-growing experience of Christ's love. These are the things that sustain believers through every circumstance.

The image of being "rooted" in love is especially rich. Roots draw nourishment and provide stability. A tree with deep roots can withstand storms and drought (Psalm 1:3; Jeremiah 17:7–8). Believers who are rooted in Christ's love can withstand the storms of life because their security does not depend on circumstances.

The phrase "with all the saints" is important. The love of Christ is so vast that no single believer can grasp it alone. Each believer, with their own experience of grace, contributes to the church's shared understanding. Believers need one another to know the love of Christ more fully.

"Filled with all the fullness of God" does not mean believers become divine. It means they are filled with God's presence and transformed to reflect His character, as much as human beings can, now partially and one day fully.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can pray this prayer for themselves and for others regularly. It is a prayer that reaches beyond surface needs to the deepest needs of the heart.

Many people live with an inner emptiness or insecurity, trying to fill it with achievement, relationships, possessions, or approval. This prayer points to the only source that can truly fill the heart: the love of Christ and the fullness of God. Being rooted and grounded in love provides a security that nothing else can give.

The call to grasp Christ's love "with all the saints" also encourages believers to share their experiences of God's love with one another. Testimony, fellowship, and worship together help each believer see more of the width, length, height, and depth of that love.

### Ephesians 3:20–21 — Exceedingly Abundantly Above All We Ask

verse: Ephesians 3:20-21

#### Simple Explanation

After praying for such great things, Paul might have worried that he was asking too much. Instead, he bursts into praise, declaring that God is able to do even more.

"Now to him who is able to do exceedingly abundantly above all that we ask or think." Paul piles up words to express the abundance of God's ability. God can do more than believers ask. He can do more than they can even imagine. And He can do it "exceedingly abundantly," far beyond every limit.

"According to the power that works in us." This is not abstract power far away. It is the same power that raised Christ from the dead (Ephesians 1:19–20), now at work within believers.

"To him be the glory in the assembly and in Christ Jesus to all generations, forever and ever. Amen." The goal of everything is God's glory. And that glory is displayed in two places: in the church and in Christ Jesus. The church, as the body of Christ, is the place where God's glory shines in the world, now and forever.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This doxology closes the first half of Ephesians. After three chapters of celebrating God's grace, plan, and power, Paul ends with praise. Theology leads to worship. The more believers understand of God, the more they are moved to glorify Him.

The phrase "above all that we ask or think" does not mean God will give believers everything they want. Rather, it means that His purposes and power exceed human imagination. Sometimes He answers prayers in unexpected ways, doing something deeper and better than what was asked. His ability is unlimited, and His wisdom determines how it is used.

The mention of glory "in the assembly" is striking. The church, with all its flaws, is the place God has chosen to display His glory. This gives dignity and purpose to the ordinary life of local congregations. When believers love one another, worship together, and serve their communities, God's glory is seen.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

When believers face situations that seem impossible, such as broken relationships, persistent struggles, or overwhelming needs, this verse reminds them that God is able to do far more than they can imagine. They can pray boldly, trusting His power and wisdom.

This verse also lifts believers' expectations of what God can do in and through His church. Local congregations may seem small or ordinary, but they are the place where God has chosen to display His glory. Believers can serve with hope, knowing that God's power is at work among them.

Finally, this doxology invites believers to make praise a habit. Every prayer, every reflection on God's goodness, can end as Paul's does: "to him be the glory... forever and ever."

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Ephesians 3 reveals the mystery hidden for ages and now made known in Christ: Gentiles are fellow heirs, fellow members of the body, and fellow partakers of God's promise. In Christ, people of every background belong fully to God's family. This truth calls churches to welcome all believers as full and equal members and to display God's wisdom by living as one reconciled people.

Paul's example shows that God uses unlikely people. Though he saw himself as the least of all God's people, he was entrusted with the privilege of proclaiming the unsearchable riches of Christ. Believers who feel unqualified can take heart. God's grace, not human merit, equips people for His work. And the riches of Christ are inexhaustible, inviting believers to keep exploring them throughout their lives.

The heart of this chapter is Paul's prayer. He asks that believers would be strengthened by the Spirit in their inner being, that Christ would make His home in their hearts, and that, rooted and grounded in love, they would grasp the immeasurable love of Christ and be filled with God's fullness. This is a prayer for the deepest needs of the human heart, and believers can pray it for themselves and for one another. To be rooted in Christ's love is to have a security that no storm can shake.

Finally, the chapter ends with praise to the God who is able to do far more than anyone can ask or imagine. His power is at work in His people, and His glory is displayed in the church and in Christ Jesus. Believers can face every challenge with bold prayer, confident hope, and a heart full of worship.

## Chapter Prayer

Father, I bow before You, from whom every family in heaven and on earth is named. Thank You for revealing the mystery of Your grace: that people from every nation are fellow heirs, members of one body, and sharers in Your promise in Christ. Thank You that You use the least likely people to share the unsearchable riches of Christ. Strengthen me with power through Your Spirit in my inner being. Let Christ make His home in my heart through faith. Root me and ground me in love. Give me, together with all Your people, the strength to grasp how wide and long and high and deep is the love of Christ, and to know this love that surpasses knowledge, so that I may be filled with all Your fullness. Now to You, who are able to do far more than all I ask or think, according to Your power at work in me, be the glory in the church and in Christ Jesus to all generations, forever and ever. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
