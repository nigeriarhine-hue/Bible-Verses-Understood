# John 13
theme: Love to the End: The Servant Lord and the New Commandment
main: On the night before His death, Jesus, fully aware of His divine authority, takes a towel and washes His disciples' feet. He tells Peter that unless He washes him, Peter has no part with Him, and He calls all His followers to serve one another as He has served them. After Judas goes out into the night to betray Him, Jesus gives His disciples a new commandment: to love one another as He has loved them, so that everyone will know they are His disciples. He also gently warns Peter that he will deny Him three times.

## Chapter Background and Story

John 13 begins the second half of John's Gospel, often called the Book of Glory. In the first twelve chapters, Jesus ministered publicly through signs and teaching. Now, in chapters 13–17, He speaks privately with His disciples on the night before His crucifixion. These chapters, sometimes called the Upper Room Discourse or Farewell Discourse, contain some of the most tender and profound teaching in the Bible.

John does not describe the institution of the Lord's Supper, which the other three Gospels record (studied at Matthew 26:28 and Mark 14:24). Instead, he tells of something the others do not mention: Jesus washing His disciples' feet. Both events reveal the meaning of His death, one through bread and cup, the other through a towel and basin.

### Jesus Washes the Disciples' Feet (13:1–11)

John begins with a summary of Jesus' heart, studied below. Then he sets the scene. The meal was underway. The devil had already put the idea of betrayal into Judas's heart. And Jesus knew [[v3|that the Father had given all things into his hands, and that he came from God and was going to God]]. John emphasizes that Jesus acted not from weakness but from full awareness of His authority and identity.

Then Jesus [[v4|arose from supper, and laid aside his outer garments. He took a towel and wrapped a towel around his waist]]. He poured water into a basin and began to wash the disciples' feet. In that culture, people walked in sandals on dusty roads, and feet became dirty. Washing guests' feet was usually done by the lowest household servant; some Jewish teachers said that even a Jewish servant should not be required to do it. Disciples might perform many services for their rabbi, but not this one. Luke records that the disciples had been arguing that same evening about which of them was the greatest (Luke 22:24). No one had volunteered to wash feet.

When Jesus came to Peter, Peter protested. This exchange is studied below. Jesus also said, [[v10|You are clean, but not all of you]], because He knew who would betray Him.

### The Example of the Servant Lord (13:12–20)

After washing their feet, Jesus put His outer garment back on, returned to His place, and asked, [[v12|Do you know what I have done to you?]] He then explained the lesson, studied below.

He added, [[v16|a servant is not greater than his lord, neither is one who is sent greater than he who sent him]]. If the Lord serves, His servants cannot consider themselves too important to serve. Then He gave a promise: [[v17|If you know these things, blessed are you if you do them]]. The blessing comes not from knowing but from doing.

Jesus made it clear that He was not speaking of all of them. He quoted Psalm 41:9: [[v18|He who eats bread with me has lifted up his heel against me]]. In the ancient Near East, sharing a meal was a sign of friendship and trust, so betrayal by a table companion was especially painful. Jesus told them ahead of time [[v19|that when it happens, you may believe that I am he]]. The betrayal would not take Him by surprise; it was part of the Father's plan foretold in Scripture.

### Jesus Foretells His Betrayal (13:21–30)

Jesus was [[v21|troubled in spirit]] and said plainly, [[v21|one of you will betray me]]. The disciples looked at one another, unsure whom He meant. At ancient banquets, guests reclined on couches around a low table, leaning on their left elbows. The disciple whom Jesus loved, traditionally identified as John, the author of the Gospel, was reclining next to Jesus. Peter signaled him to ask who it was.

Jesus answered quietly, [[v26|It is he to whom I will give this piece of bread when I have dipped it]]. Then He dipped the bread and gave it to Judas. Offering a morsel of bread to a guest was a sign of honor and friendship. Even at this moment, Jesus extended love to His betrayer. Judas had washed feet, and he now received bread from Jesus' hand. Yet [[v27|After the piece of bread, then Satan entered into him]]. Judas had repeatedly chosen his path, and now he gave himself completely to it.

Jesus said, [[v27|What you do, do quickly]]. The others did not understand; they thought Jesus might be sending Judas, who kept the money box, to buy something for the feast or to give to the poor. Judas went out. John adds four of the most haunting words in his Gospel: [[v30|It was night]]. Judas left the light of the world and went out into the darkness.

### The New Commandment (13:31–35)

When Judas had left, Jesus said, [[v31|Now the Son of Man has been glorified, and God has been glorified in him]]. The events that would lead to His death had begun, and in them God would be glorified.

He spoke tenderly: [[v33|Little children, I will be with you a little while longer]]. This is the only time in John's Gospel that Jesus calls His disciples "little children," an expression of deep affection. He was about to leave them, and they could not follow Him yet. Then He gave them the new commandment, studied below.

### Peter's Denial Foretold (13:36–38)

Peter asked, [[v36|Lord, where are you going?]] Jesus told him that he could not follow now but would follow later. Peter insisted, [[v37|Lord, why can’t I follow you now? I will lay down my life for you]]. Peter meant it sincerely, but he did not know his own weakness.

Jesus answered, [[v38|Will you lay down your life for me? Most certainly I tell you, the rooster won’t crow until you have denied me three times]]. Peter's failure is studied at Mark 14:72, and his restoration will be told in John 21. Jesus' words "you will follow afterwards" (13:36) also contain a hidden promise: Peter would one day follow Jesus even in death (John 21:18–19).

## Important Verses Explained

### John 13:1 — He Loved Them to the End

verse: John 13:1

#### Simple Explanation

This verse opens the second half of John's Gospel and introduces everything that follows: the foot washing, the farewell teaching, the prayer, the arrest, the crucifixion, and the resurrection. It explains the motive behind it all.

[[v1|Now before the feast of the Passover]]. The setting is the evening before Passover. Passover remembered God's deliverance of Israel from slavery in Egypt, when the blood of a lamb protected each household from judgment. Jesus, the Lamb of God (John 1:29), would die at Passover time.

[[v1|Jesus, knowing that his time had come that he would depart from this world to the Father]]. Throughout the Gospel, Jesus said that His "hour" had not yet come. Now it had. He would "depart from this world to the Father," a gentle way of describing His death, resurrection, and ascension. For Jesus, death meant going home to the Father.

[[v1|having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end]]. "His own" are His disciples, those who belonged to Him. He had loved them throughout His ministry, and now He would love them "to the end." The Greek phrase can mean both "to the end of His life" and "to the fullest extent," completely and perfectly. Both meanings are true. Jesus loved His disciples until His final breath, and He loved them with a love that held nothing back.

Notice that Jesus' thoughts at this moment were not focused on Himself, though He knew He would soon suffer terribly. His focus was on loving His disciples.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse reveals that love is the key to understanding the cross. Jesus' death was not merely an unfortunate event or a political execution. It was the supreme expression of His love. Later in this discourse, He says, [[John 15:13|Greater love has no one than this, that someone lay down his life for his friends]].

It also shows the faithfulness of Christ's love. The disciples were about to fail Him. One would betray Him, one would deny Him three times, and all would scatter. Yet His love for them did not change. Christ's love is not based on the worthiness of the ones He loves; it is rooted in His own character.

Finally, the verse reveals the security of belonging to Christ. Those whom Jesus calls "his own" are loved to the end. His love does not give up halfway. Paul expresses the same confidence when he writes that nothing can separate believers from the love of God in Christ (Romans 8:38–39).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse assures believers that Christ's love for them is complete and lasting. Many people fear that God's love will run out, that their failures will eventually exhaust His patience. Jesus loved His disciples to the end, even knowing how they would fail. Believers can rest in the same steadfast love.

It also shows how Jesus faced suffering. Knowing that His hour had come, He spent His last evening loving others. Believers facing difficult times can follow His example, turning their attention outward in love even when their own burdens are heavy.

Finally, the verse calls believers to love others faithfully. Many relationships end when love becomes costly or disappointing. Jesus' love endured betrayal, denial, and abandonment. His people are called to a love that perseveres, while still using wisdom and seeking safety where there is danger or abuse.

### John 13:8 — If I Don't Wash You

verse: John 13:8

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus had taken the place of a household servant, wrapping a towel around His waist and washing His disciples' dusty feet. When He came to Simon Peter, Peter was shocked: [[v6|Lord, do you wash my feet?]] In Peter's mind, it was entirely wrong for his Lord and Teacher to do such a lowly task.

Jesus answered, [[v7|You don’t know what I am doing now, but you will understand later]]. The act had a deeper meaning that Peter would grasp only after the cross and resurrection.

Peter refused firmly: [[v8|You will never wash my feet!]] His refusal came from a mixture of reverence and pride. He could not accept the idea of Jesus humbling Himself in this way.

Jesus' reply was serious: [[v8|If I don’t wash you, you have no part with me]]. To have "a part" with Jesus means to share in His life, His fellowship, and His inheritance. Jesus was saying that unless Peter allowed Him to serve him in this way, Peter could not belong to Him.

Peter immediately swung to the other extreme: [[v9|Lord, not my feet only, but also my hands and my head!]] Jesus explained that someone who has bathed only needs to wash their feet. A person who had bathed before going to a dinner would get only their feet dirty on the way. In the same way, the disciples were already "clean" through their relationship with Jesus, except for Judas, but they still needed the cleansing that comes through His continued service.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The foot washing points beyond itself to the cross. Jesus laid aside His garments, took the role of a servant, and cleansed His disciples. This mirrors what Paul describes in Philippians 2: Christ, though He existed in the form of God, [[Philippians 2:7|emptied himself, taking the form of a servant]], and humbled Himself to death on a cross. The cleansing that truly makes a person clean is His sacrificial death.

Peter's refusal reveals a common spiritual problem. Many people find it hard to receive grace. Pride prefers to serve rather than be served, to earn rather than receive. But no one can belong to Christ without first allowing Him to cleanse them. Salvation begins with receiving what He has done.

The distinction between a full bath and washing the feet has often been seen as a picture of the difference between the initial cleansing of salvation and the ongoing cleansing of daily confession. Believers who have been made clean through Christ still need to bring their daily sins to Him. John writes in his first letter, [[1 John 1:9|If we confess our sins, he is faithful and righteous to forgive us the sins]].

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites believers to humbly receive Christ's grace. Some people struggle to accept that Jesus would serve and cleanse them. They feel they must first become worthy or do something to deserve it. Jesus insists that He must wash them, and that their part is to receive.

It also encourages believers to come to Christ regularly for cleansing. Daily life leaves the "dust" of sin, failure, and weariness. Believers do not need to be saved again each day, but they do need to bring their sins to Jesus in confession and receive His forgiveness.

Finally, Peter's swing from refusal to excess reminds believers that Jesus knows exactly what they need. Believers do not need to dictate the terms of His grace. They can trust Him to give them what is right.

### John 13:14–15 — Wash One Another's Feet

verse: John 13:14-15

#### Simple Explanation

After washing His disciples' feet, Jesus put His outer garment back on and returned to His place at the table. Then He explained what He had done. He began by affirming His authority: [[v13|You call me, ‘Teacher’ and ‘Lord.’ You say so correctly, for so I am]]. Jesus was not denying His position by serving; He was showing what true authority looks like.

[[v14|If I then, the Lord and the Teacher, have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another’s feet]]. If the One who is Lord and Teacher took the lowest place, then His followers have no excuse for refusing to serve one another. No act of service is beneath them.

[[v15|For I have given you an example, that you should also do as I have done to you]]. The word "example" means a pattern to be copied. Jesus' act was a model of how His disciples should treat one another.

Some Christian traditions practice foot washing literally as a church ordinance, especially on Maundy Thursday, the day before Good Friday. Most Christians understand Jesus' command as calling for humble, practical service of every kind. Either way, the point is the same: followers of Jesus are called to serve one another humbly.

Remember the setting. The disciples had just been arguing about which of them was the greatest (Luke 22:24). None of them had offered to wash feet. Jesus answered their argument not with a lecture but with a towel.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal a new definition of greatness. In the world, greatness means being served. In God's kingdom, greatness means serving. Jesus taught this earlier: [[Mark 10:45|For the Son of Man also came not to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many]]. The foot washing is a living illustration of that truth.

They also show that Christian service flows from what Christ has done. Jesus first washed the disciples' feet, and then told them to wash one another's. Believers serve because they have been served by Christ. Service is not a way to earn His love but a response to it.

Finally, these verses show that humility and authority are not opposites. Jesus served with full awareness of His divine authority (13:3). True humility is not denying one's worth but using one's position to serve others.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses call believers to look for ways to serve, especially in tasks that others avoid. In families, churches, and workplaces, there are always unseen, unglamorous jobs. Following Jesus means being willing to do them, not for recognition but out of love.

They also challenge attitudes of pride and status. Believers can be tempted to think some tasks are beneath them because of their education, position, or experience. Jesus, the Lord of all, washed dirty feet. No act of loving service is beneath His followers.

Finally, these verses encourage leaders to serve. Those with authority in churches, homes, and organizations are called to use their position to serve others rather than to be served. Servant leadership reflects the heart of Christ. It never means tolerating abuse, but it does mean that power exists to bless and protect others.

### John 13:34–35 — Love One Another as I Have Loved You

verse: John 13:34-35

#### Simple Explanation

After Judas left to betray Him, Jesus spoke tenderly to the remaining disciples, calling them "little children." He told them that He would be with them only a little longer, and that where He was going they could not come. Then He gave them a commandment to guide them after He was gone.

[[v34|A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another]]. The command to love was not entirely new; the Law commanded, [[Leviticus 19:18|you shall love your neighbor as yourself]]. What was new?

[[v34|Just as I have loved you, you also love one another]]. The new standard is Jesus' own love. The disciples had just seen that love in action: He had washed their feet, including the feet of the one who would betray Him. Within hours, He would lay down His life for them. The new commandment calls believers to love one another with the same self-giving, humble, sacrificial love.

[[v35|By this everyone will know that you are my disciples, if you have love for one another]]. Jesus made love the identifying mark of His followers. People would recognize His disciples not primarily by their knowledge, their buildings, or their rituals, but by the way they loved one another.

This command focuses on love within the community of believers, "one another." Elsewhere Jesus commands love for neighbors and even enemies. But here He emphasizes that the love among His people will be a witness to the world.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The new commandment is new because it is rooted in a new reality: the love of Christ revealed in His death. The cross sets a standard of love that the world had never seen. John later writes, [[1 John 3:16|By this we know love, because he laid down his life for us. And we ought to lay down our lives for the brothers]].

It also reveals that the church's love is a form of witness. In a divided world, a community of people from different backgrounds who genuinely love one another points to the reality of Christ. Early Christian writers reported that outsiders said of believers, "See how they love one another." Jesus prayed that His followers would be one so that the world would believe (John 17:21).

Finally, the command shows that love is possible only because believers have first been loved. They cannot generate this love on their own. It flows from receiving Christ's love and is produced by His Spirit (Galatians 5:22).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This commandment calls believers to examine how they treat other Christians. It is easy to criticize, compete with, or ignore fellow believers, especially those with different personalities, backgrounds, or opinions. Jesus calls His followers to love one another as He loved them, with patience, forgiveness, and practical care.

It also reminds churches that their love is their witness. Programs, preaching, and buildings matter, but the world will recognize Jesus' disciples by their love. Churches marked by division, gossip, or coldness obscure the gospel. Churches marked by love display it.

Finally, the commandment encourages believers to love in concrete ways. Jesus' love was practical: He washed feet, fed the hungry, touched the sick, and gave His life. Believers can show love by serving, encouraging, forgiving, sharing resources, and standing with one another in hard times.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

John 13 shows the heart of Jesus on the night before His death. He loved His disciples to the end, knowing they would fail Him. Believers can rest in that same faithful love. Their failures do not exhaust Christ's patience. He loves His own completely and to the end.

The chapter also shows that belonging to Christ begins with receiving His cleansing. Peter had to let Jesus wash his feet. Many people find it hard to receive grace, preferring to earn God's favor or to serve Him on their own terms. Jesus calls everyone to humbly accept what He does for them, and to keep coming to Him for cleansing when the dust of daily sin builds up.

John 13 teaches that true greatness is found in serving. The Lord and Teacher took a towel and washed feet. His followers are called to serve one another, especially in humble and unnoticed ways. Leaders in particular are called to follow His example, using their authority to bless and protect rather than to be served.

Finally, the chapter gives the new commandment: to love one another as Jesus has loved us. This love is the mark by which the world will recognize Jesus' disciples. In a world full of division, a community of believers who love one another sacrificially is a powerful testimony to the reality of Christ. Each believer can contribute to that witness through patience, forgiveness, and practical care for others.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for loving Your own to the end, and for loving me with a love that does not give up. You knew the Father had given all things into Your hands, and still You took a towel and washed Your disciples' feet. Humble my pride. Help me to receive Your cleansing, and to come to You each day for forgiveness. Teach me to follow Your example by serving others gladly, even in tasks no one else wants to do. Forgive me for the times I have sought to be great instead of seeking to serve. Fill me with Your love so that I may love my brothers and sisters as You have loved me. Let the love among Your people be so real that the world will know we belong to You. When I am confident in my own strength, as Peter was, keep me humble and dependent on You. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
