# 2 Corinthians 2
theme: Tears, Forgiveness, and the Fragrance of Christ
main: Paul explains that he did not return to Corinth because he did not want another painful visit, and that his severe letter was written with many tears out of deep love, not to cause grief. A man who had caused trouble in the church had been disciplined by the majority and had repented, so Paul urges the church to forgive him, comfort him, and reaffirm their love, so that Satan will not take advantage of them. Paul describes his restlessness when he could not find Titus, and then bursts into thanks to God, who leads His people in Christ's triumphal procession and spreads the fragrance of the knowledge of Christ through them, a fragrance of life to some and death to others. Paul insists that he does not peddle God's word for profit but speaks sincerely before God.

## Chapter Background and Story

2 Corinthians 2 continues Paul's explanation of his relationship with the Corinthians during a difficult period. Paul had made a "painful visit" to Corinth, during which someone in the church apparently opposed or insulted him publicly (2:5; 7:12). Instead of returning, Paul wrote a severe letter, sometimes called the "tearful letter," calling the church to deal with the situation. He then waited anxiously for news through his co-worker Titus.

Some interpreters connect the man who caused grief with the immoral man of 1 Corinthians 5; others believe he was a different person who had personally opposed Paul. Either way, the church had disciplined him, and he had repented. Paul now urges them to restore him.

### A Letter Written with Tears (2:1–4)

[[v1|But I determined this for myself, that I would not come to you again in sorrow]]. Paul chose not to make another painful visit. [[v2|For if I make you grieve, then who will make me glad but he who is made to grieve by me?]] Paul's joy was bound up with theirs. Hurting them meant hurting himself. Paul's description of how he wrote is studied below.

### Forgive and Comfort the Offender (2:5–11)

[[v5|But if any has caused sorrow, he has caused sorrow not to me, but in part (that I not press too heavily) to you all]]. The man's offense had hurt the whole church, not only Paul. Paul does not name him or describe his offense; he protects the man's dignity. [[v6|This punishment which was inflicted by the many is sufficient for such a one]]. The church had disciplined the man, and the discipline had done its work. It was enough. Paul's call to forgiveness, and his warning about Satan, are studied below.

### Anxiety and Triumph (2:12–17)

[[v12|Now when I came to Troas for the Good News of Christ, and when a door was opened to me in the Lord]], [[v13|I had no relief for my spirit, because I didn’t find Titus my brother, but taking my leave of them, I went out into Macedonia]]. Paul had an opportunity to preach in Troas, but he was so anxious to hear how the Corinthians had responded to his letter that he could not rest. He left to find Titus. Even the great apostle felt deep anxiety for the people he loved. (He resumes this story in 7:5–7.)

Then Paul suddenly breaks into praise, studied below. He concludes: [[v17|For we are not as so many, peddling the word of God. But as of sincerity, but as of God, in the sight of God, we speak in Christ]]. The word "peddling" describes a dishonest merchant who waters down wine to make more profit. Some teachers used the word of God for financial gain, altering the message to please audiences. Paul spoke with sincerity, as one sent by God, aware that God was watching.

## Important Verses Explained

### 2 Corinthians 2:4 — Written with Many Tears

verse: 2 Corinthians 2:4

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had written a severe letter to the Corinthians, calling them to deal with someone in the church who had caused serious trouble. Some Corinthians may have thought that Paul wrote it out of anger or a desire to control them. Paul explains his true feelings.

[[v4|For out of much affliction and anguish of heart I wrote to you with many tears]]. Paul wrote with deep distress. "Affliction" means pressure or trouble; "anguish of heart" means intense inner pain. He wrote "with many tears." Writing that letter was painful for him.

[[v4|not that you should be made to grieve]]. Paul's purpose was not to hurt them. He did not enjoy confrontation or causing pain.

[[v4|but that you might know the love that I have so abundantly for you]]. The letter was an expression of love. Paul cared enough about them to confront what was wrong. His love was "abundant," overflowing.

This verse shows that hard words can be loving words. Paul confronted the Corinthians because he loved them, not because he wanted to dominate them. Love sometimes requires difficult conversations.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that true love includes correction. Proverbs says, [[Proverbs 27:6|The wounds of a friend are faithful]]. A friend who never challenges you may not truly care about you. Paul's willingness to confront showed his love.

It also shows the heart of a godly leader. Paul did not confront casually or harshly. He did it with tears, with deep sorrow and love. Godly correction comes from a broken heart, not from pride or anger.

Finally, the verse reflects the heart of God. God disciplines His people because He loves them (Hebrews 12:6). His correction comes from love and aims at their good.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to speak the truth in love when correction is needed. In families, friendships, and churches, love sometimes requires honest, difficult conversations. Believers can approach such conversations with humility, prayer, and genuine care.

It also encourages believers to examine their motives before confronting others. Are they speaking out of love, or out of anger, pride, or a desire to control? Paul's tears show the right spirit.

Finally, the verse encourages believers to receive correction well. When others confront believers lovingly, it may be painful, but it can be a sign of genuine care. Believers can listen with humility.

### 2 Corinthians 2:7–8 — Forgive, Comfort, and Reaffirm Your Love

verse: 2 Corinthians 2:7-8

#### Simple Explanation

A man in the Corinthian church had caused serious harm. The church had disciplined him, and the discipline had been effective; he had repented. Paul said that the punishment had been enough. Now it was time for something else.

[[v7|so that, on the contrary, you should rather forgive him and comfort him]]. The church should stop punishing the man and instead forgive him and comfort him. Discipline is never meant to last forever. Once a person repents, the church should restore them.

[[v7|lest by any means such a one should be swallowed up with his excessive sorrow]]. Paul was concerned that the man might be overwhelmed by grief and guilt. Excessive sorrow could lead to despair, perhaps even to giving up on faith altogether. The church needed to welcome him back before that happened.

[[v8|Therefore I beg you to confirm your love toward him]]. Paul urged them to "confirm" or "reaffirm" their love. The word was used for a formal, public decision. The church should publicly welcome the man back, making clear that he was loved and accepted. Forgiveness should be visible and real.

This shows that the purpose of church discipline is always restoration. Discipline that continues after repentance becomes harsh and unloving.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show the heart of God toward repentant sinners. God does not hold grudges against those who turn to Him. He forgives completely and welcomes them home. The father in Jesus' parable ran to embrace his returning son (Luke 15:20).

They also show that forgiveness includes comfort and love. It is not enough to stop punishing someone. True forgiveness actively restores relationship, offering comfort and affection.

Finally, these verses show the danger of excessive sorrow. Godly sorrow leads to repentance (7:10). But excessive sorrow, which continues after repentance, can lead to despair. Believers need to know that they are forgiven and loved.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to forgive those who repent. When someone who has wronged believers genuinely turns from their sin, believers can forgive them, comfort them, and restore the relationship as far as is wise.

They also encourage churches to restore repentant members. Churches that practice discipline must also practice restoration, welcoming back those who repent with genuine love.

Finally, these verses must be applied with wisdom. Forgiveness does not mean ignoring consequences or placing someone in a position where they could harm others again. In cases of abuse, forgiveness can coexist with boundaries, accountability, and protection of the vulnerable. Forgiveness restores fellowship; it does not require trust to be restored instantly or safeguards to be removed.

### 2 Corinthians 2:10–11 — Not Ignorant of Satan's Schemes

verse: 2 Corinthians 2:10-11

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had urged the Corinthians to forgive the man who had repented. Now he joins in that forgiveness.

[[v10|Now I also forgive whomever you forgive anything]]. Paul stood with the church in forgiving the man. Even though the man had personally offended Paul, Paul was ready to forgive.

[[v10|For if indeed I have forgiven anything, I have forgiven that one for your sakes in the presence of Christ]]. Paul's forgiveness was "for your sakes," for the good of the church, and "in the presence of Christ," sincere before the Lord who sees all things.

[[v11|that no advantage may be gained over us by Satan, for we are not ignorant of his schemes]]. Paul explains why forgiveness matters so much. Satan, the enemy of God's people, looks for opportunities to harm the church. If the church refused to forgive, Satan could use that unforgiveness to cause division, bitterness, and despair. The man might be driven away from the faith, and the church might become harsh and divided.

"Schemes" means plans, plots, or strategies. Satan has many tactics. One of them is to use unforgiveness. Believers who are aware of this can resist it.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that unforgiveness gives Satan an opportunity. Paul writes elsewhere, [[Ephesians 4:26|Don’t let the sun go down on your wrath]], [[Ephesians 4:27|and don’t give place to the devil]]. Bitterness and unforgiveness open doors to the enemy.

They also show that spiritual warfare is real. Satan works to divide churches, discourage believers, and destroy relationships. Believers need to be aware of his schemes, without fear but with wisdom.

Finally, these verses show that forgiveness is a weapon against the enemy. When believers forgive, they defeat Satan's plans to divide and destroy. Forgiveness reflects the grace of God and builds up the church.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to forgive quickly. Holding grudges and nursing bitterness can give the enemy an opportunity to cause harm. Believers can release resentment and choose to forgive.

They also encourage believers to be aware of spiritual realities. When conflicts arise in families or churches, believers can recognize that the enemy may be at work, seeking to divide. They can respond with prayer, humility, and forgiveness.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to stand together. Paul joined the church in forgiving. Unity in forgiveness strengthens the church against the enemy's schemes.

### 2 Corinthians 2:14–16 — The Fragrance of Christ

verse: 2 Corinthians 2:14-16

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been describing his anxiety while waiting for Titus. Suddenly, he breaks into praise.

[[v14|Now thanks be to God who always leads us in triumph in Christ]]. Paul uses the image of a Roman triumphal procession. When a Roman general won a great victory, he paraded through the streets of Rome in a triumphal procession, followed by his soldiers and the captives he had taken. Paul pictures God as the victorious general, leading His people in Christ's triumph. Paul may see himself as one of God's captives, conquered by Christ and now displayed in His victory. Despite all his troubles, Paul is part of Christ's triumphant march.

[[v14|and reveals through us the sweet aroma of his knowledge in every place]]. In triumphal processions, incense was burned, filling the air with fragrance. Paul says that God spreads the fragrance of the knowledge of Christ through His people. Wherever believers go, they carry the fragrance of Christ.

[[v15|For we are a sweet aroma of Christ to God in those who are being saved and in those who perish]]. Believers are a pleasing aroma to God. And this aroma reaches two groups of people: those being saved and those perishing.

[[v16|to the one a stench from death to death, to the other a sweet aroma from life to life]]. To those who reject Christ, the fragrance smells like death. For captives in a Roman procession, the incense meant that they were headed for execution. To those who receive Christ, the fragrance is life-giving. The same message produces opposite responses.

[[v16|Who is sufficient for these things?]] Who is adequate for such an important task? Paul will answer in 3:5–6: their sufficiency comes from God.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that believers share in Christ's victory. Even when believers face hardship, they are part of Christ's triumphal procession. His victory over sin and death is certain, and they share in it.

They also show the influence of believers. Believers carry the fragrance of Christ wherever they go. Their words, actions, and character reveal something of Him. Paul writes elsewhere that believers should walk in love, [[Ephesians 5:2|even as Christ also loved us and gave himself up for us, an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweet-smelling fragrance]].

Finally, these verses show the seriousness of the gospel. The message of Christ is a matter of life and death. People's response to it determines their eternal destiny. This is why Paul asked, "Who is sufficient for these things?"

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to give thanks in all circumstances. Even when believers are anxious or troubled, they can thank God for leading them in Christ's triumph.

They also encourage believers to spread the fragrance of Christ. Believers can ask whether their lives give off the fragrance of Christ's love, grace, and truth in their homes, workplaces, and communities.

Finally, these verses help believers understand different responses to the gospel. Some will be drawn to Christ; others will reject Him. Believers do not need to be discouraged when some respond negatively. They can faithfully share the fragrance of Christ, trusting God with the results.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

2 Corinthians 2 shows that love sometimes requires difficult words. Paul wrote his severe letter with many tears, not to hurt the Corinthians but to show his abundant love. Believers can speak hard truths when necessary, but always with humility, prayer, and genuine love, and they can receive correction from others with the same humility.

The chapter also teaches that the goal of discipline is restoration. Once the offender repented, Paul urged the church to forgive, comfort, and reaffirm their love for him, so that he would not be overwhelmed by sorrow. Believers can forgive those who repent and welcome them back, while using wisdom about boundaries and protecting the vulnerable.

2 Corinthians 2 warns that unforgiveness gives Satan an opportunity. Believers should not be ignorant of his schemes to divide and destroy. Forgiveness defeats those schemes and builds up the church.

Finally, the chapter celebrates that God leads His people in Christ's triumph and spreads the fragrance of Christ through them. Believers carry the aroma of Christ wherever they go. They can live in a way that reveals Christ, share the gospel sincerely without peddling it, and trust God for the results, knowing that their sufficiency comes from Him.
