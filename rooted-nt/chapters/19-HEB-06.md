# Hebrews 6
theme: Press On to Maturity, Anchored in Hope
main: The author urges his readers to move beyond the elementary teachings about Christ, such as repentance, faith, baptisms, laying on of hands, resurrection, and judgment, and press on to maturity. He then gives a sobering warning: those who have been enlightened, tasted the heavenly gift, shared in the Holy Spirit, and tasted God's word and the powers of the coming age, and then fall away, cannot be brought back to repentance, since they are crucifying the Son of God all over again and holding Him up to contempt. Like land that receives rain, those who produce good fruit receive God's blessing, while those who produce only thorns face judgment. Yet the author is confident of better things for his readers, because God will not forget their work and love in serving His people. He urges them to show the same diligence to the end and to imitate those who inherit the promises through faith and patience, like Abraham, to whom God made a promise and confirmed it with an oath. By these two unchangeable things, God's promise and His oath, believers who have fled to Him for refuge have strong encouragement to hold fast to the hope set before them, a hope that is a sure and steady anchor for the soul, entering behind the curtain where Jesus has gone ahead as forerunner, a high priest forever.

## Chapter Background and Story

Hebrews 6 continues the warning that began in Hebrews 5:11. The author had been about to explain Jesus' priesthood in the order of Melchizedek, but he paused because his readers had become spiritually sluggish. Now he urges them to grow up and warns them of the danger of falling away.

Verses 4–6 contain one of the most debated passages in the New Testament. Sincere Christians have understood it in several ways, and the commentary below presents those views fairly. It is important to read the whole chapter together. The author's purpose was not to make his readers doubt their salvation but to shake them out of spiritual complacency and then to reassure them. The warning is followed immediately by words of confidence (v9) and by one of the most beautiful passages about Christian hope in Scripture (vv13–20).

### Moving Beyond the Basics (6:1–3)

The call to press on to maturity is studied below. [[v3|This will we do, if God permits]]. Growth depends on God's enabling. The author intends to move forward in his teaching, trusting God to help.

### The Danger of Falling Away (6:4–8)

The warning is studied below. [[v7|For the land which has drunk the rain that comes often on it and produces a crop suitable for them for whose sake it is also tilled, receives blessing from God]]; [[v8|but if it bears thorns and thistles, it is rejected and near being cursed, whose end is to be burned]]. The author uses an agricultural image. Land that receives abundant rain should produce a good crop. If it produces only thorns, it is useless. The image echoes Isaiah 5:1–7, where God's vineyard produced wild grapes, and Jesus' parable of the sower (Matthew 13:3–9). The rain represents God's blessings; the crop represents the fruit of a life that has received them.

### Confidence of Better Things (6:9–12)

[[v9|But, beloved, we are persuaded of better things for you, and things that accompany salvation, even though we speak like this]]. After the stern warning, the author speaks tenderly. He calls them "beloved" and expresses confidence that their lives show signs of genuine salvation. The warning was meant to shake them, not to condemn them. God's faithfulness to remember their love is studied below.

[[v11|We desire that each one of you may show the same diligence to the fullness of hope even to the end]], [[v12|that you won’t be sluggish, but imitators of those who through faith and perseverance inherited the promises]]. The author wants every one of them to keep going with the same zeal they once showed. "Sluggish" is the same word translated "dull" in Hebrews 5:11. The remedy for spiritual sluggishness is to imitate those who have persevered in faith and patience, a theme that will be developed in chapter 11.

### God's Unchangeable Promise (6:13–20)

[[v13|For when God made a promise to Abraham, since he could swear by no one greater, he swore by himself]], [[v14|saying, “Surely blessing I will bless you, and multiplying I will multiply you.”]] After Abraham was willing to offer Isaac, God confirmed His promise with an oath (Genesis 22:16–17). Since there is no one greater than God, He swore by Himself.

[[v15|Thus, having patiently endured, he obtained the promise]]. Abraham waited many years for the promise to be fulfilled. He is an example of faith and patience (v12).

[[v16|For men indeed swear by a greater one, and in every dispute of theirs the oath is final for confirmation]]. Among humans, an oath settles disputes and confirms commitments. God accommodated Himself to human practice to give His people full assurance. The meaning of God's promise and oath, and the anchor of hope, are studied below.

[[v20|where as a forerunner Jesus entered for us, having become a high priest forever after the order of Melchizedek]]. Jesus has gone ahead as a "forerunner," a scout who goes before others to prepare the way. He entered God's presence on behalf of His people. This brings the author back to Melchizedek, the subject of chapter 7.

## Important Verses Explained

### Hebrews 6:1–2 — Press On to Maturity

verse: Hebrews 6:1-2

#### Simple Explanation

"Therefore leaving the teaching of the first principles of Christ, let’s press on to perfection." The author does not mean abandoning the basics of the faith, but building on them. A builder does not keep laying the foundation; once it is laid, he builds the walls. "Perfection" here means maturity, spiritual adulthood.

"Not laying again a foundation." The author lists six foundational teachings, in three pairs.

"Of repentance from dead works, of faith toward God." The starting point of the Christian life: turning from sinful or useless works that lead to death, and trusting God.

"Of the teaching of baptisms, of laying on of hands." The word "baptisms" is plural. It may refer to instruction about the difference between Christian baptism and other ritual washings practiced in Judaism, or to baptism in water and in the Spirit. "Laying on of hands" was used in the early church for blessing, healing, commissioning for ministry, and receiving the Spirit (Acts 8:17; 13:3; 1 Timothy 4:14).

"Of resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment." The future hope of believers: the resurrection of the body and God's final judgment.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These six teachings were basic truths that new believers learned, many of which had roots in Judaism. Jewish believers would have been familiar with repentance, faith in God, ritual washings, laying on of hands, resurrection, and judgment. What made Christian teaching distinctive was how these truths were fulfilled and transformed in Christ.

The danger the author faced was that his readers might cling to these basic teachings, perhaps understood in a way that did not require full commitment to Christ, and never move on to understand the deeper truths about Jesus as high priest and mediator of the new covenant. Moving forward meant growing in understanding of Christ's work.

The foundation is essential, but it is not the whole building. Believers need to keep growing in their understanding of Christ, in their obedience, and in their love.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can ask whether they have moved beyond the basics of the faith. This is not about abandoning foundational truths but about building on them: deepening understanding of Scripture, growing in character, and maturing in service.

Practical steps toward maturity might include studying Scripture more deeply, learning about the whole story of the Bible, reading books that explain Christian teaching, participating in serious discussion with other believers, and applying God's word to every area of life.

This passage also encourages patience with new believers, who need the foundation laid well. But for those who have walked with Christ for years, the call is to press on.

### Hebrews 6:4–6 — The Warning Against Falling Away

verse: Hebrews 6:4-6

#### Simple Explanation

The author describes people who have experienced remarkable spiritual blessings. They were "once enlightened": they had come to understand the gospel. They "tasted of the heavenly gift": they had experienced God's grace. They "were made partakers of the Holy Spirit": they had shared in the Spirit's work. They "tasted the good word of God and the powers of the age to come": they had experienced the truth of Scripture and the miraculous signs that accompanied the gospel (Hebrews 2:4).

"And then fell away." These people turned away from Christ, deliberately abandoning the faith.

"It is impossible to renew them again to repentance." The author says that, having rejected Christ after such experiences, they cannot be brought back to repentance.

"Seeing they crucify the Son of God for themselves again, and put him to open shame." By rejecting Christ after knowing Him, they join those who crucified Him, publicly treating Him with contempt.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Christians have understood this passage in several ways.

Some, often in the Arminian or Wesleyan tradition, believe the passage describes genuine believers who can truly fall away from salvation through deliberate, persistent rejection of Christ. They see the warning as describing a real possibility.

Others, often in the Reformed tradition, believe the passage describes people who were closely associated with the Christian community and experienced many blessings of the gospel, but who never truly trusted Christ. Like Judas, they tasted but did not truly receive. Their falling away reveals that their faith was never genuine (1 John 2:19).

Others believe the passage describes a hypothetical situation. They understand the author to be saying, in effect, "If a true believer were to fall away, there would be no other way back, because Christ's sacrifice is the only one." The warning functions to keep believers from even moving in that direction.

Still others note that the verb tense in verse 6 suggests an ongoing action, "while they keep on crucifying the Son of God." On this reading, repentance is impossible as long as they continue in deliberate rejection of Christ.

All of these views agree on several important points. The passage describes a deliberate, settled rejection of Christ, not the ordinary struggles, doubts, and failures of believers. It is a serious warning meant to keep believers from drifting. And there is no other sacrifice for sin apart from Christ (Hebrews 10:26). If someone rejects Him, there is nowhere else to go.

It is also important to say this: people who are worried that they have committed this sin, who long to return to God and grieve over their failures, are showing the very opposite of the hardened rejection the passage describes. The desire to repent is itself a sign of God's grace at work. Peter denied Jesus three times and was fully restored (John 21:15–19). Anyone who wants to come to Christ will be welcomed (John 6:37).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage calls believers to take their faith seriously. Spiritual blessings, church involvement, and religious experiences are not the same as continuing faith in Christ. Believers are called to hold fast to Him.

At the same time, this passage should not produce paralyzing fear in believers who struggle with sin or doubt. The author himself goes on to express confidence in his readers (v9). The warning is meant to wake people up, not to crush tender consciences.

Those who are anxious that they have fallen beyond God's reach can bring that anxiety to God and to trusted believers. The very desire to return is a sign that God is drawing them. His invitation stands: [[Hebrews 4:16|Draw near with boldness to the throne of grace]].

### Hebrews 6:10 — God Will Not Forget Your Work

verse: Hebrews 6:10

#### Simple Explanation

After the warning, the author encourages his readers. "For God is not unrighteous, so as to forget your work and the labor of love which you showed toward his name."

God is just. He will not overlook the good the readers have done. Their "work" and "labor of love" were done "toward his name," that is, for God's sake and for His glory.

"In that you served the saints, and still do serve them." Their love was expressed in practical service to fellow believers. Hebrews 10:33–34 describes how they had stood with those who were persecuted and had joyfully accepted the loss of their property. And they were still serving.

This service was evidence of genuine faith. It was one of the "things that accompany salvation" (v9).

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that God notices and values acts of love. Jesus said that even a cup of cold water given to one of His followers will not go unrewarded (Matthew 10:42). In the parable of the sheep and the goats, He said that whatever is done for the least of His brothers and sisters is done for Him (Matthew 25:40).

The phrase "toward his name" is important. Service to fellow believers is service to God. Love for God is shown in love for His people.

This verse also offers assurance. The readers' practical love was a sign that God was at work in them. Genuine faith produces love, and love produces service. When believers see such fruit in their lives, they can be encouraged that their faith is real.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers who serve in quiet, unnoticed ways, caring for the sick, helping the poor, encouraging the discouraged, supporting fellow believers, can take comfort that God sees and will not forget.

This verse also encourages believers to keep serving. The readers had served in the past and were still serving. Faithful, ongoing love is a mark of genuine faith.

Finally, believers can see service to others as service to God's name. Every act of love done for His people honors Him.

### Hebrews 6:18–19 — An Anchor for the Soul

verse: Hebrews 6:18-19

#### Simple Explanation

"That by two immutable things, in which it is impossible for God to lie." The "two immutable things" are God's promise and His oath. Both are unchangeable. Since it is impossible for God to lie, His promise and His oath are absolutely certain.

"We may have a strong encouragement, who have fled for refuge to take hold of the hope set before us." Believers are described as those who have "fled for refuge." This may recall the cities of refuge in the Old Testament, where someone who had accidentally killed another person could flee for safety (Numbers 35:9–15). Believers have fled to God for safety, and they hold onto the hope He has set before them.

Verse 19: "This hope we have as an anchor of the soul, a hope both sure and steadfast." An anchor keeps a ship from drifting in a storm. Christian hope is like that anchor. It is "sure," reliable and secure, and "steadfast," firm and unshakable. It keeps the soul steady when life is turbulent.

"And entering into that which is within the veil." In the temple, a thick curtain, or veil, separated the Most Holy Place, where God's presence dwelt, from the rest of the sanctuary. Only the high priest could enter, once a year. The anchor of hope is fixed not in the sea floor but in heaven itself, in God's very presence.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage connects back to the warning about drifting (Hebrews 2:1). The remedy for drifting is an anchor. Believers who are tempted to drift away can hold fast to the hope that is anchored in God's presence.

The image is striking. Ordinarily, an anchor is dropped down into the depths. Christian hope is anchored upward, in heaven, where Jesus has entered as forerunner (v20). Because Jesus is there, believers' hope is secure. Their future is tied to Him.

The certainty of God's promise is the foundation of this hope. Christian hope is not wishful thinking but confident expectation based on the character of God, who cannot lie. Titus 1:2 speaks of [[Titus 1:2|hope of eternal life, which God, who can't lie, promised before time began]].

The early Christians loved this image. The anchor, often shaped to suggest a cross, was one of the earliest Christian symbols, found in the catacombs of Rome.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

When life feels stormy, through illness, loss, uncertainty, or fear, believers can hold onto the anchor of hope. Their hope is not in circumstances but in God's unchangeable promise and in Jesus, who has gone ahead into God's presence.

This hope can steady believers who are tempted to drift spiritually. Remembering God's promises and Christ's presence in heaven can renew their commitment.

Believers can also reflect on what they are anchoring their lives to. Many people anchor their hope in wealth, relationships, health, or success, all of which can shift. Only hope anchored in God's presence is truly sure and steadfast.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Hebrews 6 calls believers to keep growing. The foundational truths of the faith, repentance, faith, baptism, laying on of hands, resurrection, and judgment, are essential, but they are a foundation to build on. Believers are called to press on to maturity, growing in their understanding of Christ and in their obedience to Him.

The chapter contains a serious warning about falling away. Christians have understood it in different ways, but all agree that it describes a deliberate, settled rejection of Christ, and that there is no other way of salvation apart from Him. The warning is meant to wake up the complacent, not to crush the tender-hearted. Those who long to return to God and grieve over their sins are showing the very sign of grace that the passage's subjects lacked.

The chapter then turns to encouragement. God will not forget His people's work and love in serving others. Their practical love is evidence of genuine faith. Believers can keep serving, knowing that God sees, and they can imitate those who have inherited God's promises through faith and patience, like Abraham.

Finally, the chapter offers a beautiful image of hope. God's promise and His oath are unchangeable, and He cannot lie. Believers who have fled to Him for refuge have a hope that is an anchor for the soul, sure and steadfast, fixed in God's very presence, where Jesus has gone ahead as forerunner and high priest. Whatever storms they face, they are anchored in Him.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord, help me to press on to maturity, building on the foundation of faith You have laid in my life. Keep me from spiritual sluggishness and from drifting away from You. Guard my heart against any hardening, and keep me faithful to Christ. Where I have been afraid that I have gone too far from You, remind me that You welcome all who come to You, and that the desire to return is Your grace at work in me. Thank You that You do not forget the love and service of Your people. Help me to keep serving with diligence until the end. Thank You for Your unchangeable promise and oath, and that it is impossible for You to lie. Thank You for the hope that anchors my soul, sure and steadfast, fixed in Your presence where Jesus has gone ahead of me. Hold me fast. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
