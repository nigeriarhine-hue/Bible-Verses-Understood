# 1 Corinthians 13
theme: The Most Excellent Way: Love That Never Fails
main: In the middle of his teaching on spiritual gifts, Paul shows the Corinthians "a most excellent way." Without love, even the most impressive gifts, knowledge, faith, and sacrifice are worthless. Paul then describes love: patient, kind, free from envy and pride, not rude or self-seeking, not easily angered or keeping a record of wrongs, delighting in truth, and always protecting, trusting, hoping, and persevering. Spiritual gifts are temporary and partial, suited to this present age, but love never fails. Now believers see as in a dim mirror, but one day they will see face to face. Faith, hope, and love remain, and the greatest of these is love.

## Chapter Background and Story

1 Corinthians 13 is one of the most famous passages in the Bible. It is often read at weddings, and its words are frequently quoted as a beautiful poem about love. But it was written in a specific context: a church divided by pride over spiritual gifts. The Corinthians were competing over who had the most impressive gifts, especially speaking in tongues. They were knowledgeable and gifted, but they were not loving.

Paul places this chapter between chapter 12, which teaches that all gifts come from one Spirit for the good of one body, and chapter 14, which gives practical instructions for using gifts in worship. Chapter 13 is the heart of the argument. It shows that love is the goal of every gift and the test of every believer's spirituality.

The Greek word Paul uses for love throughout this chapter is agape. In Christian usage, agape describes the self-giving love of God, a love that seeks the good of others regardless of their worthiness. It is the love God showed in sending His Son (John 3:16; Romans 5:8) and the love believers are called to show one another (John 13:34).

The chapter has three movements: the necessity of love (13:1–3), the character of love (13:4–7), and the permanence of love (13:8–13). Each is studied below.

### The Necessity of Love (13:1–3)

Paul lists the gifts and actions the Corinthians most admired and shows that without love, they are worthless.

### The Character of Love (13:4–7)

Paul describes love not by defining it abstractly but by describing what it does and does not do. Many readers have noticed that this description perfectly fits Jesus Christ. Reading the passage with "Jesus" in place of "love" shows a portrait of Him: Jesus is patient and kind, does not envy or boast, and so on. It is also a mirror for believers, showing how far they have yet to grow.

Several of the qualities Paul lists directly address the Corinthians' problems. They were envious and proud (3:3; 4:6), rude and self-seeking at the Lord's Supper (11:21), quick to take one another to court (6:1–8), and tolerant of sin (5:1–2). Love would heal all these problems.

### The Permanence of Love (13:8–13)

[[v8|Love never fails]]. The word "fails" means falls, collapses, or comes to an end. Love never ends. In contrast, spiritual gifts are temporary, as studied below.

[[v11|When I was a child, I spoke as a child, I felt as a child, I thought as a child. Now that I have become a man, I have put away childish things]]. Paul compares the present age to childhood and the coming age to adulthood. Spiritual gifts are suited to the "childhood" of the church in this present age. When maturity comes, they will no longer be needed. There may also be a gentle rebuke here: the Corinthians' obsession with gifts and status was childish (3:1).

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 Corinthians 13:1–3 — Without Love, Nothing

verse: 1 Corinthians 13:1-3

#### Simple Explanation

The Corinthians were proud of their spiritual gifts. Some spoke in tongues, some prophesied, some had great knowledge or faith, and some were generous. They measured spirituality by these gifts. Paul shows them that something is far more important.

[[v1|If I speak with the languages of men and of angels, but don’t have love, I have become sounding brass or a clanging cymbal]]. Speaking in tongues was the gift the Corinthians prized most. Paul says that even if he could speak every human language and even the language of angels, without love it would be mere noise. "Sounding brass" and "clanging cymbal" were instruments used in pagan worship, making loud but meaningless sounds. Corinth was famous for its bronze work. Without love, even the most spiritual speech is empty noise.

[[v2|If I have the gift of prophecy, and know all mysteries and all knowledge, and if I have all faith, so as to remove mountains, but don’t have love, I am nothing]]. Prophecy, understanding of deep truths, knowledge, and mountain-moving faith are all valuable gifts. Jesus Himself spoke of faith that could move mountains (Matthew 17:20). Yet without love, a person with all these gifts is "nothing."

[[v3|If I give away all my goods to feed the poor, and if I give my body to be burned, but don’t have love, it profits me nothing]]. Even the most extreme acts of generosity and sacrifice, giving away everything to the poor or even dying as a martyr, gain nothing without love. (Some manuscripts read "that I may boast" instead of "to be burned.") It is possible to do impressive things for wrong reasons, such as pride, guilt, or a desire for recognition.

Paul uses the word "I" throughout. He is not pointing fingers at others; he is applying the principle to himself.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that love is the essential quality of the Christian life. Gifts, knowledge, and even sacrifice are worthless without it. Jesus taught that the greatest commandments are to love God and love one's neighbor (Matthew 22:37–40). Everything else depends on these.

They also show that God looks at motives. Two people may do the same outward action, but if one does it out of love and the other out of pride, God sees the difference. Jesus warned against doing good deeds to be seen by others (Matthew 6:1–4).

Finally, these verses show that spiritual gifts are not a measure of spiritual maturity. The Corinthians had many gifts but little love. True maturity is shown in love.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses challenge believers to examine their motives. Believers can ask why they serve, give, teach, or use their gifts. Is it out of love for God and others, or out of a desire for recognition, approval, or control?

They also encourage believers to value love above impressive abilities. Churches sometimes admire gifted speakers, talented musicians, or successful leaders without asking whether they are loving. Love is what matters most.

Finally, these verses encourage believers who feel they have few gifts. Every believer can love. Love is available to all, and it is what gives meaning to everything else.

### 1 Corinthians 13:4–7 — What Love Is and Does

verse: 1 Corinthians 13:4-7

#### Simple Explanation

Paul now describes the character of love. He lists fifteen qualities, some positive and some negative.

[[v4|Love is patient and is kind]]. "Patient" means long-suffering, slow to anger, enduring people's faults without giving up on them. "Kind" means actively doing good to others. Patience endures; kindness acts.

[[v4|Love doesn’t envy]]. Love does not resent others' gifts, success, or blessings. It rejoices when others are blessed.

[[v4|Love doesn’t brag, is not proud]]. Love does not boast about itself or inflate its own importance. The Corinthians were "puffed up" (4:6, 18; 5:2; 8:1). Love is humble.

[[v5|doesn’t behave itself inappropriately]]. Love is not rude. It respects others and behaves with courtesy and consideration.

[[v5|doesn’t seek its own way]]. Love is not self-seeking. It considers the needs and interests of others.

[[v5|is not provoked]]. Love is not easily angered or irritated. It does not have a short temper.

[[v5|takes no account of evil]]. Love does not keep a record of wrongs. It does not store up grievances to bring up later. This is the language of forgiveness.

[[v6|doesn’t rejoice in unrighteousness, but rejoices with the truth]]. Love does not take pleasure in wrongdoing, including the failures of others. It celebrates what is true and right. Love and truth go together.

[[v7|bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, and endures all things]]. "Bears all things" can mean "covers" or "protects," like a roof that shelters. Love protects others and does not expose their faults unnecessarily. "Believes all things" means that love is not cynical; it is ready to believe the best. "Hopes all things" means love does not give up hope for others. "Endures all things" means love perseveres through difficulty.

It is important to understand verse 7 rightly. "Bears all things" and "believes all things" do not mean that love tolerates abuse, ignores danger, or believes lies. Love "rejoices with the truth" (13:6) and "doesn't rejoice in unrighteousness." Genuine love protects the vulnerable and confronts evil. Love may require setting boundaries, seeking safety, and holding wrongdoers accountable. Love for an abuser may include refusing to enable their sin. These verses describe love's generous, hopeful character, not a demand that anyone remain in harm's way.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses describe the love of God shown in Jesus Christ. Jesus was patient with His slow-learning disciples, kind to sinners, free from envy and pride, never rude or self-seeking, slow to anger, forgiving, rejoicing in truth, and enduring the cross. John writes, [[1 John 4:8|He who doesn’t love doesn’t know God, for God is love]].

They also show that love is active, not merely a feeling. Paul describes love by its actions and attitudes. Love is something people do, choices they make each day.

Finally, these verses show that love is the fruit of the Spirit. No one can love like this by their own strength. Paul writes, [[Galatians 5:22|But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faith]]. The Holy Spirit produces this love in believers as they walk with Him.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses give believers a mirror for their relationships. Believers can read the list slowly and ask where they need to grow: in patience with their family, in kindness toward coworkers, in freedom from envy, in forgiveness, in hope for difficult people.

They also give a description of what to look for in relationships. Marriages, friendships, and church communities can be measured by these qualities. Where love is present, these qualities will be growing.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to ask God for His love. When believers recognize how far they fall short, they can ask the Holy Spirit to fill them with Christ's love and to produce this fruit in their lives.

### 1 Corinthians 13:8–10 — Love Never Fails

verse: 1 Corinthians 13:8-10

#### Simple Explanation

Having described the character of love, Paul now shows its permanence. The Corinthians valued spiritual gifts highly. Paul shows that gifts are temporary, but love is eternal.

[[v8|Love never fails]]. Love will never end. It will continue forever, into eternity.

[[v8|But where there are prophecies, they will be done away with. Where there are various languages, they will cease. Where there is knowledge, it will be done away with]]. In contrast, the gifts the Corinthians prized will come to an end. Prophecy, tongues, and knowledge are valuable now, but they are temporary.

[[v9|For we know in part and we prophesy in part]]. Present knowledge and prophecy are incomplete. They give believers a partial understanding of God's truth.

[[v10|but when that which is complete has come, then that which is partial will be done away with]]. When "that which is complete" comes, the partial gifts will no longer be needed.

What is "that which is complete" (sometimes translated "the perfect")? Most interpreters understand it as the return of Christ and the fullness of God's kingdom, when believers will see God face to face (13:12). Some, especially those who believe certain gifts have ceased, understand it as the completion of the New Testament or the maturity of the church. Paul's description in verse 12 of seeing "face to face" and knowing fully seems to point most naturally to the age to come. Either way, the main point is clear: gifts are temporary, but love is eternal.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that love belongs to eternity. In heaven, there will be no need for prophecy, since believers will see God directly; no need for tongues, since all will understand; and no need for partial knowledge, since believers will know fully. But love will continue forever, because God is love.

They also show the humility believers should have about their knowledge. All present knowledge is partial. Even the most learned theologian knows only in part. This should keep believers humble and open to learning.

Finally, these verses show why love should be the priority. If gifts are temporary and love is eternal, believers should invest most in what will last forever.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to prioritize love. Gifts, knowledge, and accomplishments will pass away, but love lasts forever. Believers can invest their time and energy in loving God and others.

They also encourage humility. All believers know only in part. They can hold their opinions with humility, listen to others, and avoid arrogance about their understanding.

Finally, these verses give hope. The partial and imperfect will one day give way to the complete and perfect. Believers can look forward to that day with joy.

### 1 Corinthians 13:12–13 — Face to Face; the Greatest Is Love

verse: 1 Corinthians 13:12-13

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had compared the present age to childhood and the coming age to adulthood. Now he uses another image.

[[v12|For now we see in a mirror, dimly, but then face to face]]. Ancient mirrors were made of polished bronze, a product for which Corinth was famous. Even the best mirrors gave a dim, imperfect reflection. In this life, believers know God, but only partially, as if looking at a dim reflection. But one day, they will see Him "face to face," directly and clearly. This recalls God's words about Moses, with whom He spoke "mouth to mouth" and not in riddles (Numbers 12:8).

[[v12|Now I know in part, but then I will know fully, even as I was also fully known]]. Now believers know God partially. In the future, they will know Him fully, just as God already knows them fully. God knows His people completely, every thought, weakness, and need, and He loves them. One day, they will know Him with similar clarity.

[[v13|But now faith, hope, and love remain—these three]]. Paul names three virtues that last. "Faith" is trust in God. "Hope" is confident expectation of God's promises. "Love" is self-giving devotion to God and others. These three appear together often in Paul's letters (1 Thessalonians 1:3; 5:8; Colossians 1:4–5).

[[v13|The greatest of these is love]]. Why is love greatest? Faith will one day become sight, and hope will be fulfilled, but love will continue forever. And love is the very nature of God Himself. God does not have faith or hope, but God is love (1 John 4:8).

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show the glorious future of believers. One day, they will see God face to face. John writes, [[1 John 3:2|we know that when he is revealed, we will be like him; for we will see him just as he is]]. The deepest longing of the human heart will be satisfied.

They also show that God already knows His people fully. There is nothing hidden from Him, and yet He loves them. This is a source of deep security. Believers do not need to hide or pretend before God.

Finally, these verses show that love is the greatest virtue because it reflects God's nature and lasts forever. Everything else serves love. Gifts, knowledge, faith, and hope all find their purpose in love.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers who struggle with unanswered questions. In this life, believers see dimly. They will not understand everything. But one day, they will know fully. Believers can trust God with what they do not yet understand.

They also bring comfort to believers who feel unknown or misunderstood. God knows them fully and loves them completely. They can rest in His knowledge and love.

Finally, these verses call believers to make love their highest priority. Faith and hope are essential, but love is greatest. Believers can ask each day how they can grow in love for God and for others.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

1 Corinthians 13 teaches that without love, even the most impressive gifts and sacrifices are worthless. Believers can examine their motives, asking whether their service, giving, and use of gifts flow from love or from pride and the desire for recognition. Every believer, whatever their gifts, can love.

The chapter also gives a beautiful and searching description of love. Love is patient and kind, free from envy, pride, rudeness, and selfishness, slow to anger, forgiving, rejoicing in truth, protecting, trusting, hoping, and persevering. This is a portrait of Jesus and a mirror for believers. Believers can read it slowly, identify where they need to grow, and ask the Holy Spirit to produce this love in them. True love always rejoices in truth and never requires anyone to remain in abuse; it protects the vulnerable.

1 Corinthians 13 teaches that spiritual gifts are temporary, but love never fails. All present knowledge is partial; one day believers will see God face to face and know Him fully, just as He knows them. This truth encourages humility about what believers know now, and hope for what they will know then.

Finally, the chapter declares that faith, hope, and love remain, and the greatest of these is love. Love reflects the very nature of God, who is love. Believers can make love their highest aim, in their families, friendships, churches, and communities, investing in what will last forever.

## Chapter Prayer

Loving God, You are love, and You have shown Your love to me in Jesus Christ. Forgive me for the times I have valued gifts, knowledge, or recognition more than love. Without love, I am nothing. Fill me with Your love by Your Holy Spirit. Make me patient and kind. Free me from envy, boasting, and pride. Keep me from rudeness and selfishness, from a quick temper and a record of wrongs. Help me to rejoice in the truth, to protect, to trust, to hope, and to persevere, as Jesus did. Thank You that You know me fully and love me completely. I long for the day when I will see You face to face. Until then, help me to live by faith, hope, and love, and above all, by love. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
