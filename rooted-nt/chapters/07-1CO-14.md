# 1 Corinthians 14
theme: Gifts That Build Up the Church in Orderly Worship
main: Paul urges the Corinthians to pursue love and to desire spiritual gifts, especially prophecy, because prophecy speaks understandable words that build up, encourage, and comfort the church, while uninterpreted tongues benefit only the speaker. He compares unclear speech to an instrument that gives an uncertain sound and calls believers to pray and sing with both the spirit and the mind. Worship should help outsiders recognize that God is truly present. Paul gives practical guidelines: everything should be done to build up the church, speakers should take turns, tongues should be interpreted, prophecies should be weighed, and order should prevail, because God is not a God of confusion but of peace.

## Chapter Background and Story

1 Corinthians 14 is the practical conclusion of Paul's teaching on spiritual gifts. In chapter 12, he showed that all gifts come from one Spirit for the good of one body. In chapter 13, he showed that love is greater than any gift. Now he applies these principles to the church's worship gatherings.

The specific problem was that the Corinthians were overvaluing speaking in tongues. Their gatherings had apparently become chaotic, with many people speaking in tongues at once, without interpretation, so that no one could understand. Visitors would think the believers were out of their minds. Paul does not forbid tongues; he values them and speaks in tongues himself (14:18). But he insists that public worship must be understandable and must build up the whole church.

The guiding principle of the chapter is "edification," building up. Paul uses this word and its related forms repeatedly (14:3, 4, 5, 12, 17, 26). The question for every activity in worship is: Does this build up the church?

As noted in chapter 12, Christians hold different views on whether the gifts of tongues and prophecy continue today and how they should be practiced. Whatever one's view, the principles of this chapter, love, understanding, edification, and order, apply to all worship.

### Prophecy and Tongues (14:1–5)

[[v1|Follow after love and earnestly desire spiritual gifts, but especially that you may prophesy]]. Love comes first, but believers should also desire gifts, especially prophecy. The comparison between prophecy and tongues is studied below.

[[v5|Now I desire to have you all speak with other languages, but even more that you would prophesy. For he is greater who prophesies than he who speaks with other languages, unless he interprets, that the assembly may be built up]]. Paul values tongues, but in public worship, prophecy is more useful, unless tongues are interpreted.

### The Need for Understanding (14:6–19)

Paul uses illustrations. [[v7|Even lifeless things that make a sound, whether pipe or harp, if they didn’t give a distinction in the sounds, how would it be known what is piped or harped?]] Music needs distinct notes to make sense. [[v8|For if the trumpet gave an uncertain sound, who would prepare himself for war?]] Soldiers needed clear trumpet calls to know whether to advance or retreat. [[v9|So also you, unless you uttered by the tongue words easy to understand, how would it be known what is spoken? For you would be speaking into the air]].

[[v11|If then I don’t know the meaning of the language, I would be to him who speaks a foreigner, and he who speaks would be a foreigner to me]]. Speaking in an unknown language creates distance rather than fellowship.

[[v12|So also you, since you are zealous for spiritual gifts, seek that you may abound to the building up of the assembly]]. The Corinthians were eager for gifts. Paul redirects that eagerness toward building up the church.

[[v13|Therefore let him who speaks in another language pray that he may interpret]]. Paul's teaching on praying with spirit and mind is studied below.

[[v18|I thank my God, I speak with other languages more than you all]]. Paul was not against tongues. He spoke in tongues more than any of them. [[v19|However, in the assembly I would rather speak five words with my understanding, that I might instruct others also, than ten thousand words in another language]]. In the gathered church, a few words that others can understand are worth more than thousands they cannot.

### Signs for Believers and Unbelievers (14:20–25)

[[v20|Brothers, don’t be children in thoughts, yet in malice be babies, but in thoughts be mature]]. The Corinthians' fascination with tongues was a sign of immaturity. They should be innocent of evil but mature in their thinking.

Paul quotes Isaiah 28:11–12, where God warned that He would speak to unbelieving Israel through foreign invaders whose language they could not understand, as a sign of judgment. [[v22|Therefore other languages are for a sign, not to those who believe, but to the unbelieving; but prophesying is for a sign, not to the unbelieving, but to those who believe]]. Uninterpreted tongues can be a sign of judgment to unbelievers, leaving them confused and outside. [[v23|If therefore the whole assembly is assembled together and all speak with other languages, and unlearned or unbelieving people come in, won’t they say that you are crazy?]]

But prophecy has the opposite effect: [[v24|But if all prophesy, and someone unbelieving or unlearned comes in, he is reproved by all, and he is judged by all]]. [[v25|And thus the secrets of his heart are revealed. So he will fall down on his face and worship God, declaring that God is among you indeed]]. Clear, Spirit-given words can convict the heart of an unbeliever and lead them to worship. This is one of the great goals of worship: that outsiders would recognize that God is truly present.

### Orderly Worship (14:26–40)

Paul's guiding principle is studied below. He gives specific guidelines for tongues: [[v27|If any man speaks in another language, let there be two, or at the most three, and in turn; and let one interpret]]. [[v28|But if there is no interpreter, let him keep silent in the assembly, and let him speak to himself and to God]].

For prophecy: [[v29|Let two or three of the prophets speak, and let the others discern]]. Prophecies must be evaluated. Not every claimed message from God is accepted automatically; it must be tested by Scripture and by the discernment of the church (1 Thessalonians 5:20–21; 1 John 4:1). [[v30|But if a revelation is made to another sitting by, let the first keep silent]]. [[v31|For you all can prophesy one by one, that all may learn and all may be exhorted]]. [[v32|The spirits of the prophets are subject to the prophets]]. The Holy Spirit does not force people to speak uncontrollably. Those who prophesy can control themselves and wait their turn. Paul's statement about God's character is studied below.

Verses 34–35 are among the most debated in Paul's letters: [[v34|Let the wives be quiet in the assemblies, for it has not been permitted for them to be talking except in submission, as the law also says]], [[v35|if they desire to learn anything. “Let them ask their own husbands at home, for it is shameful for a wife to be talking in the assembly.”]] Paul has already assumed that women pray and prophesy in the gathered church (11:5), so he cannot mean that women must be completely silent at all times. Christians have understood these verses in several ways. Some understand them as forbidding women from evaluating prophecies or teaching with authority in the church. Others understand them as addressing a specific problem of disruptive questions or chatter during worship, perhaps by wives who were less educated and were interrupting to ask questions. Some interpreters note that the World English Bible places part of verse 35 in quotation marks, suggesting that Paul may be quoting a Corinthian view, which he then challenges in verse 36. A few ancient manuscripts place these verses after verse 40, and some scholars question whether they were originally in this location. Faithful Christians hold different conclusions on these verses and on the roles of women in church leadership. Whatever view one holds, the surrounding context emphasizes orderly worship that builds up everyone, and Paul elsewhere honors women as co-workers in the gospel (Romans 16).

[[v36|What!? Was it from you that the word of God went out? Or did it come to you alone?]] Paul reminds the Corinthians that they are not the source of God's word, and they should not act independently of the other churches. [[v37|If any man thinks himself to be a prophet or spiritual, let him recognize the things which I write to you, that they are the commandment of the Lord]]. Truly spiritual people will recognize Paul's teaching as coming from the Lord.

[[v39|Therefore, brothers, desire earnestly to prophesy, and don’t forbid speaking with other languages]]. [[v40|Let all things be done decently and in order]].

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 Corinthians 14:3–4 — Building Up, Encouraging, Comforting

verse: 1 Corinthians 14:3-4

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had urged the Corinthians to pursue love and to desire spiritual gifts, especially prophecy. Now he explains why prophecy is so valuable in the gathered church.

First, he describes speaking in tongues: [[v2|For he who speaks in another language speaks not to men, but to God, for no one understands, but in the Spirit he speaks mysteries]]. When someone speaks in tongues without interpretation, they are speaking to God, and no one else understands. Paul does not say this is bad; prayer to God is good. But it does not help others in the church.

[[v3|But he who prophesies speaks to men for their edification, exhortation, and consolation]]. Prophecy speaks to people in words they can understand. Paul describes three purposes. "Edification" means building people up, strengthening their faith. "Exhortation" means encouraging, urging, or challenging people to live faithfully. "Consolation" means comforting those who are hurting.

[[v4|He who speaks in another language edifies himself, but he who prophesies edifies the assembly]]. Tongues build up the individual; prophecy builds up the whole church. Since gifts are given "for the profit of all" (12:7), gifts that build up the church are more valuable in public worship.

Prophecy in the New Testament is not always predicting the future. It is speaking a message from God that applies His truth to people's lives. Some Christians see this gift as continuing in Spirit-prompted words of encouragement and insight; others see its function now fulfilled mainly through the faithful preaching and teaching of Scripture. In either case, its purpose is to build up, encourage, and comfort.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that the purpose of spiritual gifts is to serve others. Love seeks the good of others (13:5). A gift that builds up the church is an expression of love.

They also show the importance of understanding in worship. God's word must be understood to transform lives. Throughout Scripture, God speaks so that His people can hear and respond. Nehemiah records that the Levites read the Law [[Nehemiah 8:8|distinctly; and they gave the sense, so that they understood the reading]].

Finally, these verses describe what the church needs: building up, encouragement, and comfort. Every believer needs these things. Words spoken in the Spirit can strengthen weak faith, encourage the discouraged, and comfort the grieving.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to use their words to build others up. Whether in formal ministry or everyday conversation, believers can speak words that strengthen, encourage, and comfort. Paul writes, [[Ephesians 4:29|Let no corrupt speech proceed out of your mouth, but only what is good for building others up as the need may be, that it may give grace to those who hear]].

They also encourage churches to prioritize what builds up the whole community. Worship services, programs, and activities can be evaluated by whether they strengthen the faith of the people.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to seek gifts that serve others. Believers can ask God not only for gifts that bless them personally, but for gifts that help others grow.

### 1 Corinthians 14:15 — With the Spirit and with the Understanding

verse: 1 Corinthians 14:15

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been explaining that speaking in tongues without interpretation does not help others understand. He also pointed out that it does not engage the speaker's own mind: [[v14|For if I pray in another language, my spirit prays, but my understanding is unfruitful]]. When someone prays in tongues, their spirit is praying, but their mind is not producing understandable words.

[[v15|What should I do? I will pray with the spirit, and I will pray with the understanding also]]. Paul's answer is not to choose one or the other, but to do both. He will pray with his spirit, the deep, Spirit-led part of his being, and with his understanding, his mind and thoughts.

[[v15|I will sing with the spirit, and I will sing with the understanding also]]. The same applies to singing. Worship should engage both the heart and the mind. Singing with the spirit means singing with deep, Spirit-filled devotion. Singing with the understanding means knowing what one is singing and meaning it.

Paul explains why this matters for others: [[v16|Otherwise, if you bless with the spirit, how will he who fills the place of the unlearned say the “Amen” at your giving of thanks, seeing he doesn’t know what you say?]] In the early church, when someone prayed, others would say "Amen," meaning "So be it," agreeing with the prayer. But they could only agree if they understood it. [[v17|For you most certainly give thanks well, but the other person is not built up]].

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that worship involves the whole person. God is not honored by mindless emotion or by cold intellect alone. Jesus said that true worshipers worship [[John 4:24|in spirit and truth]]. The greatest commandment calls people to love God with all their heart, soul, mind, and strength (Mark 12:30).

It also shows that worship is communal. Public prayer and singing should be understandable so that others can join in, agree, and be built up. Worship is not only a private experience but a shared act of the body of Christ.

Finally, the verse shows that the Spirit and the mind work together. The Holy Spirit does not bypass the mind; He renews it (Romans 12:2). Spiritual experience and understanding belong together.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to engage both heart and mind in worship. When singing, believers can pay attention to the words, thinking about their meaning, while also offering their hearts to God.

It also encourages believers to pray thoughtfully. Prayer can be deeply heartfelt while also being clear and meaningful. Believers can bring both their emotions and their thoughts to God.

Finally, the verse encourages churches to make worship understandable. Songs, prayers, and teaching should be clear enough for people to understand and join in, including newcomers and those unfamiliar with church language.

### 1 Corinthians 14:26 — Let All Things Be Done to Build Up

verse: 1 Corinthians 14:26

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been explaining the value of gifts that people can understand. Now he gives practical instructions for the church's gatherings.

[[v26|What is it then, brothers? When you come together, each one of you has a psalm, has a teaching, has a revelation, has another language, or has an interpretation]]. Paul describes a worship gathering in which many people participate. One person brings a "psalm," a song of praise, perhaps from the Psalms or a new song. Another brings a "teaching," instruction from God's word. Another has a "revelation," a message from God. Another speaks in tongues, and another interprets. Worship in the early church was participatory, with many members contributing.

[[v26|Let all things be done to build each other up]]. Here is the guiding principle. Whatever is done in worship should build up the church. Every song, teaching, prayer, and gift should strengthen the faith of the people.

This principle does not require that every church follow exactly the same pattern of worship. Churches today worship in many different ways, from liturgical services to informal gatherings. But in every setting, the question remains: Does this build up the people of God?

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that every believer has something to contribute. Worship is not a performance by a few for an audience of many. The whole body participates, each member bringing their gift. Paul writes elsewhere about believers [[Colossians 3:16|teaching and admonishing one another with psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs]].

It also shows that worship has a purpose beyond personal experience. While worship is directed to God, it also builds up the church. When believers gather, they strengthen one another's faith.

Finally, the verse shows that love governs worship. Building up others is an expression of love (8:1). Worship that is self-centered or chaotic fails to love; worship that builds up others fulfills it.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to come to worship ready to contribute. Believers can come not only to receive but also to give: a song, a prayer, a word of encouragement, a gift of service.

It also encourages churches to make room for the participation of members. Churches can find ways for members to share their gifts in worship and in other gatherings.

Finally, the verse gives a test for worship practices. Churches can ask of every part of their worship: Does this build up the people? Does it help them know and love God?

### 1 Corinthians 14:33 — Not a God of Confusion but of Peace

verse: 1 Corinthians 14:33

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been giving instructions for orderly worship: tongues should be limited and interpreted, prophets should take turns, and prophecies should be evaluated. He had said that [[v32|The spirits of the prophets are subject to the prophets]], meaning that those who prophesy can control themselves. The Holy Spirit does not make people speak uncontrollably.

Now he gives the reason for these instructions: [[v33|for God is not a God of confusion but of peace]]. Worship should reflect the character of God. God is not chaotic or disorderly. He is a God of peace, harmony, and order. When worship is chaotic, with everyone speaking at once and no one understanding, it does not reflect God's character.

The word "peace" here is the opposite of "confusion" or "disorder." It means harmony, order, and well-being. God's work brings peace.

[[v33|as in all the assemblies of the saints]]. This principle applies not only to Corinth but to all the churches. The Corinthians were not free to make up their own rules; they were part of a wider family of churches.

Paul concludes the chapter with a summary: [[v40|Let all things be done decently and in order]]. Worship should be done in a fitting and orderly way.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that worship reflects the character of God. Since God is a God of peace and order, His people's worship should reflect these qualities. In creation, God brought order out of chaos (Genesis 1:2). His Spirit brings order, not confusion.

It also shows that spiritual freedom and order are not opposites. The Corinthians may have thought that the Spirit's work was shown in wild, uncontrolled behavior. Paul teaches that the Spirit produces self-control (Galatians 5:23). True spiritual freedom works within order.

Finally, the verse shows that God's peace should mark the church. Jesus said, [[John 14:27|Peace I leave with you. My peace I give to you]]. Churches marked by peace reflect the presence of the Prince of Peace.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages churches to pursue orderly worship. Order does not mean rigidity or lack of joy. It means that worship is conducted in a way that allows people to understand, participate, and be built up.

It also encourages believers to exercise self-control in worship. Whatever one's tradition or style, believers can worship in ways that are considerate of others.

Finally, the verse encourages believers to seek peace in all of life. God is a God of peace. Believers can bring His peace into their homes, workplaces, and communities, avoiding unnecessary chaos and conflict.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

1 Corinthians 14 teaches that the purpose of spiritual gifts in worship is to build up the church. Prophecy is especially valuable because it speaks understandable words that strengthen, encourage, and comfort. Believers can seek gifts that serve others and use their words to build up, encourage, and comfort those around them.

The chapter also emphasizes understanding in worship. Paul would rather speak five words that others can understand than ten thousand in an unknown language. Believers can worship with both spirit and mind, singing and praying with heartfelt devotion and clear understanding, and churches can make worship accessible to all, including newcomers and outsiders, so that they may recognize that God is truly present.

1 Corinthians 14 shows that worship is participatory. When believers gather, each may bring a song, a teaching, or another gift. Believers can come ready to contribute, and churches can make room for the gifts of their members. Everything should be done to build up the church.

Finally, the chapter teaches that God is not a God of confusion but of peace. Worship should be done decently and in order, reflecting God's character. Believers can exercise self-control, honor one another, test what is spoken by Scripture, and pursue the peace and order that the Spirit produces, whatever the style or tradition of their worship.

## Chapter Prayer

Holy Spirit, thank You for the gifts You give to build up Your church. Help me to pursue love above all, and to desire gifts that strengthen, encourage, and comfort others. Teach me to worship You with both my spirit and my understanding, offering You my heart and my mind. When Your people gather, help me to come ready to contribute, not only to receive. Let my words build others up. Give Your church wisdom to worship in ways that are clear, orderly, and welcoming, so that those who come in may recognize that You are truly among us. You are not a God of confusion but of peace. Fill Your church with Your peace, and let everything be done decently and in order, for Your glory. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
