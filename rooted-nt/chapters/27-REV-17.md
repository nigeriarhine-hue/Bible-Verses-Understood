# Revelation 17
theme: The Great Prostitute and the Beast
main: One of the seven angels with the bowls invites John to see the judgment of the great prostitute who sits by many waters, with whom the kings of the earth committed adultery and by whose wine the inhabitants of the earth were made drunk. Carried in the Spirit into a wilderness, John sees a woman sitting on a scarlet beast covered with blasphemous names, with seven heads and ten horns. She is dressed in purple and scarlet, glittering with gold, jewels, and pearls, and holds a golden cup filled with abominable things and the filth of her immorality. On her forehead is written a mysterious name: Babylon the Great, the Mother of Prostitutes and of the Abominations of the Earth. She is drunk with the blood of God's holy people and of those who bore witness to Jesus, and John is greatly astonished. The angel explains the mystery. The beast once was, now is not, and will come up out of the abyss and go to its destruction, and the inhabitants of the earth will be astonished. The seven heads are seven hills on which the woman sits, and also seven kings: five have fallen, one is, and one is yet to come for a little while. The beast is an eighth king who belongs to the seven and goes to destruction. The ten horns are ten kings who will receive authority for one hour along with the beast; they will wage war against the Lamb, but the Lamb will triumph over them because He is Lord of lords and King of kings, and with Him will be His called, chosen, and faithful followers. The waters where the prostitute sits are peoples, multitudes, nations, and languages. The beast and the ten horns will turn on the prostitute, hate her, strip her, devour her, and burn her with fire, for God has put it into their hearts to accomplish His purpose until His words are fulfilled. The woman is the great city that rules over the kings of the earth.

## Chapter Background and Story

Revelation 17 and 18 describe the judgment of Babylon the great, which was announced in 14:8 and 16:19. Chapter 17 introduces Babylon as a luxurious prostitute riding on the beast, and explains her relationship to the beast and the kings of the earth. Chapter 18 describes her fall and the mourning of those who profited from her.

The image of a city as a woman is common in Scripture. Jerusalem is often called "the daughter of Zion." When cities or nations were unfaithful to God or led others into idolatry and exploitation, the prophets sometimes described them as prostitutes. Isaiah used this image for Tyre, a great trading city (Isaiah 23:15–18), and Nahum used it for Nineveh, the capital of Assyria (Nahum 3:4). The prophets even used it for Jerusalem when it turned from God (Ezekiel 16). The image is about spiritual unfaithfulness and the seductive power of wealth and idolatry. It is not a statement about women in general, and it should never be used to demean women.

### Who Is Babylon?

The identity of Babylon has been understood in several ways. Most interpreters believe that for John's first readers, Babylon pointed to **Rome**. The city was famously built on seven hills (v9), it ruled over the kings of the earth (v18), it was the center of vast wealth and trade, and it persecuted Christians. Peter also appears to use "Babylon" as a code name for Rome (1 Peter 5:13). Some **preterist** interpreters instead identify Babylon with **Jerusalem**, which had rejected Christ and persecuted His followers, noting that the "great city" in 11:8 was where the Lord was crucified. Some **futurist** interpreters expect a future world system centered in a literal city, possibly a rebuilt Babylon in Iraq or another great world capital. **Idealist** interpreters see Babylon as a symbol of every human society, economic system, and culture that seduces people away from God through wealth, pleasure, and power, and persecutes those who remain faithful.

During the Reformation and afterward, some Protestant writers identified Babylon with the Roman Catholic Church. Most interpreters today, including many Protestants, regard that identification as a product of the bitter conflicts of that era rather than the meaning of the text. Revelation's warnings about Babylon apply to every form of worldly seduction and should not be turned into a weapon against other Christians.

Many readers combine these perspectives. Babylon was Rome for John's readers; she reappears in different forms throughout history wherever human societies make wealth and power their gods; and she will face final judgment when Christ returns.

### The Mystery of the Beast (17:7–14)

[[v7|The angel said to me, “Why do you wonder? I will tell you the mystery of the woman and of the beast that carries her, which has the seven heads and the ten horns.]] John is astonished, perhaps because the woman is so impressive and glamorous. The angel gently corrects him: there is no need to be awed by her. Her secret will be revealed.

[[v8|The beast that you saw was, and is not; and is about to come up out of the abyss and to go into destruction. Those who dwell on the earth and whose names have not been written in the book of life from the foundation of the world will marvel when they see that the beast was, and is not, and shall be present]]. The description "was, and is not, and shall be present" is a parody of God's title, "who is and who was and who is to come" (1:4). The beast imitates God's eternal existence, but its destiny is destruction. This recalls the healed fatal wound in 13:3.

[[v9|Here is the mind that has wisdom. The seven heads are seven mountains on which the woman sits]]. [[v10|They are seven kings. Five have fallen, the one is, and the other has not yet come. When he comes, he must continue a little while]]. [[v11|The beast that was, and is not, is himself also an eighth, and is of the seven; and he goes to destruction]]. The seven heads have a double meaning: seven hills, a clear reference to Rome, and seven kings. Interpreters have tried to identify the seven kings with specific Roman emperors, but the lists differ depending on where one starts counting and which emperors are included. Others understand the seven kings symbolically as the complete succession of world empires that oppose God, with the beast as the final embodiment of them all. Still others see a reference to the legend of Nero returning. The main point is clear: the time remaining for the beast's rule is short, and its end is destruction.

[[v12|The ten horns that you saw are ten kings who have received no kingdom as yet, but they receive authority as kings with the beast for one hour]]. [[v13|These have one mind, and they give their power and authority to the beast]]. The ten horns echo Daniel 7:7, 24. They may represent client kings allied with Rome, future rulers allied with the antichrist, or symbolically, all the rulers of the earth who give their power to the beast. Their authority lasts only "one hour," a very short time. The Lamb's victory over them is studied below.

### The Prostitute's Downfall (17:15–18)

[[v15|He said to me, “The waters which you saw, where the prostitute sits, are peoples, multitudes, nations, and languages.]] Ancient Babylon was built on the Euphrates and its canals (Jeremiah 51:13). Here the waters are interpreted as the peoples of the world. Babylon's influence extends over all nations. The beast's turning on the prostitute is studied below.

[[v18|The woman whom you saw is the great city which reigns over the kings of the earth.”]] The angel identifies the woman as a city, the great city that dominates the world's rulers. For John's readers, this pointed to Rome. Its larger meaning reaches to every such city and system in history.

## Important Verses Explained

### Revelation 17:3–6 — The Woman on the Scarlet Beast

verse: Revelation 17:3-6

#### Simple Explanation

The angel carries John "in the Spirit into a wilderness." There he sees "a woman sitting on a scarlet-colored beast, full of blasphemous names, having seven heads and ten horns." The beast is the same beast from the sea described in chapter 13. The woman rides it, suggesting that she is supported by its power and appears to control it.

Verse 4 describes her appearance: she is "dressed in purple and scarlet, and decked with gold and precious stones and pearls." Purple and scarlet were expensive dyes worn by royalty and the wealthy. She is dazzling and luxurious.

She holds "a golden cup full of abominations and the impurities of the sexual immorality of the earth." Her cup looks valuable on the outside but is filled with filth inside.

In verse 5, "on her forehead a name was written": "MYSTERY, BABYLON THE GREAT, THE MOTHER OF THE PROSTITUTES AND OF THE ABOMINATIONS OF THE EARTH." Her name reveals her true identity: she is the source of corruption and idolatry.

Verse 6 adds the most shocking detail: "I saw the woman drunken with the blood of the saints and with the blood of the martyrs of Jesus." Beneath her beauty, she is a murderer of God's people.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The woman presents a striking contrast with other women in Revelation. The woman in chapter 12, clothed with the sun, represents God's faithful people. The bride of the Lamb in chapters 19 and 21, clothed in fine linen, represents the redeemed church. The prostitute, dressed in luxury, represents the world's seductive system that opposes God. The contrast invites readers to ask which "woman" they belong to: the faithful bride or the seductive prostitute.

The prostitute's appeal lies in her beauty, wealth, and pleasure. She offers prosperity, status, and enjoyment. But what she offers is ultimately poison. Her golden cup holds abominations. This reflects the way sin often looks attractive while bringing ruin.

Her drunkenness with the blood of the saints reveals the violent side of worldly power. Societies that worship wealth and pleasure often turn against those who refuse to join them. In John's day, Rome's prosperity was accompanied by the persecution of Christians. Throughout history, systems built on greed and power have crushed the vulnerable and silenced faithful witnesses.

The wilderness setting is significant. In chapter 12, the woman representing God's people fled to the wilderness for protection. Now John is taken to the wilderness to see Babylon clearly. From a place set apart from the world's glamour, he can see the prostitute for what she really is.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage calls believers to see through the glamour of the world. Wealth, luxury, status, and pleasure can be enticing. Advertising, entertainment, and culture often present them as the path to happiness. But Revelation reveals that the world's system, when it sets itself against God, is ultimately corrupt and destructive.

Believers can examine their own hearts for ways they have been seduced by worldly values. They can ask whether their desires, spending, and goals are shaped more by Babylon than by the kingdom of God.

This passage also reminds believers to care for those who are crushed by systems of greed and power. Babylon's luxury was built on exploitation. Believers can stand with the vulnerable, pursue justice, and refuse to profit from oppression.

### Revelation 17:14 — The Lamb Will Overcome

verse: Revelation 17:14

#### Simple Explanation

The ten kings, united with the beast, "will war against the Lamb." All the forces of the world gather against Christ.

But the outcome is certain: "the Lamb will overcome them." The reason is given: "for he is Lord of lords and King of kings." No king or lord can stand against the One who rules over all kings and lords.

The verse ends with a description of those who are with the Lamb: "those who are with him are called, chosen, and faithful."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse is a brief preview of the final victory described in 19:11–21, where Christ appears as the rider on the white horse with the title [[Revelation 19:16|KING OF KINGS AND LORD OF LORDS]]. The outcome of the war is never in doubt.

The title "Lord of lords and King of kings" was used in the Old Testament for God (Deuteronomy 10:17; Daniel 2:47). Applying it to the Lamb affirms that Jesus shares God's supreme authority. In a world where emperors claimed to be lords, this was a bold declaration that Jesus alone is the true Lord.

The Lamb conquers as the Lamb. His victory is rooted in His sacrificial death and resurrection. The weapons of the world, armies, alliances, and power, cannot defeat the One who conquered through the cross.

Those with the Lamb are described with three words. They are "called," invited by God's grace. They are "chosen," selected by God for His purposes. And they are "faithful," remaining loyal to Christ to the end. The first two describe God's initiative; the third describes their response. God calls and chooses, and His people respond with faithfulness.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can live with confidence in Christ's victory. Whatever powers seem to dominate the world, the Lamb will overcome them. He is Lord of lords and King of kings, and no human authority can stand against Him.

This verse also reminds believers of their identity. They are called and chosen by God, not because of their own merit but because of His grace. And they are called to be faithful, remaining loyal to Christ in every circumstance.

Believers can find encouragement in standing "with him." They are not alone in the struggle. They stand with the victorious Lamb, sharing in His triumph.

### Revelation 17:16–17 — Evil Destroys Itself

verse: Revelation 17:16-17

#### Simple Explanation

In a surprising twist, the beast and the ten kings turn against the prostitute. They "will hate the prostitute, will make her desolate, will strip her naked, will eat her flesh, and will burn her utterly with fire." The very powers that supported her now destroy her.

Verse 17 reveals who is behind this: "For God has put in their hearts to do what he has in mind, to be of one mind, and to give their kingdom to the beast, until the words of God should be accomplished." God uses the plans of evil powers to accomplish His purposes.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage reveals a profound truth about evil: it is self-destructive. The alliance between the prostitute and the beast was based on mutual exploitation. Such alliances do not last. In the end, evil turns on itself. History has many examples of corrupt powers that collapsed from within, betrayed by their own allies.

The description of the prostitute's destruction draws on the prophets, especially Ezekiel 16:37–41 and 23:25–29, where Jerusalem's former lovers turn against her. The violent language reflects the horror of judgment on a corrupt system; it describes the downfall of a city and its way of life, not a pattern for how people should be treated.

Verse 17 shows God's sovereignty over even the most evil plans. The kings think they are acting on their own, but God is using their actions to accomplish His word. This echoes the way God used the Assyrians and Babylonians in the Old Testament to bring judgment, even though those nations acted out of their own pride and cruelty (Isaiah 10:5–15). God does not cause evil, but He rules over it and turns it to serve His purposes.

The phrase "until the words of God should be accomplished" assures readers that everything is moving toward the fulfillment of God's plan. Nothing happens outside His purposes.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage gives believers hope when evil seems strong and united. Evil alliances are unstable. Corrupt systems eventually collapse. Believers do not need to fear that evil will last forever.

It also reminds believers that God is sovereign even over the plans of the wicked. When believers see evil at work in the world, they can trust that God is still in control and that His purposes will be accomplished.

Finally, this passage warns against placing trust in worldly power. Those who rely on the beast and its allies will eventually be betrayed. True security is found only in the Lamb, who never betrays His people.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Revelation 17 pulls back the curtain on Babylon, the seductive world system that opposes God. Dressed in luxury and holding a golden cup, she looks beautiful and powerful. But her cup is full of corruption, and she is drunk with the blood of God's people. Believers are called to see through her glamour and recognize the emptiness and danger of a life built on wealth, pleasure, and power apart from God.

The chapter contrasts Babylon with the faithful people of God and the bride of the Lamb. Every person must choose which they will belong to. Believers are called to be faithful to Christ, refusing the world's seductions and standing with the vulnerable who are crushed by systems of greed.

The chapter also reveals that the beast's power is temporary and destined for destruction. The kings who ally with it have authority for only one hour. The Lamb, who is Lord of lords and King of kings, will overcome them, and His called, chosen, and faithful people will share His victory.

Finally, the chapter shows that God is sovereign even over evil. Corrupt alliances turn on themselves, and God uses even the plans of the wicked to accomplish His word. Believers can live with confidence, knowing that history is moving toward the fulfillment of God's purposes and the triumph of the Lamb.

Guard my heart from being seduced by wealth, pleasure, and power, and keep me faithful to You alone. Help me to care for those who are exploited and crushed by systems of greed, and to stand for justice in Your name. Thank You that I am called and chosen by Your grace; make me faithful to the end.
