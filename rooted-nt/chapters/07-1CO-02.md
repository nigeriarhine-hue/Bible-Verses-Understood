# 1 Corinthians 2
theme: Christ Crucified and the Wisdom Revealed by the Spirit
main: Paul reminds the Corinthians that when he first came to them, he did not rely on impressive speech or philosophy, but resolved to know nothing among them except Jesus Christ and Him crucified. He came in weakness and fear, so that their faith would rest on God's power rather than human wisdom. Yet there is a true wisdom that God has revealed, hidden from the rulers of this age who crucified the Lord of glory, and made known to believers by the Holy Spirit, who searches the depths of God. People without the Spirit cannot accept these things, but believers have received the Spirit and have the mind of Christ.

## Chapter Background and Story

In chapter 1, Paul showed that the message of the cross seems foolish to the world but is God's power and wisdom. In chapter 2, he continues this theme by reminding the Corinthians of how he first preached to them, and by explaining that there is a true wisdom, God's wisdom, which can only be understood through the Holy Spirit.

When Paul arrived in Corinth, he had just come from Athens, where he had spoken to philosophers at the Areopagus with mixed results (Acts 17). Some scholars think Paul resolved in Corinth to focus even more simply on the cross. Whether or not that is so, Paul's approach in Corinth was deliberately plain. The Corinthians, who loved rhetoric and philosophy, may have later compared Paul unfavorably with the eloquent Apollos. Paul explains that his plainness was intentional.

### Paul's Preaching in Corinth (2:1–5)

Paul's description of his approach is studied below in two parts. In between, he describes his own condition: [[v3|I was with you in weakness, in fear, and in much trembling]]. Paul arrived in Corinth after being beaten in Philippi, driven out of Thessalonica and Berea, and mocked in Athens. He may have been physically weak, emotionally exhausted, and afraid of what might happen in another city. The Lord later appeared to him in a vision and said, [[Acts 18:9|Don't be afraid]]. Paul did not hide his weakness. He knew that God's power is made perfect in weakness (2 Corinthians 12:9).

### God's Hidden Wisdom (2:6–9)

Paul does not reject wisdom altogether. [[v6|We speak wisdom, however, among those who are full grown, yet a wisdom not of this world nor of the rulers of this world who are coming to nothing]]. There is a true wisdom that mature believers can understand. But it is different from the wisdom of this age and of its rulers, whose power is passing away.

[[v7|But we speak God’s wisdom in a mystery, the wisdom that has been hidden, which God foreordained before the worlds for our glory]]. A "mystery" in Paul's writings is a truth God has now revealed that was previously hidden. God's wisdom, His plan of salvation through Christ, was planned before the world was made, for the purpose of bringing believers to glory.

[[v8|which none of the rulers of this world has known. For had they known it, they wouldn’t have crucified the Lord of glory]]. The "rulers of this world" may refer to human rulers, such as Pilate, Herod, and the Jewish leaders, or to spiritual powers behind them, or both. They did not understand God's plan. If they had, they would not have crucified Jesus, because the cross became the means of their defeat. Notice the title "the Lord of glory." The one who was crucified is the glorious Lord Himself. The promise of verse 9 is studied below with verse 10.

### Revealed by the Spirit (2:10–16)

Paul explains that the Spirit reveals God's wisdom, as studied below. He uses an analogy: [[v11|For who among men knows the things of a man except the spirit of the man which is in him? Even so, no one knows the things of God except God’s Spirit]]. Only a person knows their own thoughts. Only God's Spirit knows God's thoughts.

[[v12|But we received not the spirit of the world, but the Spirit which is from God, that we might know the things that were freely given to us by God]]. Believers have received God's Spirit, who helps them understand the gifts God has given them. [[v13|We also speak these things, not in words which man’s wisdom teaches but which the Holy Spirit teaches, comparing spiritual things with spiritual things]]. Paul's message came from the Spirit, not from human philosophy.

Paul's contrast between the natural person and the spiritual person, and his claim that believers have the mind of Christ, is studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 Corinthians 2:2 — Jesus Christ and Him Crucified

verse: 1 Corinthians 2:2

#### Simple Explanation

Paul was reminding the Corinthians of how he first preached the gospel to them. Corinth was a city that admired eloquent speakers and clever philosophers. Public speakers were celebrities, and people judged teachers by how impressive they sounded.

[[v1|When I came to you, brothers, I didn’t come with excellence of speech or of wisdom, proclaiming to you the testimony of God]]. Paul did not try to impress them with fancy speech or philosophical arguments. He simply proclaimed God's message.

[[v2|For I determined not to know anything among you except Jesus Christ and him crucified]]. Paul made a deliberate decision. "Determined" means he resolved, he made up his mind. He chose to focus his message entirely on Jesus Christ, and specifically on His crucifixion.

This does not mean Paul never taught anything else. He taught about the resurrection, Christian living, the church, and many other topics. But everything was centered on Christ and the cross. The cross was the heart of his message, the lens through which he understood everything else.

Why did Paul focus on the cross? Because the cross is where God's love, justice, power, and wisdom are most fully revealed. It is where sins are forgiven and sinners are reconciled to God. And the cross humbles human pride. The Corinthians were proud of their wisdom and status. The cross shows that salvation comes not through human achievement but through God's grace in the death of His Son.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that the cross is the center of Christian faith and preaching. Paul writes elsewhere, [[Galatians 6:14|But far be it from me to boast, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ]]. Everything in the Christian life flows from what Christ accomplished on the cross.

It also shows the power of simplicity. Paul could have used his education to impress the Corinthians. He was highly trained and could argue brilliantly. But he chose simplicity so that people's faith would rest on God, not on him.

Finally, the verse shows that the person of Christ is inseparable from His work. Paul focused on "Jesus Christ and him crucified," both who Jesus is and what He did. The gospel is not merely a set of ideas but a Person who died and rose for sinners.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse calls believers to keep Christ and the cross at the center of their faith. It is easy to drift toward focusing on secondary matters: programs, techniques, opinions, or personalities. Believers can return again and again to the cross.

It also encourages those who teach and preach. Pastors, teachers, and parents do not need to impress others with eloquence or clever ideas. The most powerful message they can share is Jesus Christ and Him crucified.

Finally, the verse invites believers to see everything through the lens of the cross. Suffering, relationships, forgiveness, humility, and service all take on new meaning in light of Christ's sacrifice.

### 1 Corinthians 2:4–5 — Faith Resting on God's Power

verse: 1 Corinthians 2:4-5

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had told the Corinthians that he came to them in weakness, fear, and much trembling. He was not a confident, polished speaker trying to impress them. He continued describing his preaching.

[[v4|My speech and my preaching were not in persuasive words of human wisdom]]. Paul did not rely on the techniques of persuasion that were popular in Corinth. Greek orators were trained in rhetoric, the art of persuasive speaking. They used clever arguments, emotional appeals, and impressive language to win audiences. Paul deliberately avoided relying on these methods.

[[v4|but in demonstration of the Spirit and of power]]. Instead, Paul's preaching was accompanied by the Holy Spirit's power. The Spirit convicted hearts, opened minds, and changed lives. There may also have been miraculous signs (2 Corinthians 12:12). The "demonstration" was the proof that the message came from God.

[[v5|that your faith wouldn’t stand in the wisdom of men, but in the power of God]]. Paul had a purpose. If the Corinthians had believed because of Paul's eloquence, their faith would rest on Paul. If someone more eloquent came along, they might be swayed. But because their faith came through the Spirit's power, it rested on God. Faith built on God is secure.

This was especially important in Corinth, where the believers were dividing into groups around favorite teachers. Paul wanted them to understand that their faith did not depend on any human teacher.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that genuine faith is the work of God, not human persuasion. Paul writes, [[1 Thessalonians 1:5|and that our Good News came to you not in word only, but also in power, and in the Holy Spirit, and with much assurance]]. Human words can inform and persuade, but only the Spirit can create saving faith.

They also show that God uses weakness to display His power. Paul came in weakness and fear, yet God worked powerfully through him. Paul later wrote that God told him, [[2 Corinthians 12:9|My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness]].

Finally, these verses show the importance of the foundation of faith. Faith that rests on human wisdom can be shaken when that wisdom is challenged. Faith that rests on God's power is secure.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers who feel weak or inadequate when sharing their faith. Believers do not need to be eloquent or persuasive. God's Spirit does the work of convincing hearts. Believers can share simply and trust God.

They also caution believers against basing their faith on human personalities. Some believers' faith depends heavily on a favorite pastor, speaker, or author. When that person disappoints them, their faith is shaken. Believers can make sure their faith rests on God Himself.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to pray for the Spirit's power. When believers share the gospel, teach, or serve, they can ask the Holy Spirit to work through them, demonstrating God's power in ways human effort cannot.

### 1 Corinthians 2:9–10 — What God Has Prepared, Revealed by the Spirit

verse: 1 Corinthians 2:9-10

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had explained that there is a true wisdom from God, hidden from the rulers of this world who crucified the Lord of glory. Now he quotes Scripture to describe this wisdom.

[[v9|But as it is written, “Things which an eye didn’t see, and an ear didn’t hear, which didn’t enter into the heart of man, these God has prepared for those who love him.”]] This quotation is based on Isaiah 64:4 and perhaps other Old Testament passages. It describes the blessings God has prepared for those who love Him. These blessings are beyond human perception. No eye has seen them, no ear has heard them, and no human mind has imagined them.

This verse is often quoted in reference to heaven, and that is a fitting application. The glories of the age to come are beyond imagination. But in this context, Paul is also speaking about God's wisdom revealed in the gospel: the plan of salvation through the crucified Christ, which no human could have discovered or imagined on their own.

[[v10|But to us, God revealed them through the Spirit]]. Here is the wonderful news: what humans could never discover, God has revealed. He has made it known to believers through the Holy Spirit. Believers do not have to guess about God's plan; He has shown it to them.

[[v10|For the Spirit searches all things, yes, the deep things of God]]. The Holy Spirit knows everything, even the deepest truths about God. Because the Spirit is God, He knows God's thoughts completely. And He reveals them to believers.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show the limits of human knowledge and the necessity of revelation. Humans cannot discover God's deepest truths by their own reasoning. God must reveal Himself. Jesus said, [[Matthew 11:27|No one knows the Son, except the Father; neither does anyone know the Father, except the Son, and he to whom the Son desires to reveal him]].

They also show the role of the Holy Spirit as revealer. The Spirit inspired the Scriptures (2 Peter 1:21) and illuminates believers' minds to understand them. Jesus promised that the Spirit would guide His disciples into all truth (John 16:13).

Finally, these verses inspire hope and wonder. God has prepared things for those who love Him that are beyond imagination. Whatever believers experience now is only a foretaste of what is to come.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to depend on the Holy Spirit when reading Scripture. Understanding God's truth is not merely an intellectual exercise. Believers can ask the Spirit to open their minds and hearts to what God has revealed.

They also give believers hope in difficult times. Whatever believers face now, God has prepared good things for those who love Him, things beyond their imagination. This hope can sustain them through suffering.

Finally, these verses invite believers to grow in love for God. The promise is for "those who love him." Believers can cultivate their love for God through worship, prayer, obedience, and gratitude.

### 1 Corinthians 2:14–16 — The Mind of Christ

verse: 1 Corinthians 2:14-16

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had explained that God reveals His wisdom through the Holy Spirit, and that believers have received the Spirit so that they can understand what God has given them. Now he contrasts two kinds of people.

[[v14|Now the natural man doesn’t receive the things of God’s Spirit, for they are foolishness to him]]. The "natural man" refers to a person without the Holy Spirit, someone who lives only by natural human abilities. Such a person does not accept the things that come from God's Spirit. They seem foolish.

[[v14|and he can’t know them, because they are spiritually discerned]]. The problem is not intelligence but spiritual perception. Spiritual truths can only be understood with the help of the Spirit. A person without the Spirit is like someone without sight trying to appreciate a painting.

[[v15|But he who is spiritual discerns all things, and he himself is to be judged by no one]]. The "spiritual" person is someone who has the Spirit. Such a person can evaluate all things with spiritual insight. And they cannot be properly judged by those without the Spirit, because unspiritual people do not understand spiritual things.

[[v16|“For who has known the mind of the Lord that he should instruct him?” But we have Christ’s mind]]. Paul quotes Isaiah 40:13. No one can instruct God or fully know His mind. Yet believers "have Christ's mind." Through the Spirit, believers share in Christ's way of thinking. They can understand God's purposes as revealed in Christ.

Paul's words would also challenge the Corinthians. They considered themselves "spiritual," but in the next chapter, Paul tells them they are still acting like "fleshly" people because of their jealousy and quarreling (3:1–3). Truly spiritual people show it by their humility and love.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show why some people cannot understand the gospel. It is not because the gospel is irrational, but because spiritual truth requires spiritual perception. Paul writes, [[2 Corinthians 4:4|in whom the god of this world has blinded the minds of the unbelieving]]. Only the Spirit can open blind eyes.

They also show the privilege of believers. Believers have the mind of Christ. This does not mean they know everything or are never wrong. It means that through the Spirit, they can understand God's truth, see things from His perspective, and grow in wisdom.

Finally, these verses show that spiritual maturity is measured by Christlikeness. Having the mind of Christ means thinking as He thinks: humble, loving, self-giving. Paul writes, [[Philippians 2:5|Have this in your mind, which was also in Christ Jesus]], and then describes Christ's humility.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses help believers be patient with those who do not understand the gospel. Spiritual truth may seem foolish to someone without the Spirit. Believers can pray for God to open their eyes rather than becoming frustrated.

They also encourage believers to rely on the Spirit for understanding. When believers read Scripture or face decisions, they can ask the Spirit to help them think with the mind of Christ.

Finally, these verses call believers to develop the mind of Christ. Believers can examine their thoughts and attitudes, asking whether they reflect Christ's humility, love, and wisdom. Growing in the mind of Christ is a lifelong process.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

1 Corinthians 2 calls believers to keep Jesus Christ and Him crucified at the center of their faith and witness. Paul deliberately chose simplicity over eloquence so that the cross would not be emptied of its power. Believers today can resist the temptation to rely on impressive techniques or personalities, and instead point people clearly to Christ and His cross.

The chapter also shows that God works through weakness. Paul came to Corinth in weakness, fear, and trembling, yet God's Spirit worked powerfully through him. Believers who feel inadequate can trust that God's power is displayed in their weakness, and that faith built on God's power is secure.

1 Corinthians 2 teaches that God's wisdom is revealed by the Holy Spirit. What no eye has seen and no ear has heard, God has revealed to those who love Him. Believers can depend on the Spirit to understand Scripture and God's purposes, and they can live with hope, knowing that God has prepared things beyond imagination for those who love Him.

Finally, the chapter shows that believers have the mind of Christ. People without the Spirit may find the gospel foolish, but believers can understand spiritual truths through the Spirit. Believers can be patient with those who do not yet understand, and they can grow in thinking as Christ thinks, with humility, love, and wisdom.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, help me to keep You and Your cross at the center of my life. Like Paul, I want to know nothing except Jesus Christ and Him crucified. When I feel weak, afraid, or inadequate, remind me that Your power is made perfect in weakness. Let my faith rest not on human wisdom or impressive personalities but on the power of God. Holy Spirit, thank You for revealing the deep things of God. Open my mind and heart to understand Your word. Thank You for the wonderful things You have prepared for those who love You, things no eye has seen and no ear has heard. Help me to love You more. Give me the mind of Christ, so that I may think as He thinks and live as He lived. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
