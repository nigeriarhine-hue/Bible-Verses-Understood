# 1 Corinthians 10
theme: Warnings from Israel, a Way of Escape, and Doing All for God's Glory
main: Paul warns the Corinthians not to presume on their spiritual privileges, pointing to Israel in the wilderness, who experienced God's deliverance and provision yet fell into idolatry, immorality, testing God, and grumbling, and perished. These things were written as examples, so believers who think they stand should take care not to fall, trusting that God is faithful and will provide a way of escape in every temptation. Paul then forbids participation in idol feasts, because sharing in the Lord's Supper unites believers with Christ, and pagan sacrifices involve fellowship with demons. He closes with practical guidance about food bought in the market or served by unbelievers, summed up in the principle: whatever you do, do it all for the glory of God, seeking the good of others.

## Chapter Background and Story

1 Corinthians 10 concludes Paul's discussion of food offered to idols (chapters 8–10). In chapter 8, he said that idols are nothing and food does not bring people closer to God, but believers should not use their freedom in a way that harms weaker believers. In chapter 9, he gave his own example of giving up rights for the sake of others. Now he warns about the real danger of idolatry and gives practical guidelines.

Some Corinthians apparently felt spiritually secure. They had been baptized and shared in the Lord's Supper. They believed that because idols were nothing, they could attend feasts in pagan temples without danger. Paul shows that this confidence was misplaced. Spiritual privileges do not protect people who willfully participate in idolatry.

### Lessons from Israel's History (10:1–11)

Paul reminds the Corinthians of Israel's experience in the wilderness. [[v1|our fathers were all under the cloud, and all passed through the sea]]. God led Israel with a pillar of cloud and brought them through the Red Sea (Exodus 13:21; 14:22). [[v2|and were all baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea]]. Their passage through the sea under the cloud was like a baptism, uniting them with Moses as their leader. [[v3|and all ate the same spiritual food]], the manna (Exodus 16), [[v4|and all drank the same spiritual drink. For they drank of a spiritual rock that followed them, and the rock was Christ]]. God provided water from the rock (Exodus 17:6; Numbers 20:11). Paul sees Christ present with Israel, the source of their spiritual sustenance. Some Jewish traditions spoke of a rock that followed Israel through the wilderness; Paul's point is that Christ was with them throughout their journey.

Israel had experiences similar to Christian baptism and the Lord's Supper. Yet [[v5|However with most of them, God was not well pleased, for they were overthrown in the wilderness]]. Despite their privileges, most of that generation died in the wilderness and never entered the promised land.

[[v6|Now these things were our examples, to the intent we should not lust after evil things as they also lusted]]. Paul lists four sins of Israel. Idolatry: [[v7|Don’t be idolaters, as some of them were. As it is written, “The people sat down to eat and drink, and rose up to play.”]] This refers to the golden calf (Exodus 32:6), when Israel ate a feast and engaged in revelry before an idol, exactly the kind of thing that happened at pagan temple feasts in Corinth. Sexual immorality: [[v8|Let’s not commit sexual immorality, as some of them committed, and in one day twenty-three thousand fell]] (Numbers 25:1–9, where Israelite men joined Moabite women in sexual immorality and idol worship). Testing God: [[v9|Let’s not test Christ, as some of them tested, and perished by the serpents]] (Numbers 21:5–6). Grumbling: [[v10|Don’t grumble, as some of them also grumbled, and perished by the destroyer]] (Numbers 14; 16).

(Numbers 25:9 records twenty-four thousand deaths; Paul's figure of twenty-three thousand may refer to those who died "in one day," or may be a rounded number. Ancient writers often used approximate figures.)

[[v11|Now all these things happened to them by way of example, and they were written for our admonition, on whom the ends of the ages have come]]. Believers live in the last era of history, the time between Christ's first and second comings. Israel's history serves as a warning for them.

### A Warning and a Promise (10:12–13)

Paul's warning and his promise about temptation are studied below.

### Flee from Idolatry (10:14–22)

[[v14|Therefore, my beloved, flee from idolatry]]. Paul tells them to run away from idolatry, just as he told them to flee sexual immorality (6:18). His teaching on the Lord's Supper is studied below.

[[v18|Consider Israel according to the flesh. Don’t those who eat the sacrifices participate in the altar?]] In Israel, those who ate the sacrifices shared in the worship at the altar. Eating was participation.

[[v19|What am I saying then? That a thing sacrificed to idols is anything, or that an idol is anything?]] Paul has not changed his view that idols are nothing in themselves. [[v20|But I say that the things which the Gentiles sacrifice, they sacrifice to demons and not to God, and I don’t desire that you would have fellowship with demons]]. Behind idol worship stand demonic powers (Deuteronomy 32:17). Participating in pagan worship meant entering into fellowship with these spiritual forces.

[[v21|You can’t both drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons. You can’t both partake of the table of the Lord and of the table of demons]]. Believers cannot be loyal to Christ and participate in idol worship. [[v22|Or do we provoke the Lord to jealousy? Are we stronger than he?]] God is a jealous God, who will not share His people's worship with idols (Exodus 20:5). It is foolish to test Him.

### Practical Guidelines (10:23–33)

Paul returns to a Corinthian slogan, studied below. He then gives practical advice. [[v25|Whatever is sold in the butcher shop, eat, asking no question for the sake of conscience]], [[v26|for “the earth is the Lord’s, and its fullness.”]] (Psalm 24:1). Meat sold in the market could be eaten freely. Believers did not need to investigate whether it had been offered to idols. Everything belongs to God.

[[v27|But if one of those who don’t believe invites you to a meal, and you are inclined to go, eat whatever is set before you, asking no questions for the sake of conscience]]. Believers could accept invitations to unbelievers' homes and eat what was served. But [[v28|But if anyone says to you, “This was offered to idols,” don’t eat it for the sake of the one who told you, and for the sake of conscience]]. If someone pointed out that the food had been offered to idols, perhaps an unbeliever testing the Christian's loyalty or a weaker believer troubled by it, the believer should not eat it, out of consideration for that person's conscience. Paul's summary principle is studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 Corinthians 10:12–13 — God Will Make a Way of Escape

verse: 1 Corinthians 10:12-13

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had just reminded the Corinthians of Israel's history. The Israelites had experienced God's deliverance at the Red Sea, eaten manna from heaven, and drunk water from the rock. Yet many of them fell into idolatry, sexual immorality, testing God, and grumbling, and they died in the wilderness.

[[v12|Therefore let him who thinks he stands be careful that he doesn’t fall]]. Some Corinthians were overconfident. They believed that because they had been baptized and shared in the Lord's Supper, they were safe, no matter what they did. Paul warns them: those who think they are standing firm should be careful. Overconfidence is dangerous. Pride comes before a fall (Proverbs 16:18).

But Paul does not leave them in fear. He immediately gives a promise.

[[v13|No temptation has taken you except what is common to man]]. The temptations believers face are not unique. Others have faced the same struggles. Believers are not alone.

[[v13|God is faithful, who will not allow you to be tempted above what you are able]]. God is faithful. He sets limits on temptation. He will not allow believers to face temptation beyond what they can bear, with His help.

[[v13|but will with the temptation also make the way of escape, that you may be able to endure it]]. With every temptation, God provides a way out. This does not always mean the temptation disappears. It means God gives believers the strength and the means to resist it and endure it without falling into sin. The "way of escape" may be fleeing the situation, calling a friend, turning to prayer, remembering Scripture, or simply saying no.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses balance warning and assurance. Believers should not be overconfident, assuming they cannot fall. But neither should they despair, believing temptation is too strong. God is faithful. Peter writes, [[2 Peter 2:9|the Lord knows how to deliver the godly out of temptation]].

They also show that temptation is not sin. Being tempted is a common human experience; even Jesus was tempted (Hebrews 4:15). Sin occurs when people give in to temptation. God provides the strength to resist.

Finally, these verses show God's faithful care. He knows His people's limits. He does not abandon them to temptation but walks with them through it and provides a way out. This is part of His loving fatherhood.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses warn believers against spiritual pride. No believer is beyond temptation or immune from falling. Believers can stay humble, watchful, and dependent on God.

They also encourage believers facing temptation. Whatever temptation they face, they are not alone, and God will provide a way of escape. Believers can look for that way out: leaving a situation, setting boundaries, seeking accountability, or turning to God in prayer.

Finally, these verses encourage believers who feel that their temptation is too great. God is faithful. With His help, they can resist. If they fall, they can confess and receive forgiveness (1 John 1:9), and keep walking with Him.

### 1 Corinthians 10:16–17 — Sharing in the Body and Blood of Christ

verse: 1 Corinthians 10:16-17

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had told the Corinthians to flee from idolatry. Some of them were attending feasts in pagan temples, believing that because idols were nothing, it did not matter. Paul now explains why participating in such feasts was incompatible with following Christ.

[[v16|The cup of blessing which we bless, isn’t it a sharing of the blood of Christ?]] In the Lord's Supper, believers drink from the cup. "The cup of blessing" was a phrase used at Jewish Passover meals for the cup over which a blessing was spoken. Jesus used such a cup at the Last Supper. When believers drink it, they have "sharing" (koinonia) in the blood of Christ. They participate in the benefits of His death.

[[v16|The bread which we break, isn’t it a sharing of the body of Christ?]] When believers eat the bread, they share in the body of Christ. They are united with Him.

[[v17|Because there is one loaf of bread, we, who are many, are one body; for we all partake of the one loaf of bread]]. The one loaf symbolizes the unity of believers. Although there are many of them, they all eat from the same loaf, showing that they are one body in Christ. The Lord's Supper unites believers not only with Christ but with one another.

Paul's point is that eating is participation. If eating the Lord's Supper means sharing in Christ, then eating at a pagan sacrifice means sharing in what that sacrifice represents. Believers cannot share in both.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show the deep meaning of the Lord's Supper. It is not merely a reminder of a past event; it is a "sharing" in Christ. Christians have understood this sharing in different ways. Some traditions believe that Christ's body and blood are truly present in the bread and cup. Others believe Christ is spiritually present, nourishing believers by faith. Others understand the Supper mainly as a memorial and proclamation of His death. All agree that the Supper is a sacred act that unites believers with Christ and with one another.

They also show the unity of the church. The one loaf represents the one body. Believers who share the Lord's Supper together declare that they belong to one another. This makes divisions in the church especially serious, as Paul will discuss in chapter 11.

Finally, these verses show that worship involves loyalty. Believers cannot divide their worship between Christ and idols. Jesus said, [[Matthew 6:24|No one can serve two masters]].

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to approach the Lord's Supper with reverence. When believers share the bread and cup, they participate in Christ and His sacrifice. They can come with gratitude, faith, and self-examination.

They also call believers to unity. The Lord's Supper declares that believers are one body. Believers can examine whether there are divisions or broken relationships that need to be healed.

Finally, these verses challenge believers to give their undivided loyalty to Christ. Believers can examine whether they are trying to serve Christ while also giving their hearts to other things that compete for their worship.

### 1 Corinthians 10:23–24 — Not All Things Build Up

verse: 1 Corinthians 10:23-24

#### Simple Explanation

Paul returns to a slogan the Corinthians liked to use: [[v23|“All things are lawful for me,”]]. He had addressed this slogan in 6:12 in the context of sexual immorality. Now he addresses it in the context of food and freedom.

[[v23|but not all things are profitable]]. Even if something is permitted, it may not be beneficial. Believers should consider whether an action is good for them and for others.

[[v23|“All things are lawful for me,” but not all things build up]]. Paul repeats the slogan and adds a second qualification. Even if something is permitted, it may not "build up," strengthen others' faith or the community. In 6:12, Paul's second point was about not being mastered by anything. Here it is about edification, the good of others.

[[v24|Let no one seek his own, but each one his neighbor’s good]]. This is the guiding principle. Believers should not seek only their own good, comfort, or freedom. They should seek the good of others.

These verses summarize the whole discussion of chapters 8–10. Freedom is real, but love governs it. The question is not only "Am I allowed?" but "Will this help others?"

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that Christian freedom is shaped by love. Paul writes elsewhere, [[Philippians 2:4|each of you not just looking to his own things, but each of you also to the things of others]]. Love considers others before self.

They also show that the Christian life is not merely about avoiding sin but about building up others. Believers can ask not only "Is this wrong?" but "Is this good? Does it help others grow?"

Finally, these verses show the pattern of Christ. Jesus did not seek His own good but the good of others, even to the point of death. His followers are called to the same self-giving love.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses give believers a helpful test for decisions. Before acting, believers can ask: Is this beneficial? Will this build others up? Am I seeking my own good or my neighbor's good?

They also encourage believers to think beyond themselves. In a culture that emphasizes individual rights and self-fulfillment, believers can consider how their choices affect their families, churches, and communities.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to seek opportunities to help others. Seeking a neighbor's good is not only about avoiding harm but about actively doing good, encouraging, serving, and supporting others.

### 1 Corinthians 10:31 — Do All to the Glory of God

verse: 1 Corinthians 10:31

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been giving practical guidelines about eating food that may have been offered to idols. Believers could eat food bought in the market without asking questions. They could eat at unbelievers' homes. But if someone pointed out that the food had been offered to idols, they should refrain for the sake of that person's conscience.

Now Paul states the principle that governs everything: [[v31|Whether therefore you eat or drink, or whatever you do, do all to the glory of God]].

This verse applies to every area of life. Eating and drinking are the most ordinary activities. If even these can be done for God's glory, then everything can. "Whatever you do" includes work, rest, relationships, conversations, recreation, and every decision.

To do something "to the glory of God" means to do it in a way that honors Him, shows His worth, and reflects His character. It means doing it with gratitude, in obedience to Him, in love for others, and with awareness that He is the source of all good things.

Paul continues: [[v32|Give no occasion for stumbling, whether to Jews, to Greeks, or to the assembly of God]]. Glorifying God includes not causing others to stumble. [[v33|even as I also please all men in all things, not seeking my own profit, but the profit of the many, that they may be saved]]. Paul's goal in everything was the salvation of others.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that all of life is worship. There is no division between sacred and secular for believers. Everything can be done for God's glory. Paul writes, [[Colossians 3:17|Whatever you do, in word or in deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through him]].

It also shows the purpose of human life. People were created to glorify God. The Westminster Shorter Catechism famously begins, "Man's chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever." Believers find their deepest purpose in living for God's glory.

Finally, the verse shows that glorifying God involves love for others. Paul connects glorifying God with not causing others to stumble and seeking their salvation. God is glorified when His people love one another and point others to Him.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites believers to see every part of life as an opportunity to glorify God. Washing dishes, studying, working, exercising, and resting can all be done for His glory. Believers can begin each day by offering everything to Him.

It also gives believers a guide for decisions. When unsure about a choice, believers can ask, "Will this glorify God?" This question can clarify many decisions.

Finally, the verse encourages believers to live with gratitude. Glorifying God in eating and drinking means receiving food as His gift and thanking Him for it. Believers can practice gratitude in all things, recognizing that every good gift comes from God.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

1 Corinthians 10 warns believers not to presume on their spiritual privileges. Israel experienced God's deliverance and provision, yet many fell into idolatry, immorality, testing God, and grumbling. These things were written as warnings. Believers who think they stand firm should be careful not to fall, staying humble and watchful.

The chapter also offers a wonderful promise: no temptation is beyond what is common to humanity, and God is faithful. He will not allow believers to be tempted beyond what they can bear, and He will provide a way of escape. Believers facing temptation can look for that way out and trust God's strength.

1 Corinthians 10 teaches that the Lord's Supper is a sharing in the body and blood of Christ, and that believers cannot share in both the Lord's table and the table of demons. Believers can come to the Lord's Supper with reverence and give their undivided loyalty to Christ, fleeing every form of idolatry.

Finally, the chapter gives practical wisdom for living in freedom. Not everything permitted is beneficial or builds others up. Believers should seek their neighbor's good, not merely their own. And in everything, from eating and drinking to the greatest decisions, believers can do all for the glory of God, giving no cause for stumbling and seeking the salvation of others.

## Chapter Prayer

Faithful God, thank You for the warnings You have given through Israel's history. Keep me humble, so that I never think I stand so firmly that I cannot fall. Thank You that no temptation I face is beyond what others have faced, and that You will not let me be tempted beyond what I can bear. Show me the way of escape when temptation comes, and give me strength to take it. Lord Jesus, thank You for the cup of blessing and the broken bread, which unite me with You and with Your people. Help me to give You my undivided loyalty and to flee every form of idolatry. Teach me to seek my neighbor's good rather than my own. Whether I eat or drink or whatever I do, help me to do it all for Your glory. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
