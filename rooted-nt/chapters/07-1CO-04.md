# 1 Corinthians 4
theme: Faithful Stewards, Humble Hearts, and a Father's Love
main: Paul tells the Corinthians to regard him and Apollos as servants of Christ and stewards of God's mysteries, who must above all be faithful. He does not rely on human judgment or even his own, but on the Lord, who will reveal the motives of every heart when He comes. Paul confronts the Corinthians' pride by reminding them that everything they have is a gift, and he contrasts their imagined status with the apostles' humble suffering as fools for Christ. Writing not to shame them but as a loving father, he urges them to imitate him, sends Timothy to remind them of his ways in Christ, and warns that God's kingdom is a matter of power, not talk.

## Chapter Background and Story

1 Corinthians 4 concludes Paul's long discussion of divisions in the church (chapters 1–4). The Corinthians had been comparing leaders, judging them by worldly standards, and boasting about their favorites. Some had also begun to criticize Paul, perhaps saying that he was less impressive than Apollos or that he was not coming back to Corinth.

Paul responds by explaining how leaders should be viewed, by exposing the Corinthians' pride, and by appealing to them as their spiritual father. The chapter moves from sharp irony to tender affection, revealing both Paul's frustration and his deep love for this church.

### Servants and Stewards (4:1–5)

Paul's description of leaders as servants and stewards is studied below, as is his teaching on judgment. Paul says, [[v3|But with me it is a very small thing that I should be judged by you, or by a human court. Yes, I don’t even judge my own self]]. Paul was not overly concerned about the Corinthians' opinion of him. He did not even trust his own judgment of himself. [[v4|For I know nothing against myself. Yet I am not justified by this, but he who judges me is the Lord]]. Paul's conscience was clear, but a clear conscience did not prove he was innocent. Only the Lord's judgment counted.

### Do Not Be Puffed Up (4:6–13)

Paul explains that he has applied these things to himself and Apollos as an example, [[v6|that in us you might learn not to think beyond the things which are written, that none of you be puffed up against one another]]. The phrase [[v6|not to think beyond the things which are written]] may have been a familiar saying, urging believers to stay within the boundaries of Scripture and not go beyond it in pride. Paul's piercing questions about gifts are studied below.

Paul then uses biting irony: [[v8|You are already filled. You have already become rich. You have come to reign without us]]. The Corinthians acted as if they had already arrived spiritually, as if they were already reigning like kings in God's kingdom. Paul wishes it were true, so that he could reign with them!

Then he contrasts their imagined glory with the apostles' reality: [[v9|For I think that God has displayed us, the apostles, last of all, like men sentenced to death. For we are made a spectacle to the world, both to angels and men]]. The image comes from a Roman triumphal procession, where captured prisoners condemned to death were displayed at the end of the parade, or from the arena, where condemned criminals fought to the death for public entertainment.

[[v10|We are fools for Christ’s sake, but you are wise in Christ. We are weak, but you are strong. You have honor, but we have dishonor]]. Paul's irony is sharp. The Corinthians considered themselves wise, strong, and honored. The apostles were considered fools, weak, and disgraced.

Paul describes the apostles' life: [[v11|Even to this present hour we hunger, thirst, are naked, are beaten, and have no certain dwelling place]]. [[v12|We toil, working with our own hands. When people curse us, we bless. Being persecuted, we endure]]. [[v13|Being defamed, we entreat. We are made as the filth of the world, the dirt wiped off by all, even until now]]. The words "filth" and "dirt wiped off" describe the scrapings and garbage people throw away. Yet in this humility, the apostles followed the way of Jesus, who blessed those who cursed Him and endured suffering.

### A Father's Appeal (4:14–21)

Paul's tone changes completely: [[v14|I don’t write these things to shame you, but to admonish you as my beloved children]]. His harsh words came from love. His appeal as their spiritual father is studied below.

Paul sent Timothy to them, [[v17|who is my beloved and faithful child in the Lord, who will remind you of my ways which are in Christ, even as I teach everywhere in every assembly]]. Timothy would remind them of Paul's teaching and example, which were the same in every church.

Some in Corinth were arrogant, acting as if Paul would never come back. Paul assured them, [[v19|But I will come to you shortly, if the Lord is willing. And I will know, not the word of those who are puffed up, but the power]]. Paul would test whether their claims were backed by real spiritual power. [[v20|For God’s Kingdom is not in word, but in power]]. God's kingdom is not about impressive talk but about the transforming power of God in people's lives.

[[v21|What do you want? Shall I come to you with a rod, or in love and a spirit of gentleness?]] Paul gave them a choice. If they repented, he would come with gentleness. If not, he would come with discipline. Like a loving father, Paul preferred gentleness, but he was willing to discipline if necessary.

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 Corinthians 4:1–2 — Stewards Must Be Faithful

verse: 1 Corinthians 4:1-2

#### Simple Explanation

The Corinthians had been comparing and ranking their leaders, Paul, Apollos, and Peter, as if they were celebrities. Paul wanted them to understand how leaders should really be viewed.

[[v1|So let a man think of us as Christ’s servants]]. The word translated "servants" here originally described rowers on the lower deck of a ship, workers who simply followed orders. It came to mean any subordinate servant. Paul and other leaders are servants of Christ. They do not have their own agenda; they serve their Master.

[[v1|and stewards of God’s mysteries]]. A "steward" was a household manager, often a trusted slave, who took care of the master's property and household. The steward did not own the property; he managed it on behalf of the owner. Paul and other leaders are stewards of "God's mysteries," the truths of the gospel that God has revealed. They are entrusted with God's message and must manage it faithfully.

[[v2|Here, moreover, it is required of stewards that they be found faithful]]. The most important quality of a steward is faithfulness. A steward does not need to be impressive, eloquent, or popular. He needs to be faithful, reliable, trustworthy, carrying out the master's wishes.

This was a different standard from the one the Corinthians used. They judged leaders by eloquence, charisma, and impressive appearance. Paul says God judges by faithfulness.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that ministry is stewardship. Leaders do not own the gospel or the church; they manage what God has entrusted to them. Peter writes, [[1 Peter 4:10|As each has received a gift, employ it in serving one another, as good managers of the grace of God in its various forms]].

They also show that faithfulness is what God values most. Jesus told the parable of the talents, in which the master praised his servants: [[Matthew 25:21|Well done, good and faithful servant. You have been faithful over a few things, I will set you over many things]]. God does not measure success by human standards but by faithfulness.

Finally, these verses show that every believer is a steward. Not only leaders, but every believer has been entrusted with gifts, time, resources, and the gospel itself. All will give an account for how they have managed what God has given.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses help believers view leaders correctly. Pastors and teachers are not celebrities to be idolized or competitors to be ranked. They are servants and stewards who answer to Christ. Believers can honor them while remembering that they are accountable to God.

They also encourage leaders to focus on faithfulness rather than popularity. Leaders can ask not "Am I impressive?" but "Am I faithful to what God has entrusted to me?"

Finally, these verses remind every believer of their stewardship. Believers can ask how faithfully they are managing their time, money, gifts, relationships, and opportunities to share the gospel. Faithfulness in small things matters to God.

### 1 Corinthians 4:5 — Judge Nothing Before the Time

verse: 1 Corinthians 4:5

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had said that he was not concerned about being judged by the Corinthians or by any human court. He did not even judge himself, because although his conscience was clear, that did not make him innocent. The Lord is the one who judges.

[[v5|Therefore judge nothing before the time, until the Lord comes]]. The Corinthians had been passing judgment on their leaders, comparing them and declaring some better than others. Paul tells them to stop. It is not their place to make final judgments about the worth of God's servants. That judgment belongs to the Lord, and it will happen when He returns.

[[v5|who will both bring to light the hidden things of darkness and reveal the counsels of the hearts]]. When the Lord comes, He will reveal everything. Hidden things will be brought into the light. He will expose the motives of every heart. Human beings can see only outward actions; God sees the heart.

[[v5|Then each man will get his praise from God]]. This is a surprising and encouraging conclusion. Paul does not say "each will receive condemnation," but [[v5|each man will get his praise from God]]. For faithful servants, the day of judgment will be a day of commendation. God will recognize and praise what was done faithfully, even what was hidden from human eyes.

This verse does not forbid all evaluation. Elsewhere, Paul calls the church to judge sin within the community (5:12) and to test teaching (1 Thessalonians 5:21). But it forbids the kind of final, condemning judgment of other people's worth and motives that only God can make.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that only God can judge the heart. Humans see the outside; God sees the inside. Samuel was told, [[1 Samuel 16:7|For man looks at the outward appearance, but the LORD looks at the heart]]. Believers cannot fully know others' motives, and they should not presume to.

It also shows that God will bring justice. Nothing hidden will remain hidden. Those who have served faithfully in secret will be recognized, and those who have hidden wrongdoing will be exposed. Jesus said, [[Luke 8:17|For nothing is hidden that will not be revealed, nor anything secret that will not be known and come to light]].

Finally, the verse brings hope. God's judgment is not only about punishment. For faithful believers, it includes praise. God delights to commend His servants.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse calls believers to stop passing final judgment on others. It is easy to assume the worst about people's motives or to rank others as better or worse. Believers can leave the judgment of hearts to God.

It also encourages believers who feel misjudged. When others criticize or misunderstand them, believers can remember that God sees their hearts and will reveal the truth.

Finally, the verse encourages believers to serve faithfully, even in secret. God sees what others do not see, and He will praise faithfulness. Believers do not need human recognition; they can wait for God's commendation.

### 1 Corinthians 4:7 — What Do You Have That You Did Not Receive?

verse: 1 Corinthians 4:7

#### Simple Explanation

The Corinthians were proud. They boasted about their favorite leaders, their spiritual gifts, their knowledge, and their wisdom. They were "puffed up," inflated with self-importance, looking down on one another.

Paul asks three piercing questions.

[[v7|For who makes you different?]] Who decided that you are better than others? What gives you the right to consider yourself superior? The answer implied is: no one. If there is any difference between believers, it is God's doing, not theirs.

[[v7|And what do you have that you didn’t receive?]] This question goes to the heart of human pride. Everything believers have, their life, abilities, opportunities, spiritual gifts, faith, and salvation, came from God. They received it as a gift. They did not create it themselves.

[[v7|But if you did receive it, why do you boast as if you had not received it?]] If everything is a gift, why boast as if it were an achievement? Boasting about a gift makes no sense. It would be like a child boasting about a present they received, as if they had made it themselves.

These questions demolish pride. No one can boast before God, because everything is grace.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that everything is a gift from God. James writes, [[James 1:17|Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights]]. Life, breath, talents, opportunities, and salvation itself all come from God.

It also shows that pride is a form of forgetfulness. Proud people forget that what they have came from God. Humility is simply remembering the truth: everything is grace.

Finally, the verse shows that gifts are meant to lead to gratitude, not pride. When believers recognize that everything comes from God, their response should be thanksgiving and humble service, using their gifts to bless others.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse challenges believers to examine their hearts for pride. Believers can ask whether they take credit for things God has given them, such as their intelligence, abilities, success, or even spiritual growth.

It also encourages believers to respond to gifts with gratitude. Instead of boasting, believers can thank God for every good thing in their lives.

Finally, the verse helps believers treat others with respect. If everything is a gift, then no believer is inherently better than another. Believers can honor others' gifts without envy and use their own gifts without pride.

### 1 Corinthians 4:15–16 — Imitate Me

verse: 1 Corinthians 4:15-16

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had just used sharp irony to expose the Corinthians' pride. Now his tone becomes tender. He explains that he wrote these hard words not to shame them but to warn them as his beloved children.

[[v15|For though you have ten thousand tutors in Christ, you don’t have many fathers]]. A "tutor" (paidagogos) was a slave in a wealthy household who supervised a child's education and conduct. Children might have many tutors over the years, but only one father. The Corinthians had many teachers, perhaps more than they needed, but only one spiritual father.

[[v15|For in Christ Jesus, I became your father through the Good News]]. Paul had brought them to faith by preaching the gospel. In this sense, he was their spiritual father. He did not mean that he replaced God as Father, but that he had a unique role in their spiritual birth. His relationship with them was not that of a hired teacher but of a loving parent.

[[v16|I beg you therefore, be imitators of me]]. Children learn by imitating their parents. Paul urged the Corinthians to follow his example. This may seem proud, but Paul's example was one of humility, service, and suffering for Christ, the very opposite of the Corinthians' pride. He writes later, [[1 Corinthians 11:1|Be imitators of me, even as I also am of Christ]]. Paul was not asking them to imitate him instead of Christ, but to imitate him as he imitated Christ.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show the deep love that should mark spiritual leadership. Paul was not a distant teacher but a father who loved his children. His correction came from love. Hebrews says, [[Hebrews 12:6|For whom the Lord loves, he disciplines]].

They also show the importance of example in discipleship. People learn not only from teaching but from seeing faith lived out. Paul did not only teach; he lived what he taught, and he invited others to follow his example.

Finally, these verses show that Christian maturity is passed on through relationships. Paul sent Timothy, his "beloved and faithful child," to remind the Corinthians of his ways. Faith is often passed from one generation to the next through mentoring relationships.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage mature believers to invest in others. Believers can serve as spiritual mothers and fathers to younger believers, guiding them with love, patience, and example.

They also challenge believers to live in a way that others can imitate. Believers can ask whether their lives reflect Christ in a way that would help others grow if they followed their example.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to look for godly examples. Believers can find mature believers whose lives reflect Christ and learn from them, imitating their faith (Hebrews 13:7).

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

1 Corinthians 4 teaches that leaders are servants of Christ and stewards of God's mysteries, and that what God requires most is faithfulness. Believers can view their leaders through this lens, honoring them as servants rather than idolizing them as celebrities. And every believer can see themselves as a steward, faithfully managing the gifts and opportunities God has given.

The chapter also warns against judging others before the time. Only the Lord sees the hidden motives of the heart, and He will reveal them when He comes. Believers can leave final judgment to God, trusting that He will bring justice and that faithful servants will receive His praise.

1 Corinthians 4 exposes pride with a simple question: What do you have that you did not receive? Everything believers have is a gift from God. This truth leads to gratitude and humility. Paul's description of the apostles' suffering shows that following Christ often involves weakness, hardship, and dishonor in the world's eyes, the opposite of the pride the Corinthians displayed.

Finally, the chapter shows Paul's tender love as a spiritual father. His correction came from love, and he invited the Corinthians to imitate him as he imitated Christ. Believers can invest in younger believers as spiritual mothers and fathers, live in a way that others can follow, and remember that God's kingdom is a matter not of talk but of transforming power.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for entrusting me with Your gospel and with the gifts You have given me. Help me to be a faithful steward, more concerned with faithfulness than with being impressive. Keep me from judging others before the time, and help me to leave the judgment of hearts to You. Thank You that You see what others do not see, and that You will praise those who serve You faithfully. Humble me, Lord. Remind me that everything I have, I have received from You. Turn my pride into gratitude. Give me the heart of Paul, who suffered for Your sake and blessed those who cursed him. Make me a spiritual father or mother to others, and help me to live in a way that others can imitate as I imitate You. Let my faith be not just in words but in Your power. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
