# Revelation 6
theme: The Opening of the Seals
main: The Lamb begins to open the seven seals of the scroll. As He opens the first four, each of the four living creatures calls out, and four horsemen ride out. The first rides a white horse, holding a bow, wearing a crown, and going out to conquer. The second rides a red horse and is given power to take peace from the earth so that people kill one another. The third rides a black horse and holds a pair of scales, while a voice announces prices that signal severe scarcity, yet the oil and wine are not to be harmed. The fourth rides a pale horse; his name is Death, and Hades follows him, and they are given authority over a fourth of the earth to kill by sword, famine, plague, and wild beasts. When the Lamb opens the fifth seal, John sees under the altar the souls of those who were killed for the word of God and their testimony. They cry out, asking how long until God judges and avenges their blood. Each is given a white robe and told to rest a little longer until the number of their fellow servants who will be killed is complete. When the sixth seal is opened, there is a great earthquake; the sun turns black, the moon turns like blood, the stars fall, the sky recedes like a scroll, and every mountain and island is moved. People of every rank hide in caves and call on the mountains to fall on them and hide them from the face of the One on the throne and from the wrath of the Lamb, for the great day of their wrath has come, and who can stand?

## Chapter Background and Story

In chapter 5, the Lamb received the sealed scroll. Now He begins to open it. As each seal is broken, a new scene unfolds. The opening of the seals is the first of three series of seven judgments in Revelation, followed by the seven trumpets (chapters 8–11) and the seven bowls (chapters 15–16).

A key point to notice is who opens the seals: the Lamb. The events that follow are not random disasters or the work of evil powers acting freely. They unfold under the authority of the crucified and risen Christ. Even the troubles of history are within His control.

### How the Seals Are Understood

Interpreters understand the seals in several ways. **Futurist** interpreters generally see them as events of a future period of tribulation before Christ's return. **Preterist** interpreters connect them with events of the first century, such as wars, famines, and the persecution of Christians under Rome. **Historicist** interpreters have linked them with successive stages in the history of the Roman Empire and the church. **Idealist** interpreters see them as describing conditions that mark the whole period between Christ's first and second comings: war, conflict, scarcity, death, persecution, and the certainty of final judgment.

Many readers notice that the first four seals closely resemble Jesus' teaching in Matthew 24, Mark 13, and Luke 21, where He describes wars, famines, earthquakes, persecution, and cosmic signs as [[Matthew 24:8|the beginning of birth pains]]. On this reading, the seals depict the kind of suffering that will mark history until the end, leading up to the final day of judgment in the sixth seal. The four horsemen also echo Zechariah 1:8–11 and 6:1–8, where horses of different colors are sent out over the earth by God.

### The Four Horsemen (6:1–8)

The first horseman is studied below.

[[v3|When he opened the second seal, I heard the second living creature saying, “Come!”]] [[v4|Another came out, a red horse. To him who sat on it was given power to take peace from the earth, and that they should kill one another. There was given to him a great sword]]. The red horse represents war and bloodshed. The Roman Empire boasted of the "Pax Romana," the peace of Rome, but this rider takes peace from the earth. Human beings turn against one another in violence. Notice the repeated phrase "was given": this rider acts only by permission. His power is limited by God.

[[v5|When he opened the third seal, I heard the third living creature saying, “Come and see!” And behold, a black horse, and he who sat on it had a balance in his hand]]. [[v6|I heard a voice in the middle of the four living creatures saying, “A choenix of wheat for a denarius, and three choenix of barley for a denarius! Don’t damage the oil and the wine!”]] The black horse represents famine and economic hardship. The scales were used to measure food. A choenix was roughly a quart, about a day's ration for one person, and a denarius was about a day's wage for a laborer. These prices are many times higher than normal. A worker's whole wage would buy only enough wheat for himself, or enough cheaper barley for a small family. The command not to harm the oil and wine may suggest that the famine is limited, or it may highlight an injustice in which basic foods become unaffordable while luxuries remain available to the wealthy. Either way, the voice from among the living creatures shows that even this hardship has limits set by God.

[[v7|When he opened the fourth seal, I heard the fourth living creature saying, “Come and see!”]] [[v8|And behold, a pale horse, and the name of he who sat on it was Death. Hades followed with him. Authority over one fourth of the earth, to kill with the sword, with famine, with death, and by the wild animals of the earth was given to him]]. The word translated "pale" describes a sickly greenish-gray color, the color of a corpse. The rider is named Death, and Hades, the realm of the dead, follows him. The four means of death, sword, famine, plague ("death"), and wild beasts, echo God's "four severe judgments" in Ezekiel 14:21. Again, the destruction is limited: one fourth of the earth. And it was "given" to him. Death itself acts only within limits set by God, and Christ holds the keys of Death and Hades (Revelation 1:18).

### The Great Day of Wrath (6:12–17)

[[v12|I saw when he opened the sixth seal, and there was a great earthquake. The sun became black as sackcloth made of hair, and the whole moon became as blood]]. [[v13|The stars of the sky fell to the earth, like a fig tree dropping its unripe figs when it is shaken by a great wind]]. [[v14|The sky was removed like a scroll when it is rolled up. Every mountain and island was moved out of its place]].

The sixth seal uses imagery drawn from the Old Testament prophets, who described the day of the Lord with cosmic signs: the sun darkened, the moon turned to blood, the stars falling, and the heavens rolled up like a scroll (Isaiah 13:10; 34:4; Joel 2:31; Haggai 2:6). Jesus used the same language to describe the time of His coming (Matthew 24:29).

Interpreters understand these images in different ways. Some take them as literal cosmic upheavals at the end of the age. Others understand them as prophetic symbols for the collapse of earthly powers and the shaking of the whole order of the world, since the prophets used such language for the fall of nations like Babylon and Edom. Still others see both: symbolic language that points to a real, final, world-shaking day of judgment. In every view, the sixth seal portrays the overturning of everything human beings thought was stable, as God comes in judgment. The response of humanity is studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### Revelation 6:1–2 — The Rider on the White Horse

verse: Revelation 6:1-2

#### Simple Explanation

The Lamb opens the first seal, and one of the four living creatures calls out with a voice like thunder: "Come and see!" The command may be addressed to John, inviting him to look, or to the rider, summoning him to go out.

Then John sees "a white horse," and its rider "had a bow." The rider is given a crown, and "he came out conquering, and to conquer." He is a conqueror on a mission of conquest.

Unlike the other three riders, this one's identity is not obvious, and interpreters have understood him in different ways.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Some interpreters see this rider as Christ, or as the victorious spread of the gospel. They point out that white in Revelation usually represents purity and victory, and that Christ later appears on a white horse (19:11). Jesus also said that the gospel would be preached to all nations before the end (Matthew 24:14). On this view, the first seal shows the gospel going out victoriously even as war, famine, and death follow.

Many other interpreters see the rider as a symbol of conquest and militarism, or as a false messiah or deceiving power that imitates Christ. They note that the other three riders all represent destructive forces, so the first likely does as well. The bow may recall the Parthians, mounted archers who were Rome's feared enemies on its eastern border. Jesus warned that many would come in His name and deceive many, and that this would come before wars and famines (Matthew 24:4–7). On this view, the white horse represents a deceptive imitation of Christ's victory, the conquering ambition that leads to war, famine, and death.

The second view fits more naturally with the pattern of the four horsemen as a group, though both views agree on an essential truth: all of these forces are released by the Lamb and remain under His authority.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can learn from this passage to be discerning. Not everything that looks victorious or pure is from God. Conquering powers, political movements, and charismatic leaders can promise peace and glory while leading to destruction. Jesus' warning not to be deceived remains urgent.

At the same time, believers can take comfort that even the forces of conquest and destruction do not act independently of God. The Lamb opens the seals. History is under His control, and nothing can separate His people from His love.

This passage also invites believers to place their hope in the true Conqueror, Jesus Christ, whose victory comes through the cross and whose kingdom brings true and lasting peace.

### Revelation 6:9–11 — How Long, O Lord?

verse: Revelation 6:9-11

#### Simple Explanation

When the Lamb opens the fifth seal, the scene shifts from earth to heaven. John sees "underneath the altar the souls of those who had been killed for the Word of God, and for the testimony of the Lamb which they had." These are martyrs, believers who were killed because of their faithfulness to God's word and their witness to Jesus.

They are "underneath the altar." In the Old Testament sacrificial system, the blood of sacrifices was poured out at the base of the altar (Leviticus 4:7). The martyrs' lives have been poured out like an offering to God.

They cry out in verse 10: "How long, Master, the holy and true, until you judge and avenge our blood on those who dwell on the earth?" They are not asking for personal revenge. They are appealing to God, the holy and true Judge, to bring justice.

In verse 11, each of them is given "a long white robe," a sign of victory, purity, and honor. They are told to "rest yet for a while." Their prayer will be answered, but not yet. More of their fellow believers will also suffer and die before God's justice is complete.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The cry "How long?" echoes the prayers of God's people throughout Scripture (Psalm 13:1–2; 79:5–10; Habakkuk 1:2). It is not a cry of unbelief but of faith. The martyrs know that God is "holy and true." They trust that He will judge justly. Their question is about timing, not about whether justice will come.

This passage shows that God takes injustice seriously. The suffering of His people is not forgotten. Their blood cries out to Him, as Abel's blood cried out from the ground (Genesis 4:10). God will act. Many interpreters see the later judgments in Revelation, especially the trumpets that follow the prayers of the saints (Revelation 8:3–5), as God's answer to this very cry.

The instruction to "rest yet for a while" acknowledges a painful mystery. God's timing is not always what His people would choose. Yet the martyrs are not left without comfort. They are given white robes and invited to rest. They are safe in God's presence, honored, and assured that justice will come. Their deaths were not defeats; they were victories.

It is worth noticing that the martyrs entrust vengeance to God rather than taking it themselves. This matches the teaching of the New Testament: [[Romans 12:19|Don’t seek revenge yourselves, beloved, but give place to God’s wrath. For it is written, “Vengeance belongs to me; I will repay, says the Lord.”]]

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many believers around the world today suffer and die for their faith. This passage assures them, and all believers, that God sees their suffering and will bring justice. Their faithfulness is honored in heaven.

Believers who face injustice of any kind can bring their cries to God, as the martyrs did. It is not wrong to ask "How long?" Honest lament is part of faithful prayer. Believers can pour out their grief and longing for justice to the God who is holy and true.

At the same time, this passage calls believers to leave vengeance in God's hands. Rather than seeking revenge, they can trust God to judge rightly, work for justice by lawful and loving means, and continue to bear faithful witness, even under pressure.

### Revelation 6:15–17 — Who Is Able to Stand?

verse: Revelation 6:15-17

#### Simple Explanation

When the sixth seal is opened and the world is shaken, people of every rank try to hide: "The kings of the earth, the princes, the commanding officers, the rich, the strong, and every slave and free person, hid themselves in the caves and in the rocks of the mountains." No social position, wealth, or power can protect them.

They cry out in verse 16: "Fall on us, and hide us from the face of him who sits on the throne, and from the wrath of the Lamb." They would rather be crushed by mountains than face God.

Verse 17 explains their terror: "for the great day of his wrath has come, and who is able to stand?" The day of judgment has arrived, and no one can stand on their own strength before a holy God.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The phrase "the wrath of the Lamb" is striking. Lambs are usually symbols of gentleness. But the Lamb who was slain for sinners is also the Judge of all. Those who reject His sacrifice will face His judgment. God's wrath is not uncontrolled anger, but His holy and just opposition to evil. The same Jesus who offers grace will hold the world accountable.

The cry for the mountains to fall on them echoes Hosea 10:8 and Jesus' words to the women of Jerusalem (Luke 23:30). It shows the terror of facing God without having been reconciled to Him. The list of people, from kings to slaves, shows that judgment comes to all. Every human being will stand before God.

The question "who is able to stand?" is answered in the very next chapter. Revelation 7 shows the servants of God sealed for protection and a great multitude standing before the throne, clothed in white robes washed in the blood of the Lamb. Those who belong to the Lamb can stand. Their confidence rests not on their own righteousness, but on His sacrifice.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage is a sober reminder that God's judgment is real. Every person will one day stand before Him. Wealth, power, and status will make no difference. The only question will be whether one belongs to the Lamb.

For those who trust in Christ, this passage is not a cause for terror but for gratitude. They do not need to hide from God's face, because the Lamb has borne their judgment. They can approach God with confidence because of His grace.

This passage also gives believers urgency in sharing the gospel. The day of the Lord will come. The good news is that the Lamb who will judge the world is the same Lamb who died to save it, and He invites all to come to Him while there is time.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Revelation 6 shows that the troubles of history, war, famine, disease, and death, are real, but they are not out of control. The Lamb opens the seals. Each rider acts only by permission and within limits. Believers can face the turmoil of the world with confidence that Christ holds history in His hands.

The chapter also warns against deception. The rider on the white horse may represent a false conqueror who imitates Christ. Believers are called to discern carefully and to place their hope only in the true King, whose victory comes through the cross.

The cry of the martyrs reminds believers that God sees the suffering of His people. Injustice will not have the final word. Believers can bring their honest laments to God, asking "How long?" while trusting Him to bring justice in His time. They can leave vengeance in His hands and continue to bear faithful witness.

Finally, the sixth seal reminds believers that a day of judgment is coming when every human power will be shaken. On that day, no one will be able to stand on their own. But those who belong to the Lamb, washed in His blood, will stand before the throne. This truth calls believers to rest in His grace and to share the good news with others while there is time.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, Lamb of God, thank You that history is in Your hands. When I see war, famine, sickness, and death in the world, help me to remember that nothing happens outside Your authority. Guard me from every deception, and keep my hope fixed on You, the true Conqueror. I lift up to You all who suffer for their faith around the world; comfort them, strengthen them, and bring Your justice in Your perfect time. When I cry out, "How long?" help me to trust that You are holy and true. Teach me to leave vengeance in Your hands. Thank You that because You bore my judgment, I do not need to hide from Your face, but can stand before You by Your grace. Give me a heart to share this hope with others. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
