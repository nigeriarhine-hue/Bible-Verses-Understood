# Revelation 11
theme: The Two Witnesses and the Seventh Trumpet
main: John is given a measuring rod and told to measure the temple of God, the altar, and those who worship there, but to leave out the outer court, because it has been given to the nations, who will trample the holy city for forty-two months. God will empower His two witnesses, clothed in sackcloth, to prophesy for 1,260 days. They are the two olive trees and the two lampstands that stand before the Lord of the earth. No one can harm them until their work is done: fire comes from their mouths to consume their enemies, and they have power to shut the sky, turn waters into blood, and strike the earth with plagues. When they have finished their testimony, the beast that comes up from the abyss will attack, overpower, and kill them. Their bodies will lie in the street of the great city, which is figuratively called Sodom and Egypt, where their Lord was crucified. For three and a half days people from every nation will gaze on their bodies and refuse them burial, and the inhabitants of the earth will celebrate and exchange gifts. But after three and a half days, the breath of life from God enters them, they stand up, and terror strikes those who see them. A loud voice from heaven calls them to come up, and they ascend in a cloud as their enemies watch. At that moment a great earthquake strikes; a tenth of the city falls, seven thousand people are killed, and the survivors are terrified and give glory to the God of heaven. Then the seventh angel sounds his trumpet, and loud voices in heaven proclaim that the kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of His Messiah, and He will reign forever. The twenty-four elders fall on their faces and worship, thanking God for taking His great power and beginning to reign, for the time has come to judge the dead, to reward His servants, and to destroy those who destroy the earth. God's temple in heaven is opened, and the ark of His covenant is seen, with lightning, thunder, an earthquake, and a great hailstorm.

## Chapter Background and Story

Revelation 11 continues the interlude that began in chapter 10. In chapter 10, John was commissioned to prophesy. In chapter 11, the prophetic witness of God's people is portrayed through the image of two witnesses. The chapter then closes with the sounding of the seventh trumpet and a great announcement of God's kingdom.

This is one of the most debated chapters in Revelation. Its images, the measuring of the temple, the forty-two months, the two witnesses, the great city, are understood in different ways by different interpreters. Yet its central message is clear: God protects His people, empowers them to bear witness, allows them to suffer, and vindicates them in the end. And His kingdom will triumph over the kingdoms of this world.

### Measuring the Temple (11:1–2)

[[v1|A reed like a rod was given to me. Someone said, “Rise and measure God’s temple, and the altar, and those who worship in it.]] [[v2|Leave out the court which is outside of the temple, and don’t measure it, for it has been given to the nations. They will tread the holy city under foot for forty-two months.]]

Measuring in the prophets is a sign of ownership, protection, or preparation for rebuilding (Ezekiel 40–42; Zechariah 2:1–5). John is told to measure the temple, the altar, and the worshipers, marking them as God's own. The outer court, however, is left out and given over to the nations.

Interpreters understand this in several ways. Some **futurists** see a literal rebuilt temple in Jerusalem in a future period. Some **preterists** relate it to the temple in Jerusalem before its destruction in A.D. 70. Many interpreters understand the temple symbolically as the people of God, since the New Testament often describes the church as God's temple (1 Corinthians 3:16; 2 Corinthians 6:16; Ephesians 2:21). On this reading, God's people are spiritually protected even as they are outwardly exposed to persecution, much like the sealing in chapter 7.

"Forty-two months" equals three and a half years, the same as 1,260 days (v3) and "a time, times, and half a time" (12:14). This period comes from Daniel 7:25 and 12:7, where it describes a limited time of persecution of God's people. Some take it as a literal three and a half years in the future. Others understand it as a symbolic number for the whole period of the church's witness and suffering between Christ's first and second comings: a limited period, half of seven, a broken and incomplete time that will come to an end.

### The Two Witnesses (11:3–14)

The identity and ministry of the two witnesses, and their death and vindication, are studied below.

[[v8|Their dead bodies will be in the street of the great city, which spiritually is called Sodom and Egypt, where also their Lord was crucified]]. "The great city" is described in symbolic terms. It is "spiritually," or figuratively, called Sodom, known for its wickedness, and Egypt, known for its oppression of God's people. It is also identified as the place "where also their Lord was crucified," which points to Jerusalem. Elsewhere in Revelation, "the great city" refers to Babylon, the symbol of the world system opposed to God (16:19; 17:18). Many interpreters therefore see the great city as a picture of every human society that rejects God, including the Jerusalem that rejected Jesus, the Rome that persecuted the church, and the world in every age that opposes God's witnesses.

[[v13|In that day there was a great earthquake, and a tenth of the city fell. Seven thousand people were killed in the earthquake, and the rest were terrified and gave glory to the God of heaven]]. This verse is remarkable. In contrast to the sixth trumpet, where survivors did not repent (9:20–21), here the survivors "gave glory to the God of heaven." Some interpreters see this as genuine repentance and conversion; others see it as a terrified acknowledgment of God's power without true faith. The proportions are notable: in the Old Testament, judgment often left only a remnant of a tenth or seven thousand (Isaiah 6:13; 1 Kings 19:18). Here the pattern is reversed. Only a tenth fall, and the great majority survive and give glory to God. Many see this as a sign that the faithful witness and vindication of God's people will lead many to turn to Him.

[[v14|The second woe is past. Behold, the third woe comes quickly]].

### The Seventh Trumpet (11:15–19)

The great announcement at the seventh trumpet and the elders' song are studied below.

[[v19|God’s temple that is in heaven was opened, and the ark of the Lord’s covenant was seen in his temple. Lightnings, sounds, thunders, an earthquake, and great hail followed]]. The ark of the covenant was the most sacred object in Israel's worship, kept in the Most Holy Place. It represented God's presence and His covenant with His people. It had been lost when Babylon destroyed the temple. Now John sees it in heaven, a sign that God's covenant with His people remains and that access to His presence is open. The lightning, thunder, earthquake, and hail mark this as a climactic moment, as at the end of the seals (8:5) and the bowls (16:18–21).

## Important Verses Explained

### Revelation 11:3–6 — Two Witnesses Empowered by God

verse: Revelation 11:3-6

#### Simple Explanation

God says: "I will give power to my two witnesses, and they will prophesy one thousand two hundred sixty days, clothed in sackcloth." Their mission is to bear witness, speaking God's message. Sackcloth was a rough garment worn in mourning and repentance. Their message is a call to repent.

Verse 4 describes them: "These are the two olive trees and the two lamp stands, standing before the Lord of the earth." This recalls Zechariah 4, where two olive trees supply oil to a lampstand, representing Zerubbabel the governor and Joshua the high priest, God's anointed servants (Zechariah 4:11–14). In Revelation, lampstands represent churches (Revelation 1:20).

Verses 5–6 describe their power. No one can harm them during their ministry: "fire proceeds out of their mouth and devours their enemies." They have power "to shut up the sky, that it may not rain," like Elijah (1 Kings 17:1). And they have power "over the waters, to turn them into blood, and to strike the earth with every plague," like Moses in Egypt.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The identity of the two witnesses has been widely discussed. Some interpreters believe they are two individual prophets who will appear in the future, often identified as Moses and Elijah, because of the miracles described, or as Enoch and Elijah, because neither experienced ordinary death. Others identify them with figures in the early church, such as Peter and Paul. Many interpreters understand the two witnesses as a symbol of the witnessing church, the people of God bearing testimony to Christ in the world. They note that the witnesses are called "two lamp stands," and lampstands in Revelation represent churches. Two may also reflect the biblical requirement of two witnesses for valid testimony (Deuteronomy 19:15), and the fact that Jesus sent His disciples out two by two.

Whether individuals or a symbol of the church, the witnesses share the character of Moses and Elijah, the great representatives of the Law and the Prophets. They speak God's word with power. Their ministry is protected by God until it is complete. The "fire" from their mouths likely represents the power of God's word, as when God told Jeremiah, [[Jeremiah 5:14|I will make my words in your mouth fire]].

The key point is that God's witnesses are invincible until their work is done. No opposition can stop the testimony God has appointed. This is a powerful encouragement for believers facing opposition.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers today share in the calling of the two witnesses. The church is called to bear faithful witness to Christ in the world, speaking God's truth, calling people to repentance, and shining as a lampstand in the darkness.

This passage encourages believers that their witness is empowered by God. They do not speak in their own strength but by the Spirit, like the oil that fed Zechariah's lampstand: [[Zechariah 4:6|Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit]].

Believers can also take courage that God protects His witnesses until their work is done. They do not need to live in fear. Their lives are in God's hands, and nothing can cut short the work He has given them.

### Revelation 11:7–12 — Killed, Raised, and Vindicated

verse: Revelation 11:7-12

#### Simple Explanation

When the witnesses "have finished their testimony," their protection is lifted. "The beast that comes up out of the abyss will make war with them, and overcome them, and kill them." This is the first mention of the beast, who will be described in detail in chapter 13. Evil seems to win.

The world's response is cruel. People "will not allow their dead bodies to be laid in a tomb," a terrible insult in the ancient world. "Those who dwell on the earth will rejoice over them, and they will be glad. They will give gifts to one another." The world celebrates the silencing of God's witnesses, "because these two prophets tormented those who dwell on the earth." Their message of truth was unwelcome.

But the story does not end there. "After the three and a half days, the breath of life from God entered into them, and they stood on their feet." God raises them from the dead. "Great fear fell on those who saw them."

Then "a loud voice from heaven" says, "Come up here!" and "they went up into heaven in a cloud, and their enemies saw them." The witnesses are publicly vindicated.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The story of the two witnesses follows the pattern of Jesus' own life. Like Jesus, they bear witness, are killed in the city "where also their Lord was crucified," are raised after three days (here, three and a half), and ascend to heaven in a cloud (Acts 1:9). The witnesses share in their Lord's suffering, death, resurrection, and vindication.

"The breath of life from God entered into them" recalls Ezekiel 37, where God breathes life into a valley of dry bones and they stand on their feet, a vast army. It also recalls the creation of Adam, when God breathed into him the breath of life (Genesis 2:7). God is the giver of life, and death cannot hold those who belong to Him.

The three and a half days of apparent defeat are brief compared with the 1,260 days of witness. Evil's triumph is short-lived. The world's celebration turns to terror as God reverses the verdict.

Whether one understands the witnesses as individuals or as the church, the message is the same: faithful witnesses may be silenced and even killed, but God will raise and vindicate them. Their apparent defeat is not the end of the story.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers who suffer for their faith can take courage from this passage. Faithful witness may lead to rejection, mockery, and even death. The world may celebrate when God's people are silenced. But God has the final word.

This passage also reminds believers that the pattern of the Christian life is the pattern of Jesus: faithful witness, suffering, death, and resurrection. Believers are called to follow Him, trusting that suffering is not the end.

Finally, believers can hold on to the hope of resurrection. The breath of life from God will one day enter all who belong to Him. Death will not have the last word for any of God's people.

### Revelation 11:15–18 — The Kingdom of Our Lord

verse: Revelation 11:15-18

#### Simple Explanation

When the seventh angel sounds, there are "great voices in heaven" proclaiming: "The kingdom of the world has become the Kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ. He will reign forever and ever!"

This is the great announcement of the whole book. The kingdom of the world, all the power and authority that human beings and evil forces have claimed, now belongs to God and His Messiah.

In verses 16–17, the twenty-four elders fall on their faces and worship: "We give you thanks, Lord God, the Almighty, the one who is and who was, because you have taken your great power and reigned."

Verse 18 describes what this reign means: "The nations were angry, and your wrath came, as did the time for the dead to be judged." God's reign brings judgment on the rebellious, reward for "your bondservants the prophets," "the saints and those who fear your name, to the small and the great," and destruction for "those who destroy the earth."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This announcement fulfills the hope of the whole Bible. Psalm 2 described the nations raging against the Lord and His anointed, while God laughs from heaven and establishes His King. Daniel 7 foresaw that the kingdom would be given to the Son of Man and to the holy ones. Here, that kingdom is declared to have come.

Notice a subtle change in God's title. Earlier He was called the One "who is and who was and who is to come" (1:4, 8; 4:8). Now the elders say only "the one who is and who was." The phrase "who is to come" is missing, because He has come. His reign is now fully established.

Interestingly, the seventh trumpet does not describe a final battle or catastrophe. It describes worship. The ultimate goal of history is not destruction but the reign of God, celebrated by His people.

The phrase "to destroy those who destroy the earth" is significant. God's judgment is aimed at those who ruin His creation, whether through violence, oppression, idolatry, or greed. God is not the destroyer of His world; He is its restorer. His judgment removes those who corrupt it so that it can be made new.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can live with confident hope. However powerful the kingdoms of this world may seem, they will not last. The kingdom of God and of His Christ will reign forever. This truth can give believers courage, perspective, and peace.

This passage also calls believers to worship. The elders respond to God's reign with thanksgiving. Believers can thank God now for His sovereignty and for the certainty of His coming kingdom.

The promise of reward for "the small and the great" assures believers that no act of faithfulness is forgotten. Whether their service is visible or hidden, God will reward those who fear His name.

Finally, God's concern for "those who destroy the earth" reminds believers that His creation matters to Him. They can honor Him by caring for His world and by opposing what corrupts and destroys it.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Revelation 11 portrays the calling and destiny of God's witnesses. Measured and protected by God, they bear testimony in a hostile world. Like Moses and Elijah, they speak with power. Like Jesus, they suffer, die, and are raised. Believers today share in this calling. They are lampstands, called to shine Christ's light, empowered by the Spirit, protected until their work is done.

The chapter is honest about the cost of witness. The world may reject, mock, and even kill God's messengers, and it may celebrate their silencing. But God has the final word. He raises His witnesses and vindicates them before their enemies. Believers can bear witness with courage, knowing that their faithfulness is never wasted.

The chapter also shows that faithful witness can lead others to God. After the vindication of the witnesses, many give glory to the God of heaven. Believers can hope and pray that their witness, even through suffering, will draw others to Christ.

Finally, the seventh trumpet declares the triumph of God's kingdom. The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of His Christ, and He will reign forever. This is the hope that sustains God's people through every trial. Believers can live in that hope, worship God with thanksgiving, and look forward to the day when His reign is fully revealed.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord God Almighty, thank You that You know and protect Your people. Make me a faithful witness, a lampstand that shines with the light of Christ. Fill me with Your Spirit, so that I speak Your truth not by my own might or power, but by Your Spirit. When faithfulness brings rejection or suffering, give me courage to follow Jesus in the way of the cross, trusting that You will raise and vindicate Your people. Use the witness of Your church to draw many to give You glory. Thank You that the kingdom of the world will become the kingdom of our Lord and of His Christ, and that He will reign forever and ever. I give You thanks, for You have taken Your great power and reigned. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
