# 1 John 3
theme: Children of God, Loving in Deed and Truth
main: John marvels at the great love the Father has given, that believers should be called children of God, which they truly are, though the world does not recognize them. What they will be has not yet been revealed, but when Christ appears they will be like Him, for they will see Him as He is, and everyone with this hope purifies themselves as He is pure. Sin is lawlessness, and Christ appeared to take away sins and to destroy the devil's works; those born of God do not keep on living in sin, because God's seed remains in them. The children of God are known by doing what is right and by loving their brothers and sisters. Believers are not to be like Cain, who murdered his brother, and should not be surprised if the world hates them. They know they have passed from death to life because they love one another; whoever hates a brother or sister is a murderer at heart. Love is known in this: Christ laid down His life for us, and so we ought to lay down our lives for one another, not loving with words only, but with actions and in truth, sharing with those in need. In this way believers reassure their hearts before God, who is greater than their hearts and knows everything. God's command is to believe in the name of His Son Jesus Christ and to love one another, and those who obey remain in Him, knowing it by the Spirit He has given.

## Chapter Background and Story

At the end of chapter 2, John said that those who practice righteousness have been born of God (1 John 2:29). The idea of being "born of God" leads him to celebrate one of the most wonderful truths of the gospel: believers are God's children.

From this truth, John draws two practical implications. First, God's children resemble their Father in righteousness. They do not continue in a settled pattern of sin. Second, God's children love one another. Love is the family resemblance that marks them as God's own.

John's readers had been shaken by the departure of false teachers, who claimed special spiritual status but did not live righteously or love the community. John reassures the faithful believers that they truly belong to God, and he gives them clear signs by which to recognize genuine faith. He also addresses believers whose consciences trouble them, assuring them that God is greater than their hearts.

### See What Love (3:1–3)

The wonder of being God's children is studied below. [[v3|Everyone who has this hope set on him purifies himself, even as he is pure]]. The hope of seeing Christ and becoming like Him motivates purity now. Those who look forward to meeting Him want to be ready.

### Children of God Do Not Continue in Sin (3:4–10)

[[v4|Everyone who sins also commits lawlessness. Sin is lawlessness]]. Sin is not a minor matter. It is rebellion against God's law, a rejection of His authority.

[[v5|You know that he was revealed to take away our sins, and no sin is in him]]. Christ came to remove sin, and He Himself was sinless. [[v6|Whoever remains in him doesn’t sin. Whoever sins hasn’t seen him and doesn’t know him]]. The meaning of these strong statements is studied below.

[[v7|Little children, let no one lead you astray. He who does righteousness is righteous, even as he is righteous]]. The false teachers may have claimed that behavior did not matter. John warns against being deceived: righteousness is shown in action.

The relationship between being born of God and sin is studied below.

[[v10|In this the children of God are revealed, and the children of the devil. Whoever doesn’t do righteousness is not of God, neither is he who doesn’t love his brother]]. Two tests reveal who belongs to God: doing what is right and loving fellow believers. These are not ways of earning God's favor but signs of a genuine relationship with Him.

### Love One Another (3:11–15)

[[v11|For this is the message which you heard from the beginning, that we should love one another]]— [[v12|unlike Cain, who was of the evil one and killed his brother. Why did he kill him? Because his deeds were evil, and his brother’s righteous]]. Cain killed Abel out of jealousy, because Abel's offering was accepted and his own was not (Genesis 4:1–8). Cain is the opposite of brotherly love. Hatred and murder come from the evil one.

[[v13|Don’t be surprised, my brothers, if the world hates you]]. Just as Cain hated Abel because Abel was righteous, the world may hate believers. Jesus warned of this (John 15:18–19).

[[v14|We know that we have passed out of death into life, because we love the brothers. He who doesn’t love his brother remains in death]]. Love is evidence of new life. Those who love have moved from spiritual death to life. Jesus said that whoever hears His word and believes has passed from death to life (John 5:24).

[[v15|Whoever hates his brother is a murderer, and you know that no murderer has eternal life remaining in him]]. Hatred is murder in the heart. Jesus taught the same, saying that anger and insults are connected to murder (Matthew 5:21–22). A life characterized by hatred is incompatible with eternal life. This does not mean that a person who has felt anger or even committed murder can never be forgiven; the gospel offers forgiveness to all who repent, as Paul's own story shows. It means that a heart settled in hatred does not have God's life within it.

### Love in Action (3:16–18)

The definition of love and the call to love in deed and truth are studied below.

### Confidence Before God (3:19–24)

The assurance God gives to troubled consciences is studied below. [[v21|Beloved, if our hearts don’t condemn us, we have boldness toward God]]; [[v22|so whatever we ask, we receive from him, because we keep his commandments and do the things that are pleasing in his sight]]. When believers' consciences are clear, they can approach God with confidence in prayer. Answered prayer is connected with obedience, not because obedience earns answers, but because those who obey God desire what He desires and pray according to His will (1 John 5:14).

[[v23|This is his commandment, that we should believe in the name of his Son, Jesus Christ, and love one another, even as he commanded]]. John summarizes God's command in two parts: believe in Jesus and love one another. Faith and love belong together.

[[v24|He who keeps his commandments remains in him, and he in him. By this we know that he remains in us, by the Spirit which he gave us]]. Those who obey God live in mutual relationship with Him. The Holy Spirit within them is the evidence that God dwells in them.

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 John 3:1–2 — Called Children of God

verse: 1 John 3:1-2

#### Simple Explanation

"See how great a love the Father has given to us." John invites his readers to look and marvel. The phrase "how great" suggests something astonishing, a love from beyond this world. God's love is not merely shown but "given," lavished on believers as a gift.

"That we should be called children of God!" The evidence of God's love is that He calls believers His children. This is not just a title; it is a reality. Some manuscripts add "and we are."

"For this cause the world doesn’t know us, because it didn’t know him." The world does not recognize believers as God's children because it did not recognize Jesus as God's Son. Believers should not expect the world to understand them.

Verse 2: "Beloved, now we are children of God." Believers are God's children now, not only in the future. This is their present identity.

"It is not yet revealed what we will be." There is more to come. Believers' future glory has not yet been fully revealed.

"But we know that when he is revealed, we will be like him, for we will see him just as he is." When Christ returns, believers will be transformed to be like Him. Seeing Christ in His glory will complete their transformation.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses describe the wonder of adoption. Believers were once separated from God, but now they are His children, loved and welcomed into His family. Paul writes that believers have received [[Romans 8:15|the Spirit of adoption, by whom we cry, 'Abba! Father!']]. John 1:12 says that to all who received Jesus, He gave the right to become children of God.

The phrase "now we are children of God" gives believers a secure identity. Their status does not depend on their feelings, achievements, or others' opinions. It rests on God's love.

The promise "we will be like him" is the ultimate hope of believers. They are being transformed now (2 Corinthians 3:18), but on the day of Christ's return, the transformation will be complete. Paul says the same: Christ will transform their lowly bodies to be like His glorious body (Philippians 3:21). They will see Him face to face (1 Corinthians 13:12).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers who struggle with feelings of worthlessness or rejection can rest in this truth: they are God's children, deeply loved. Their identity is secure.

When believers feel misunderstood or rejected by the world, they can remember that the world also did not recognize Jesus. Being misunderstood is not a sign that they do not belong to God.

Finally, the hope of seeing Christ and becoming like Him can shape daily life. It gives believers a reason to pursue purity and to endure hardship, knowing that glory awaits.

### 1 John 3:8–9 — The Son Came to Destroy the Works of the Devil

verse: 1 John 3:8-9

#### Simple Explanation

"He who sins is of the devil, for the devil has been sinning from the beginning." In context, "he who sins" refers to someone who lives in a settled pattern of sin. Such a life reflects the devil's character, since he has been sinning from the beginning.

"To this end the Son of God was revealed: that he might destroy the works of the devil." Jesus came to undo everything the devil has done: sin, deception, bondage, and death.

Verse 9: "Whoever is born of God doesn’t commit sin, because his seed remains in him, and he can’t sin, because he is born of God." Those born of God do not live in ongoing, habitual sin. God's "seed," His life or His word or His Spirit planted within them, remains in them and changes them. They "can't sin" in the sense that they cannot continue comfortably in a life of sin, because God's nature within them resists it.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses must be read alongside 1 John 1:8–10, where John says that anyone who claims to be without sin deceives themselves, and 2:1, where he provides for believers who do sin. John is not teaching that believers become sinless in this life. That would contradict his earlier statements.

Most interpreters understand John to be describing the direction and pattern of a believer's life. The Greek verbs in verses 6 and 9 are in the present tense, which often indicates ongoing, habitual action. Believers still sin, but they do not live in settled, unrepentant sin. When they sin, they confess and turn back. Their new nature, God's seed within them, will not allow them to be comfortable in sin.

Other interpreters suggest that John is describing the ideal of the new nature, which does not sin, or that he is countering false teachers who claimed sin did not matter. All agree that genuine faith produces a life that is increasingly free from sin's dominion.

The statement that the Son of God was revealed [[v8|that he might destroy the works of the devil]] is a powerful summary of His mission. Through His life, death, and resurrection, Jesus defeated the devil and set people free (Hebrews 2:14–15; Colossians 2:15).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can take comfort that struggling with sin does not mean they are not God's children. The very fact that sin troubles them shows God's seed is at work in them.

At the same time, these verses call believers not to make peace with sin. Patterns of sin that a believer refuses to confront are serious. Believers can bring those patterns to God, confess them, seek help from others, and rely on the Spirit's power to change.

Finally, believers can rejoice that Jesus came to destroy the devil's works. No sin, addiction, or bondage is too strong for Him.

### 1 John 3:16–18 — Love in Deed and Truth

verse: 1 John 3:16-18

#### Simple Explanation

"By this we know love, because he laid down his life for us." What is love? John points to the cross. Jesus gave His life for us. That is the definition and model of love.

"And we ought to lay down our lives for the brothers." Because Christ loved this way, believers should love one another the same way, being willing to give their lives for one another.

Verse 17 makes it practical: "But whoever has the world’s goods and sees his brother in need, then closes his heart of compassion against him, how does God’s love remain in him?" Most believers will not be asked to die for others. But they will often see fellow believers in need. If they have resources and refuse to help, how can they claim that God's love is in them?

Verse 18: "My little children, let’s not love in word only, or with the tongue only, but in deed and truth." Love is not just words or feelings. It is shown in action and in sincerity.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage moves from the greatest act of love to the most ordinary. Christ's death on the cross is the ultimate demonstration of love. But love is also shown in daily, practical ways: sharing food, money, time, and care with those in need.

Interestingly, the reference "1 John 3:16" parallels "John 3:16." Both speak of God's love shown in giving. John 3:16 shows God's love for the world; 1 John 3:16 shows how believers should love one another in response.

James makes a similar point: if someone sees a brother or sister in need and says only "Go in peace," without helping, what good is that? Faith without works is dead (James 2:15–17). Love that does not act is not genuine love.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can look for practical ways to love others: providing a meal for someone who is struggling, helping with bills, offering a ride, spending time with someone who is lonely, or giving to those in need.

This passage also challenges believers to examine whether their love is only in words. It is easy to say "I'll pray for you" or "Let me know if you need anything." Genuine love takes initiative and acts.

Finally, believers can remember Christ's example. His love was costly. Following Him means being willing to sacrifice for others, whether time, money, comfort, or convenience.

### 1 John 3:19–20 — God Is Greater Than Our Heart

verse: 1 John 3:19-20

#### Simple Explanation

"And by this we know that we are of the truth and persuade our hearts before him." "By this" refers to loving in deed and truth (v18). When believers see genuine love in their lives, they gain assurance that they belong to the truth. This assurance can quiet their hearts before God.

Verse 20: "because if our heart condemns us, God is greater than our heart, and knows all things." Sometimes believers' consciences accuse them. They feel guilty, unworthy, or uncertain of their standing with God. John reassures them: God is greater than their heart. He knows everything, including their genuine faith, their love, and their desire to please Him. His knowledge is more complete and more merciful than their self-condemnation.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses are a gift to sensitive consciences. Some believers struggle with constant self-condemnation, even after confessing their sins. They wonder whether they are truly saved. John points them away from their feelings to God's greater knowledge.

"God is greater than our heart" can be understood in two ways. It may mean God is more merciful than our self-condemning hearts, seeing the genuine faith and love that we overlook. Or it may mean God knows everything, so nothing can be hidden from Him. Both are true and both comfort. God sees the whole picture, and He is merciful.

Peter's restoration illustrates this. After denying Jesus, Peter was asked three times, "Do you love me?" Finally Peter said, [[John 21:17|Lord, you know everything. You know that I have affection for you]]. Peter appealed to Jesus' complete knowledge. God knows the heart better than we know ourselves.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers who struggle with guilt or doubt about their salvation can bring their accusing hearts to God. They can confess any sin and then rest in His forgiveness, trusting that He is greater than their feelings.

Believers can also look for evidence of God's work in their lives, such as love for others, desire to obey, and faith in Christ, which can bring assurance.

For those who struggle with severe and persistent guilt, scrupulosity, or anxiety, help from a wise pastor or counselor can be valuable. God's grace is greater than any accusing voice.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

First John 3 begins with wonder. The Father has lavished His love on believers, calling them His children, and that is what they truly are. Though the world does not recognize them, their identity is secure. And when Christ appears, they will be like Him, for they will see Him as He is. This hope motivates them to pursue purity now.

God's children resemble their Father. They do not live in settled, unrepentant sin, because God's seed remains in them. Christ came to destroy the works of the devil, and His power is at work in them. Believers still struggle and still need to confess their sins, but the direction of their lives is toward righteousness.

God's children also love one another. Hatred is murder in the heart, but love is evidence of passing from death to life. True love is defined by Christ, who laid down His life for us. Believers are called to love the same way, not only in words but in practical actions, sharing with those in need.

Finally, the chapter offers assurance to troubled consciences. When believers' hearts condemn them, God is greater than their hearts and knows everything. Those who believe in Jesus and love one another can rest in His mercy, with confidence in prayer and the assurance of His Spirit living within them.

## Chapter Prayer

Father, thank You for the great love You have given me, that I should be called Your child, and that I truly am. When the world does not understand me, remind me that I belong to You. Thank You for the hope that when Christ appears, I will be like Him and see Him as He is. Help me to purify myself as He is pure. Thank You, Lord Jesus, that You came to destroy the works of the devil. Free me from every pattern of sin, and let Your life grow in me. Teach me to love as You loved, laying down my life for others. Give me eyes to see those in need and a heart to act, loving not only in words but in deeds and truth. When my heart condemns me, remind me that You are greater than my heart and know all things. Help me to believe in the name of Your Son and to love others, as You have commanded. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
