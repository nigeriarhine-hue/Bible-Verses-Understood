# Luke 8
theme: Hearing the Word and Holding It Fast
main: Supported by women whom He had healed, Jesus travels from town to town preaching the kingdom. He teaches that God's word bears fruit in honest and good hearts that hold it fast with perseverance, and that His true family are those who hear and do God's word. He then calms a storm, frees a man tormented by many demons, heals a woman who had suffered for twelve years, and raises a synagogue leader's daughter, showing His power over nature, evil, disease, and death.

## Chapter Background and Story

Luke 8 continues Jesus' ministry in Galilee. The previous chapter ended with a woman known as a sinner washing Jesus' feet with her tears and receiving His forgiveness. Luke 8 begins by mentioning other women who followed Jesus, and much of the chapter focuses on hearing God's word rightly. The second half contains a series of miracles that answer the disciples' question in the storm: [[v25|Who is this then, that he commands even the winds and the water, and they obey him?]]

Many events in this chapter are also recorded in Matthew and Mark and are studied in depth there: the parable of the sower (Matthew 13:23; Mark 4:24), the storm (Matthew 8:26; Mark 4:38), the man with a legion of demons (Mark 5:19), and the healing of Jairus's daughter and the woman with bleeding (Mark 5:34, 5:36). This chapter highlights details distinctive to Luke.

### The Women Who Followed Jesus (8:1–3)

Jesus traveled through towns and villages, proclaiming the good news of the kingdom of God. The Twelve were with Him, and so were [[v2|certain women who had been healed of evil spirits and infirmities]]. Luke names three: Mary called Magdalene, from whom seven demons had gone out; Joanna, the wife of Chuza, the manager of Herod's household; and Susanna. There were many others too, and they [[v3|served them from their possessions]]. This passage, found only in Luke, is studied below.

### The Parable of the Sower (8:4–15)

A large crowd gathered from many towns, and Jesus told a parable about a farmer sowing seed. Some fell on the path and was trampled and eaten by birds. Some fell on rock and withered because it had no moisture. Some fell among thorns and was choked. Some fell on good soil and produced a hundred times as much. Jesus called out, [[v8|He who has ears to hear, let him hear!]]

When the disciples asked what it meant, Jesus said they had been given knowledge of the secrets of the kingdom, but others heard only parables. Then He explained: [[v11|The seed is the word of God]]. Those along the path hear, but the devil takes the word away [[v12|that they may not believe and be saved]]. Those on the rock receive the word with joy but have no root; they believe for a while and fall away in time of testing. Those among the thorns hear but are choked by worries, riches, and pleasures of life, and their fruit does not mature. Those on good soil hear the word with an honest and good heart, hold it fast, and bear fruit with perseverance. Luke's wording of this last description is studied below.

### The Lamp and Careful Hearing (8:16–18)

No one lights a lamp and hides it under a jar or a bed; it is put on a stand so people can see. Nothing hidden will remain hidden. Then Jesus warned, [[v18|Be careful therefore how you hear. For whoever has, to him will be given; and whoever doesn’t have, from him will be taken away even that which he thinks he has]]. Luke's phrase "even what he thinks he has" suggests that some people believe they possess spiritual understanding, but if they do not truly receive and act on the word, even that illusion will be taken away. (A similar saying is studied at Mark 4:24.)

### Jesus' True Family (8:19–21)

Jesus' mother and brothers came to see Him but could not get near because of the crowd. When told they were outside, He said, [[v21|My mother and my brothers are these who hear the word of God and do it]]. This saying fits the chapter's theme perfectly. The good soil is the person who hears the word and holds it fast; Jesus' family are those who hear God's word and do it. Luke's version is gentler than Mark's, where Jesus' family had come thinking He was out of His mind (Mark 3:21). Luke had already presented Mary as one who heard and believed God's word (Luke 1:38, 45). (See also Mark 3:35.)

### Calming the Storm (8:22–25)

Jesus and His disciples got into a boat to cross the lake. As they sailed, Jesus fell asleep. A windstorm came down on the lake, and the boat was filling with water and in danger. The disciples woke Him, crying, [[v24|Master, Master, we are dying!]] Jesus got up and rebuked the wind and the raging water, and they became calm. Then He asked, [[v25|Where is your faith?]] This question is studied below. The disciples, afraid and amazed, asked one another who this was, whom even the wind and water obey.

### The Man With Many Demons (8:26–39)

They sailed to the region of the Gerasenes, across the lake from Galilee, a largely Gentile area. A man from the town who had been possessed by demons for a long time met Jesus. He wore no clothes and lived among the tombs rather than in a house. When he saw Jesus, he cried out, fell down before Him, and shouted, [[v28|What do I have to do with you, Jesus, you Son of the Most High God? I beg you, don’t torment me!]] Luke explains that the unclean spirit had often seized him, and though he had been kept under guard and chained, he broke the chains and was driven by the demon into the wilderness.

Jesus asked his name, and he said, "Legion," because many demons had entered him. The demons begged Jesus not to order them into [[v31|the abyss]], the place of confinement for evil spirits (Revelation 9:1–2; 20:1–3). They asked to enter a large herd of pigs, and Jesus allowed it. The pigs rushed down the steep bank into the lake and drowned.

When the people of the region came and found the man [[v35|sitting at Jesus’ feet, clothed and in his right mind]], they were afraid. Luke's phrase "sitting at Jesus' feet" describes the posture of a disciple learning from a teacher, the same posture Mary of Bethany takes in Luke 10:39. The man who had been wild and naked had become a learner of Jesus. But the people asked Jesus to leave because they were overcome with fear.

The healed man begged to go with Jesus, but Jesus told him, [[v39|Return to your house, and declare what great things God has done for you]]. The man went and proclaimed throughout the town [[v39|what great things Jesus had done for him]]. Notice that Jesus said "God," and the man said "Jesus." Luke may be showing that what Jesus did was the work of God.

### A Dying Girl and a Suffering Woman (8:40–56)

When Jesus returned, the crowd welcomed Him. Jairus, a ruler of the synagogue, fell at His feet and begged Him to come to his house, because his only daughter, about twelve years old, was dying. As Jesus went, the crowds pressed around Him.

A woman who had suffered from bleeding for twelve years, and who had spent all her money on doctors without being healed, came up behind Him and touched the fringe of His cloak. Luke, the physician, records honestly that she [[v43|could not be healed by any]]. Immediately her bleeding stopped. Jesus asked who touched Him. Peter pointed out that the crowds were pressing against Him from every side. But Jesus said, [[v46|Someone did touch me, for I perceived that power has gone out of me]]. The woman, realizing she could not remain hidden, came trembling and told everyone why she had touched Him and how she had been healed. Jesus said, [[v48|Daughter, cheer up. Your faith has made you well. Go in peace]].

While He was speaking, someone came from Jairus's house and said the girl had died. Jesus told Jairus, [[v50|Don’t be afraid. Only believe, and she will be healed]]. At the house, He allowed only Peter, John, James, and the girl's parents to come in. Everyone was weeping, but Jesus said she was not dead but sleeping. They laughed at Him. He took her by the hand and called, [[v54|Child, arise!]] Luke says [[v55|Her spirit returned]], and she got up at once. Jesus told them to give her something to eat, and told her astonished parents not to tell anyone what had happened.

The two stories are woven together. A twelve-year-old girl and a woman who had suffered for twelve years both received new life through Jesus. Both were called "daughter" in effect, and both were restored to their families and communities.

## Important Verses Explained

### Luke 8:2–3 — The Women Who Provided for Jesus

verse: Luke 8:2-3

#### Simple Explanation

These verses describe some of the people who traveled with Jesus as He went from town to town preaching the good news of God's kingdom. Luke has just said that the Twelve were with Him. Now he adds that [[v2|certain women who had been healed of evil spirits and infirmities]] were also with Him.

Luke names three of them. First, [[v2|Mary who was called Magdalene, from whom seven demons had gone out]]. "Magdalene" means she came from Magdala, a fishing town on the western shore of the Sea of Galilee. Jesus had freed her from severe demonic oppression; "seven" suggests the completeness or severity of her bondage. (There is no biblical evidence that Mary Magdalene was the sinful woman of Luke 7 or a prostitute, a later tradition that has been widely questioned.) Mary Magdalene would be present at Jesus' crucifixion and burial and would be the first witness to His resurrection (Luke 24:10; John 20:1–18).

Second, [[v3|Joanna, the wife of Chuzas, Herod’s steward]]. Chuza managed the household or estate of Herod Antipas, the ruler of Galilee. This was a position of wealth and influence. Joanna's presence shows that Jesus' followers included people from Herod's court. She would also be among the women at the empty tomb (Luke 24:10).

Third, Susanna, mentioned only here.

Luke adds that there were [[v3|many others who served them from their possessions]]. These women used their own money and resources to support Jesus and His disciples as they traveled. They were not merely followers; they were partners in His ministry.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that women played a significant role in Jesus' ministry. In first-century Jewish culture, it was unusual for women to travel with a religious teacher. Rabbis did not usually have female disciples. Jesus welcomed women as followers and partners. Luke, more than any other Gospel writer, highlights women: Elizabeth, Mary, Anna, the widow of Nain, the sinful woman, Mary and Martha, and the women at the cross and tomb.

They also show that grace leads to gratitude and service. These women had been healed and set free by Jesus. Their response was to follow Him and support His work generously. Those who have received God's grace naturally want to give back in love.

Finally, these verses reveal that Jesus' ministry was supported by ordinary people's generosity. Jesus did not perform miracles to supply His own needs; He depended on the generosity of His followers. God works through the gifts of His people to advance His kingdom.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to respond to God's grace with gratitude and service. Like these women, believers who have been forgiven, healed, or set free can follow Jesus and use their resources to support His work.

They also affirm the valuable role of women in God's kingdom. Churches can recognize, honor, and encourage women's contributions to ministry, as Jesus did. The women who supported Jesus were faithful when many others were not; they stayed at the cross and were first at the empty tomb.

Finally, these verses encourage generosity in supporting the spread of the gospel. Believers can support pastors, missionaries, and ministries with their resources, seeing this as a way of partnering in Jesus' mission, just as these women did.

### Luke 8:15 — An Honest and Good Heart That Holds Fast

verse: Luke 8:15

#### Simple Explanation

This verse is Jesus' explanation of the good soil in the parable of the sower. In the parable, a farmer scattered seed, which represents [[v11|the word of God]]. Some fell on the path, where the devil took it away. Some fell on rock, where it sprang up but withered because it had no root, representing those who believe for a while but fall away when tested. Some fell among thorns, where worries, riches, and pleasures choked it so its fruit never matured.

Then Jesus described the good soil: [[v15|Those in the good ground, these are those who with an honest and good heart, having heard the word, hold it tightly, and produce fruit with perseverance]]. Luke's wording adds details not found in Matthew or Mark. (The parable is studied from Matthew's perspective at Matthew 13:23.)

[[v15|with an honest and good heart]]. The phrase "honest and good" was a common Greek expression for a person of noble character. Here it describes a heart that is sincere, open, and ready to receive God's word without deceit or resistance.

[[v15|having heard the word, hold it tightly]]. To "hold tightly" means to keep firmly, to retain and not let go. Unlike the path, where the word was snatched away, and the rock, where it was quickly lost, the good soil holds onto the word.

[[v15|and produce fruit with perseverance]]. "Perseverance" means patient endurance, steady faithfulness over time, especially through difficulty. Fruit takes time to grow. The good soil does not produce a crop overnight; it keeps holding onto the word through seasons of testing until fruit matures.

Luke's emphasis on perseverance contrasts with the rocky soil, which fell away [[v13|in time of temptation]], and the thorny soil, whose fruit did not mature. The good hearer keeps going.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse teaches that fruitful faith requires both openness and endurance. An honest heart receives God's word sincerely. Holding it fast means continuing to believe and obey it. Perseverance means remaining faithful through trials, temptations, and the passing of time. Jesus said elsewhere that the one who endures to the end will be saved (Mark 13:13).

It also shows that spiritual fruit takes time. Believers sometimes become discouraged when they do not see immediate change in themselves or others. But fruit grows slowly. The good soil produces fruit "with perseverance," through patient faithfulness over the long haul.

Finally, the verse connects the condition of the heart with the outcome of faith. Hearing is the same for all four soils; the difference is the heart. This calls believers to examine their hearts and ask God to make them honest and good soil.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites believers to receive God's word with honest hearts. That means approaching Scripture not to find support for what they already want, but to hear what God is truly saying, even when it challenges them.

It also calls believers to hold God's word tightly. In a world full of distractions and competing messages, it is easy to forget what God has said. Believers can hold onto His word through regular reading, memorization, reflection, and obedience.

Finally, this verse encourages patience and perseverance. Spiritual growth is often slow, and fruit may take years to mature. Believers can keep trusting and obeying God through dry seasons, trials, and temptations, confident that God will bring fruit in His time.

### Luke 8:25 — "Where Is Your Faith?"

verse: Luke 8:25

#### Simple Explanation

This verse comes immediately after Jesus calms a violent storm on the Sea of Galilee. Jesus had told His disciples, "Let's go over to the other side of the lake." As they sailed, He fell asleep. A windstorm swept down on the lake, and the boat began filling with water. Several of the disciples were experienced fishermen, but they were terrified. They woke Jesus, crying, "Master, Master, we are dying!" Jesus got up, rebuked the wind and the raging water, and everything became calm.

Then Jesus asked them, [[v25|Where is your faith?]] Luke's version of this question differs slightly from Matthew's ("Why are you fearful, O you of little faith?") and Mark's ("How is it that you have no faith?"). Luke's wording suggests that the disciples did have faith, but in the moment of crisis, they did not use it. Their faith was somewhere else; it had not been brought to bear on the storm.

The disciples responded with fear and amazement: [[v25|Who is this then, that he commands even the winds and the water, and they obey him?]] They had seen Jesus heal the sick and cast out demons, but this display of power over nature filled them with awe. In the Old Testament, only God controls the wind and the sea (Psalm 107:29).

The setting is significant. The disciples were in the storm because they had followed Jesus' instruction to cross the lake. Jesus was with them in the boat. His question invited them to remember who was with them and to trust Him even in danger.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that faith is not only something we possess but something we must exercise. The disciples believed in Jesus, but in the storm, fear overwhelmed their trust. Faith must be brought into the real situations of life, especially the frightening ones.

It also shows that Jesus is the Lord of creation. The disciples' question, "Who is this?", runs throughout the Gospels. Jesus' power over the wind and water reveals that He shares God's authority. The One who created the world has authority over it.

Finally, the question "Where is your faith?" is gentle rather than harsh. Jesus did not abandon His frightened disciples; He calmed the storm and then invited them to grow. He continues to patiently teach His followers to trust Him.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites believers to ask themselves where their faith is when trouble comes. It is easy to trust God when life is calm, but storms reveal whether faith is active. When fear rises, believers can remind themselves of who Jesus is and choose to trust Him.

It also encourages believers to bring their fears to Jesus. The disciples were afraid, but they went to the right person. Believers can cry out to Jesus in their storms, trusting that He hears and cares.

Finally, this verse reminds believers that storms can deepen their understanding of Jesus. The disciples came away from the storm asking, "Who is this?" Each experience of Jesus' faithfulness in difficulty can strengthen believers' faith and deepen their awe of Him.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Luke 8 centers on how people respond to God's word and to Jesus Himself. The parable of the sower shows that the same word produces different results depending on the condition of the heart. Believers can ask God to make their hearts honest and good, receiving His word sincerely, holding it fast, and bearing fruit with perseverance. This involves guarding against distractions, worries, riches, and pleasures that choke spiritual growth.

The chapter emphasizes that hearing must lead to doing. Jesus' true family are those who hear God's word and do it. Believers can move beyond knowing about God's word to living it out in daily obedience.

The women who followed Jesus show that grace leads to gratitude and service. Those who have been healed, forgiven, and freed by Jesus can respond by following Him and using their resources to support His work. Their example also affirms the valuable role of women in God's kingdom.

The miracles in the second half of the chapter reveal Jesus' authority over storms, demons, disease, and death. Believers can trust Him in every kind of crisis. When fear rises, they can ask themselves, "Where is my faith?" and turn to Jesus. The healed man's commission to tell what God had done reminds believers to share their stories of Jesus' work in their lives. Finally, the stories of the woman and Jairus show that Jesus sees individuals, honors persistent faith, and gives new life even when all hope seems gone.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for sowing the word of God in my heart. Make my heart honest and good soil that receives Your word, holds it fast, and bears fruit with perseverance. Guard me from the worries, riches, and pleasures that choke my faith, and help me to be careful how I hear. Make me part of Your family by hearing God's word and doing it.

Thank You for the women who followed and supported You out of gratitude. Help me to respond to Your grace with generous service. When storms rise in my life, remind me that You are with me, and when You ask, "Where is your faith?", help me to trust You. Thank You for Your power over evil, sickness, and death. Help me to tell others what great things You have done for me. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
