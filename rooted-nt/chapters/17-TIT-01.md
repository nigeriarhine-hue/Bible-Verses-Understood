# Titus 1
theme: Sound Leaders for a Godly Church
main: Paul greets Titus as a servant of God and apostle, whose mission is to further the faith of God's chosen people and their knowledge of the truth that leads to godliness, all resting on the hope of eternal life promised by God, who cannot lie. He reminds Titus that he left him in Crete to put things in order and appoint elders in every town. These leaders must be blameless, faithful in marriage, with households of good reputation, not arrogant, quick-tempered, drunken, violent, or greedy, but hospitable, lovers of good, self-controlled, upright, holy, and disciplined, holding firmly to the trustworthy message so they can encourage others with sound teaching and correct those who oppose it. Paul warns against many rebellious, empty talkers and deceivers who were upsetting whole households for dishonest gain and who must be silenced and sharply rebuked so they may become sound in the faith. To the pure all things are pure, but those who are corrupt claim to know God while denying Him by their actions.

## Chapter Background and Story

Crete is a large island in the Mediterranean Sea, south of Greece. People from Crete were present in Jerusalem on the day of Pentecost (Acts 2:11), and Paul's ship stopped at Crete on his voyage to Rome (Acts 27:7–13). The book of Acts does not record Paul planting churches there, so this ministry likely took place after his release from his first Roman imprisonment.

The churches in Crete were young and needed structure and leadership. Titus's task was to appoint elders in every town and to deal with false teachers who were causing serious harm. These teachers were especially "of the circumcision," Jewish in background, and they promoted "Jewish fables and commandments of men" (v14), likely a mixture of speculative stories and human rules, perhaps similar to the problems in Ephesus. They were also motivated by money (v11).

Crete had a poor reputation in the ancient world. Greek writers used the word "to Cretanize" to mean "to lie." Paul's instructions show that the gospel was meant to transform even a culture known for dishonesty, through leaders of godly character and through sound teaching that produces godly living.

### Greeting (1:1–4)

[[v1|Paul, a servant of God and an apostle of Jesus Christ, according to the faith of God’s chosen ones and the knowledge of the truth which is according to godliness]]. Paul's purpose as an apostle is to build up the faith of God's people and their knowledge of the truth. Notice that truth is connected with godliness. Sound doctrine and godly living belong together; this is the theme of the whole letter. The foundation of Paul's ministry, the hope of eternal life, is studied below.

[[v3|but in his own time revealed his word in the message with which I was entrusted according to the commandment of God our Savior]]. God's promise, made before time began, was revealed at the right time through the preaching of the gospel.

[[v4|to Titus, my true child according to a common faith: Grace, mercy, and peace from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ our Savior]]. Titus was Paul's "true child" in the faith, probably converted through Paul's ministry. Notice that both "God" and "the Lord Jesus Christ" are called "Savior" in this letter (1:3, 4; 2:10, 13; 3:4, 6).

### Appointing Elders (1:5–9)

[[v5|I left you in Crete for this reason, that you would set in order the things that were lacking and appoint elders in every city, as I directed you]]. "Set in order" was a medical term for setting a broken bone. Titus's job was to bring health and order to the young churches.

[[v6|if anyone is blameless, the husband of one wife, having children who believe, who are not accused of loose or unruly behavior]]. As in 1 Timothy 3, the qualifications focus on character. "Blameless" means no one can rightly bring a serious charge against him. "The husband of one wife" means faithful in marriage (see the discussion at 1 Timothy 3:2). "Having children who believe" may mean children who are believers, or children who are "faithful," trustworthy and well-behaved. Interpreters differ. Either way, the leader's household should reflect his faith, though even godly parents cannot control every choice their adult children make.

[[v7|For the overseer must be blameless, as God’s steward, not self-pleasing, not easily angered, not given to wine, not violent, not greedy for dishonest gain]]. An overseer is "God's steward," managing God's household, not his own. He must not be arrogant, hot-tempered, a heavy drinker, violent, or greedy. These are exactly the traits that harm those in one's care.

[[v8|but given to hospitality, a lover of good, sober minded, fair, holy, self-controlled]]. Positively, he must be welcoming, love what is good, be sensible, just, devoted to God, and disciplined. The leader's role regarding sound teaching is studied below.

### Silencing False Teachers (1:10–14)

[[v10|For there are also many unruly men, vain talkers and deceivers, especially those of the circumcision]], [[v11|whose mouths must be stopped: men who overthrow whole houses, teaching things which they ought not, for dishonest gain’s sake]]. The false teachers were rebellious, full of empty talk, and deceptive. They were "overthrowing whole houses," disrupting entire families, perhaps house churches. And they did it for money.

[[v12|One of them, a prophet of their own, said, “Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, and idle gluttons.”]] Paul quotes a Cretan poet, traditionally identified as Epimenides, who lived around the sixth century B.C. and was regarded by some as a prophet. This was a well-known saying about Crete's reputation.

[[v13|This testimony is true. For this cause, reprove them sharply, that they may be sound in the faith]]. Paul is not endorsing ethnic stereotypes as a way of judging individuals. He is applying the poet's observation to the false teachers, whose behavior fit the saying exactly. Scripture never permits believers to judge people by the reputation of their nationality or ethnicity; in Christ, every person is to be known and valued as an individual made in God's image, and the Cretan believers were full members of God's family. Paul's goal, even in sharp rebuke, is restoration: "that they may be sound in the faith."

[[v14|not paying attention to Jewish fables and commandments of men who turn away from the truth]]. The false teaching included myths and human rules. Jesus also warned against putting human traditions above God's commands (Mark 7:6–8).

### Pure and Impure (1:15–16)

Paul's statements about purity and about those whose deeds deny God are studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### Titus 1:2 — The Hope of Eternal Life from God, Who Cannot Lie

verse: Titus 1:2

#### Simple Explanation

Paul describes the foundation of his ministry. Everything he does is "in hope of eternal life." Eternal life is not just endless existence but the life of God's kingdom, a life of knowing and enjoying God forever (John 17:3). Believers already begin to experience it now, and they will know it fully when Christ returns.

This hope rests on God's promise: "which God, who can’t lie, promised before time began." Before the world was created, God planned and promised eternal life to His people. And His promise is certain, because God cannot lie.

The contrast with Crete is striking. The Cretans were known as liars (v12). God is the opposite. He is completely truthful. His word can always be trusted.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The truthfulness of God is a foundation of Christian faith. Numbers 23:19 says, [[Numbers 23:19|God is not a man, that he should lie]]. Hebrews 6:18 says that it is impossible for God to lie, and that His promise and oath give strong encouragement to those who have taken refuge in Him. Jesus called Himself [[John 14:6|the truth]].

Because God cannot lie, every promise He has made is sure. The promise of eternal life is not a wish or a guess. It is as certain as God's character.

The phrase "before time began" points to God's eternal purpose. Salvation was not an afterthought or a response to unexpected events. It was God's plan from the beginning, revealed in history through Christ (2 Timothy 1:9–10).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

In a world full of broken promises, misinformation, and deception, believers can rest in the truthfulness of God. Whatever He has said, He will do.

This truth brings comfort in times of doubt or fear. When believers wonder whether God's promises are real, they can remember that God cannot lie. His word is the most reliable foundation for life.

It also calls believers to reflect God's truthfulness in their own speech and lives. As children of the God who cannot lie, they are called to be people of honesty and integrity.

### Titus 1:9 — Holding Fast to the Faithful Word

verse: Titus 1:9

#### Simple Explanation

Paul describes an essential quality of church leaders. They must be "holding to the faithful word which is according to the teaching." The "faithful word" is the trustworthy message of the gospel, as it was taught by the apostles. Leaders must hold onto it firmly, like someone gripping something valuable.

Why? "That he may be able to exhort in the sound doctrine, and to convict those who contradict him." Leaders have two tasks with regard to teaching. First, they must "exhort," or encourage, believers with "sound doctrine," healthy teaching that nourishes spiritual life. Second, they must "convict," or refute, those who oppose the truth, showing them their error.

A leader who does not hold firmly to the truth cannot do either. He will have nothing solid to offer believers and no basis for correcting error.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that church leadership is not only about character but also about teaching. Leaders must know and hold to the truth. In a church threatened by false teaching, as Crete was, this was essential. Leaders were the first line of defense.

The two tasks, encouraging and refuting, reflect the full ministry of God's word. Encouragement builds up those who believe; refutation protects them from error. Both are acts of love: love for the flock and love for the truth.

"Sound doctrine" is a key phrase in the Pastoral Letters. The word "sound" means healthy. Just as healthy food nourishes the body, healthy teaching nourishes the soul. False teaching, by contrast, is like a disease (2 Timothy 2:17).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Churches can seek leaders who know Scripture well, hold firmly to the gospel, and can explain it clearly. Training and equipping leaders in sound teaching is an important investment.

All believers can also hold fast to the faithful word. Knowing the core truths of the faith helps believers encourage one another and recognize error when they encounter it.

When correcting error, believers can follow the pattern of 2 Timothy 2:24–25: with gentleness and patience, aiming for restoration, while still being clear about the truth.

### Titus 1:15 — To the Pure, All Things Are Pure

verse: Titus 1:15

#### Simple Explanation

The false teachers were apparently teaching rules about purity, perhaps about foods or rituals, similar to those in Colossae and Ephesus (Colossians 2:20–23; 1 Timothy 4:3). They taught that certain things defiled people.

Paul responds: "To the pure, all things are pure." For those whose hearts have been cleansed by Christ, God's good gifts, such as food and the ordinary things of life, are not defiling. They can be received with thanksgiving.

"But to those who are defiled and unbelieving, nothing is pure." For those whose hearts are corrupt and who do not believe, even good things become occasions for sin. Their impurity is not in the things themselves but in their hearts.

"But both their mind and their conscience are defiled." The problem is internal. Their thinking and their moral sense have been corrupted.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse echoes Jesus' teaching that it is not what goes into a person that defiles them, but what comes out of the heart (Mark 7:14–23). Purity is a matter of the heart, not merely of external rules. Paul makes a similar point in Romans 14:14, 20: nothing is unclean in itself, and all food is clean.

This does not mean that "anything goes" for believers. Paul is talking about matters like food and ritual purity, not moral sin. Sexual immorality, greed, and cruelty are always wrong. The point is that external rules cannot make a person pure; only a cleansed heart can.

The verse also shows that the condition of the heart shapes how a person sees the world. A pure heart sees God's good gifts as good. A corrupt heart twists even good things into opportunities for sin. Jesus said that the eye is the lamp of the body; if it is healthy, the whole body is full of light (Matthew 6:22–23).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can be free from legalistic rules that treat God's good gifts as defiling. They can enjoy food, rest, creativity, and the ordinary pleasures of life with gratitude, as gifts from God.

At the same time, this verse invites believers to examine their hearts. Purity is not achieved by avoiding certain things but by having a heart cleansed by Christ and directed toward Him.

This verse also warns against assuming that outward religious rules make someone holy. A person may follow many rules and still have a corrupt heart. True holiness begins within.

### Titus 1:16 — Professing God but Denying Him by Deeds

verse: Titus 1:16

#### Simple Explanation

Paul ends the chapter with a sobering description of the false teachers. "They profess that they know God." They claimed to know God, perhaps even to have special knowledge of Him.

"But by their deeds they deny him." Their actions contradicted their words. Their lives showed that they did not really know God.

"Being abominable, disobedient, and unfit for any good work." Paul uses strong words. Their behavior was detestable in God's sight. They were disobedient to God's will. And they were "unfit for any good work," literally "disqualified," like a coin that fails the test of genuineness. They could not do the good works that true faith produces.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse expresses a theme that runs throughout Titus: genuine faith is shown by godly living. Paul will emphasize good works repeatedly in this letter (2:7, 14; 3:1, 8, 14). The false teachers had words without works; true believers are to have both.

Jesus warned about those who say "Lord, Lord" but do not do the will of His Father (Matthew 7:21–23). He also said that people would be known by their fruit (Matthew 7:16–20). James wrote that faith without works is dead (James 2:17). John said that whoever claims to know God but does not keep His commandments is a liar (1 John 2:4).

This does not mean that salvation is earned by good works. Paul will make clear in Titus 3:5 that God saves [[Titus 3:5|not by works of righteousness, which we did ourselves]]. But those who are truly saved will show it in their lives. Good works are the evidence of genuine faith, not its cause.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites believers to examine whether their lives match their words. It is easy to profess faith; it is harder to live it. Do their actions, relationships, and choices reflect the God they claim to know?

It also offers a test for evaluating teachers and leaders. Words alone are not enough. A leader whose life contradicts their teaching should be approached with caution, no matter how persuasive their message.

Finally, this verse encourages believers to pursue good works, not to earn God's favor, but as the natural fruit of knowing Him. A life full of good works shows the reality of faith to a watching world.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Titus 1 shows that the health of a church depends on sound teaching and godly leaders. Paul begins by grounding his whole ministry in the hope of eternal life promised by God, who cannot lie. Believers can rest in that same hope, trusting every promise of the God whose word never fails, and they can reflect His truthfulness in their own lives.

Paul then describes the kind of leaders the church needs: blameless in character, faithful in marriage, with households that reflect their faith, not arrogant, hot-tempered, drunken, violent, or greedy, but hospitable, self-controlled, upright, and devoted to God. They must hold firmly to the trustworthy message so they can encourage believers with sound teaching and correct those who oppose it. Churches can use these standards when choosing leaders, giving character and faithfulness priority over charisma.

The chapter also warns against false teachers who disrupt households, promote human rules, and seek dishonest gain. They must be confronted, with the goal of restoration. Paul's use of a well-known saying about Crete is not permission for ethnic stereotyping; the gospel calls believers to see each person as an individual created and loved by God.

Finally, Paul teaches that purity is a matter of the heart, not external rules, and that those who claim to know God must show it in their actions. Believers can enjoy God's good gifts with gratitude and pursue a life in which their words and deeds both bear witness to the God they know.

## Chapter Prayer

Father, thank You for the hope of eternal life, which You, who cannot lie, promised before time began. Help me to trust every promise You have made and to reflect Your truthfulness in my own life. Raise up leaders in Your church who are blameless in character, gentle, self-controlled, and faithful to Your word. Give them wisdom to encourage Your people with sound teaching and courage to correct error with love. Protect families and churches from those who would deceive them for their own gain. Cleanse my heart, so that I may see Your good gifts as good and receive them with thanksgiving. Let my life match my words, so that my deeds show that I truly know You. Make me fit for every good work. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
