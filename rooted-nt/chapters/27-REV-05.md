# Revelation 5
theme: The Lamb Who Is Worthy
main: In the right hand of the One seated on the throne, John sees a scroll written on both sides and sealed with seven seals. A mighty angel asks who is worthy to break the seals and open the scroll, but no one in heaven, on earth, or under the earth can open it or even look inside. John weeps bitterly. Then one of the elders tells him not to weep, because the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has triumphed and is able to open the scroll. When John looks, he sees not a lion but a Lamb, standing as though it had been slain, in the center of the throne, with seven horns and seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of God sent out into all the earth. The Lamb takes the scroll, and the four living creatures and the twenty-four elders fall down before Him, holding harps and golden bowls of incense, which are the prayers of God's people. They sing a new song: He is worthy to take the scroll and open its seals, because He was slain, and with His blood He purchased people for God from every tribe, language, people, and nation, and made them a kingdom and priests to serve God, and they will reign on the earth. Then countless angels join in, proclaiming that the Lamb who was slain is worthy to receive power, wealth, wisdom, strength, honor, glory, and blessing. Finally every creature in heaven, on earth, under the earth, and in the sea praises the One on the throne and the Lamb forever, and the elders fall down and worship.

## Chapter Background and Story

Revelation 5 continues the heavenly vision of chapter 4. In chapter 4, God is worshiped as Creator. In chapter 5, the focus shifts to the Lamb, Jesus Christ, who is worshiped as Redeemer. Together these two chapters form one great vision of the throne room of heaven, and they provide the foundation for everything that follows in the book.

The central question of the chapter is: Who is worthy to open the scroll? The answer reveals the heart of the Christian faith. The One who is worthy is the Lamb who was slain. His victory came not through military force or political power, but through sacrificial death. This is the key to understanding all of Revelation: God's purposes are accomplished through the cross.

### The Sealed Scroll (5:1–4)

[[v1|I saw, in the right hand of him who sat on the throne, a book written inside and outside, sealed shut with seven seals]]. The "book" is a scroll, the usual form of written documents in John's day. It is written on both sides, suggesting that its contents are complete. It is sealed with seven seals, meaning it is fully secured and can only be opened by someone with proper authority.

What does the scroll represent? Interpreters have offered several views. Many see it as God's plan for history, His purposes of judgment and redemption that will bring the world to its intended end. Others see it as a kind of title deed to creation, which the rightful heir alone can claim. Others connect it with the sealed scroll in Daniel 12:4, which contained revelation about the end times, or with the scroll of lament and judgment in Ezekiel 2:9–10. In each view, the scroll contains God's purposes for bringing history to its goal, and the opening of its seals sets those purposes in motion.

[[v2|I saw a mighty angel proclaiming with a loud voice, “Who is worthy to open the book, and to break its seals?”]] [[v3|No one in heaven above, or on the earth, or under the earth, was able to open the book or to look in it]]. The search covers all creation, and no one is found worthy. No angel, no human ruler, no prophet, and no spiritual power has the authority to carry out God's purposes for the world.

[[v4|Then I wept much, because no one was found worthy to open the book or to look in it]]. John's tears are not merely disappointment. If no one can open the scroll, then God's purposes of justice and redemption will remain unfulfilled. Evil will continue unchecked, and the suffering of God's people will have no resolution.

### The Lion Who Is a Lamb (5:5–7)

The elder's announcement and John's vision of the Lamb are studied below. [[v7|Then he came, and he took it out of the right hand of him who sat on the throne]]. The Lamb takes the scroll directly from God's hand. This simple action has enormous meaning. The Lamb has authority to carry out God's plan for history, and He shares the authority of the One on the throne.

### The New Song (5:8–10)

[[v8|Now when he had taken the book, the four living creatures and the twenty-four elders fell down before the Lamb, each one having a harp, and golden bowls full of incense, which are the prayers of the saints]]. The worship that was given to God in chapter 4 is now given to the Lamb. This shows that Jesus is fully divine, worthy of the same worship as God the Father. The harps represent praise. The golden bowls of incense are explained as "the prayers of the saints." The prayers of God's people, often offered in suffering and weakness, rise before God's throne like fragrant incense (Psalm 141:2). These prayers will play an important role later in the book (Revelation 8:3–5). The song itself is studied below.

### All Creation Worships (5:11–14)

[[v11|I looked, and I heard something like a voice of many angels around the throne, the living creatures, and the elders. The number of them was ten thousands of ten thousands, and thousands of thousands,]] The circle of worship widens. Countless angels join the song. The number is meant to express a multitude beyond counting (compare Daniel 7:10).

[[v12|saying with a loud voice, “Worthy is the Lamb who has been killed to receive the power, wealth, wisdom, strength, honor, glory, and blessing!”]] The angels ascribe seven qualities to the Lamb, a number of completeness. Every form of honor and power belongs to Him.

[[v13|I heard every created thing which is in heaven, on the earth, under the earth, on the sea, and everything in them, saying, “To him who sits on the throne and to the Lamb be the blessing, the honor, the glory, and the dominion, forever and ever! Amen!”]] Finally, all creation joins in. The worship of the One on the throne and the worship of the Lamb are united. They receive the same praise, together, forever.

[[v14|The four living creatures said, “Amen!” Then the elders fell down and worshiped]]. The scene ends as the living creatures affirm the praise with "Amen," and the elders fall down in worship. The circle of worship that began around the throne has expanded to include the entire universe.

## Important Verses Explained

### Revelation 5:5–6 — The Lion Who Is a Lamb

verse: Revelation 5:5-6

#### Simple Explanation

As John weeps, one of the elders speaks to him: "Don’t weep." There is someone who can open the scroll. The elder describes Him with two titles from the Old Testament.

He is "the Lion who is of the tribe of Judah." This recalls Jacob's blessing in Genesis 49:9–10, where Judah is called a lion's cub and promised that the scepter will not depart from him. The Lion of Judah is the promised King from the line of Judah.

He is also "the Root of David." This recalls Isaiah 11:1 and 11:10, which promise that a shoot will come from the stump of Jesse, David's father, and that the Root of Jesse will stand as a banner for the nations. Jesus is the promised descendant of David who will reign forever.

The elder announces that this One "has overcome." His victory qualifies Him to open the scroll.

Then John turns to see the Lion, and he sees something unexpected: "a Lamb standing, as though it had been slain." The Lion is a Lamb. And the Lamb bears the marks of death, yet He is standing, alive.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This is one of the most important moments in all of Revelation. John hears about a Lion, but he sees a Lamb. The Messiah's victory did not come through force or violence, as many expected, but through sacrificial death. Jesus conquered by dying for His people. The Lion of Judah won His triumph as the Lamb of God.

The image of the Lamb recalls the Passover lamb, whose blood protected Israel from judgment and whose sacrifice marked their deliverance from Egypt (Exodus 12). It also recalls Isaiah 53:7, where the Servant of the Lord is led like a lamb to the slaughter. John the Baptist had pointed to Jesus and said, [[John 1:29|Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!]] The word "Lamb" appears about twenty-nine times in Revelation, more than any other title for Jesus.

The Lamb is "standing," showing that He is risen and alive. Yet He bears the marks of having been slain. His death is not hidden but is the very basis of His glory. The "seven horns" represent complete power, since horns symbolized strength in the Old Testament. The "seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of God, sent out into all the earth" represent complete knowledge and the presence of the Holy Spirit throughout the world.

The Lamb stands "in the middle of the throne." He shares God's own place of rule. The crucified Jesus is now enthroned as Lord of all.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage shows that God's power is revealed in the cross. The world values strength, force, and domination. But Jesus conquered through self-giving love. Believers are called to follow the Lamb, overcoming evil not by imitating the world's methods, but by faithfulness, truth, and sacrificial love.

When believers weep over the brokenness of the world or the apparent triumph of evil, they can hear the elder's words: "Don’t weep." The Lamb has conquered. History is in His hands.

Believers can also find comfort in the Lamb's wounds. The One who reigns over all still bears the marks of His suffering. He understands pain and sacrifice, and His wounds are the source of their healing and hope.

### Revelation 5:9–10 — Purchased from Every Nation

verse: Revelation 5:9-10

#### Simple Explanation

The living creatures and elders sing "a new song." In the Old Testament, a new song was sung to celebrate a fresh act of God's salvation (Psalm 96:1; 98:1; Isaiah 42:10). The death and resurrection of the Lamb is the greatest act of salvation in history, and it calls for a new song.

The song begins: "You are worthy to take the book and to open its seals." Then it gives the reason: "for you were killed, and bought us for God with your blood." The Lamb's death was a purchase. His blood was the price paid to redeem people for God.

These people come "out of every tribe, language, people, and nation." The Lamb's redemption is not limited to one nation or group. It reaches every part of humanity.

Verse 10 describes what the Lamb has made them: "and made us kings and priests to our God." The redeemed share in God's rule and serve Him in worship. Then comes a promise: "and we will reign on the earth."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The idea of being "bought" or redeemed recalls the practice of buying a slave's freedom or redeeming property that had been lost. Humanity was in bondage to sin and death. Jesus paid the price of redemption with His own blood (1 Corinthians 6:20; 1 Peter 1:18–19). Those He redeemed now belong to God.

The global scope of redemption is one of the great themes of Revelation. God promised Abraham that all the families of the earth would be blessed through him (Genesis 12:3). That promise is fulfilled through the Lamb, who has redeemed people from every culture, language, and nation. The church is not meant to be a single ethnic or cultural group but a diverse family united in worship of the Lamb. This vision leaves no room for racial or ethnic pride or prejudice among God's people.

The promise that the redeemed will "reign on the earth" points to the future renewal of creation. God's purpose is not to abandon the earth but to restore it. His people will share in Christ's reign over a renewed world (Revelation 22:5). Interpreters differ on how this reign relates to the thousand years of chapter 20 and to the new creation of chapters 21–22, but all agree that God's people will share in Christ's victory and rule.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can rejoice that they have been purchased by the blood of the Lamb. Their worth and identity come from the price He paid. They belong to God.

This passage calls believers to embrace the global and diverse nature of God's family. Every tribe, language, people, and nation is represented around the throne. Believers can celebrate this diversity now, welcoming people of all backgrounds, rejecting prejudice, and supporting the spread of the gospel to every people.

The promise of reigning on the earth gives believers hope. The present world, with its injustice and suffering, is not the end of the story. God's people will one day share in Christ's righteous rule over a renewed creation. This hope encourages faithful living now.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Revelation 5 reveals the heart of the gospel. The central question of history, who is worthy to carry out God's purposes, is answered by the Lamb who was slain. Jesus is the Lion of Judah, the Root of David, the promised King. But He conquered not through force but through sacrificial death. His cross is the key to history.

This chapter invites believers to see the world through the lens of the Lamb. When evil seems strong and God's purposes seem hidden, they can remember that the Lamb has conquered and holds the scroll of history in His hands. There is no need for despair. God's purposes will be fulfilled.

The chapter also calls believers to follow the way of the Lamb. Just as Jesus overcame through self-giving love, His people are called to overcome evil not with the world's weapons, but with faithfulness, truth, and love.

Believers can rejoice in their redemption. They were purchased by the Lamb's blood from every tribe, language, people, and nation, and made into a kingdom and priests to serve God. This gives them a secure identity, a purpose in worship and service, and a sense of belonging to a worldwide family.

Finally, the chapter calls believers to join the worship of heaven. The prayers of God's people rise before the throne like incense, and all creation sings the praise of the One on the throne and the Lamb. Believers can add their voices to this song, now and forever.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, You are the Lion of the tribe of Judah and the Root of David, and You are the Lamb who was slain. Thank You that You conquered not by force, but by giving Your life for me. Thank You that with Your blood You purchased me for God, and that You have redeemed people from every tribe, language, people, and nation. Help me to love and welcome all whom You have redeemed. When I weep over the brokenness of the world, remind me that You hold history in Your hands and that You have overcome. Teach me to follow Your way of faithful, sacrificial love. Receive my prayers like incense before Your throne. You are worthy to receive power, wealth, wisdom, strength, honor, glory, and blessing. With all creation, I worship You and the One who sits on the throne, forever and ever. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
