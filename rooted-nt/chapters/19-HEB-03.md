# Hebrews 3
theme: Greater Than Moses, and Today If You Hear His Voice
main: The author calls believers, who share in a heavenly calling, to fix their thoughts on Jesus, the apostle and high priest of their confession. Jesus was faithful to God, just as Moses was faithful in God's house, but Jesus deserves greater honor, as the builder of a house has more honor than the house itself. Moses was faithful as a servant in God's house, pointing to what was to come; Christ is faithful as the Son over God's house, and believers are that house if they hold firmly to their confidence and hope. Quoting Psalm 95, the author warns them not to harden their hearts as Israel did in the wilderness, when the people who had seen God's works for forty years rebelled and could not enter His rest because of unbelief. He urges them to watch out for an unbelieving heart that turns away from the living God and to encourage one another every day, as long as it is called "Today," so that none are hardened by sin's deceitfulness.

## Chapter Background and Story

After showing that Jesus is greater than the angels (chapters 1–2), the author now compares Jesus with Moses. For Jewish readers, few figures were more honored than Moses. He led Israel out of slavery in Egypt, received the Law at Mount Sinai, built the tabernacle according to God's instructions, and spoke with God [[Numbers 12:8|mouth to mouth]] (Numbers 12:7–8). In Numbers 12:7, God Himself says of Moses, [[Numbers 12:7|He is faithful in all my house]].

The author does not criticize Moses. On the contrary, he honors Moses' faithfulness. But he shows that Jesus is greater, as a son is greater than a servant and a builder is greater than a house.

The second half of the chapter draws on one of the most painful episodes in Israel's history. After God rescued Israel from Egypt, the people repeatedly complained and rebelled in the wilderness. At Kadesh, when they were on the edge of the promised land, they refused to enter because they did not trust God to give them victory (Numbers 13–14). As a result, that generation wandered for forty years and died in the wilderness. Psalm 95 recalls this event as a warning. The author applies it to his readers, who were also tempted to turn back because of fear and hardship.

[[v4|For every house is built by someone; but he who built all things is God]]. A builder deserves more honor than the building. "House" here means household or family, the people of God. ### Today, If You Will Hear His Voice (3:7–11)

The warning from Psalm 95 is studied below. [[v9|where your fathers tested me and tried me, and saw my deeds for forty years]]. [[v10|Therefore I was displeased with that generation, and said, ‘They always err in their heart, but they didn’t know my ways.’]] [[v11|As I swore in my wrath, ‘They will not enter into my rest.’”]] Israel had seen God's mighty works: the plagues in Egypt, the parting of the Red Sea, manna from heaven, water from the rock. Yet they kept testing Him. Their problem was a heart problem: "they always err in their heart." God's oath, "they will not enter into my rest," refers to the promised land, the place of rest God had prepared for them. In chapter 4, the author will explain that God's "rest" points beyond Canaan to a deeper rest that remains for God's people.

### Encourage One Another Daily (3:12–15)

The warning against an unbelieving heart and the call to daily encouragement are studied below. [[v14|For we have become partakers of Christ, if we hold the beginning of our confidence firm to the end]]. Believers share in Christ. The phrase "if we hold... firm to the end" shows that genuine participation in Christ is shown by perseverance. As discussed elsewhere, Christians understand the relationship between perseverance and salvation in different ways: some emphasize that those truly in Christ will certainly persevere because God keeps them, while others emphasize that believers must continue in faith and can fall away. Both take this call to endurance seriously.

[[v15|while it is said, “Today if you will hear his voice, don’t harden your hearts, as in the rebellion.”]] The author repeats the key line from Psalm 95.

### Unbelief Kept Them Out (3:16–19)

[[v16|For who, when they heard, rebelled? Wasn’t it all those who came out of Egypt led by Moses?]] [[v17|With whom was he displeased forty years? Wasn’t it with those who sinned, whose bodies fell in the wilderness?]] [[v18|To whom did he swear that they wouldn’t enter into his rest, but to those who were disobedient?]] [[v19|We see that they weren’t able to enter in because of unbelief]]. The author asks a series of questions to drive the point home. The very people who had experienced God's great rescue from Egypt were the ones who rebelled. Having a great spiritual beginning does not guarantee finishing well. The root problem was unbelief, a refusal to trust God's promise, which showed itself in disobedience.

## Important Verses Explained

### Hebrews 3:1–2 — Consider Jesus

verse: Hebrews 3:1-2

#### Simple Explanation

"Therefore, holy brothers, partakers of a heavenly calling." The author reminds his readers of their identity. They are "holy," set apart for God. They are "brothers," family members, as Jesus Himself is not ashamed to call them (Hebrews 2:11). And they share in "a heavenly calling," an invitation from God that comes from heaven and leads to heaven.

"Consider the Apostle and High Priest of our confession: Jesus." "Consider" means to fix one's thoughts on, to look closely and carefully. Believers are to focus their attention on Jesus.

Jesus is called "the Apostle," the only time this title is used for Him in the New Testament. An apostle is one who is sent. Jesus was sent by God to represent God to humanity. He is also "High Priest," one who represents humanity before God. In Jesus, God comes to us, and in Jesus, we come to God.

"Of our confession" means Jesus is the one whom believers publicly confess and trust.

Verse 2: "who was faithful to him who appointed him, as also Moses was in all his house." Jesus was completely faithful to the Father who sent Him, just as Moses was faithful in his role.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse summarizes the whole message of Hebrews in one command: consider Jesus. The readers were struggling with discouragement, fear, and the temptation to drift. The remedy was not first to try harder but to look at Jesus more closely. Hebrews 12:2 echoes this: [[Hebrews 12:2|looking to Jesus, the author and perfecter of faith]].

The two titles, Apostle and High Priest, together capture Jesus' role as mediator. As Apostle, He brings God's word to humanity (Hebrews 1:1–2). As High Priest, He brings humanity to God (Hebrews 2:17). He bridges the gap between God and people completely.

The comparison with Moses begins with honor. Moses was faithful, and so was Jesus. The author does not diminish Moses to exalt Jesus; he shows that Jesus fulfills and surpasses all that Moses represented.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

When believers are discouraged, confused, or tempted to give up, the first step is to "consider Jesus": to fix their thoughts on who He is and what He has done. This might involve reading the Gospels, meditating on passages about Christ, or simply pausing in prayer to remember His faithfulness.

Believers can also remember their identity: holy, part of God's family, and sharing in a heavenly calling. Their lives have a purpose and destination beyond this world.

Finally, Jesus' faithfulness to the Father is an example for believers. Like Him, they can be faithful in the tasks and relationships God has given them.

### Hebrews 3:5–6 — Moses the Servant, Christ the Son

verse: Hebrews 3:5-6

#### Simple Explanation

"Moses indeed was faithful in all his house as a servant." Moses was a faithful servant in God's household, Israel. "Servant" here is an honorable title, used for someone trusted with important responsibilities.

"For a testimony of those things which were afterward to be spoken." Moses' ministry pointed forward. The Law, the tabernacle, and the sacrifices were a witness to things that would be revealed later, in Christ. Jesus said, [[John 5:46|if you believed Moses, you would believe me; for he wrote about me]].

Verse 6: "but Christ is faithful as a Son over his house." Jesus' relationship to God's household is different. He is not a servant in the house but the Son over it, the heir and master.

"We are his house." Believers are God's household. The church is God's family, the place where He dwells.

"If we hold fast our confidence and the glorying of our hope firm to the end." Belonging to God's house is shown by holding firmly to confidence and hope in Christ until the end.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The contrast between servant and son shows the superiority of the new covenant over the old. Under Moses, God's people had the Law and the tabernacle, which were good but pointed beyond themselves. In Christ, they have the reality those things pointed to. Jesus is not another servant in the line of Moses and the prophets; He is the Son, the one the whole household belongs to.

The statement "we are his house" is profound. In the Old Testament, God's "house" could mean the tabernacle or temple where He dwelt. Now His house is His people. As Paul writes, believers are being built together into a dwelling place for God (Ephesians 2:21–22).

The phrase "if we hold fast" is the first of several in Hebrews that emphasize perseverance. Faith is not a one-time event but an ongoing confidence that holds firm to the end.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can read the Old Testament with appreciation, recognizing Moses and the Law as faithful witnesses that point to Christ. The Law is not discarded; it is fulfilled.

Being part of God's house gives believers a deep sense of belonging. They are not strangers but members of God's household, under the care of Christ the Son.

The call to "hold fast our confidence" invites believers to persevere, especially in seasons of doubt or difficulty. Confidence in Christ is not maintained by feelings but by continually returning to the truth of who He is and what He has promised.

### Hebrews 3:7–8 — Do Not Harden Your Hearts

verse: Hebrews 3:7-8

#### Simple Explanation

"Therefore, even as the Holy Spirit says." The author introduces a quotation from Psalm 95:7–11. Notice that he says "the Holy Spirit says," in the present tense. Although the psalm was written centuries earlier, the author believes the Spirit is still speaking through it to believers now.

"Today if you will hear his voice." God is speaking. The question is whether people will listen. "Today" emphasizes urgency. The opportunity to respond is now.

Verse 8: "don't harden your hearts as in the rebellion, in the day of the trial in the wilderness." The "rebellion" and "trial" refer to the events at Meribah and Massah, where Israel complained about lack of water and tested God (Exodus 17:1–7), and more broadly to the wilderness generation's repeated unbelief, especially their refusal to enter the promised land (Numbers 14).

To "harden the heart" is to become resistant to God, unresponsive to His voice, stubborn in unbelief. A hard heart refuses to trust or obey.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Hardening of the heart is a process. It often begins with small acts of resistance to God's voice: ignoring conviction, delaying obedience, choosing one's own way. Over time, the heart becomes less sensitive. Pharaoh's story in Exodus shows a heart that grew harder with each refusal (Exodus 8:15, 32).

The word "today" is central to this chapter and the next. Each day brings a new opportunity to hear and respond to God. But the opportunity is not unlimited. The wilderness generation missed their chance and died in the wilderness. Paul echoes this urgency: [[2 Corinthians 6:2|Behold, now is the acceptable time. Behold, now is the day of salvation]].

The fact that the Holy Spirit "says" these words today shows that Scripture is living. God speaks through it to every generation (Hebrews 4:12). When believers read Scripture, they are not merely reading ancient history; they are hearing God's voice.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can ask whether there are areas of their lives where they have been resisting God's voice: a conviction they have ignored, a step of obedience they have delayed, or a sin they have refused to give up. Today is the day to respond.

Keeping a soft heart involves regular practices of listening to God: reading Scripture, praying, confessing sin, and responding quickly when God speaks.

This passage also brings hope. As long as it is "today," it is not too late. God is still speaking, and His invitation to trust and obey is still open.

### Hebrews 3:12–13 — Encourage One Another Every Day

verse: Hebrews 3:12-13

#### Simple Explanation

"Beware, brothers, lest perhaps there might be in any one of you an evil heart of unbelief, in falling away from the living God." The author warns believers to watch carefully. The danger is "an evil heart of unbelief," a heart that does not trust God. Unbelief is not merely intellectual doubt but a heart that refuses to rely on God. It leads to "falling away from the living God," turning away from Him.

Notice the phrase "any one of you." The author is concerned for every individual in the community. No one should be left behind.

Verse 13 gives the remedy: "but exhort one another day by day, so long as it is called 'today.'" "Exhort" means to encourage, to urge, to come alongside. Believers are to encourage one another daily, not occasionally.

"Lest any one of you be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin." Sin is deceitful. It promises pleasure, freedom, or satisfaction, but it leads to bondage and death. It hardens hearts gradually, often without the person realizing it. Encouragement from others helps protect against this deception.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage shows that perseverance in faith is not only an individual responsibility but a community one. Believers need one another. Alone, a person can be deceived by sin and drift away without noticing. In community, believers can see warning signs in one another, offer encouragement, and help each other stay on course. Ecclesiastes 4:9–10 says that two are better than one, because if one falls, the other can lift him up.

"The deceitfulness of sin" is a powerful phrase. Sin rarely presents itself as destructive. It disguises itself as something good or harmless. Eve was deceived by the serpent (Genesis 3:13). Paul speaks of "the deceitful lusts" of the old self (Ephesians 4:22). Encouragement from others helps believers see clearly.

The call to encourage "day by day" emphasizes regular, ongoing involvement in one another's lives. Sunday gatherings are important, but this passage envisions believers caring for one another throughout the week.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can take active roles in encouraging others: sending a message of encouragement, calling a friend who is struggling, praying with someone, or gently speaking up when they see someone drifting.

This passage also invites believers to welcome encouragement and correction from others. Being part of a small group, having trusted friends, or meeting regularly with a mentor can provide the daily encouragement this passage describes.

Finally, believers can be aware of sin's deceitfulness in their own lives. Sin often justifies itself. Honest friends who speak truth in love are a gift from God in resisting that deception.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Hebrews 3 calls believers to fix their thoughts on Jesus, the Apostle and High Priest of their confession. When they are weary or discouraged, the first remedy is to look at Him more closely. Jesus is faithful, and He is greater than Moses, as a builder is greater than a house and a son is greater than a servant. Moses faithfully pointed forward; Jesus is the Son over God's house, and believers are that house.

The chapter also contains a serious warning drawn from Israel's history. The generation that came out of Egypt had seen God's mighty works, yet they hardened their hearts in unbelief and could not enter the land of rest. Their story warns believers that a strong beginning does not guarantee a faithful ending. Unbelief, a refusal to trust God's promises, can lead people away from the living God.

The remedy is twofold. First, believers must listen to God's voice "today," responding with soft and trusting hearts, not delaying obedience. Second, they must encourage one another daily, so that no one is hardened by the deceitfulness of sin. Perseverance is a community effort. Believers need each other to stay on course.

As long as it is called "today," God's invitation stands. Believers can hold fast their confidence and hope to the end, trusting the faithful Son who leads His household, and helping one another reach the rest He has promised.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, You are the Apostle and High Priest of our confession, faithful to the Father in everything. Help me to fix my thoughts on You. Thank You that You are the Son over God's house and that I belong to that house. Help me to hold fast my confidence and hope to the end. Holy Spirit, as You speak today, give me a heart that hears and obeys. Keep me from hardening my heart through unbelief or delayed obedience. Guard me from the deceitfulness of sin. Make me an encourager of others, day by day, so that none of us falls away from the living God. Thank You that it is still "today," and that Your invitation is still open. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
