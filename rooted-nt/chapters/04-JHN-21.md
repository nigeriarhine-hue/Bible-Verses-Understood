# John 21
theme: Breakfast on the Shore: Peter Restored and Called to Follow
main: Beside the Sea of Galilee, the risen Jesus meets seven disciples after a fruitless night of fishing, fills their net with a miraculous catch, and serves them breakfast over a charcoal fire. Then He asks Peter three times, "Do you love me?", restoring the disciple who had denied Him three times and entrusting him with the care of His sheep. Jesus foretells the kind of death by which Peter will glorify God and calls him simply to follow, without comparing his path to anyone else's. John closes his Gospel by affirming that his testimony is true and that Jesus did far more than could ever be written.

## Chapter Background and Story

John 20 ended with what sounds like a conclusion: "these are written that you may believe" (20:31). Chapter 21 is often described as an epilogue. It ties up an important unfinished thread, Peter's restoration after his denial, and it looks forward to the mission of the church and the future of two key apostles. All Greek manuscripts of John include this chapter, and its style and themes fit closely with the rest of the Gospel.

The setting moves from Jerusalem back to Galilee, where Jesus first called these men to follow Him. Matthew and Mark record that Jesus told the disciples He would meet them in Galilee (Matthew 28:7; Mark 16:7).

### A Night of Fishing (21:1–3)

Seven disciples were together by the Sea of Tiberias, another name for the Sea of Galilee: Simon Peter, Thomas, Nathanael, the sons of Zebedee (James and John), and two others. Peter said, [[v3|I’m going fishing]], and the others joined him. Some see this as Peter returning to his old life in discouragement. Others think the disciples were simply waiting in Galilee as instructed and needed to eat and earn a living. Either way, the scene recalls the beginning of Peter's discipleship. Fishing was usually done at night on the Sea of Galilee. [[v3|That night, they caught nothing]].

### The Miraculous Catch (21:4–14)

At dawn, Jesus stood on the shore, though the disciples did not recognize Him. He called out, [[v5|Children, have you anything to eat?]] "Children" was a friendly, familiar way of addressing them, a little like "lads" or "friends." They answered, "No." Jesus' instruction and the result are studied below.

The beloved disciple recognized Him first: [[v7|It’s the Lord!]] Peter, impulsive as ever, wrapped his outer garment around himself (he had stripped down for work) and jumped into the water to reach Jesus quickly. The others followed in the boat, dragging the net, since they were only about a hundred yards from shore.

On the shore, [[v9|they saw a fire of coals there, with fish and bread laid on it]]. The phrase "fire of coals" (a charcoal fire) appears in only one other place in the New Testament: the courtyard fire where Peter warmed himself as he denied Jesus (18:18). The scene of Peter's failure is quietly echoed in the scene of his restoration.

Jesus told them to bring some of the fish they had caught. Peter dragged the net ashore, [[v11|full of one hundred fifty-three great fish. Even though there were so many, the net wasn’t torn]]. Many interpreters have looked for symbolic meaning in the number 153. One ancient suggestion is that it represents all the kinds of fish then known, pointing to the gospel reaching all nations. Others see no special symbolism, only the careful memory of an eyewitness who remembered counting the catch. Many also see in the unbroken net a picture of the church, which holds people of every nation without tearing apart.

Jesus said, [[v12|Come and eat breakfast!]] The disciples knew it was the Lord, though they did not dare to ask Him. [[v13|Then Jesus came and took the bread, gave it to them, and the fish likewise]]. The risen Lord of glory served them breakfast, recalling His feeding of the five thousand by the same lake (6:11). John notes that this was the third time Jesus appeared to His disciples as a group after His resurrection.

### "Do You Love Me?" (21:15–19)

After breakfast, Jesus turned to Peter and asked him three times whether he loved Him. This conversation is studied below, along with Jesus' prophecy of Peter's death and His call to follow.

### "What About This Man?" (21:20–23)

Peter turned and saw the beloved disciple following them. He asked, [[v21|Lord, what about this man?]] Jesus' answer is studied below. John then corrects a rumor that had spread in the early church. Some believers had understood Jesus' words to mean that this disciple would not die before Jesus returned. John explains that Jesus did not say that; He only said, "If I desire that he stay until I come, what is that to you?" The correction may suggest that the beloved disciple was very old, or had recently died, when the Gospel was completed.

### The Testimony of the Beloved Disciple (21:24–25)

[[v24|This is the disciple who testifies about these things, and wrote these things. We know that his witness is true]]. The "we" may be the church or a group of John's companions who knew him and affirmed his testimony. The beloved disciple, traditionally identified as John the son of Zebedee, is the source and author of this Gospel.

The Gospel closes with a striking statement: [[v25|There are also many other things which Jesus did, which if they would all be written, I suppose that even the world itself wouldn’t have room for the books that would be written]]. John has selected only some of Jesus' works and words. The full story of who Jesus is and what He has done can never be exhausted.

## Important Verses Explained

### John 21:6 — Cast the Net on the Right Side

verse: John 21:6

#### Simple Explanation

Seven disciples had fished all night on the Sea of Galilee and caught nothing. These were experienced fishermen. Peter, James, and John had grown up fishing on this lake. They knew where the fish were and how to catch them. Yet after a whole night of work, their nets were empty.

At dawn, a man on the shore called out and asked whether they had caught anything. They said no. Then He said, [[v6|Cast the net on the right side of the boat, and you will find some]]. From a human perspective, this advice made little sense. The disciples were the experts. A stranger on the shore could hardly know better than they did. Yet they obeyed.

[[v6|They cast it therefore, and now they weren’t able to draw it in for the multitude of fish]]. The net was suddenly so full that they could not haul it into the boat.

This event recalled something that had happened years earlier. Luke records that when Jesus first called Peter, Peter had also fished all night and caught nothing. Jesus told him to let down his nets, and they caught so many fish that the nets began to break (Luke 5:4–7). Then Jesus said, "From now on you will be catching people" (Luke 5:10). The beloved disciple seems to have recognized this pattern, and he said, "It's the Lord!"

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This miracle shows the difference between working in human strength and working at Jesus' word. The disciples were skilled, and they worked hard, but without Jesus, they caught nothing. When they obeyed His word, their nets were filled. Jesus had taught them, [[John 15:5|apart from me you can do nothing]].

It also points to the mission of the church. The disciples had been called to be fishers of people. The great catch is a picture of the many who would come to faith through their witness after Jesus' resurrection. The fruitfulness of mission depends on the Lord's direction and power.

Finally, the miracle shows the generosity of the risen Christ. He did not merely give them enough for a meal; He gave them an abundance. He already had fish cooking on the fire, yet He invited them to bring some of what they had caught. He provides abundantly, and He also delights to include His followers in His work.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage speaks to believers who feel that their efforts are fruitless. Many people work hard in their families, churches, or jobs and see little result. The disciples' empty nets remind believers that fruitfulness ultimately comes from Christ. Believers can bring their discouragement to Him and listen for His direction.

It also encourages obedience even when Jesus' instructions seem unexpected. The disciples were experts, yet they followed the word of a stranger on the shore. Believers may sometimes find that God's word challenges their experience or expectations. Obedience to Him leads to blessing.

Finally, the passage reminds believers that Jesus is present in ordinary life. He met His disciples not in the temple but at work, by the lake, at breakfast. He meets His people today in their daily tasks, offering His presence and guidance.

### John 21:15–17 — "Do You Love Me?"

verse: John 21:15-17

#### Simple Explanation

After breakfast on the shore, Jesus turned to Simon Peter. Only days before, Peter had denied knowing Jesus three times beside a charcoal fire in the high priest's courtyard. Now, beside another charcoal fire, Jesus spoke to him.

[[v15|Simon, son of Jonah, do you love me more than these?]] Jesus called him "Simon, son of Jonah," his original name, rather than "Peter," the name Jesus had given him meaning "rock." The phrase "more than these" may mean "more than these other disciples love Me," recalling Peter's earlier boast that even if all others fell away, he would not (Matthew 26:33). Or it may mean "more than these things," such as fishing and boats. Either way, Jesus was asking Peter to examine his heart.

Peter answered, [[v15|Yes, Lord; you know that I have affection for you]]. Jesus said, [[v15|Feed my lambs]].

Jesus asked a second time, and Peter gave the same answer. Jesus said, [[v16|Tend my sheep]].

Then Jesus asked a third time: [[v17|Simon, son of Jonah, do you have affection for me?]] [[v17|Peter was grieved because he asked him the third time]]. The third question surely reminded Peter of his three denials. He answered, [[v17|Lord, you know everything. You know that I have affection for you]]. Peter no longer boasted about his own loyalty. He appealed to Jesus' knowledge of his heart. And Jesus said, [[v17|Feed my sheep]].

The World English Bible translates two different Greek words for love here. In His first two questions, Jesus used the word agapao (translated "love"), and in the third question He used phileo (translated "have affection for"), the word Peter used each time. Some interpreters believe the change is significant, with Jesus coming down to Peter's level. Others note that John often uses these words interchangeably and see the variation as stylistic. Either way, the central point is clear: three denials are answered by three affirmations of love, and each affirmation is followed by a commission.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage is a beautiful picture of restoration. Peter had failed badly, but Jesus did not reject him. He did not lecture him or demand a long period of proving himself. He invited him to affirm his love and then restored him to service. The grace of Christ is greater than our greatest failures.

It also shows that love for Jesus is the foundation of ministry. Jesus did not ask Peter about his skill, his knowledge, or even his courage. He asked about his love. Service flows from love for Christ. And love for Christ shows itself in caring for His people: "Feed my sheep."

Finally, notice that Jesus calls them "my lambs" and "my sheep." The flock belongs to Christ, not to Peter. Peter would serve as a shepherd under the Good Shepherd. Peter later wrote to church leaders, urging them to shepherd God's flock willingly and humbly, and calling Jesus [[1 Peter 5:4|the chief Shepherd]].

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage brings hope to anyone who has failed Jesus. Many believers carry guilt over moments when they denied Christ by their words, actions, or silence. Peter's story shows that failure is not the end. Jesus seeks out those who have failed and restores them.

It also invites believers to examine their love for Jesus. The question "Do you love me?" is asked of every follower. Love for Christ is not measured by bold promises but by humble dependence on His knowledge of the heart and by faithful care for His people.

Finally, the passage reminds believers that loving Jesus means caring for others. Feeding and tending His sheep can take many forms: teaching, encouraging, serving, praying for, and supporting other believers. Every act of loving care for God's people is an expression of love for Christ.

### John 21:18–19 — "Follow Me"

verse: John 21:18-19

#### Simple Explanation

After restoring Peter and giving him the task of caring for His sheep, Jesus spoke about Peter's future: [[v18|Most certainly I tell you, when you were young, you dressed yourself and walked where you wanted to]]. As a young man, Peter had been independent, going where he wished and making his own choices.

[[v18|But when you are old, you will stretch out your hands, and another will dress you and carry you where you don’t want to go]]. Jesus was describing a future in which Peter would lose his freedom. The phrase "stretch out your hands" was used in the ancient world to describe crucifixion.

John explains, [[v19|Now he said this, signifying by what kind of death he would glorify God]]. Peter would die as a martyr. Early Christian tradition reports that Peter was crucified in Rome during the persecution under Emperor Nero, around A.D. 64–68. One tradition says he asked to be crucified upside down, feeling unworthy to die in the same way as his Lord.

Notice the phrase [[v19|he would glorify God]]. Peter's death would not be a tragedy without meaning. It would bring glory to God, just as Jesus' death glorified the Father.

Then Jesus said to him, [[v19|Follow me]]. These were the same words Jesus spoke when He first called Peter by the lake years before (Mark 1:17). Now, after Peter's failure and restoration, and knowing the cost, Jesus called him again.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage shows that following Jesus may lead to suffering. Peter had once promised to lay down his life for Jesus (13:37) but failed when the moment came. Now Jesus told him that he would indeed lay down his life, not in his own strength, but by God's grace. The Peter who once denied Jesus would one day die for Him.

It also shows that God can be glorified in the death of His people. Death is still an enemy, but for believers it can become a final act of faithful witness. Paul wrote that he hoped Christ would be magnified in his body [[Philippians 1:20|whether by life, or by death]].

Finally, the call "Follow me" summarizes the whole Christian life. It is not a one-time decision but a daily walk with Jesus wherever He leads, including through suffering. Peter's restoration was not merely forgiveness; it was a renewed call to follow.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage reminds believers that following Jesus involves surrender. Like Peter, believers may have once lived as they pleased, going where they wanted. Following Christ means allowing Him to lead, even when His path goes where they would not choose.

It also encourages believers facing suffering, illness, or the losses of old age. Many people experience a loss of independence as they grow older, being "carried where they don't want to go." Even in such seasons, believers can glorify God through their faith, patience, and trust.

Finally, the passage renews Jesus' call to every believer: "Follow me." Whatever failures lie in the past, and whatever challenges lie ahead, the call remains the same. Each day, believers can respond by following Jesus with trust and love.

### John 21:21–22 — "What Is That to You? You Follow Me"

verse: John 21:21-22

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus had just told Peter that he would die a martyr's death and called him to follow. Peter turned and saw the beloved disciple following them. He asked, [[v21|Lord, what about this man?]] Peter wanted to know what would happen to this other disciple. Would he also suffer? Would his path be easier?

Jesus answered, [[v22|If I desire that he stay until I come, what is that to you? You follow me]]. Jesus' answer was firm but not harsh. He told Peter, in effect, that the other disciple's future was not Peter's concern. Jesus had a plan for each of them. Peter's responsibility was to follow Jesus himself.

The word "You" in "You follow me" is emphatic in Greek. Jesus was calling Peter to focus on his own walk with Him rather than comparing himself with others.

John adds that this saying led to a rumor among believers that the beloved disciple would not die before Jesus returned. He corrects this misunderstanding: Jesus did not say that he would not die; He only said, "If I desire that he stay until I come, what is that to you?"

Peter and John would have very different paths. Peter would die as a martyr, probably in Rome in the 60s. According to early Christian tradition, John lived to old age in Ephesus and died a natural death near the end of the first century. Both glorified God, but in different ways.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage teaches that each disciple has a unique calling. God does not lead all His people in the same way. Some suffer greatly; others are spared certain trials. Some have public ministries; others serve quietly. God's plans for each person are His to decide. Paul writes that believers have different gifts according to the grace given to them (Romans 12:6).

It also reveals the danger of comparison. Comparing oneself with others can lead to envy, resentment, pride, or discouragement. Peter's question shows a very human tendency. Jesus' answer redirects attention to the most important thing: personal faithfulness to Him.

Finally, the mention of Jesus' coming ("until I come") points to the hope of His return. The Gospel ends with the expectation that Jesus will come again. Until then, His followers are called to follow Him faithfully, each in the path He gives.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage frees believers from the trap of comparison. It is easy to look at other Christians and wonder why their lives seem easier, why their gifts seem greater, or why they have been spared certain sufferings. Jesus' words apply: "What is that to you? You follow me." Each believer is responsible for their own faithfulness to Christ.

It also helps believers accept their unique path. God's plan for one person may involve hardships that another person does not face. Trusting God's wisdom and love means accepting His plan without demanding that it look like someone else's.

Finally, the passage encourages believers to keep their eyes on Jesus. Distraction, comparison, and curiosity about others can pull attention away from Him. The call is simple and personal: follow Him.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

John 21 shows that the risen Jesus meets His people in ordinary life. He came to tired fishermen at dawn, after a night of failure, and served them breakfast. Believers can expect to meet Him not only in church but at work, at home, and in the routines of daily life. When their efforts feel fruitless, they can listen for His word and trust Him for fruitfulness.

The chapter is also a powerful story of restoration. Peter had denied Jesus three times, and Jesus restored him with three questions of love and three commissions to care for His sheep. Believers who have failed Christ can take heart. He does not discard His people when they fall. He invites them to renewed love and renewed service.

John 21 reminds believers that love for Jesus is expressed in caring for His people. Feeding and tending His sheep is the calling not only of pastors but of every believer who encourages, serves, and supports others in their faith. Love for Christ and love for His church go together.

Finally, the chapter calls each believer to follow Jesus personally, without comparing their path to anyone else's. Peter would die as a martyr; the beloved disciple would live long. Each glorified God in his own way. Believers today are invited to trust the path Jesus gives them, to follow Him faithfully until He comes, and to remember that His story is greater than all the books the world could hold.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for meeting Your disciples on the shore after their night of failure, and for meeting me in the ordinary places of my life. When my efforts seem empty, help me to listen for Your voice and to trust You for fruitfulness. Thank You for the grace You showed Peter, restoring him after his denial and calling him again to follow You. You know everything; You know that I love You, even when my love is weak. Restore me where I have failed You, and help me to show my love by caring for Your people. Give me courage to follow You wherever You lead, even when the path is hard or not what I would choose. Keep me from comparing my path with others, and help me to keep my eyes on You until You come again. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
