# Hebrews 2
theme: Do Not Drift, and Behold Our Brother and High Priest
main: Because the Son is so much greater than the angels, believers must pay careful attention to what they have heard, so they do not drift away. If the message delivered by angels was binding, how much more serious it would be to neglect the great salvation first announced by the Lord and confirmed by God through signs, wonders, and gifts of the Spirit. God has not placed the coming world under angels but under humanity, as Psalm 8 says, and although we do not yet see all things under human rule, we see Jesus, made for a little while lower than the angels, now crowned with glory and honor because He suffered death for everyone. Jesus shared fully in human flesh and blood so that by His death He might destroy the one who has the power of death, the devil, and free those held in slavery by the fear of death. He became like His brothers and sisters in every way so that He could be a merciful and faithful high priest, making atonement for sins, and because He Himself suffered when tempted, He is able to help those who are tempted.

## Chapter Background and Story

Hebrews 2 contains the first of five warning passages in the letter (2:1–4; 3:7–4:13; 5:11–6:12; 10:26–39; 12:14–29). These warnings are addressed to believers who were tempted to drift away from Christ. They are serious, but they are spoken in love, by a pastor who wants his readers to hold fast and reach the goal.

After the warning, the chapter turns to a surprising truth. Chapter 1 emphasized the Son's divine glory, far above the angels. Chapter 2 emphasizes His humanity. For a time, He was made lower than the angels. He shared human flesh and blood, suffered, was tempted, and died. Some of the first readers may have found it hard to believe that a crucified man could be the glorious Son of God. The author explains that Jesus' humanity and suffering were not a contradiction of His glory but the very path by which He brought many children to glory.

This chapter introduces a central theme of Hebrews: Jesus as our high priest. Because He became fully human, He understands human weakness and can represent humanity before God.

### Do Not Drift Away (2:1–4)

The first warning is studied below.

### Jesus Crowned with Glory (2:5–9)

[[v5|For he didn’t subject the world to come, of which we speak, to angels]]. "The world to come" is the new creation that God is bringing, which has already begun in Christ. God did not appoint angels to rule it.

[[v6|But one has somewhere testified, saying, “What is man, that you think of him? Or the son of man, that you care for him?]] [[v7|You made him a little lower than the angels. You crowned him with glory and honor.]] [[v8|You have put all things in subjection under his feet.”]] The author quotes Psalm 8:4–6. This psalm marvels at God's care for human beings and celebrates the honor God gave humanity at creation: to rule over the world as His representatives (Genesis 1:26–28). "Somewhere" does not mean the author had forgotten where the quotation came from; it was a common way of quoting a well-known passage, emphasizing God as the speaker rather than the human author.

[[v8|For in that he subjected all things to him, he left nothing that is not subject to him. But now we don’t yet see all things subjected to him]]. God's purpose was that humanity would rule over all creation. But that is not what we see. Sin, death, suffering, and chaos show that humanity has failed to fulfill its calling. The world is not yet as God intended. How Jesus fulfills this purpose is studied below.

### The Pioneer of Salvation (2:10–13)

The meaning of Jesus' suffering is studied below. [[v11|For both he who sanctifies and those who are sanctified are all from one, for which cause he is not ashamed to call them brothers]]. Jesus, who makes people holy, and those He makes holy share the same origin, whether that means they share one Father, God, or share one human nature. Because of this, Jesus is "not ashamed" to call them His brothers and sisters. This is a stunning statement: the radiance of God's glory (Hebrews 1:3) is not embarrassed to be family with weak and sinful people.

[[v12|saying, “I will declare your name to my brothers. Among the congregation I will sing your praise.”]] [[v13|Again, “I will put my trust in him.” Again, “Behold, here I am with the children whom God has given me.”]] The author quotes Psalm 22:22, the psalm Jesus prayed on the cross, which moves from suffering to praise among God's people. He also quotes Isaiah 8:17–18, where the prophet trusts God and stands with the children God has given him. In each case, the speaker identifies with God's people and stands among them.

### Destroying the Power of Death (2:14–16)

Jesus' victory over death and the devil is studied below. [[v16|For most certainly, he doesn’t give help to angels, but he gives help to the offspring of Abraham]]. Jesus did not become an angel to help angels. He became human, a descendant of Abraham, to help humanity. "The offspring of Abraham" refers to all who share Abraham's faith (Galatians 3:7, 29).

### A Merciful and Faithful High Priest (2:17–18)

The chapter's climax, introducing Jesus as high priest, is studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### Hebrews 2:1–3 — So Great a Salvation

verse: Hebrews 2:1-3

#### Simple Explanation

"Therefore we ought to pay greater attention to the things that were heard, lest perhaps we drift away." The word "therefore" connects this warning to chapter 1. Because the Son is so great, believers must listen carefully to His message. The danger is drifting. The word was used for a boat that slips its mooring and drifts away from the harbor, or for a ring slipping off a finger. Drifting is usually not a dramatic decision but a gradual, almost unnoticed movement away.

Verse 2: "For if the word spoken through angels proved steadfast, and every transgression and disobedience received a just penalty." "The word spoken through angels" is the Law given at Sinai, which Jewish tradition said was delivered through angels (Acts 7:53; Galatians 3:19). That Law was binding, and disobedience was punished.

Verse 3: "how will we escape if we neglect so great a salvation." If ignoring the Law brought consequences, how much more serious is neglecting the salvation brought by the Son? Notice the word "neglect." The danger is not necessarily open rejection but carelessness, failing to give the gospel the attention it deserves.

"Which at the first having been spoken through the Lord, was confirmed to us by those who heard." This salvation was first proclaimed by Jesus Himself, and then confirmed by eyewitnesses who heard Him.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This warning is gentle in tone but serious in content. The author includes himself: "we ought," "lest we drift," "how will we escape." He is not condemning his readers from a distance but standing alongside them.

The image of drifting is powerful. Spiritual decline is often slow. A believer may stop gathering with other believers, stop praying, stop reading Scripture, or grow indifferent, without ever consciously deciding to abandon faith. Over time, they find themselves far from where they once were. Jesus described seed choked by [[Mark 4:19|the cares of this age, and the deceitfulness of riches, and the lusts of other things]].

The remedy is "greater attention." Believers anchor themselves by giving careful, ongoing attention to the gospel: hearing it, remembering it, and responding to it.

Verse 4 adds that God confirmed this message "by signs and wonders, by various works of power, and by gifts of the Holy Spirit, according to his own will." The gospel came with God's own testimony. It is not merely a human idea but God's word.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can ask whether they are drifting. Have prayer, Scripture, worship, and fellowship become less important than they once were? Has indifference crept in? Recognizing drift is the first step toward returning.

Practical anchors include regular time in Scripture, consistent participation in a church community, honest friendships with other believers, and habits of prayer. These help believers "pay greater attention" to the gospel.

This warning also highlights the greatness of salvation. The gospel is not a small matter to be treated casually. It is "so great a salvation," worth our full attention and devotion.

### Hebrews 2:9–10 — Crowned with Glory Through Suffering

verse: Hebrews 2:9-10

#### Simple Explanation

The author has just said that we do not yet see all things subject to humanity (v8). "But we see him." Though humanity has failed, there is one human being in whom God's purpose is fulfilled: Jesus.

"Who has been made a little lower than the angels." Jesus, the eternal Son, became human. For a time, He took a position lower than the angels, entering human weakness and mortality.

"Because of the suffering of death crowned with glory and honor." Through His death and resurrection, Jesus was exalted. He now holds the glory and honor that Psalm 8 describes. He is the true human being, ruling as God intended.

"That by the grace of God he should taste of death for everyone." Jesus died "for everyone." His death was an act of God's grace, on behalf of all humanity. "Taste of death" means to experience death fully.

Verse 10: "For it became him, for whom are all things and through whom are all things, in bringing many children to glory, to make the author of their salvation perfect through sufferings." "It became him" means it was fitting, appropriate to God's character. God's purpose is "bringing many children to glory," leading His people to share in Christ's glory. Jesus is "the author," also translated "pioneer" or "champion," of their salvation: the one who goes ahead, opens the way, and leads others along it.

"Perfect through sufferings." This does not mean Jesus was ever morally imperfect. "Perfect" in Hebrews means complete, fully qualified for His role. Through suffering, Jesus became fully equipped to be the Savior and high priest of His people.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses explain why the Son of God suffered. Suffering was not a defeat or a contradiction of His glory; it was the path to glory, both for Him and for His people. Jesus said that the Messiah had to suffer these things and then enter His glory (Luke 24:26). Philippians 2:8–9 says that because Jesus humbled Himself to death on a cross, God highly exalted Him.

The idea of Jesus as "author" or "pioneer" is rich. In the ancient world, the word could describe a founder of a city, a leader of an expedition, or a hero who blazes a trail. Jesus goes ahead of His people through suffering and death into glory, and He brings them with Him. Hebrews 12:2 calls Him [[Hebrews 12:2|the author and perfecter of faith]].

"For everyone" emphasizes the breadth of Christ's death. His grace reaches all people, and His invitation is open to all.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

When believers look at the world and see suffering, injustice, and brokenness, they may wonder whether God's purposes are failing. These verses say: "But we see Jesus." In Him, God's purpose is already being fulfilled, and He will bring it to completion.

These verses also give meaning to believers' own suffering. Jesus walked the path of suffering to glory, and He leads His people along it. Their suffering is not meaningless; it is part of the journey He has already traveled, and it leads to glory.

Finally, believers can rest in the truth that Jesus tasted death for them. Because He has gone through death, they need not face it alone.

### Hebrews 2:14–15 — Freed from the Fear of Death

verse: Hebrews 2:14-15

#### Simple Explanation

"Since then the children have shared in flesh and blood, he also himself in the same way partook of the same." God's children are human beings with physical bodies, subject to weakness and death. So Jesus became fully human too. He did not merely appear human; He truly shared human nature.

"That through death he might bring to nothing him who had the power of death, that is, the devil." The purpose of Jesus' becoming human was to die, and through His death to defeat the devil. "Bring to nothing" means to make powerless, to break the grip of. The devil "had the power of death" in the sense that he uses death, and the fear of death, to hold humanity captive. Sin leads to death, and the devil accuses sinners. Jesus' death dealt with sin, and so broke the devil's hold.

Verse 15: "and might deliver all of them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage." The fear of death enslaves people. It shapes their choices, fuels their anxieties, and keeps them from living freely. Jesus came to set them free.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that the incarnation and the cross belong together. The Son became human in order to die, and He died in order to destroy the power of death. This is the great paradox of the gospel: death was defeated by death. Jesus went into death and came out the other side, breaking its power for all who belong to Him.

The fear of death is one of the deepest human fears. People spend enormous energy avoiding it, denying it, or trying to escape it. Many philosophies and religions have tried to address it. The gospel offers something unique: not merely a way to cope with death, but a victory over it. Paul wrote, [[1 Corinthians 15:54|Death is swallowed up in victory]] and [[1 Corinthians 15:55|Death, where is your sting?]].

This does not mean believers will never feel fear or sorrow in the face of death. Jesus Himself wept at Lazarus's tomb and was deeply troubled in Gethsemane. But death no longer has the final word. For believers, it is a defeated enemy and a doorway into the presence of Christ.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers who struggle with fear of death, their own or that of loved ones, can find comfort here. Jesus has entered death and defeated it. Those who belong to Him do not need to be enslaved by fear.

Freedom from the fear of death also brings freedom to live. Those who are not ruled by fear can take risks for the gospel, serve others sacrificially, and face suffering with courage.

Finally, these verses remind believers that Jesus truly shared their humanity. He knows what it is to have a body, to feel pain, and to face death. He understands.

### Hebrews 2:17–18 — A Merciful and Faithful High Priest

verse: Hebrews 2:17-18

#### Simple Explanation

"Therefore he was obligated in all things to be made like his brothers." To accomplish His mission, Jesus had to become like His people in every way, sharing their human nature, experiences, and struggles, though without sin (Hebrews 4:15).

"That he might become a merciful and faithful high priest in things pertaining to God." This is the first time Hebrews calls Jesus a high priest, a theme it will develop at length. In Israel, the high priest represented the people before God. Jesus became human so that He could represent humanity perfectly.

He is "merciful": compassionate toward human weakness, understanding and gentle. He is "faithful": completely trustworthy and reliable in His service to God.

"To make atonement for the sins of the people." The high priest's central task was to offer sacrifices for sin, especially on the Day of Atonement. Jesus made atonement by offering Himself (Hebrews 9:11–14).

Verse 18: "For in that he himself has suffered being tempted, he is able to help those who are tempted." Jesus experienced real temptation, and it involved real suffering. Because He knows what temptation feels like, He is able to help those who face it.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal the heart of Christ toward His people. He is not a distant, unfeeling priest. He is merciful. He knows human weakness from the inside. He experienced hunger, exhaustion, grief, rejection, betrayal, pain, and temptation. And so He understands.

The word "atonement" refers to dealing with sin so that the relationship between God and humanity can be restored. Some translations use "propitiation," which emphasizes turning away God's righteous wrath against sin; others use "expiation," which emphasizes removing sin itself. Both ideas are part of what Christ accomplished. Through His sacrifice, sin is dealt with and reconciliation with God is made possible.

Jesus' temptation was real. He was tempted in the wilderness (Matthew 4:1–11), in Gethsemane (Mark 14:32–42), and throughout His life. The fact that He never sinned does not mean the temptations were easy. In fact, He felt the full force of temptation in a way that those who give in never do. That is why He can help.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

When believers face temptation, they can turn to Jesus with confidence. He understands exactly what they are facing, and He is able and willing to help. They do not need to hide from Him in shame.

When believers feel weak or struggle with failure, they can remember that their high priest is merciful. He does not look down on them with contempt but with compassion.

This passage also invites believers to bring every part of their human experience to Jesus: their pain, their struggles, their fears. Because He has shared their humanity, nothing they bring to Him is foreign to Him.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Hebrews 2 begins with a loving but serious warning: pay close attention to the gospel so that you do not drift away. Spiritual drift is usually gradual and often unnoticed. Believers can guard against it by staying anchored to Christ through Scripture, prayer, worship, and fellowship. The salvation they have received is "so great" that it deserves their full attention.

The chapter then reveals the wonder of Christ's humanity. God created humanity to rule over creation, but we do not yet see that purpose fulfilled. "But we see Jesus," who became lower than the angels for a time, tasted death for everyone, and is now crowned with glory and honor. Through His suffering, He became the pioneer of salvation, leading many sons and daughters to glory. He is not ashamed to call them His brothers and sisters.

Jesus shared fully in human flesh and blood so that, through His death, He could break the power of the devil and free those enslaved by the fear of death. Believers no longer need to live in bondage to that fear. They can face death, and life, with courage and hope.

Finally, the chapter introduces Jesus as a merciful and faithful high priest, who made atonement for sins and who, having suffered when tempted, is able to help those who are tempted. Whatever believers face, they have a Savior who understands, who sympathizes, and who stands ready to help.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, keep me from drifting away from You. Help me to pay close attention to the great salvation You have brought, and anchor my heart in Your word. Thank You for becoming lower than the angels for a time, for tasting death for everyone, and for being crowned with glory and honor. Thank You that You are not ashamed to call me Your brother or sister. Thank You for sharing my flesh and blood, and for breaking the power of the devil and setting me free from the fear of death. When I am tempted, help me to turn to You, knowing that You understand and are able to help. Thank You that You are a merciful and faithful high priest who has made atonement for my sins. Lead me, with all Your people, into glory. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
