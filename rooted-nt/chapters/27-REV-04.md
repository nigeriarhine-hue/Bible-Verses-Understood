# Revelation 4
theme: The Throne Room of Heaven
main: After the messages to the churches, John sees a door standing open in heaven and hears the voice like a trumpet inviting him to come up and see what must take place. In the Spirit, he sees a throne in heaven with One seated on it, shining like jasper and carnelian, surrounded by a rainbow like an emerald. Around the throne are twenty-four other thrones with twenty-four elders dressed in white and wearing golden crowns. From the throne come flashes of lightning and peals of thunder, and before it burn seven lamps of fire, the seven Spirits of God. In front of the throne is something like a sea of glass, clear as crystal. Around the throne are four living creatures, full of eyes, resembling a lion, an ox, a man, and a flying eagle. Each has six wings, and day and night they never stop saying, Holy, holy, holy is the Lord God Almighty, who was and is and is to come. Whenever they give glory, honor, and thanks to the One on the throne, the twenty-four elders fall down and worship Him, casting their crowns before the throne and declaring that He is worthy to receive glory, honor, and power, because He created all things and by His will they exist.

## Chapter Background and Story

With chapter 4, Revelation moves from earth to heaven. In chapters 2 and 3, John saw the churches struggling with persecution, compromise, and complacency. Now he is shown the reality behind and above all earthly events: God reigning on His throne. Before John sees any of the judgments that follow, he sees the One who rules over all.

This vision would have been deeply encouraging to the first readers. The Roman emperor sat on a throne, surrounded by attendants and receiving honor from his subjects. Some emperors were even hailed as "lord and god." Revelation 4 shows that the true throne is in heaven, and the true Lord and God is the One who created all things.

The scene draws on earlier visions of God's throne in the Old Testament, especially Isaiah 6, Ezekiel 1, and Daniel 7. It is a picture of worship at the center of all reality.

### The Open Door and the Invitation (4:1–2a)

[[v1|After these things I looked and saw a door opened in heaven; and the first voice that I heard, like a trumpet speaking with me, was one saying, “Come up here, and I will show you the things which must happen after this.”]] The voice is the same trumpet-like voice John heard in 1:10, the voice of the risen Christ. The open door invites John into heaven's perspective.

Some futurist interpreters, especially those who hold a pretribulation view, see John's being called up to heaven as a picture of the church being taken up before the tribulation, noting that the word "church" (or "assembly") does not appear again in chapters 4–19 until the end of the book. Most other interpreters see this as John's personal visionary experience, similar to the prophets who were shown heavenly scenes, and point out that God's people (called "saints" and "servants") remain present on earth throughout the following chapters. What is clear is that John is given a heavenly vantage point to understand what happens on earth.

[[v2|Immediately I was in the Spirit. Behold, there was a throne set in heaven, and one sitting on the throne]] The vision of the throne and the One seated on it is studied below.

### The Elders and the Throne (4:4–6a)

[[v4|Around the throne were twenty-four thrones. On the thrones were twenty-four elders sitting, dressed in white garments, with crowns of gold on their heads]]. The twenty-four elders have been understood in several ways. Many interpreters see them as representing the whole people of God, perhaps the twelve tribes of Israel and the twelve apostles together (compare 21:12–14). Others see them as a high order of angels who serve in God's heavenly court. Their white garments suggest purity, their thrones suggest a share in God's rule, and their golden crowns suggest victory. Whatever their exact identity, they lead heaven in worship.

[[v5|Out of the throne proceed lightnings, sounds, and thunders. There were seven lamps of fire burning before his throne, which are the seven Spirits of God]]. Lightning and thunder recall God's appearance at Mount Sinai (Exodus 19:16), showing His awesome power and holiness. The seven lamps represent the Holy Spirit in His fullness (compare 1:4; Zechariah 4:2–6).

[[v6|Before the throne was something like a sea of glass, similar to crystal. In the middle of the throne, and around the throne were four living creatures full of eyes before and behind]]. The sea of glass may recall the "expanse" like crystal above the living creatures in Ezekiel 1:22, or the great bronze basin called "the sea" in Solomon's temple (1 Kings 7:23). In the ancient world, the sea often symbolized chaos and danger. Here it is calm and still as glass before God's throne, suggesting that nothing threatens His rule.

### The Four Living Creatures (4:6b–8)

[[v7|The first creature was like a lion, the second creature like a calf, the third creature had a face like a man, and the fourth was like a flying eagle]]. These creatures resemble the cherubim in Ezekiel 1 and the seraphim in Isaiah 6. Their four forms have been understood to represent the noblest of created beings: the lion among wild animals, the ox among domestic animals, humanity as the crown of creation, and the eagle among birds. Together they may represent all creation worshiping God. Being "full of eyes" suggests constant watchfulness and knowledge. In later Christian tradition, these four figures came to be used as symbols of the four Gospel writers, though that is a later application rather than John's original meaning. Their unceasing song is studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### Revelation 4:2–3 — A Throne in Heaven

verse: Revelation 4:2-3

#### Simple Explanation

When John enters heaven in the Spirit, the first thing he sees is a throne: "Behold, there was a throne set in heaven, and one sitting on the throne." The throne is the center of the whole vision. Everything else in the chapter is arranged around it.

John does not describe the appearance of the One on the throne in human terms. Instead, he uses the language of precious stones: He "looked like a jasper stone and a sardius." Jasper was a brilliant, often clear stone, and sardius (carnelian) was a deep red stone. John can only describe God's glory by comparison with the most dazzling things he knows.

Then he adds: "There was a rainbow around the throne, like an emerald to look at." A rainbow encircles the throne, glowing with a green light.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The throne is one of the central images of Revelation. The word appears more than forty times in the book. The repeated message is that God reigns. No matter what chaos unfolds on earth, no matter how powerful earthly rulers appear, God is seated on His throne, and His rule is unshaken.

John's reluctance to describe God's form reflects the reverence of Scripture. Ezekiel, seeing a similar vision, spoke only of [[Ezekiel 1:28|the appearance of the likeness of the LORD’s glory]]. God is beyond human description. Precious stones and light are the closest comparisons.

The rainbow is especially meaningful. After the flood, God placed a rainbow in the sky as a sign of His covenant promise never again to destroy the earth by flood (Genesis 9:12–17). A rainbow also surrounded the throne in Ezekiel's vision (Ezekiel 1:28). Around the throne from which judgments will come, there is a reminder of God's mercy and faithfulness. His judgments will be just, but His covenant love remains.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

When life feels chaotic or frightening, believers can remember that there is a throne in heaven, and it is not empty. God reigns over every nation, every circumstance, and every moment of history. This truth brings stability and peace.

This vision also invites believers to worship. Before asking God for help or seeking answers about the future, believers can pause to behold who He is: glorious, holy, and faithful.

The rainbow reminds believers that God's power is always joined to His promises. They can trust that the One who rules over all is also the God who keeps His covenant with His people.

### Revelation 4:8 — Holy, Holy, Holy

verse: Revelation 4:8

#### Simple Explanation

The four living creatures each have "six wings" and are "full of eyes around and within." These details recall the seraphim in Isaiah's vision (Isaiah 6:2) and the cherubim in Ezekiel's vision (Ezekiel 1:18; 10:12).

Their worship never stops: "They have no rest day and night." Their song is constant: "Holy, holy, holy is the Lord God, the Almighty, who was and who is and who is to come!"

They declare three things about God. He is holy, utterly pure and set apart from all that is sinful and created. He is "the Lord God, the Almighty," all-powerful and sovereign over all. And He is eternal, the One "who was and who is and who is to come."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The threefold "holy" echoes the song of the seraphim in [[Isaiah 6:3|Holy, holy, holy, is the LORD of Armies! The whole earth is full of his glory!]] In Hebrew, repetition is a way of emphasizing something. Saying "holy" three times expresses the highest degree of holiness. Holiness is at the very heart of who God is. Many Christians have also seen in the threefold "holy" a hint of the Trinity, though the primary meaning is the supreme holiness of God.

The phrase "who was and who is and who is to come" emphasizes God's eternal nature. He existed before all things, He is present now, and He will come to complete His purposes. The order here, beginning with "who was," highlights God's faithfulness through all of history.

The fact that these creatures worship "day and night" without rest shows that worship is not an occasional activity in heaven but the constant atmosphere. Worship is the proper response of all creation to its Creator, and it will be the joy of God's people forever.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites believers to grow in their awareness of God's holiness. In a culture that often treats God casually or ignores Him altogether, believers can recover a sense of awe and reverence.

Recognizing God's holiness also leads to humility. Like Isaiah, who cried out about his own uncleanness when he saw God's holiness (Isaiah 6:5), believers become aware of their need for God's grace. Yet through Christ, they are invited to approach the holy God with confidence.

Believers can join heaven's worship in their own lives, in personal devotion, in gathered worship, and in daily living. Every act of praise on earth echoes the unending song of heaven.

### Revelation 4:10–11 — Worthy Are You, Our Lord and God

verse: Revelation 4:10-11

#### Simple Explanation

When the living creatures give glory to God, the twenty-four elders respond. They "fall down before him who sits on the throne and worship him who lives forever and ever." Falling down is a posture of complete humility and reverence.

Then they "throw their crowns before the throne." The crowns they wear, symbols of honor and victory, are laid down before God. They acknowledge that every honor they have comes from Him and belongs to Him.

Their song, in verse 11, declares: "Worthy are you, our Lord and God, the Holy One, to receive the glory, the honor, and the power." The reason for God's worthiness is given: "for you created all things, and because of your desire they existed and were created!"

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The title "our Lord and God" is striking. The Roman emperor Domitian is reported to have demanded to be addressed as "our lord and god." The heavenly worshipers give this title to the true God alone. This would have encouraged believers who were pressured to honor the emperor as divine. There is only one who truly deserves such worship.

The basis of God's worthiness in this chapter is creation. He made all things, and everything exists because of His will. Creation is not an accident; it is the expression of God's purpose and desire. Because He is the Creator, He is the rightful owner and ruler of all things. In chapter 5, the song will add a second reason for worship: redemption through the Lamb.

The casting of crowns teaches something about the nature of worship. Even the highest honors given to God's servants are to be returned to Him. True worship recognizes that all good things come from God and exist for His glory.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can follow the example of the elders by giving God the honor He deserves. Every achievement, gift, and success can be laid before Him with gratitude, recognizing that He is the source of all good things.

This passage also gives believers a foundation for their sense of worth and purpose. They exist because God willed it. Their lives are not accidents. They were created by a loving Creator, for His glory.

Recognizing God as Creator can also shape how believers treat the world around them. Creation belongs to God and reflects His glory. Believers can care for it, enjoy it with thanksgiving, and see in it reasons to worship.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Revelation 4 lifts believers' eyes from the struggles of earth to the throne of heaven. Before any of the book's judgments unfold, John sees that God reigns. This is the foundation for everything that follows. Whatever happens in history, whatever trials believers face, God is seated on His throne, and His rule cannot be shaken.

The chapter also shows that worship is the center of heaven. The living creatures cry "holy" day and night, and the elders cast their crowns before the throne. Believers are invited to join this worship now, with reverence for God's holiness, gratitude for His creation, and humble surrender of every honor to Him.

The vision encourages believers to trust God's character. His throne is surrounded by a rainbow, the sign of His covenant faithfulness. His holiness is perfect. His power is unlimited. He is eternal, the One who was and is and is to come. Believers can rest in His care and live with confidence, knowing that the One who created all things holds all things in His hands.

Finally, the chapter challenges every rival claim to ultimate authority. Earthly rulers, systems, and powers may demand loyalty and honor, but only God is worthy to be called "our Lord and God." Believers are called to give Him first place in their hearts and lives.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord God Almighty, You are seated on the throne of heaven, and Your rule cannot be shaken. When my world feels uncertain, help me to lift my eyes to You and remember that You reign. You are holy, holy, holy, the One who was and is and is to come. Fill me with awe at Your holiness and gratitude for Your faithfulness. Thank You that You created all things, and that I exist because of Your will. I lay before You every honor, every gift, and every success, for they all belong to You. Teach me to worship You with my whole life, joining the song of heaven. You alone are worthy, my Lord and my God, to receive glory, honor, and power. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
