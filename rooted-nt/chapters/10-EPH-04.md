# Ephesians 4
theme: Walking Worthy in Unity and New Life
main: Paul urges believers to live worthily of their calling, with humility, gentleness, patience, and love, eagerly keeping the unity the Spirit creates, because there is one body, one Spirit, one hope, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, and one God and Father of all. The risen Christ has given gifts to His people, including apostles, prophets, evangelists, shepherds, and teachers, to equip every believer for service so that the whole body grows to maturity in Christ, speaking the truth in love. Paul then calls believers to put off the old self, with its darkened and deceitful ways, and put on the new self created to be like God, replacing lies with truth, theft with generosity, harmful words with words that build up, and bitterness with kindness and forgiveness, just as God in Christ forgave them.

## Chapter Background and Story

Ephesians 4 marks the turning point of the letter. In chapters 1–3, Paul celebrated what God has done: chosen, redeemed, raised, and united His people in Christ. Now, beginning with the word "therefore," he explains how believers should live in light of all that. Christian ethics flows from Christian identity. Paul does not say, "Live this way so that God will accept you," but "God has made you His people; now live like it."

The chapter has two main sections. First, Paul focuses on the unity and growth of the church (4:1–16). The church is one body made up of many members, each with gifts from Christ, growing together toward maturity. Second, he describes the personal transformation that marks the new life in Christ (4:17–32). Believers are to put off the old way of life they once lived and put on the new self, created to be like God.

The original readers lived in a city full of idolatry, magic, sexual immorality, and economic exploitation. Many had come out of that world. Paul's instructions helped them see what it meant to live differently, not as isolated individuals, but as members of a new community.

### Walk Worthily in Unity (4:1–6)

[[v1|I therefore, the prisoner in the Lord, beg you to walk worthily of the calling with which you were called]]. "Walk" again means to live day by day. To walk "worthily" means to live in a way that fits or matches one's calling, like a person whose actions suit their position. The qualities that mark this walk, and the call to keep unity, are studied below.

[[v4|There is one body and one Spirit, even as you also were called in one hope of your calling]], [[v5|one Lord, one faith, one baptism]], [[v6|one God and Father of all, who is over all and through all and in us all]]. Paul lists seven "ones" that form the foundation of Christian unity. The list includes the Spirit, the Lord Jesus, and God the Father, the three persons of the Trinity. Believers are united not by their preferences or opinions but by these shared realities. The church does not create unity; it receives it from God and is called to guard it.

### Gifts for Building Up the Body (4:7–16)

[[v7|But to each one of us, the grace was given according to the measure of the gift of Christ]]. Unity does not mean sameness. Each believer receives a particular gift of grace from Christ.

[[v8|Therefore he says, “When he ascended on high, he led captivity captive, and gave gifts to people.”]] Paul adapts Psalm 68:18, which pictures God ascending Mount Zion in triumph after victory, receiving tribute from captives. Paul applies it to Christ's ascension. Having triumphed over sin, death, and evil powers, the risen Christ now distributes gifts to His people.

[[v9|Now this, “He ascended”, what is it but that he also first descended into the lower parts of the earth?]] Interpreters understand "the lower parts of the earth" in different ways. Some see it as Christ's descent to earth in the incarnation, the earth itself being "lower" than heaven. Others see it as His burial or His descent to the realm of the dead (see 1 Peter 3:18–19). Others connect it with the coming of the Spirit at Pentecost. The main point is that the one who humbled Himself is now exalted. [[v10|He who descended is the one who also ascended far above all the heavens, that he might fill all things]].

The gifts Christ gave to the church are studied below. [[v13|until we all attain to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a full grown man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ]]. The goal is maturity. The church is to grow up together until it reflects Christ fully.

[[v14|that we may no longer be children, tossed back and forth and carried about with every wind of doctrine, by the trickery of men, in craftiness, after the wiles of error]]. Immature believers are like small boats on a stormy sea, pushed around by every new teaching. Maturity brings stability.

[[v15|but speaking truth in love, we may grow up in all things into him who is the head, Christ]]. Truth and love belong together. Truth without love can be harsh and wounding; love without truth can be weak and deceptive. The church grows when its members speak honestly and lovingly to one another.

[[v16|from whom all the body, being fitted and knit together through that which every joint supplies, according to the working in measure of each individual part, makes the body increase to the building up of itself in love]]. Paul describes the church as a living body. Each part contributes, and each joint provides support. Growth happens when every member does its part, and the result is a body built up in love.

### The Old Life and the New (4:17–24)

[[v17|This I say therefore, and testify in the Lord, that you no longer walk as the rest of the Gentiles also walk, in the futility of their mind]]. Paul uses "Gentiles" here to describe those who do not know God. His readers were Gentiles by birth, but they were no longer to live as their pagan neighbors did.

[[v18|being darkened in their understanding, alienated from the life of God because of the ignorance that is in them, because of the hardening of their hearts]]. Paul traces a sad progression. Hardened hearts lead to ignorance, which leads to darkened understanding and separation from God's life. [[v19|They, having become callous, gave themselves up to lust, to work all uncleanness with greediness]]. When people lose their sensitivity to God, they abandon themselves to self-indulgence, always wanting more.

[[v20|But you didn’t learn Christ that way]], [[v21|if indeed you heard him and were taught in him, even as truth is in Jesus]]. The phrase "learn Christ" is unusual. Believers do not merely learn about Christ; they learn Christ Himself, His character and His way. The transformation that follows is studied below.

### Practical Holiness (4:25–32)

Paul now gives specific examples of putting off the old and putting on the new. Each has a negative command, a positive replacement, and a reason.

[[v25|Therefore, putting away falsehood, speak truth each one with his neighbor, for we are members of one another]]. Lying harms the body, because believers are connected like parts of one body. Deceiving another believer is like one hand deceiving the other.

[[v26|“Be angry, and don’t sin.” Don’t let the sun go down on your wrath]], [[v27|and don’t give place to the devil]]. Paul quotes Psalm 4:4. Anger is not always sinful. God Himself is angry at injustice, and Jesus was angry at the corruption of the temple (Mark 11:15–17) and at hardness of heart (Mark 3:5). Anger at wrong, including anger at abuse and injustice, can be right. But anger can easily become sinful when it turns into bitterness, revenge, or cruelty. "Don't let the sun go down on your wrath" encourages dealing with anger promptly rather than nursing it. Unresolved anger gives the devil a foothold. This does not mean every conflict must be fully resolved by sunset, or that those who have been seriously harmed must pretend all is well; it means not allowing anger to settle in and harden into hatred.

[[v28|Let him who stole steal no more; but rather let him labor, producing with his hands something that is good, that he may have something to give to him who has need]]. Notice the transformation. The thief does not just stop stealing; he becomes a worker and then a giver. The new life moves from taking, to earning, to sharing.

[[v29|Let no corrupt speech proceed out of your mouth, but only what is good for building others up as the need may be, that it may give grace to those who hear]]. "Corrupt" means rotten, like spoiled fruit. Words can either decay and spread harm, or they can build up and give grace. Believers are called to choose words that fit the need of the moment and bless the hearer.

[[v30|Don’t grieve the Holy Spirit of God, in whom you were sealed for the day of redemption]]. The Holy Spirit is a person who can be grieved. When believers harm one another through lies, anger, theft, or corrupt speech, they grieve the Spirit who lives in them and unites them. The call to replace bitterness with kindness and forgiveness is studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### Ephesians 4:2–3 — Keeping the Unity of the Spirit

verse: Ephesians 4:2-3

#### Simple Explanation

Paul describes what it looks like to walk worthily of one's calling. He lists four qualities.

"Lowliness and humility." In the Greek and Roman world, humility was often seen as weakness, the attitude of a slave. Christianity transformed it into a virtue, modeled by Christ who humbled Himself (Philippians 2:5–8). Humility means not thinking too highly of oneself and putting others first.

"With patience." Patience means being slow to anger and willing to endure others' faults and slowness without giving up on them.

"Bearing with one another in love." This means putting up with one another's weaknesses, quirks, and irritations, not grudgingly, but lovingly. Every community includes people who are difficult at times, and everyone is difficult to someone.

Then Paul gives the goal: "being eager to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace." The word "eager" means making every effort, being diligent. Unity is not automatic; it must be guarded actively. Notice that it is "the unity of the Spirit." The Spirit has already created unity among believers. Their task is to keep it, protect it, and not destroy it. Peace is the "bond," like a cord or ligament, that holds the body together.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that Christian unity is both a gift and a task. It is a gift because the Spirit has made all believers one in Christ (Ephesians 2:14–18). It is a task because sin, pride, and selfishness constantly threaten to break it apart. Jesus prayed for the unity of His followers, [[John 17:21|that they may all be one]], so that the world would believe. Unity among believers is a powerful witness to the gospel.

The qualities Paul lists are the very qualities of Christ. He was gentle and lowly in heart (Matthew 11:29), patient with His disciples' slowness, and bore with their failures in love. When believers display these qualities, they reflect Christ to one another and to the world.

Unity does not mean uniformity, as Paul will show in the following verses about diverse gifts. Nor does it mean ignoring serious error or harm. It means a shared commitment to Christ and to one another, expressed in humility, patience, and love, so that differences do not become divisions.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Churches, families, and friendships are often damaged not by major doctrinal disputes but by pride, impatience, and small irritations allowed to grow. These verses call believers to practice humility, patience, and forbearance in everyday relationships. That might mean letting minor offenses go, listening before reacting, or assuming the best about others.

Keeping the unity of the Spirit also requires effort. When tension arises, believers can take initiative to seek peace, through honest conversation, apology, or forgiveness. Bearing with one another in love does not mean tolerating abuse or ignoring harm, but it does mean extending the same grace believers have received.

### Ephesians 4:11–12 — Equipping the Saints for Ministry

verse: Ephesians 4:11-12

#### Simple Explanation

Paul lists the gifts the ascended Christ gave to the church. Notice that the gifts are people. Christ gave the church "apostles," "prophets," "evangelists," and "shepherds and teachers."

Apostles were those sent by Christ to establish the church and lay its foundation (Ephesians 2:20). Prophets spoke God's revelation and encouragement to the church. Evangelists proclaimed the gospel and helped spread the faith to new people and places (Philip is called an evangelist in Acts 21:8). "Shepherds and teachers" may describe one role, pastors who teach, or two closely related roles. "Shepherd" is the word often translated "pastor." These leaders care for, guide, protect, and teach God's people.

Verse 12 explains the purpose of these gifts. They were given "for the perfecting of the saints, to the work of serving, to the building up of the body of Christ." The word "perfecting" means equipping, preparing, or restoring, like mending nets or setting a bone. Leaders are to equip "the saints," all believers, for "the work of serving," or ministry. The result is that the body of Christ is built up.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage overturns the idea that ministry is only for professional clergy. Leaders are given to equip all believers to serve. Every member of the church has a part in the work of ministry. Pastors and teachers succeed not when they do all the work themselves but when they help others grow and serve.

Christians differ on how these roles continue today. Many believe that apostles and prophets in the foundational sense belonged to the first generation of the church, while their functions continue in other forms, such as missionaries and church planters, and those who encourage and exhort. Others believe the gifts of apostle and prophet continue in the church today in a similar way to the New Testament, though not adding new Scripture. Evangelists, pastors, and teachers are recognized across nearly all traditions. Whatever the view, Paul's main point is clear: Christ provides leaders so that the whole church can be equipped and built up.

The fact that these gifts come from the ascended Christ is also important. Church leadership is not merely a human institution. It is Christ's provision for His people, and leaders are accountable to Him for how they serve.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Every believer has a role in the work of ministry. Serving is not just for pastors or leaders. Believers can ask how God has gifted them, whether in teaching, encouraging, serving practically, giving, showing hospitality, or caring for others, and how they can use those gifts to build up the body.

For church leaders, this passage provides a clear job description: equip others. Healthy leadership empowers people, trains them, and releases them to serve, rather than controlling or doing everything alone.

This passage also encourages believers to value and support faithful leaders who help them grow, and to receive their teaching and care with gratitude.

### Ephesians 4:22–24 — Put Off the Old, Put On the New

verse: Ephesians 4:22-24

#### Simple Explanation

Paul uses the image of changing clothes. Believers are to "put away" the old self and "put on" the new self.

"The old man" refers to the old self, the person a believer was before coming to Christ, shaped by sin and self-centeredness. Paul describes it as "growing corrupt after the lusts of deceit." The old self is decaying, and its desires are deceptive. They promise satisfaction but lead to ruin.

"Be renewed in the spirit of your mind." Between putting off and putting on, there is the renewal of the mind. Transformation is not merely external behavior change. It involves a new way of thinking, renewed by God's Spirit and truth (see Romans 12:2).

"Put on the new man, who in the likeness of God has been created in righteousness and holiness of truth." The new self is a new creation, made to be like God. It is marked by righteousness, doing what is right, and holiness, being set apart for God. And it is rooted in truth, in contrast to the deceit of the old self.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

In Colossians 3:9–10, Paul uses similar language, saying that believers have already put off the old self and put on the new. In Ephesians 4, he speaks of it as an ongoing practice. Both are true. At conversion, believers decisively became new people in Christ. Each day, they live out that reality by continuing to set aside old habits and embrace new ones.

The new self is "created in the likeness of God." This recalls Genesis 1:26–27, where humanity was created in God's image. Sin marred that image, but in Christ it is being restored. The goal of the Christian life is to become more fully what God created humans to be.

Renewal of the mind is the key. Old habits are rooted in old ways of thinking. As believers fill their minds with God's truth, through Scripture, prayer, and Christian community, their thinking changes, and their behavior follows.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Change can feel slow and difficult. Old habits, attitudes, and reactions do not disappear overnight. This passage encourages believers to see transformation as a daily practice of putting off what belongs to the old life and putting on what belongs to the new.

Practical steps may include recognizing the lies behind certain behaviors and replacing them with God's truth, avoiding situations that feed old desires, and intentionally practicing new habits of kindness, honesty, and generosity. Help from trusted believers, and in some situations from counselors or support groups, can also be part of this process.

Most of all, believers can remember that the new self is already theirs in Christ. They are not trying to become someone they are not; they are learning to live as who they truly are.

### Ephesians 4:31–32 — Kind, Tenderhearted, Forgiving

verse: Ephesians 4:31-32

#### Simple Explanation

Paul concludes this chapter with a list of things to put away and things to put on.

Verse 31 lists attitudes and behaviors that destroy relationships. "Bitterness" is a settled resentment that refuses to let go of hurts. "Wrath" is a sudden outburst of rage. "Anger" here is a lasting, smoldering hostility. "Outcry" refers to shouting and quarreling. "Slander" is speaking evil of others. "Malice" is a desire to harm. Paul says all of these must be "put away."

Verse 32 describes what should replace them. "Be kind to one another." Kindness is generous goodness toward others. "Tender hearted" means compassionate, deeply moved by others' needs. "Forgiving each other" means releasing others from the debt of their wrongs.

The reason is given at the end: "just as God also in Christ forgave you." Believers forgive because they have been forgiven. God's forgiveness of them, through Christ's death, is both the motivation and the model for their forgiveness of others.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Jesus taught that those who have been forgiven much should forgive others. In the parable of the unforgiving servant, a man forgiven a huge debt refused to forgive a small one, and Jesus said that such a heart contradicts the mercy it has received (Matthew 18:21–35). When believers remember how much God has forgiven them, it becomes possible to forgive others.

Forgiveness is often misunderstood. Biblical forgiveness means releasing the desire for revenge and entrusting justice to God (Romans 12:19). It does not mean pretending the wrong did not happen, excusing it, or immediately restoring trust. Forgiveness is not the same as reconciliation, which requires repentance and rebuilt trust. And forgiveness does not require a person to remain in danger. Someone who has suffered abuse can forgive while also seeking safety, setting boundaries, and pursuing justice through proper authorities. Forgiveness is often a process, not a single moment, especially for deep wounds.

The words "kind" and "tender hearted" reflect the character of God, who is kind even to the ungrateful and evil (Luke 6:35) and full of compassion. As believers forgive and show kindness, they reflect their Father's character.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Bitterness can poison a person's heart and relationships for years. This passage invites believers to bring their hurts to God and begin the process of letting go of resentment. That process may involve prayer, honest conversation, time, and sometimes the help of a pastor or counselor.

Kindness and compassion can be practiced in small, daily ways: a gentle word, a patient response, a thoughtful act of help. These small choices shape relationships and reflect God's character.

When forgiveness feels impossible, believers can remember the cross. God forgave them at great cost. That truth does not make forgiveness easy, but it makes it possible, and it reminds believers that they never forgive alone. The Spirit helps them do what they cannot do on their own.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Ephesians 4 shows how believers live out their high calling in Christ. The first emphasis is unity. Believers are one body, with one Spirit, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, and one God and Father. The Spirit has already made them one; their task is to guard that unity through humility, gentleness, patience, and love. In a world full of division, a church that bears with one another in love bears powerful witness to Christ.

The second emphasis is growth. The risen Christ has given leaders to equip all believers for ministry, so that the whole body grows toward maturity. Every believer has a part to play. As the church speaks the truth in love and each member contributes, the body is built up and becomes stable, no longer tossed around by every new idea.

The third emphasis is transformation. Believers are called to put off the old self, with its deceitful desires and darkened thinking, and to put on the new self, created to be like God in righteousness and holiness. This involves the renewal of the mind and practical changes in daily life: telling the truth instead of lying, dealing with anger before it becomes sin, working and giving instead of stealing, speaking words that build up rather than tear down.

Finally, the chapter calls believers to replace bitterness, rage, and malice with kindness, compassion, and forgiveness, forgiving others as God in Christ has forgiven them. This forgiveness does not excuse wrongdoing or require anyone to stay in harm's way, but it releases the heart from the poison of resentment. As believers live this way, they honor the Spirit who lives in them and display the beauty of the new life in Christ.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, You have called me to a life worthy of Your name. Make me humble, gentle, and patient, bearing with others in love. Help me to guard the unity Your Spirit has created among Your people. Thank You for the gifts You have given Your church; show me how You have equipped me to serve, and help me to build up others. Teach me to speak the truth in love. Renew my mind by Your truth. Help me to put off my old self and put on the new self, created to be like God in righteousness and holiness. Guard my words, so that they build up and give grace. Holy Spirit, I do not want to grieve You. Remove all bitterness, rage, and malice from my heart. Make me kind and tenderhearted, and give me grace to forgive others as God in Christ has forgiven me. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
