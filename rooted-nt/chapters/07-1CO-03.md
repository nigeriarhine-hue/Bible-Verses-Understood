# 1 Corinthians 3
theme: God's Field, God's Building, God's Temple
main: Paul tells the Corinthians that their jealousy and quarreling show they are still spiritually immature, like infants who need milk. Leaders such as Paul and Apollos are only servants; one plants and another waters, but God gives the growth. The church is God's field and God's building, and its only foundation is Jesus Christ. Each worker must build carefully, because the quality of every person's work will be tested by fire on the day of judgment. The church is also God's holy temple, where His Spirit dwells, and anyone who destroys it will face God's judgment. So no one should boast in human leaders, because all things belong to believers, and they belong to Christ.

## Chapter Background and Story

In chapters 1–2, Paul contrasted the wisdom of the world with the wisdom of God revealed in the cross and made known by the Spirit. He ended chapter 2 by saying that believers have "the mind of Christ." The Corinthians probably liked that idea; they considered themselves spiritually advanced. Now Paul brings them down to earth. Their divisions over leaders show that they are not as spiritual as they think.

Paul uses three images for the church in this chapter: a field, a building, and a temple. Each image shows that the church belongs to God, not to any human leader, and that leaders are simply God's servants.

### Spiritual Infants (3:1–4)

Paul's diagnosis of the Corinthians' condition is studied below.

### Servants Through Whom You Believed (3:5–9)

[[v5|Who then is Apollos, and who is Paul, but servants through whom you believed, and each as the Lord gave to him?]] Paul and Apollos were not rivals or heads of competing parties. They were "servants" (diakonoi), people who wait on others. Each did the task the Lord assigned. The image of planting and watering is studied below.

[[v8|Now he who plants and he who waters are the same, but each will receive his own reward according to his own labor]]. Paul and Apollos had a common purpose. They were not competitors but partners. Each would be rewarded by God for faithful work.

[[v9|For we are God’s fellow workers. You are God’s farming, God’s building]]. Paul and Apollos work together with God, or as God's co-workers. The church belongs to God: it is His field, which He cultivates, and His building, which He constructs.

### The Foundation and the Building (3:10–15)

Paul shifts from the farming image to a building image. [[v10|According to the grace of God which was given to me, as a wise master builder I laid a foundation, and another builds on it. But let each man be careful how he builds on it]]. Paul laid the foundation when he founded the church in Corinth. Others, like Apollos and later teachers, built on it. Each must be careful. The foundation and the testing of the work are studied below.

### God's Temple (3:16–17)

Paul then uses his third image, the church as God's temple, studied below.

### Don't Boast in Men (3:18–23)

[[v18|Let no one deceive himself. If anyone thinks that he is wise among you in this world, let him become a fool that he may become wise]]. Those who think they are wise by the world's standards need to humble themselves and accept God's "foolish" message of the cross. Only then will they find true wisdom.

[[v19|For the wisdom of this world is foolishness with God. For it is written, “He has taken the wise in their craftiness.”]] Paul quotes Job 5:13, and then Psalm 94:11: [[v20|The Lord knows the reasoning of the wise, that it is worthless]].

[[v21|Therefore let no one boast in men. For all things are yours]]. The Corinthians were saying, "I belong to Paul," or "I belong to Apollos." Paul turns this upside down. They do not belong to these leaders; the leaders belong to them. [[v22|whether Paul, or Apollos, or Cephas, or the world, or life, or death, or things present, or things to come. All are yours]]. Every leader, every gift, even the whole world, life and death, present and future, all serve the good of believers. Why limit themselves to one leader when all God's servants are given for their benefit?

[[v23|and you are Christ’s, and Christ is God’s]]. Believers belong to Christ, and Christ belongs to God. This is the true chain of belonging. Believers do not belong to human leaders; they belong to Christ.

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 Corinthians 3:1–3 — Still Infants in Christ

verse: 1 Corinthians 3:1-3

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had just told the Corinthians that spiritual people understand God's wisdom and have the mind of Christ. The Corinthians may have thought, "That's us! We are spiritual." But Paul quickly corrected them.

[[v1|Brothers, I couldn’t speak to you as to spiritual, but as to fleshly, as to babies in Christ]]. When Paul first came to Corinth, he had to speak to them as "fleshly," not yet mature, like babies. This was natural; they were new believers. Paul still calls them "brothers," showing that they were genuine believers. But they were spiritually young.

[[v2|I fed you with milk, not with solid food, for you weren’t yet ready]]. Paul used the image of feeding a child. Babies need milk; they are not ready for solid food. Paul had given them the basic truths of the gospel, the "milk," because they were not ready for deeper teaching.

[[v2|Indeed, you aren’t ready even now]]. The surprising part is that years later, they were still not ready. They should have grown, but they had remained infants.

[[v3|for you are still fleshly. For insofar as there is jealousy, strife, and factions among you, aren’t you fleshly, and don’t you walk in the ways of men?]] Paul gives the evidence of their immaturity: jealousy and quarreling. These behaviors are signs of living by the flesh, by sinful human nature, rather than by the Spirit. They were acting "in the ways of men," like people who do not know God.

The Corinthians thought they were advanced because of their spiritual gifts and knowledge. But Paul measures maturity differently. Spiritual maturity is shown not by gifts or knowledge, but by love, humility, and unity.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that spiritual maturity is measured by character, not by gifts or knowledge. The Corinthians had many gifts (1:7), yet they were immature because of their jealousy and quarreling. Paul later writes that without love, even the greatest gifts are worthless (13:1–3).

They also show that believers are meant to grow. Just as babies should grow into adults, believers should grow into maturity in Christ. Hebrews makes a similar point: [[Hebrews 5:12|For although by this time you should be teachers, you again need to have someone teach you the rudiments of the first principles of the revelations of God]]. Growth is the normal path of the Christian life.

Finally, these verses show that division is a sign of immaturity. When believers fight over leaders, preferences, or status, they show that they are still living by worldly values. Mature believers seek unity and peace.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses invite believers to evaluate their own maturity. Believers can ask themselves whether their lives show jealousy, quarreling, and division, or love, humility, and unity. These qualities reveal true spiritual maturity more than knowledge or gifts.

They also encourage believers to keep growing. No believer should remain an infant. Believers can grow by studying Scripture, praying, obeying God, and learning from mature believers.

Finally, these verses remind leaders to be patient with those who are spiritually young. Paul adjusted his teaching to the Corinthians' level of maturity. Teachers and mentors can meet people where they are while encouraging them to grow.

### 1 Corinthians 3:6–7 — God Gives the Growth

verse: 1 Corinthians 3:6-7

#### Simple Explanation

The Corinthians were dividing into groups around different leaders, especially Paul and Apollos. Some preferred Paul, the founder of the church; others preferred Apollos, the eloquent teacher. Paul wanted them to understand the true role of these leaders.

He used an image from farming: [[v6|I planted. Apollos watered. But God gave the increase]]. Paul had "planted" the church in Corinth, preaching the gospel there first and seeing people come to faith. Apollos had "watered," coming later and teaching and strengthening the believers. Both had important roles. But neither could make anything grow. Only God gives growth.

Anyone who has grown a garden understands this. A gardener can plant seeds and water them faithfully, but the gardener cannot make the seeds sprout or grow. Life and growth come from God.

[[v7|So then neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters, but God who gives the increase]]. Paul draws the conclusion. Compared to God, the planter and the waterer are nothing. They are simply servants. All the credit belongs to God.

This was a direct challenge to the Corinthians' factions. If Paul and Apollos were simply servants, and God was the one who gave the growth, then it made no sense to divide over them. Believers should honor God, not human leaders.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that God is the source of all spiritual growth. Human efforts are important, since God uses people to plant and water, but the life-giving power belongs to God alone. Jesus told a parable about a farmer who scatters seed, and the seed grows [[Mark 4:27|he doesn’t know how]].

They also show the humility that should mark Christian leaders. Paul, a great apostle, called himself "nothing" compared to God. Leaders should not seek glory for themselves but point people to God.

Finally, these verses show that different ministries work together. Planting and watering are both necessary. Different leaders have different roles, but they serve the same purpose. Paul writes, "he who plants and he who waters are the same" (3:8).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses free believers from the pressure to produce results. Believers who share the gospel, teach, or serve may feel responsible for whether people grow. Paul reminds them that God gives the growth. Their job is to plant and water faithfully.

They also encourage believers who serve in less visible roles. Some plant; others water. Both are essential. Believers who serve faithfully behind the scenes are as important as those whose work is more visible.

Finally, these verses call believers to give God the glory. When churches grow or lives are changed, believers can thank God rather than exalting human leaders.

### 1 Corinthians 3:11–13 — The Foundation and the Testing Fire

verse: 1 Corinthians 3:11-13

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had compared himself to a master builder who laid the foundation of the Corinthian church. Others were building on that foundation. He warned that each person must be careful how they build.

[[v11|For no one can lay any other foundation than that which has been laid, which is Jesus Christ]]. The foundation of the church is Jesus Christ. There is no other foundation. Everything in the church must rest on Him: His person, His death and resurrection, His teaching. Any church built on another foundation is not truly a church.

[[v12|But if anyone builds on the foundation with gold, silver, costly stones, wood, hay, or straw]]. Paul describes different building materials. Gold, silver, and costly stones are valuable and durable; they survive fire. Wood, hay, and straw are cheap and burn easily. These materials represent the quality of work done in the church, the teaching, ministry, and influence of those who build. Some work is valuable and lasting; some is worthless and temporary.

[[v13|each man’s work will be revealed. For the Day will declare it, because it is revealed in fire; and the fire itself will test what sort of work each man’s work is]]. "The Day" refers to the day of judgment, when Christ returns. On that day, every person's work will be tested by fire. Fire reveals the true quality of materials. Gold survives; straw is consumed.

Paul continues: [[v14|If any man’s work remains which he built on it, he will receive a reward]]. [[v15|If any man’s work is burned, he will suffer loss, but he himself will be saved, but as through fire]]. This passage is about believers who build on the foundation of Christ. Their salvation is not in question. But the quality of their work will be revealed. Those who built well will receive a reward; those who built poorly will lose their reward, though they themselves will be saved, like someone escaping a burning house.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that Christ is the only foundation for faith and the church. Peter writes, [[1 Peter 2:6|Behold, I lay in Zion a chief cornerstone, chosen and precious: He who believes in him will not be disappointed]]. Every true work of God rests on Christ.

They also show that believers will give an account for their work. Salvation is by grace, but believers' service will be evaluated. Paul writes, [[2 Corinthians 5:10|For we must all be revealed before the judgment seat of Christ; that each one may receive the things in the body according to what he has done, whether good or bad]].

Some traditions, especially the Roman Catholic Church, have connected this passage with the doctrine of purgatory, a state of purification after death. Many other Christians understand the passage as referring to the evaluation of believers' works at Christ's return, not to a period of purification. All agree that the passage teaches accountability for how believers build on the foundation of Christ.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to build their lives and ministries on Christ alone. Believers can examine whether their faith, teaching, and service are truly grounded in Him.

They also encourage believers to do work that lasts. Believers can ask whether their efforts are aimed at things of eternal value, such as faithful teaching, genuine love, and Christlike character, or at things that will not survive the test, such as self-promotion or shallow results.

Finally, these verses remind church leaders of their responsibility. Those who teach and lead will give an account for how they have built. Leaders can be careful to teach truth, serve faithfully, and build up the church with lasting materials.

### 1 Corinthians 3:16–17 — You Are God's Temple

verse: 1 Corinthians 3:16-17

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had compared the church to a field and a building. Now he uses a third, even more powerful image: [[v16|Don’t you know that you are God’s temple and that God’s Spirit lives in you?]]

In the ancient world, a temple was the dwelling place of a god. In Israel, the temple in Jerusalem was where God's presence dwelt among His people. It was the holiest place on earth. Now Paul tells the Corinthians that they, the church, are God's temple. God does not live in a building made of stones but in His people.

The word "you" is plural here. Paul is speaking about the whole church community, not individual believers. (In 6:19, he applies the same image to the individual believer's body.) The church as a community is where God's Spirit dwells.

[[v17|If anyone destroys God’s temple, God will destroy him]]. This is a serious warning. In context, those who "destroy" God's temple are those who damage the church through division, false teaching, or worldly behavior. The Corinthians' factions were tearing God's temple apart. God takes this seriously.

[[v17|for God’s temple is holy, which you are]]. God's temple is holy, set apart for Him. Because the church is God's temple, it is holy. This holiness is both a gift and a calling. The church belongs to God and must be treated with reverence.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show the incredible privilege of the church. God's presence, once limited to the tabernacle and the temple in Jerusalem, now dwells in His people by the Holy Spirit. Paul writes, [[Ephesians 2:21|in whom the whole building, fitted together, grows into a holy temple in the Lord]], [[Ephesians 2:22|in whom you also are built together for a habitation of God in the Spirit]].

They also show how seriously God takes the unity and holiness of the church. Damaging the church is not a small matter. It is an attack on God's dwelling place. This should cause believers to treat the church with great care.

Finally, these verses show that the church is more than a human organization. It is a spiritual reality, the dwelling place of God. When believers gather, God is present among them in a special way.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses call believers to treat the church with reverence. The church is God's temple. Believers can honor it by protecting its unity, guarding its teaching, and living holy lives.

They also warn against harming the church. Gossip, division, false teaching, and selfish behavior can damage God's temple. Believers can examine whether their words and actions build up or tear down the church.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to value gathering together. When believers meet, they are God's temple, and His Spirit dwells among them. Believers can gather with expectation, aware of God's presence.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

1 Corinthians 3 teaches that spiritual maturity is measured by love and unity, not by gifts or knowledge. The Corinthians were gifted, but their jealousy and quarreling showed that they were still infants. Believers can examine their own lives, asking whether they show the marks of maturity, and can commit themselves to growing in Christ.

The chapter also shows that leaders are servants, and God gives the growth. Paul planted, Apollos watered, but God made the church grow. Believers can stop dividing over human leaders and give all the glory to God. Those who serve can do so faithfully, trusting God for the results.

1 Corinthians 3 teaches that Jesus Christ is the only foundation, and that each person's work will be tested by fire. Believers can build their lives and ministries on Christ alone, using materials that last: faithful teaching, genuine love, and Christlike character. They will give an account for how they have built.

Finally, the chapter declares that the church is God's holy temple, where His Spirit dwells. Believers can treat the church with reverence, protecting its unity and holiness. And they can remember that they belong not to any human leader but to Christ, and that in Him, all things are theirs.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord God, forgive me for the times I have acted like a spiritual infant, with jealousy, quarreling, and division. Help me to grow up in Christ, so that my life shows love, humility, and unity. Thank You for the leaders You have given Your church; help me to honor them as Your servants, without dividing over them. Thank You that You give the growth. Help me to plant and water faithfully, trusting You for the results. Lord Jesus, You are the only foundation. Help me to build my life on You with materials that will last. Holy Spirit, thank You for dwelling in Your church as Your temple. Help me to treat Your church with reverence and to protect its unity and holiness. Thank You that all things are mine because I belong to Christ, and Christ belongs to You. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
