# Acts 20
theme: Finishing the Race: Paul's Farewell to the Ephesian Elders
main: Paul travels through Macedonia and Greece, then meets the believers at Troas on the first day of the week, where he talks until midnight and raises a young man named Eutychus who falls from a window. Hurrying toward Jerusalem, he calls the elders of Ephesus to Miletus and gives a moving farewell. He reminds them of his humble, tearful, and faithful ministry, declares that he counts his life as nothing compared to finishing his race and testifying to God's grace, charges them to shepherd the church Christ purchased with His blood, warns of wolves to come, and commends them to God and the word of His grace. They kneel, pray, and weep together as he departs.

## Chapter Background and Story

Acts 20 marks the end of Paul's third missionary journey and the beginning of his final journey to Jerusalem, which will lead to his arrest and eventually to Rome. During the months described briefly at the beginning of this chapter, Paul was collecting a financial gift from the Gentile churches for the poor believers in Jerusalem (Romans 15:25–28; 1 Corinthians 16:1–4; 2 Corinthians 8–9). The men listed in 20:4 were probably representatives of the churches who traveled with Paul to deliver the gift. During his three months in Greece, probably in Corinth, Paul likely wrote his letter to the Romans.

The heart of this chapter is Paul's speech to the Ephesian elders at Miletus. It is the only speech in Acts addressed to Christian leaders, and it reveals Paul's heart as a pastor. Many of its themes echo his letters, especially his letters to Timothy, who was later a leader in Ephesus.

### Journeys in Macedonia and Greece (20:1–6)

After the riot in Ephesus, Paul encouraged the disciples and left for Macedonia, where he strengthened the churches with many words. He then spent three months in Greece. When he learned of a plot against him as he was about to sail for Syria, he changed his route and traveled back through Macedonia.

He was accompanied by a group of men from different churches: Sopater from Berea, Aristarchus and Secundus from Thessalonica, Gaius from Derbe, Timothy, and Tychicus and Trophimus from Asia. The "we" passages resume in verse 5, indicating that Luke rejoined Paul at Philippi. They sailed from Philippi after the days of Unleavened Bread and joined the others at Troas.

### Eutychus Raised at Troas (20:7–12)

The gathering at Troas is studied below. During it, a young man named Eutychus, whose name means "fortunate," was sitting in a window. [[v9|As Paul spoke still longer, being weighed down by his sleep, he fell down from the third floor and was taken up dead]]. Luke the physician says plainly that he was "taken up dead." The many oil lamps in the upper room may have made the room warm and stuffy, adding to his drowsiness.

[[v10|Paul went down and fell upon him, and embracing him said, “Don’t be troubled, for his life is in him.”]] Paul's action recalls Elijah and Elisha, who stretched themselves over dead children and brought them back to life (1 Kings 17:21; 2 Kings 4:34). Paul then went back upstairs, broke bread, ate, and kept talking until dawn. [[v12|They brought the boy in alive, and were greatly comforted]].

### Journey to Miletus (20:13–16)

Paul's companions sailed ahead to Assos, while Paul walked there by land, about twenty miles, perhaps wanting time alone to pray. Then they sailed together along the coast. Paul decided to sail past Ephesus, because [[v16|he was hastening, if it were possible for him, to be in Jerusalem on the day of Pentecost]]. A visit to Ephesus would have involved many people and delays.

### Paul's Farewell Speech (20:17–35)

From Miletus, about thirty miles south of Ephesus, Paul sent for the elders of the Ephesian church. When they arrived, he spoke to them from his heart.

He reminded them of his conduct among them: [[v19|serving the Lord with all humility, with many tears, and with trials which happened to me by the plots of the Jews]]. He had not held back anything helpful: [[v20|teaching you publicly and from house to house]]. His message to everyone, Jews and Greeks, was [[v21|repentance toward God and faith toward our Lord Jesus]].

Then he spoke of the future: [[v22|Now, behold, I go bound by the Spirit to Jerusalem, not knowing what will happen to me there]], except that the Spirit warned him in every city that imprisonment and hardships awaited him. His attitude toward this is studied below.

He told them they would never see him again. He declared, [[v26|I am clean from the blood of all men]], [[v27|for I didn’t shrink from declaring to you the whole counsel of God]]. Like the watchman in Ezekiel 33, Paul had faithfully warned and taught them. He had declared not only the parts of God's message that were popular, but all of it.

His charge to the elders is studied below. He warned them, [[v29|For I know that after my departure, vicious wolves will enter in among you, not sparing the flock]]. [[v30|Men will arise from among your own selves, speaking perverse things, to draw away the disciples after them]]. False teachers would come from outside and arise from within. Paul's later letters to Timothy show that this warning came true (1 Timothy 1:3–7; 2 Timothy 2:17–18). He reminded them that for three years he had warned each of them [[v31|night and day with tears]].

[[v32|Now, brothers, I entrust you to God and to the word of his grace, which is able to build up and to give you the inheritance among all those who are sanctified]]. Paul could not stay with them, but he left them in the best hands: God's, and the word of His grace. Scripture would build them up and lead them to their eternal inheritance.

Paul concluded by reminding them of his example of hard work and generosity, studied below.

### A Tearful Farewell (20:36–38)

[[v36|When he had spoken these things, he knelt down and prayed with them all]]. [[v37|They all wept freely, and fell on Paul’s neck and kissed him]], grieving most of all at his words that they would never see his face again. Then they accompanied him to the ship. The scene shows the deep love between Paul and the believers he had served. Ministry, for Paul, was not a job but a bond of love.

## Important Verses Explained

### Acts 20:7 — Gathered on the First Day of the Week to Break Bread

verse: Acts 20:7

#### Simple Explanation

Paul and his companions had arrived in Troas, a port city on the northwest coast of Asia Minor. They stayed there for seven days. On their last full day, the believers gathered together.

[[v7|On the first day of the week, when the disciples were gathered together to break bread]]. The "first day of the week" is Sunday, the day Jesus rose from the dead (Luke 24:1). This verse is one of the earliest pieces of evidence that Christians gathered for worship on Sunday. Paul also told the Corinthians to set aside money for the Jerusalem collection [[1 Corinthians 16:2|On the first day of every week]], and John refers to "the Lord's day" (Revelation 1:10).

The purpose of their gathering was "to break bread." This likely refers to the Lord's Supper, possibly shared as part of a fellowship meal. Since Acts 2:42, breaking bread had been a central part of Christian gatherings.

Jewish days began at sunset, but Roman days began at midnight. Luke may be using either reckoning. Many believers worked during the day, since Sunday was not a day off in the Roman world, so they may have gathered in the evening after work.

[[v7|Paul talked with them, intending to depart on the next day; and continued his speech until midnight]]. Paul knew this was his last time with these believers, so he taught them for a long time. The believers were eager to listen. Even after Eutychus fell and was raised, Paul continued talking until dawn.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that Sunday became the day of Christian worship because of the resurrection. Jewish believers continued to honor the Sabbath, but the first day of the week, the day of Jesus' resurrection, became the special day when believers gathered. It celebrated the beginning of the new creation.

It also shows the central place of the Lord's Supper and teaching in Christian worship. The believers gathered to break bread, and Paul taught them. Word and sacrament, teaching and the Lord's Supper, have been at the heart of Christian worship from the beginning.

Finally, the verse shows the eagerness of the early believers. They gathered after a day's work and listened late into the night. Their hunger for God's word and for fellowship is an example for believers in every generation.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to gather regularly for worship. Hebrews urges believers not to give up meeting together (Hebrews 10:25). Sunday worship connects believers with the resurrection of Jesus and with Christians throughout history.

It also encourages believers to value the Lord's Supper and the teaching of God's word. These are not empty rituals but means by which believers remember Christ, grow in faith, and share fellowship.

Finally, the verse invites believers to consider their own hunger for God. The believers at Troas listened until midnight and beyond. Believers can ask God to deepen their desire for His word and for fellowship with His people.

### Acts 20:24 — That I May Finish My Race with Joy

verse: Acts 20:24

#### Simple Explanation

Paul was speaking to the elders of the church in Ephesus at Miletus. He told them he was going to Jerusalem, [[v22|bound by the Spirit]], without knowing exactly what would happen there. But he did know one thing: [[v23|the Holy Spirit testifies in every city, saying that bonds and afflictions wait for me]]. In city after city, prophets and believers warned him that he would be arrested and suffer.

How did Paul respond? [[v24|But these things don’t count; nor do I hold my life dear to myself]]. Paul did not consider his own life, safety, or comfort to be the most important thing. He did not cling to his life. He had a greater goal.

[[v24|so that I may finish my race with joy]]. Paul often compared the Christian life to a race (1 Corinthians 9:24–27; Philippians 3:13–14). His goal was not to avoid suffering but to finish his race faithfully. And he wanted to finish it "with joy," not with regret.

[[v24|and the ministry which I received from the Lord Jesus, to fully testify to the Good News of the grace of God]]. Paul's ministry was given to him by Jesus. His task was to testify to "the Good News of the grace of God," the message that God freely forgives and saves sinners through Jesus. This was Paul's life purpose.

Years later, near the end of his life, Paul wrote to Timothy, [[2 Timothy 4:7|I have fought the good fight. I have finished the course. I have kept the faith]]. He did finish his race.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse reveals the heart of a disciple whose life is fully surrendered to Christ. Paul did not value his life above his calling. Jesus taught, [[Mark 8:35|For whoever wants to save his life will lose it; and whoever will lose his life for my sake and the sake of the Good News will save it]]. Paul lived this out.

It also shows that the Christian life has a purpose and a goal. Paul did not drift through life. He knew what he was called to do and pursued it with focus. Every believer has a race to run and a ministry received from the Lord.

Finally, the verse shows the centrality of grace. Paul summarized his whole message as "the Good News of the grace of God." Grace is the heart of the gospel: God's undeserved favor shown in Christ. Paul, who had once persecuted the church, had experienced this grace powerfully himself.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse challenges believers to consider what they value most. Many people make comfort, safety, or success their highest goals. Paul's example calls believers to value faithfulness to Christ above everything, even their own lives.

It also encourages believers to see their lives as a race with a purpose. Every believer has a ministry received from the Lord, a calling to serve Him and testify to His grace in their particular setting. Believers can ask God to show them their race and give them strength to run it.

Finally, the verse invites believers to aim for a joyful finish. At the end of life, the greatest joy will be to have been faithful to Christ. Believers can live today in a way that will bring joy, not regret, when their race is done.

### Acts 20:28 — Shepherd the Flock Purchased with His Blood

verse: Acts 20:28

#### Simple Explanation

Paul was giving his final charge to the elders of the church in Ephesus. He knew he would never see them again, and he wanted to prepare them to lead the church after he was gone.

[[v28|Take heed, therefore, to yourselves and to all the flock]]. Paul gave two responsibilities. First, the elders must watch over themselves, their own lives, character, and faith. Leaders who neglect their own spiritual health cannot care for others well. Second, they must watch over "all the flock," every member of the church.

[[v28|in which the Holy Spirit has made you overseers]]. The elders' authority came from the Holy Spirit. The church may have recognized and appointed them, but it was the Spirit who made them overseers. The word "overseers" (episkopoi) is related to the word later translated "bishop." In this verse, "elders" (20:17) and "overseers" refer to the same people, showing that in the early church these terms described the same role.

[[v28|to shepherd the assembly of the Lord and God]]. Elders are shepherds. Their task is to feed, guide, protect, and care for God's people, just as a shepherd cares for sheep. Notice that the church belongs to God, not to the elders. (Manuscripts differ here; some read "the church of God" and others "the church of the Lord.")

[[v28|which he purchased with his own blood]]. The church was bought at the greatest possible price: the blood of Christ. This shows how precious the church is to God. The phrase may also be translated "with the blood of his own [Son]." Either way, it points to the cross.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows the high value God places on His church. The church is not a human organization; it is God's flock, purchased by the blood of Christ. Peter writes that believers were redeemed [[1 Peter 1:19|with precious blood, as of a lamb without blemish or spot, the blood of Christ]]. Those who lead the church must treat it with the care that its value deserves.

It also shows the pattern of Christian leadership. Leaders are shepherds, following the example of Jesus, the Good Shepherd who laid down His life for the sheep (John 10:11). Peter urges elders to shepherd the flock [[1 Peter 5:2|not under compulsion, but voluntarily]], not for money but eagerly, not domineering but being examples.

Finally, the verse shows that leaders must care for themselves as well as for others. Paul told Timothy, [[1 Timothy 4:16|Pay attention to yourself and to your teaching]]. Leaders who fall into sin or false teaching can harm the whole flock.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse calls church leaders to faithful, loving shepherding. Pastors and elders are called to care for every member of the church, teaching, guiding, protecting, and loving them. They are also called to guard their own hearts and lives.

It also calls all believers to value the church. Since Christ purchased the church with His blood, believers should not treat it lightly. They can love their church, pray for it, serve it, and seek its good.

Finally, the verse reminds believers that leaders are accountable to God. Church leaders are not owners of the flock but servants of the Chief Shepherd. Believers can pray for their leaders and support them, and leaders can lead with humility, knowing that the church belongs to God. Shepherds must never use their position to harm, control, or exploit those in their care.

### Acts 20:35 — More Blessed to Give than to Receive

verse: Acts 20:35

#### Simple Explanation

Paul concluded his farewell speech to the Ephesian elders by reminding them of his own example. He said, [[v33|I coveted no one’s silver, gold, or clothing]]. Paul had not ministered for money or material gain. [[v34|You yourselves know that these hands served my necessities, and those who were with me]]. He had worked as a tentmaker to support himself and his companions.

[[v35|In all things I gave you an example, that so laboring you ought to help the weak]]. Paul's hard work was not only to support himself, but also to be able to help others. He showed the elders that leaders should work hard and use their resources to care for those in need, "the weak," which may refer to the poor, the sick, or those who cannot support themselves.

[[v35|and to remember the words of the Lord Jesus, that he himself said, ‘It is more blessed to give than to receive.’]] This saying of Jesus is not recorded in any of the four Gospels. It is one of the few sayings of Jesus preserved outside the Gospels. John wrote that Jesus did many things that were not written down (John 21:25), and the early church remembered some of His sayings through oral tradition.

The saying is simple but profound: giving brings greater blessing than receiving. The world often teaches that happiness comes from getting more. Jesus taught that true blessing comes from giving.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse reflects the heart of God, who is the greatest giver. [[John 3:16|For God so loved the world, that he gave his only born Son]]. Jesus Himself gave His life for others. When believers give, they reflect the character of God.

It also shows that giving is a source of joy. Paul wrote that [[2 Corinthians 9:7|God loves a cheerful giver]]. Giving is not merely a duty but a privilege that brings blessing to both the giver and the receiver.

Finally, the verse shows that leaders should model generosity. Paul did not ask others to do what he was unwilling to do. He worked hard and gave generously. Leaders who live generously set an example for others to follow.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to be generous. Believers can look for opportunities to give their time, money, skills, and care to those in need. Giving is not reserved for the wealthy; everyone can give something.

It also challenges the values of a consumer culture. Many people seek happiness through acquiring more. Jesus' words invite believers to find joy in giving. Believers can test this by practicing generosity and experiencing the blessing it brings.

Finally, the verse encourages believers to work hard so that they can help others. Paul wrote elsewhere that believers should work [[Ephesians 4:28|that he may have something to give to him who has need]]. Work is not only for personal provision but also for the blessing of others.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Acts 20 shows the importance of gathering for worship. The believers at Troas met on the first day of the week to break bread and hear God's word, staying late into the night. Believers today can value regular worship, the Lord's Supper, and the teaching of Scripture, coming with hunger for God and love for His people.

The chapter also presents Paul's example of faithful ministry. He served with humility and tears, taught the whole counsel of God, and did not hold his life dear compared to finishing his race and testifying to God's grace. Believers can ask God to give them the same focus, seeing their lives as a race with a purpose and aiming to finish with joy.

Acts 20 contains a powerful charge to church leaders. Elders are called to watch over themselves and the whole flock, shepherding the church that Christ purchased with His blood, and protecting it from false teaching. Believers can pray for their leaders, and leaders can serve with humility and care, remembering that the church belongs to God.

Finally, the chapter calls believers to generosity. Paul worked hard to support himself and help the weak, and he reminded the elders of Jesus' words: "It is more blessed to give than to receive." Believers can find joy in giving and can entrust themselves, as Paul entrusted the Ephesians, to God and to the word of His grace.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for rising from the dead on the first day of the week, and for gathering Your people to break bread and hear Your word. Give me a hunger for worship and for Your truth. Help me, like Paul, to not hold my life dear compared to finishing the race You have given me, and to testify to the Good News of Your grace. Thank You for purchasing Your church with Your own blood. Bless the leaders of Your church; help them to watch over themselves and over the flock, and protect Your people from false teaching. Make me generous, finding joy in giving rather than receiving. I entrust myself to You and to the word of Your grace, which is able to build me up and give me an inheritance among Your people. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
