# 1 Corinthians 7
theme: Marriage, Singleness, and Serving the Lord in Every Calling
main: Responding to questions from the Corinthians, Paul affirms both marriage and singleness as good gifts from God. Within marriage, husband and wife belong to each other and should not deprive one another of intimacy, except by mutual agreement for a time of prayer. Paul wishes others could share his gift of singleness, which frees a person for undivided devotion to the Lord, but he says it is better to marry than to burn with desire. Passing on the Lord's command, he teaches that married believers should not divorce, and that a believer married to an unbeliever should not leave if the unbeliever is willing to stay, though the believer is not bound if the unbeliever departs. Believers should serve God faithfully in whatever situation they were called, remembering that the present form of this world is passing away.

## Chapter Background and Story

With chapter 7, Paul begins answering questions the Corinthians had written to him. The phrase "Now concerning" introduces each new topic (7:1, 25; 8:1; 12:1; 16:1, 12). The first questions concern marriage, sexual relations, singleness, divorce, and remarriage.

The Corinthian church faced pressures from two opposite directions. Some, as chapters 5 and 6 show, believed that sexual freedom was acceptable. Others seem to have gone to the opposite extreme, believing that sexual relations, even within marriage, were unspiritual. They may have been influenced by philosophies that saw the body and physical pleasure as inferior to the spirit. Some may have been considering abstaining from sex within marriage, or even divorcing their spouses in order to be more "spiritual." Paul addresses both errors.

This chapter must be read carefully. Paul distinguishes between commands from the Lord (7:10), his own apostolic guidance (7:12, 25), and his personal preferences (7:7, 40). He also speaks in light of a particular "present distress" (7:26), which may refer to persecution, famine, or the general hardships of that time. And his teaching on marriage and divorce must be read alongside Jesus' teaching (Matthew 5:31–32; 19:3–12; Mark 10:2–12) and the rest of Scripture. Christians hold differing views on some questions this chapter raises, especially about divorce and remarriage.

One principle applies throughout: nothing in this chapter requires anyone to remain in a situation of abuse or danger. Scripture's teaching on marriage assumes love, faithfulness, and the protection of each spouse (Ephesians 5:25–29). A person facing abuse should seek safety and help, and churches must protect them.

### Mutual Belonging in Marriage (7:1–9)

[[v1|Now concerning the things about which you wrote to me: it is good for a man not to touch a woman]]. Many interpreters believe this was a slogan from the Corinthians' letter, which Paul quotes and then qualifies, as he did with the slogans in chapter 6. Some Corinthians were saying that it was best to avoid sexual relations entirely. Paul responds: [[v2|But, because of sexual immoralities, let each man have his own wife, and let each woman have her own husband]]. Given the temptations around them, marriage is the right context for sexual desire. Paul's teaching on mutual belonging is studied below, as is his teaching on singleness as a gift.

### Married Believers and Divorce (7:10–16)

Paul's teaching for married believers, passing on the Lord's command, is studied below. He then addresses mixed marriages, where one spouse had become a believer and the other had not. [[v12|But to the rest I—not the Lord—say, if any brother has an unbelieving wife, and she is content to live with him, let him not leave her]]. Paul speaks here with apostolic authority on a situation Jesus did not directly address during His earthly ministry. [[v13|The woman who has an unbelieving husband, and he is content to live with her, let her not leave her husband]].

Some believers may have worried that being married to an unbeliever made them or their children unclean. Paul reassures them: [[v14|For the unbelieving husband is sanctified in the wife, and the unbelieving wife is sanctified in the husband. Otherwise your children would be unclean, but now they are holy]]. This does not mean the unbelieving spouse is saved automatically. It means that the marriage is not defiled, and the believing spouse's presence brings God's influence into the home. The children are not excluded from God's people. Paul's teaching about when the unbeliever leaves is studied below.

### Remain in Your Calling (7:17–24)

Paul then gives a general principle: [[v17|Only, as the Lord has distributed to each man, as God has called each, so let him walk. So I command in all the assemblies]]. Coming to faith does not require believers to change their outward circumstances. They can serve God where they are.

He applies this to circumcision: [[v19|Circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing, but what matters is keeping God’s commandments]]. Ethnic identity markers do not make someone more or less acceptable to God.

He also applies it to slavery: [[v21|Were you called being a bondservant? Don’t let that bother you, but if you get an opportunity to become free, use it]]. Slavery was widespread in the Roman Empire. Paul encourages slaves not to despair, because their worth before God does not depend on their social status. But he also encourages them to gain freedom if they can. [[v22|For he who was called in the Lord being a bondservant is the Lord’s free man. Likewise he who was called being free is Christ’s bondservant]]. In Christ, a slave is spiritually free, and a free person is Christ's servant. [[v23|You were bought with a price. Don’t become bondservants of men]]. Paul's teaching contains the seeds that would later lead many Christians to oppose slavery entirely, recognizing that it is incompatible with the dignity of people redeemed by Christ (compare Philemon 16; 1 Timothy 1:10, which condemns slave traders).

### Concerning the Unmarried (7:25–40)

[[v25|Now concerning virgins, I have no commandment from the Lord, but I give my judgment as one who has obtained mercy from the Lord to be trustworthy]]. Paul gives his advice to those who had never married. [[v26|Therefore I think that because of the distress that is on us, it’s good for a man to remain as he is]]. Because of the difficult times, Paul thought it wise to remain unmarried. But [[v28|But if you marry, you have not sinned. If a virgin marries, she has not sinned]]. Marriage is not sinful; it simply brings additional responsibilities and troubles in hard times.

[[v29|But I say this, brothers: the time is short]]. Believers should live with an awareness that this world is temporary. [[v31|For the mode of this world passes away]]. Marriage, sorrow, joy, buying, and using the world are all real, but none is ultimate. Believers should hold them loosely.

Paul explains his preference for singleness: [[v32|He who is unmarried is concerned for the things of the Lord, how he may please the Lord]]; [[v33|but he who is married is concerned about the things of the world, how he may please his wife]]. Married people rightly care about their spouses' needs; this is not sinful but part of the calling of marriage. Unmarried people can focus more fully on the Lord. [[v35|This I say for your own benefit, not that I may ensnare you, but for that which is appropriate, and that you may attend to the Lord without distraction]]. Paul is not trying to restrict them but to help them serve the Lord wholeheartedly.

Verses 36–38 address a man and "his virgin," which may refer to a father and his daughter or to an engaged couple. Either way, Paul says that marrying is good, and not marrying is also good, or even better in the present circumstances.

[[v39|A wife is bound by law for as long as her husband lives; but if the husband is dead, she is free to be married to whomever she desires, only in the Lord]]. Widows may remarry, but they should marry a believer. Paul adds his opinion that she might be happier remaining single, [[v40|and I think that I also have God’s Spirit]].

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 Corinthians 7:3–5 — Mutual Belonging in Marriage

verse: 1 Corinthians 7:3-5

#### Simple Explanation

Some in the Corinthian church apparently believed that sexual relations were unspiritual, even within marriage. Some married couples may have been abstaining from intimacy in an effort to be more holy. Paul corrects this.

[[v3|Let the husband give his wife the affection owed her, and likewise also the wife her husband]]. Both husband and wife have a responsibility to meet each other's needs for intimacy. This is mutual. Paul gives the same instruction to both.

[[v4|The wife doesn’t have authority over her own body, but the husband does. Likewise also the husband doesn’t have authority over his own body, but the wife does]]. In marriage, husband and wife belong to each other. This was a remarkable statement in the ancient world, where husbands were usually considered to have rights over their wives, but not the reverse. Paul affirms complete mutuality: the husband's body belongs to his wife just as much as hers belongs to him.

This mutual belonging is not about control or demands. It is about self-giving love. Each spouse gives themselves to the other, seeking the other's good. It never justifies coercion, force, or abuse, which violate the love and respect that Scripture requires in marriage (Ephesians 5:25–33; 1 Peter 3:7).

[[v5|Don’t deprive one another, unless it is by consent for a season, that you may give yourselves to fasting and prayer, and may be together again, that Satan doesn’t tempt you because of your lack of self-control]]. Couples should not withhold intimacy from each other. There may be times when they agree to abstain for a short period for prayer. But this should be by mutual agreement, temporary, and followed by coming together again. Prolonged abstinence can expose them to temptation.

[[v6|But this I say by way of concession, not of commandment]]. Paul allows temporary abstinence for prayer but does not command it.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that sexual intimacy within marriage is good. God created it as a gift for husband and wife. Genesis says, [[Genesis 2:24|Therefore a man will leave his father and his mother, and will join with his wife, and they will be one flesh]]. The Song of Songs celebrates marital love. Paul affirms this goodness against those who considered it unspiritual.

They also show the equality and mutuality of husband and wife. In a culture where women often had few rights, Paul taught that husband and wife have equal responsibilities to each other in this area. Each belongs to the other.

Finally, these verses show that marriage requires self-giving love. The focus is not on getting but on giving. Each spouse considers the other's needs. Paul writes elsewhere that husbands should love their wives as Christ loved the church and gave Himself up for her (Ephesians 5:25).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage married couples to honor each other through intimacy. Intimacy is not merely physical; it is an expression of love, commitment, and unity. Couples can seek to meet each other's needs with tenderness and care.

They also encourage couples to communicate. Times of abstinence should be by mutual agreement. Couples can talk openly about their needs and seek to understand each other.

Finally, these verses must never be used to justify coercion. Mutual belonging means mutual love and respect. Any form of pressure, force, or abuse violates God's design. Couples facing difficulties in this area, whether physical, emotional, or relational, can seek help from trusted counselors, doctors, or pastors.

### 1 Corinthians 7:7–8 — Each Has His Own Gift

verse: 1 Corinthians 7:7-8

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had just been discussing marriage and the importance of intimacy between husband and wife. Now he shares his personal perspective.

[[v7|Yet I wish that all men were like me]]. Paul was unmarried at this time. He may have never married, or he may have been a widower. He found his single life a great blessing because it allowed him to serve the Lord without the responsibilities of a family. He wished others could experience the same freedom.

[[v7|However, each man has his own gift from God, one of this kind, and another of that kind]]. But Paul recognized that not everyone has the same gift. Both marriage and singleness are gifts from God. Some people are gifted for marriage; others are gifted for singleness. Neither is better in God's eyes. Each person should recognize and embrace the gift God has given them.

[[v8|But I say to the unmarried and to widows, it is good for them if they remain even as I am]]. Paul encouraged those who were unmarried or widowed to consider remaining single, as he was.

[[v9|But if they don’t have self-control, let them marry. For it’s better to marry than to burn with passion]]. However, if they struggled with strong sexual desire, they should marry. "Burn with passion" means being consumed by sexual desire. Marriage is God's good provision for this desire.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that singleness is a valuable gift, not a lesser state. In many cultures, then and now, singleness is seen as a failure or a problem to be solved. Paul, along with Jesus, who was also unmarried, shows that a single life can be full, fruitful, and devoted to God. Jesus spoke of those who are single [[Matthew 19:12|for the Kingdom of Heaven’s sake]].

They also show that marriage is a gift. Paul does not disparage marriage. He recognizes that it is God's good design for many people. Both marriage and singleness can be lived for God's glory.

Finally, these verses show that God gives different gifts to different people. Believers should not compare themselves with others or feel pressure to conform to one pattern. Each can embrace the calling God has given.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage single believers. Singleness is not a waiting room or a second-class status. Single believers can embrace their gift, using their freedom to serve God and others with undivided devotion.

They also encourage churches to honor single people. Churches often focus on families and couples, sometimes leaving single people feeling overlooked. Churches can value, include, and support single members, recognizing their gifts and contributions.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to seek God's guidance about marriage. Some are called to marriage, others to singleness. Believers can pray, seek counsel, and trust God's wisdom in this important area.

### 1 Corinthians 7:10–11 — The Lord's Command on Divorce

verse: 1 Corinthians 7:10-11

#### Simple Explanation

Paul now turns to married believers. Some in Corinth may have been considering divorce, perhaps believing that marriage was unspiritual, or that they could serve God better without a spouse.

[[v10|But to the married I command—not I, but the Lord—that the wife not leave her husband]]. Paul emphasizes that this is not merely his opinion. It is a command from the Lord Jesus Himself, who taught that marriage is a lifelong union and that divorce was not God's original intention (Mark 10:2–12; Matthew 19:3–9). [[Mark 10:9|What therefore God has joined together, let no man separate]].

[[v11|(but if she departs, let her remain unmarried, or else be reconciled to her husband), and that the husband not leave his wife]]. Paul recognizes that separation may sometimes happen. If it does, the person should either remain unmarried or seek reconciliation. The goal is restoration of the marriage, where that is possible and safe. The same instruction applies to husbands: they should not divorce their wives.

Christians have understood this teaching in different ways. Many believe that divorce is permitted in certain circumstances, such as sexual unfaithfulness, which Jesus mentions in Matthew 5:32 and 19:9, and desertion by an unbelieving spouse, which Paul addresses in 7:15. Many also believe that serious abuse and persistent cruelty destroy the covenant of marriage and may justify divorce. Others hold stricter views. Some traditions do not permit remarriage after divorce while a former spouse is living; others do in certain circumstances. These are serious questions, and believers facing them should seek wise counsel from their church.

What all should agree on is this: God's design is for marriage to be a lifelong covenant of love and faithfulness, and no one is required by this teaching to remain in a situation where they or their children are in danger. Separation for safety in a case of abuse is not disobedience to God. Paul's own words, "if she departs," recognize that separation sometimes occurs. Churches must never pressure victims of abuse to return to danger.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that marriage is sacred. God designed it as a lifelong covenant reflecting His faithful love for His people and Christ's love for the church (Ephesians 5:31–32). Divorce breaks something precious; through Malachi, God links the breaking of the marriage covenant with unfaithfulness and violence (Malachi 2:14–16).

They also show that reconciliation is often the goal. Paul encourages separated spouses to seek reconciliation where possible. Many marriages that seemed broken have been restored through repentance, forgiveness, and God's grace.

Finally, these verses must be read alongside God's concern for the oppressed. Throughout Scripture, God defends the vulnerable and condemns violence and cruelty (Psalm 11:5; Malachi 2:16). The command not to divorce was never meant to trap people in abuse. God's design for marriage includes the safety and dignity of both spouses.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage married believers to honor their commitment. Marriage takes work, patience, and forgiveness. Couples facing difficulties can seek help from counselors, pastors, and mature believers, and they can pray for God's grace to strengthen their marriage.

They also encourage believers to support marriages in their churches. Churches can offer marriage preparation, counseling, and encouragement, helping couples build strong relationships.

Finally, these verses call churches to care well for those affected by divorce and those in harmful marriages. Divorced people should be treated with compassion, not condemnation. Victims of abuse should be protected, believed, and helped to safety. Churches should never use these verses to keep anyone in danger.

### 1 Corinthians 7:15–16 — God Has Called Us to Peace

verse: 1 Corinthians 7:15-16

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been addressing believers married to unbelievers. In many cases, one spouse had come to faith after the marriage, while the other had not. Paul said that if the unbelieving spouse was willing to stay, the believer should not seek divorce. The believer's presence would bring God's influence into the home.

But what if the unbeliever did not want to stay? [[v15|Yet if the unbeliever departs, let there be separation]]. If the unbelieving spouse chose to leave the marriage, the believer should let them go. The believer cannot force the unbeliever to stay.

[[v15|The brother or the sister is not under bondage in such cases]]. In this situation, the believing spouse is not "bound." Christians have understood this phrase in different ways. Many understand it to mean that the believer is free from the marriage and may remarry, since the marriage has been broken by the unbeliever's departure. Others understand it more narrowly, as meaning that the believer is not obligated to pursue the unbeliever or prevent the separation, without necessarily implying freedom to remarry.

[[v15|but God has called us in peace]]. God's desire for His people is peace. Believers should not live in constant conflict, trying to hold together a marriage the other person has abandoned. Peace is God's purpose.

[[v16|For how do you know, wife, whether you will save your husband? Or how do you know, husband, whether you will save your wife?]] This verse can be read in two ways. It may mean that believers should stay in the marriage if possible, because they might lead their spouse to faith. Or it may mean that believers should not hold on to a departing spouse with the hope of converting them, since they cannot know whether that will happen. Either way, the believer can trust God with the outcome.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that God's design includes peace. While marriage is meant to be lifelong, God does not call His people to endless conflict when a spouse chooses to leave. Paul writes elsewhere, [[Romans 12:18|If it is possible, as much as it is up to you, be at peace with all men]].

They also show that believers cannot control others' choices. A believer can be faithful, loving, and committed, but they cannot force an unbelieving spouse to stay. Paul releases them from responsibility for a decision that was not theirs.

Finally, these verses show God's care for believers in painful situations. Being abandoned by a spouse is deeply painful. Paul's words bring comfort: the believer is not under bondage, and God has called them to peace.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses bring comfort to believers whose spouses have left them. Believers who have been abandoned can release the guilt of a decision that was not theirs, trust God with their future, and seek His peace.

They also encourage believers married to unbelievers to live faithfully. Peter writes that believing wives may win their husbands [[1 Peter 3:1|without a word]] by their conduct. Believers can show Christ's love in their marriages, praying for their spouses' salvation.

Finally, these verses encourage churches to support believers in mixed marriages and those who have been abandoned. These situations bring unique challenges. Churches can offer understanding, prayer, practical help, and wise counsel.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

1 Corinthians 7 affirms that both marriage and singleness are good gifts from God. Neither is superior in His eyes. Married believers can honor their spouses with self-giving love, including the gift of intimacy, which God created to be good. Single believers can embrace their gift, using their freedom to serve the Lord with undivided devotion. Churches can honor and include people in every season of life.

The chapter also teaches that marriage is meant to be a lifelong covenant. The Lord's command is that married believers should not divorce, and that separated spouses should seek reconciliation where possible. At the same time, believers abandoned by an unbelieving spouse are not bound, for God has called His people to peace. Believers facing difficult questions about divorce and remarriage can seek wise counsel, and churches must ensure that no one is ever pressured to remain in abuse or danger.

1 Corinthians 7 teaches believers to serve God in whatever circumstances they were called. Their worth before God does not depend on their marital status, ethnicity, or social position. Each can live faithfully where God has placed them, while taking opportunities to improve their situation when appropriate.

Finally, the chapter reminds believers that the present form of this world is passing away. Marriage, joy, sorrow, and possessions are real, but none is ultimate. Believers can hold all these things loosely, devoting themselves to the Lord without distraction, and living with an eternal perspective.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord God, thank You for the gifts of marriage and singleness, and for calling each of Your people to serve You in different ways. Help married believers to love their spouses with self-giving, faithful love, and to honor one another in every way. Strengthen marriages that are struggling, bring reconciliation where it is possible and safe, and protect all who are in danger. Comfort those who have been abandoned or who carry the pain of divorce, and give them Your peace. Help single believers to embrace their calling and to serve You with undivided devotion, and make Your church a family where everyone belongs. Teach me to serve You faithfully in whatever situation You have called me, remembering that this world is passing away and that I belong to You. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
