# Revelation 20
theme: The Thousand Years and the Final Judgment
main: John sees an angel coming down from heaven with the key to the abyss and a great chain. He seizes the dragon, the ancient serpent who is the devil and Satan, binds him for a thousand years, throws him into the abyss, and locks and seals it so that he can no longer deceive the nations until the thousand years are ended; after that he must be released for a short time. John sees thrones on which those given authority to judge are seated, and the souls of those who had been beheaded for their testimony about Jesus and for the word of God, who had not worshiped the beast or its image or received its mark. They come to life and reign with Christ for a thousand years. The rest of the dead do not come to life until the thousand years are ended. This is the first resurrection, and those who share in it are blessed and holy; the second death has no power over them, and they will be priests of God and of Christ and reign with Him. When the thousand years are over, Satan is released and goes out to deceive the nations in the four corners of the earth, Gog and Magog, gathering them for battle in numbers like the sand of the sea. They march across the earth and surround the camp of God's people, the city He loves, but fire comes down from heaven and devours them. The devil is thrown into the lake of burning sulfur, where the beast and the false prophet were thrown. Then John sees a great white throne and Him who is seated on it; earth and sky flee from His presence. The dead, great and small, stand before the throne, and books are opened, including the book of life. The dead are judged according to what they have done, as recorded in the books. The sea, death, and Hades give up their dead, and each person is judged. Then death and Hades are thrown into the lake of fire, the second death. Anyone whose name is not found written in the book of life is thrown into the lake of fire.

## Chapter Background and Story

Revelation 20 is one of the most discussed chapters in the Bible. It describes a period of a thousand years, often called the "millennium," from the Latin word for a thousand years. During this time Satan is bound, and faithful believers reign with Christ. At the end of the thousand years, Satan is released, leads a final rebellion, and is defeated forever. The chapter closes with the final judgment before the great white throne.

Christians throughout history have held different views about the thousand years. These differences are not about whether Christ will return, whether there will be a resurrection and final judgment, or whether Satan will be defeated. All orthodox Christians affirm these things. The differences concern the timing and nature of the thousand years and how they relate to Christ's return. Sincere, faithful believers hold each of these views, and they have long been held side by side within the church.

### The Main Views of the Thousand Years

**Premillennialism** holds that Christ will return before ("pre-") the thousand years and will then reign on earth for a literal or extended period before the final judgment and the new creation. Premillennialists read Revelation 20 as following chronologically after Christ's return in chapter 19. This view was common in the early church, held by writers such as Justin Martyr and Irenaeus. Among premillennialists, **historic premillennialists** generally see the church going through the final tribulation before Christ returns, while **dispensational premillennialists** generally hold that the church will be taken up before a seven-year tribulation, and that the millennium will include the fulfillment of specific promises to the nation of Israel.

**Amillennialism** holds that the thousand years is a symbolic number representing the present age, the period between Christ's first and second comings. On this view, Satan was bound by Christ's victory at the cross so that he cannot prevent the gospel from going to the nations (Matthew 12:29), and believers who have died now reign with Christ in heaven. The "first resurrection" is understood as either spiritual new birth or the entrance of believers into Christ's presence at death. This view was taught by Augustine and has been widely held in Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran, and Reformed traditions. Many amillennialists see chapter 20 as recapitulating, or retelling from a new angle, the history of the church age rather than following chapter 19 in time.

**Postmillennialism** holds that Christ will return after ("post-") the thousand years, which is understood as a long period in which the gospel spreads and transforms the world, leading to a time of great righteousness and peace before Christ's return. This view was popular among many Protestants in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.

Each view has strengths and difficulties, and each is held by thoughtful, Bible-believing Christians. This commentary will note how each view reads key verses, while focusing on the truths all believers share.

### Satan Bound (20:1–3)

[[v1|I saw an angel coming down out of heaven, having the key of the abyss and a great chain in his hand]]. [[v2|He seized the dragon, the old serpent, who is the devil and Satan, who deceives the whole inhabited earth, and bound him for a thousand years,]] [[v3|and cast him into the abyss, and shut it and sealed it over him, that he should deceive the nations no more until the thousand years were finished. After this, he must be freed for a short time]].

The binding of Satan is described with strong language: seized, bound, thrown, locked, and sealed. Satan, who seemed so powerful in earlier chapters, is overpowered by a single angel. The purpose of the binding is specific: "that he should deceive the nations no more."

Premillennialists understand this as a complete removal of Satan's influence on earth during a future thousand-year reign of Christ. Amillennialists understand it as a restriction on Satan's power that began with Christ's victory at the cross and resurrection, so that he can no longer prevent the nations from hearing the gospel, as he once held them in darkness. They point to Jesus' words about binding the strong man (Matthew 12:29), His statement that He saw Satan fall like lightning (Luke 10:18), and the declaration that the ruler of this world is cast out (John 12:31). Both views agree that Satan's power is limited by God and that he will be finally defeated.

The phrase "he must be freed for a short time" shows that even Satan's release is under God's control. "Must" points to God's purpose.

### Satan's Final Defeat (20:7–10)

[[v7|And after the thousand years, Satan will be released from his prison]] [[v8|and he will come out to deceive the nations which are in the four corners of the earth, Gog and Magog, to gather them together to the war, whose number is as the sand of the sea]]. Gog and Magog come from Ezekiel 38–39, where Gog of the land of Magog leads a vast coalition of nations against God's people and is destroyed by God. In Revelation, Gog and Magog represent all the nations of the earth gathered in a final rebellion against God. Their vast number shows the scale of the rebellion.

The fact that people rebel even after a long period of Satan's binding is sobering. It shows that the problem of evil lies not only in Satan's deception but also in the human heart. Even in ideal conditions, humanity apart from God's grace turns away from Him.

[[v9|They went up over the width of the earth and surrounded the camp of the saints and the beloved city. Fire came down out of heaven from God and devoured them]]. "The camp of the saints" recalls Israel camped in the wilderness. "The beloved city" is the city of God's people, the holy city. The enemies surround it, but there is no battle. Fire from heaven ends the rebellion instantly, as in Ezekiel 38:22 and 39:6. Some interpreters see this battle as the same as the battle described in Revelation 19:17–21 and 16:14–16, retold from a different angle; others see it as a separate, later event. Either way, God wins effortlessly.

## Important Verses Explained

### Revelation 20:4–6 — Reigning with Christ

verse: Revelation 20:4-6

#### Simple Explanation

John sees "thrones," and those seated on them are given authority to judge. He also sees "the souls of those who had been beheaded for the testimony of Jesus and for the word of God." These are martyrs, along with all who "didn’t worship the beast nor his image, and didn’t receive the mark." They are the faithful who refused to compromise.

These faithful believers "lived and reigned with Christ for a thousand years." Those who seemed to lose everything on earth now share in Christ's reign.

Verse 5 adds: "The rest of the dead didn’t live until the thousand years were finished. This is the first resurrection."

Verse 6 pronounces a blessing: "Blessed and holy is he who has part in the first resurrection. Over these, the second death has no power, but they will be priests of God and of Christ, and will reign with him one thousand years." This is the fifth blessing in Revelation.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage answers the cry of the martyrs in 6:9–11. Those who were killed for their faith are not forgotten. They are vindicated and honored, reigning with Christ. The scene also fulfills Daniel 7:22, where judgment is given in favor of the saints and they possess the kingdom.

Interpreters understand "the first resurrection" in different ways. Premillennialists generally see it as a bodily resurrection of believers at Christ's return, before the thousand-year reign; the rest of the dead are raised a thousand years later for the final judgment. Amillennialists generally understand it as a spiritual resurrection, either the new birth of believers (Ephesians 2:5–6; Colossians 3:1) or the entrance of believers' souls into Christ's presence at death, where they reign with Him now; the second, bodily resurrection comes at Christ's return. Each view seeks to be faithful to the text and to the wider teaching of Scripture.

What all agree on is the wonderful promise to those who belong to Christ: "the second death has no power" over them. The second death is the lake of fire (v14), final separation from God. Believers may die physically, but they will never experience the second death. They will be "priests of God and of Christ," serving Him in His presence, and they will reign with Him. The promise to the overcomers in Smyrna (2:11) and Laodicea (3:21) is fulfilled.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers who suffer for their faith can take great comfort in this passage. Their faithfulness is seen and honored by God. Those who lose their lives for Christ will reign with Him.

This passage also gives all believers a secure hope. Whatever view of the millennium they hold, they can rejoice that the second death has no power over them. Their future is life with Christ, not judgment.

The promise that believers will be priests and reign with Christ gives dignity and purpose to the Christian life. Believers are not merely rescued; they are called to share in Christ's rule and service. This future calling can shape how they live now: serving faithfully, worshiping wholeheartedly, and representing Christ in the world.

Finally, the different views of the millennium invite believers to hold their interpretations with humility and charity. Christians can discuss these questions honestly while maintaining unity around the central truths of Christ's return, the resurrection, and the final judgment.

### Revelation 20:10 — The Devil's Final Defeat

verse: Revelation 20:10

#### Simple Explanation

After the final rebellion is crushed, "The devil who deceived them was thrown into the lake of fire and sulfur, where the beast and the false prophet are also."

The deceiver, who has led humanity astray since the garden of Eden, is finally removed. He joins the beast and the false prophet, who were thrown into the lake of fire in 19:20.

The verse ends: "They will be tormented day and night forever and ever." The judgment of Satan is complete and permanent.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse marks the final fulfillment of God's promise in Genesis 3:15, that the offspring of the woman would crush the serpent's head. From the first pages of the Bible, Satan has been the deceiver and accuser. Now he is defeated forever.

The unholy trinity, the dragon, the beast, and the false prophet, are all removed. The forces that deceived the nations, persecuted God's people, and opposed God's purposes will never trouble creation again.

Jesus spoke of [[Matthew 25:41|the eternal fire which is prepared for the devil and his angels]]. The lake of fire was prepared for Satan and his allies. Human beings who share their fate do so because they chose to follow them rather than God. As noted earlier, Christians hold different understandings of the nature of final punishment, but all agree that Satan's defeat is total and final.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can take courage from knowing that Satan's defeat is certain. Though he still deceives and accuses, his time is limited, and his end is sure. Believers do not need to live in fear of him.

This verse also reminds believers that evil will not last forever. Every deception, every accusation, and every work of the enemy will be brought to an end. This hope can sustain believers through every spiritual battle.

Believers can resist the devil now, standing firm in faith (James 4:7; 1 Peter 5:8–9), knowing that the victory belongs to Christ and that the enemy's final destiny has already been decided.

### Revelation 20:11–15 — The Great White Throne

verse: Revelation 20:11-15

#### Simple Explanation

John sees "a great white throne and him who sat on it." The throne is great, showing God's supreme authority, and white, showing His perfect purity and justice. The presence of God is so overwhelming that "the earth and the sky fled away. There was found no place for them." The old creation passes away before the Judge.

In verse 12, John sees "the dead, the great and the small, standing before the throne." Every person, regardless of status, stands before God. "They opened books." Then "Another book was opened, which is the book of life." The dead are "judged out of the things which were written in the books, according to their works."

Verse 13 shows that no one is left out: "The sea gave up the dead who were in it. Death and Hades gave up the dead who were in them." Everyone is raised and judged.

Verse 14 announces the end of death itself: "Death and Hades were thrown into the lake of fire. This is the second death, the lake of fire."

Verse 15 gives the final verdict: "If anyone was not found written in the book of life, he was cast into the lake of fire."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This is the final judgment that Scripture repeatedly promises (Daniel 7:9–10; Matthew 25:31–46; Romans 2:5–16; 2 Corinthians 5:10). Every human being will stand before God and give an account. No one will escape, and no one will be judged unfairly.

There are two kinds of books. "The books" record the deeds of every person. God's judgment is according to works, meaning it takes into account how each person has lived. No one is judged arbitrarily. Yet "the book of life" is also opened. This is the book that records those who belong to the Lamb (13:8; 21:27). Ultimately, it is the book of life that determines each person's destiny.

This reveals the heart of the gospel. If people were judged only by their deeds, no one could stand, for all have sinned (Romans 3:23). But those whose names are written in the Lamb's book of life are saved by grace, through the Lamb who was slain. Their deeds still matter, as evidence of genuine faith and as the basis of reward, but their salvation rests on Christ.

The destruction of death and Hades is a glorious moment. Paul wrote, [[1 Corinthians 15:26|The last enemy that will be abolished is death]]. Death, the great enemy of humanity since the fall, is finally destroyed. Never again will anyone die.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage reminds every person that they will one day stand before God. This is a sobering truth. It gives weight and meaning to every choice and every action. Life matters, because God will judge it.

For believers, this passage brings assurance rather than terror. Their names are written in the Lamb's book of life. They will not face condemnation, because Christ has borne it for them (John 5:24; Romans 8:1). They can approach the final judgment with confidence in His grace.

This passage also gives urgency to sharing the gospel. Everyone will stand before the great white throne. Believers can lovingly invite others to trust in Christ, so that their names too may be written in the book of life.

Finally, believers can rejoice in the promise that death will be destroyed. Every grave will be emptied, and death will be no more. This hope can comfort those who grieve and give courage to those who face death.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Revelation 20 describes the final defeat of evil and the final judgment of all people. Though Christians hold different views about the thousand years, all affirm the central truths of this chapter: Satan's power is limited and will be completely destroyed, faithful believers will reign with Christ, and every person will stand before God's throne.

The chapter invites believers to hold their views about the millennium with humility and to maintain unity with fellow believers who see these things differently. What matters most is not settling every detail, but trusting the Christ who will return and reign.

The chapter gives deep comfort to those who suffer for their faith. Those who refused to worship the beast and were even put to death will live and reign with Christ. The second death has no power over them. Their faithfulness is honored forever.

The chapter also shows that evil is rooted not only in Satan's deception but in the human heart. Even after a long period of Satan's binding, the nations rebel when he is released. This reminds believers of their deep need for God's transforming grace.

Finally, the great white throne calls every person to take God's judgment seriously. All will stand before Him, and only those whose names are written in the Lamb's book of life will escape the second death. Believers can rest in His grace, live with integrity, and share the gospel with urgency and love, looking forward to the day when death itself is destroyed.
