# Revelation 15
theme: The Song of Moses and the Song of the Lamb
main: John sees another great and marvelous sign in heaven: seven angels with the seven last plagues, for with them God's wrath is completed. He sees what looks like a sea of glass glowing with fire, and standing beside it are those who have been victorious over the beast, its image, and the number of its name. They hold harps given by God and sing the song of God's servant Moses and of the Lamb: Great and marvelous are Your deeds, Lord God Almighty; just and true are Your ways, King of the nations. Who will not fear You, Lord, and bring glory to Your name? For You alone are holy. All nations will come and worship before You, for Your righteous acts have been revealed. After this, the temple in heaven, the tabernacle of the covenant law, is opened. Out come the seven angels with the seven plagues, dressed in clean, shining linen with golden sashes around their chests. One of the four living creatures gives them seven golden bowls filled with the wrath of God, who lives forever. The temple fills with smoke from the glory of God and His power, and no one can enter it until the seven plagues are completed.

## Chapter Background and Story

Revelation 15 is a short chapter, but it is full of meaning. It introduces the third and final series of seven judgments, the seven bowls, which will be poured out in chapter 16. These are called "the seven last plagues," because with them God's wrath is completed.

Before the bowls are poured out, however, John is shown a scene of worship. Those who have overcome the beast stand beside a sea of glass and sing a song of praise. As in earlier parts of Revelation, worship comes before judgment. The faithful are shown safe in God's presence before the final judgments fall on the world.

The chapter is filled with echoes of the exodus. God's people stand beside a sea, as Israel stood beside the Red Sea after their deliverance from Egypt. They sing the song of Moses, as Israel sang after crossing the sea (Exodus 15). The plagues recall the plagues on Egypt. And the temple is described as "the tabernacle of the testimony," recalling the tent of meeting in the wilderness, which was filled with God's glory (Exodus 40:34–35). Revelation presents the final victory of God's people as a new and greater exodus.

### The Seven Last Plagues (15:1)

[[v1|I saw another great and marvelous sign in the sky: seven angels having the seven last plagues, for in them God’s wrath is finished]]. This is the third "sign" in heaven, following the woman (12:1) and the dragon (12:3). The bowls are the "last" plagues, because they complete God's judgment on a rebellious world. The word "finished" means brought to its goal. God's wrath is not endless rage; it has a purpose and an end. Once these judgments are complete, the way will be open for the new creation.

Modern readers sometimes find talk of God's wrath uncomfortable. But in Scripture, God's wrath is not a loss of temper or a cruel streak in His character. It is the settled, holy opposition of a good God to everything that destroys what He loves: violence, oppression, idolatry, and the deception that enslaves people. A God who never opposed evil would not be good. Revelation shows that God's wrath is measured, purposeful, and always joined with patience, giving time and warning before it falls. And the gospel declares that Jesus Himself bore God's judgment on the cross for all who trust Him, so that believers can say with Paul that they have been [[Romans 5:9|saved from God’s wrath through him]].

The seven bowls are closely connected with the seals and trumpets, but they are more intense. The seals affected a fourth of the earth, the trumpets a third, but the bowls affect the whole. This progression suggests that God's judgments increase in severity as the end approaches, while also giving repeated opportunities for repentance before the final outpouring.

## Important Verses Explained

### Revelation 15:2 — Victorious Over the Beast

verse: Revelation 15:2

#### Simple Explanation

John sees "something like a sea of glass mixed with fire." The sea of glass first appeared before God's throne in chapter 4, calm and clear like crystal. Now it is "mixed with fire," perhaps reflecting the fire of judgment about to fall, or the fiery trials through which God's people have passed.

Standing on or beside the sea of glass are "those who overcame the beast, his image, and the number of his name." These are believers who refused to worship the beast or receive its mark. In chapter 13, the beast seemed to conquer them (13:7). Now they are revealed as the true victors.

They are "having harps of God," instruments given by God for worship. They are ready to sing.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The phrase "those who overcame the beast" is striking. On earth, many of these believers may have been imprisoned, excluded from economic life, or killed. By worldly standards, they lost. But in heaven's perspective, they won. They overcame the beast not by defeating it in battle, but by refusing to bow to it. Their faithfulness was their victory.

This reflects one of the central messages of Revelation: victory belongs to those who remain faithful to the Lamb, even unto death. As chapter 12 declared, [[Revelation 12:11|They overcame him because of the Lamb’s blood, and because of the word of their testimony. They didn’t love their life, even to death]].

The image of standing beside a sea recalls Israel at the Red Sea. The sea that had seemed to block their escape became the means of their deliverance and the destruction of their enemies. In the same way, the trials that seemed to threaten God's people have become the setting for their victory. They stand safely on the other side.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers who are struggling to remain faithful in the face of pressure can take courage from this vision. Faithfulness may look like loss in the eyes of the world, but in God's eyes it is victory. Every time a believer refuses to compromise, they share in Christ's triumph.

This passage also invites believers to look beyond their present circumstances to their future. Those who are faithful will one day stand in God's presence with harps of praise, safe on the far side of every trial.

Finally, this vision encourages believers to redefine success. In God's kingdom, success is not measured by power, wealth, or popularity, but by faithful loyalty to Christ.

### Revelation 15:3–4 — Great and Marvelous Are Your Works

verse: Revelation 15:3-4

#### Simple Explanation

The victors "sang the song of Moses, the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb." This is one song, celebrating God's deliverance in both the exodus and the work of Christ.

The song begins: "Great and marvelous are your works, Lord God, the Almighty!" God's deeds are amazing and powerful. Then: "Righteous and true are your ways, you King of the nations." God's actions are always just and faithful, and He rules over every nation.

Verse 4 continues with a question: "Who wouldn’t fear you, Lord, and glorify your name?" The obvious answer is that everyone should. "For you only are holy." God alone is perfectly holy.

The song ends with a great promise: "For all the nations will come and worship before you. For your righteous acts have been revealed."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The song of Moses in Exodus 15 celebrated God's victory over Pharaoh at the Red Sea. Another song of Moses, in Deuteronomy 32, celebrated God's faithfulness and justice. The song in Revelation 15 brings these together with the song of the Lamb, celebrating the greater deliverance accomplished through Christ's death and resurrection. The exodus from Egypt was a preview of the greater exodus from sin, death, and the power of evil.

Interestingly, the song does not mention the victors themselves. It is entirely about God. The redeemed do not boast in their own faithfulness. They praise God's works, ways, holiness, and righteous acts. This is the mark of true worship.

The words of the song are woven from Old Testament texts. "Great and marvelous are your works" echoes Psalm 111:2 and 139:14. "Righteous and true are your ways" echoes Deuteronomy 32:4. "Who wouldn’t fear you" echoes Jeremiah 10:7. "All the nations will come and worship before you" echoes Psalm 86:9: [[Psalms 86:9|All nations you have made will come and worship before you, Lord. They shall glorify your name]].

The last line contains a remarkable hope. Even in the context of final judgment, the song looks forward to a day when all nations will worship God. Revelation consistently holds out the hope that people from every nation will turn to God. Judgment does not cancel this hope; it clears the way for its fulfillment.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This song gives believers words for worship. They can praise God for His great and marvelous works in creation, in history, and in their own lives. They can affirm that His ways are righteous and true, even when they do not fully understand them.

The focus of this song on God, rather than on the worshipers, can shape how believers worship. True worship turns attention away from ourselves and toward God's greatness, holiness, and goodness.

This passage also encourages believers to trust God's justice. When the world seems unjust, they can hold on to the truth that God's ways are righteous and true. In the end, His righteous acts will be revealed to all.

Finally, believers can share the hope that all nations will come and worship God. This hope can fuel prayer and mission, as believers long for people from every nation to know and worship the true God.

### Revelation 15:5–8 — The Temple Filled with Glory

verse: Revelation 15:5-8

#### Simple Explanation

After the song, John looks and sees that "the temple of the tabernacle of the testimony in heaven was opened." The tabernacle was the tent where God dwelt among Israel in the wilderness. It was called the "tabernacle of the testimony" because it housed the ark containing the tablets of the law, God's covenant testimony (Exodus 38:21).

The seven angels come out "clothed with pure, bright linen, and wearing golden sashes around their chests." Their clothing resembles that of priests and of Christ Himself in chapter 1.

In verse 7, "One of the four living creatures gave to the seven angels seven golden bowls full of the wrath of God, who lives forever and ever." The bowls are golden, like the bowls of incense that held the prayers of the saints (5:8).

Verse 8 describes the temple: "The temple was filled with smoke from the glory of God and from his power." So intense is God's presence that "No one was able to enter into the temple until the seven plagues of the seven angels would be finished."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The opening of the temple shows that the judgments come directly from God's presence. They are not random disasters or the work of evil forces, but holy acts of a holy God. The angels who carry out the judgments come from the sanctuary, dressed in priestly purity.

The connection with the "testimony," God's covenant law, is significant. The judgments that follow are the outworking of God's justice according to His covenant. God is faithful to His word, both in blessing those who trust Him and in judging those who persistently reject Him.

The golden bowls recall the golden bowls of incense in 5:8, which held the prayers of the saints. Many interpreters see a connection: the prayers of God's people for justice and for His kingdom to come are now answered in the outpouring of these bowls.

The smoke filling the temple recalls moments when God's glory filled the tabernacle and the temple so that no one could enter (Exodus 40:34–35; 1 Kings 8:10–11; Isaiah 6:4). God's glory is overwhelming. The fact that no one can enter until the plagues are finished may suggest that the time for intercession has passed; judgment must now take its course. It is a solemn moment.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage reminds believers of the holiness and majesty of God. His presence is so glorious that no one can stand before it on their own. Yet through Christ, believers have been given access to God's presence (Hebrews 10:19–22). This is a privilege to be received with gratitude and reverence.

The solemnity of this scene also calls believers to take seriously the reality of God's judgment. God is patient, but there will come a time when the opportunity for repentance ends. This truth gives urgency to sharing the gospel and to responding to God's call today.

Finally, believers can trust that God's judgments are holy and just. They come from His presence and reflect His perfect character. Believers do not need to fear that God will act unjustly. They can rest in His righteousness.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Revelation 15 shows that before God's final judgments fall, His people are safe in His presence, singing His praise. Those who overcame the beast, often at great cost, stand beside the sea of glass like Israel after the exodus, celebrating God's deliverance. Believers can take courage that faithfulness, even when it looks like defeat, is true victory.

The song of Moses and the Lamb gives believers a model for worship. It focuses entirely on God: His great and marvelous works, His righteous and true ways, His holiness, and His coming glory among the nations. Believers can make this song their own, praising God in every season and trusting His justice even when they cannot see it.

The chapter also calls believers to reverence. The heavenly temple, filled with the smoke of God's glory, reminds them of the overwhelming holiness of God. His judgments are holy and just, flowing from His perfect character. Believers can approach Him with awe and gratitude, knowing that through Christ they have been given access to His presence.

Finally, the chapter holds out hope for the nations. Even as final judgment approaches, the song looks forward to a day when all nations will come and worship God. Believers can pray and work toward that day, sharing the good news with people from every nation.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord God Almighty, great and marvelous are Your works, and righteous and true are Your ways. You alone are holy, King of the nations. Who would not fear You and glorify Your name? Thank You for the victory You give to those who remain faithful, even when it costs them everything. Help me to see faithfulness as true success, and give me courage to refuse every compromise. Teach me to worship You with a heart focused on Your greatness, not my own. Thank You that through Jesus I have access to Your holy presence; help me to come with reverence and gratitude. I long for the day when all nations will come and worship before You. Use my life to point others to You. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
