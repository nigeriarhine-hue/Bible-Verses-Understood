# Revelation 22
theme: The River of Life and the Promise of His Coming
main: The angel shows John the river of the water of life, clear as crystal, flowing from the throne of God and of the Lamb down the middle of the city's great street. On each side of the river stands the tree of life, bearing twelve kinds of fruit and yielding its fruit every month, and its leaves are for the healing of the nations. There will no longer be any curse. The throne of God and of the Lamb will be in the city, and His servants will serve Him. They will see His face, and His name will be on their foreheads. There will be no more night, and they will not need lamplight or sunlight, for the Lord God will give them light, and they will reign forever. The angel declares that these words are trustworthy and true, and Jesus says, Look, I am coming soon! Blessed is the one who keeps the words of the prophecy in this scroll. John falls down to worship the angel, but the angel again tells him to worship God alone. John is told not to seal up the prophecy, because the time is near; let the one who does wrong continue, and let the holy continue to be holy. Jesus promises again that He is coming soon, bringing His reward to give to each according to what they have done. He is the Alpha and the Omega, the First and the Last, the Beginning and the End. Blessed are those who wash their robes, or keep His commands, that they may have the right to the tree of life and enter the city, while those who practice evil remain outside. Jesus declares that He has sent His angel to give this testimony for the churches; He is the Root and Offspring of David and the bright Morning Star. The Spirit and the bride say, Come! Let the one who hears say, Come! Let the thirsty come and take the free gift of the water of life. John warns that no one should add to or take away from the words of this prophecy. Jesus says, Yes, I am coming soon, and John responds, Come, Lord Jesus. The book closes with a blessing: the grace of the Lord Jesus be with God's people.

## Chapter Background and Story

Revelation 22 brings the book, and the whole Bible, to its close. The first five verses complete the vision of the new Jerusalem begun in chapter 21, focusing on the river of life and the tree of life. The rest of the chapter is an epilogue, a series of final words from the angel, from John, and from Jesus Himself.

The opening verses draw a beautiful connection between the first pages of the Bible and the last. In Genesis 2, a river flowed out of Eden, and the tree of life stood in the garden. After humanity sinned, they were barred from the tree of life, and the ground was cursed (Genesis 3:17–24). Now, in Revelation 22, the river flows from God's throne, the tree of life is available again, and there is no more curse. What was lost in Eden is restored and surpassed in the new creation. The Bible's story moves from a garden to a garden city.

The epilogue repeats key themes from the beginning of the book: the trustworthiness of the prophecy, the blessing on those who keep it, the nearness of Christ's coming, and the call to worship God alone. It also contains three declarations by Jesus that He is "coming soon" (vv7, 12, 20). The book that began with a vision of the risen Christ ends with His promise to return and the church's longing response: "Come, Lord Jesus."

### The River and the Tree of Life (22:1–5)

The river of life, the tree of life, and the blessings of God's servants are studied below.

### The Epilogue: These Words Are True (22:6–11)

[[v6|He said to me, “These words are faithful and true. The Lord God of the spirits of the prophets sent his angel to show to his bondservants the things which must happen soon.”]] The angel affirms that the entire revelation is trustworthy. The phrase echoes the opening of the book (1:1). The same God who inspired the prophets of old has inspired this prophecy.

[[v7|“Behold, I am coming soon! Blessed is he who keeps the words of the prophecy of this book.”]] Jesus Himself speaks. This is the sixth blessing in Revelation, repeating the first blessing in 1:3. The purpose of the book is not to satisfy curiosity but to call people to keep its words, to live faithfully in light of Christ's coming.

[[v8|Now I, John, am the one who heard and saw these things. When I heard and saw, I fell down to worship before the feet of the angel who had shown me these things]]. [[v9|He said to me, “You must not do that! I am a fellow bondservant with you and with your brothers, the prophets, and with those who keep the words of this book. Worship God.”]] For the second time (compare 19:10), John is so overwhelmed that he falls before the angel, and for the second time the angel firmly refuses worship. The repetition emphasizes how important this lesson is. However glorious a messenger may be, worship belongs to God alone.

[[v10|He said to me, “Don’t seal up the words of the prophecy of this book, for the time is at hand.]] Daniel was told to seal up his prophecy until the time of the end (Daniel 12:4, 9). John is told the opposite: do not seal it. The message is for now. The time is near, and the churches need to hear it.

[[v11|He who acts unjustly, let him act unjustly still. He who is filthy, let him be filthy still. He who is righteous, let him do righteousness still. He who is holy, let him be holy still.”]] This verse echoes Daniel 12:10. It is not an encouragement to continue in sin, but a sober statement that people's choices are shaping their character and destiny. As the end approaches, people become more fixed in the direction they have chosen. It is also a call to the righteous and holy to keep going, persevering in faithfulness. The time to choose is now.

### The Final Words of Jesus (22:12–16)

[[v12|“Behold, I am coming soon! My reward is with me, to repay to each man according to his work.]] [[v13|I am the Alpha and the Omega, the First and the Last, the Beginning and the End]]. Jesus applies to Himself the titles that God used of Himself (1:8; 21:6). He is fully divine, the beginning and goal of all things. He will come to reward everyone according to their deeds. For believers, this means that their faithful service will be honored (Matthew 16:27; 1 Corinthians 3:12–14).

[[v14|Blessed are those who do his commandments, that they may have the right to the tree of life, and may enter in by the gates into the city]]. This is the seventh and final blessing in Revelation. Many ancient manuscripts read instead that those who wash their robes are blessed, recalling 7:14, where the redeemed washed their robes in the blood of the Lamb. The two readings express complementary truths: entrance into the city comes through the cleansing that Christ provides, and those who are cleansed live in obedience to Him.

[[v15|Outside are the dogs, the sorcerers, the sexually immoral, the murderers, the idolaters, and everyone who loves and practices falsehood]]. In the ancient world, "dogs" was a term of contempt for those considered morally impure (compare Philippians 3:2). The list describes those who persist in evil and reject God. This is not a statement that such people exist in the new creation just outside the walls; it is a vivid way of saying that unrepentant evil has no place in God's city. Like 21:8, this list is a warning, not a final word to those who still have time to turn to God.

[[v16|I, Jesus, have sent my angel to testify these things to you for the assemblies. I am the root and the offspring of David, the Bright and Morning Star.”]] Jesus personally confirms that the whole revelation comes from Him for the churches. He calls Himself "the root and the offspring of David." He is the source of David's line (as God) and its descendant (as the Messiah), fulfilling Isaiah 11:1, 10. He is also "the Bright and Morning Star," the star that announces the dawn of a new day (Numbers 24:17). This fulfills the promise to the overcomers in Thyatira, to whom He promised the morning star (Revelation 2:28).

### The Final Invitation and Warning (22:17–21)

The invitation to come and drink the water of life is studied below.

[[v18|I testify to everyone who hears the words of the prophecy of this book: if anyone adds to them, God will add to him the plagues which are written in this book]]. [[v19|If anyone takes away from the words of the book of this prophecy, God will take away his part from the tree of life, and out of the holy city, which are written in this book]]. This warning echoes Deuteronomy 4:2 and 12:32, where Moses warned Israel not to add to or subtract from God's commands. It protects the integrity of the prophecy. No one should distort its message by adding their own ideas or removing what they find uncomfortable. This warning applies specifically to the book of Revelation, but it reflects a principle that applies to all of Scripture: God's word is to be received, not altered. It also serves as a caution against overconfident speculation that claims to know more than the book reveals.

The closing promise, prayer, and blessing are studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### Revelation 22:1–5 — The River and the Tree of Life

verse: Revelation 22:1-5

#### Simple Explanation

The angel shows John "a river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb." The river flows from God's throne, the source of all life. It is pure and clear.

The river runs "in the middle of its street." On both sides of the river stands "the tree of life, bearing twelve kinds of fruits, yielding its fruit every month." The tree provides continual abundance. And "The leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations."

Verse 3 announces: "There will be no curse any more." Everything broken by sin is restored. "The throne of God and of the Lamb will be in it, and his servants will serve him."

Verse 4 gives the greatest blessing of all: "They will see his face, and his name will be on their foreheads." God's people will see Him directly and belong to Him completely.

Verse 5 adds: "There will be no night." God Himself will be their light. "They will reign forever and ever."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage completes the story that began in Eden. The river recalls the river that watered the garden (Genesis 2:10). The tree of life, from which humanity was barred after the fall, is now freely available. The curse pronounced in Genesis 3 is lifted. God's people, who were driven out of the garden, now dwell with Him forever.

The river also recalls Ezekiel 47:1–12, where a river flows from the temple, bringing life wherever it goes, with trees on its banks whose fruit is for food and whose leaves are for healing. Jesus promised that whoever believes in Him would have rivers of living water flowing from within, speaking of the Holy Spirit (John 7:38–39). The water of life represents the abundant, eternal life that God gives.

"The healing of the nations" does not imply that there will be sickness in the new creation. Rather, it suggests that the wounds of history, the divisions, hostilities, and injuries among nations, will be fully healed. The nations will live in harmony, enjoying God's life together.

"They will see his face" is perhaps the most astonishing promise in all of Scripture. Moses was told that no one could see God's face and live (Exodus 33:20). Yet Jesus promised, [[Matthew 5:8|Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God]]. In the new creation, God's people will see Him face to face. This is the fulfillment of every longing of the human heart: to know God fully and to be fully known by Him (1 Corinthians 13:12).

Notice also that God's servants "will serve him" and "will reign forever and ever." Eternity is not idleness. It is joyful service and shared rule, the fulfillment of humanity's original calling in Genesis 1:26–28 to care for God's world under His authority.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage gives believers a glorious hope. The brokenness of this world, sin, sickness, conflict, death, and the curse, will not last forever. God will restore everything, and His people will live in His presence, enjoying abundant life.

This hope can sustain believers through every trial. When they experience the effects of the curse, pain, loss, conflict, or decay, they can remember that a day is coming when there will be no curse any more.

The promise that God's people will see His face invites believers to long for God above all else. The greatest joy of eternity is not anything God gives, but God Himself. Believers can cultivate this longing now by seeking His presence in prayer, worship, and Scripture.

The healing of the nations also gives believers a vision for the present. They can work for reconciliation, peace, and healing among peoples now, as a sign of the healing God will one day complete.

### Revelation 22:17 — Come!

verse: Revelation 22:17

#### Simple Explanation

Near the end of the book, a series of invitations rings out. "The Spirit and the bride say, ‘Come!’" The Holy Spirit and the church, the bride of Christ, call out together.

"He who hears, let him say, ‘Come!’" Everyone who hears this message is invited to join in the invitation.

Then the invitation turns to those who need life: "He who is thirsty, let him come. He who desires, let him take the water of life freely."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The first "Come!" may be addressed to Christ, as the Spirit and the bride long for His return (compare v20). Or it may be addressed to the world, inviting people to come to Christ. Most likely, the verse moves from one to the other. The church calls on Christ to come, and the church calls the world to come to Christ. Longing for Christ's return and inviting others to Him belong together.

The invitation is wide open: "He who is thirsty, let him come. He who desires, let him take." The only requirement is thirst and willingness. And the water of life is given "freely," without cost. This echoes Isaiah 55:1: [[Isaiah 55:1|Hey! Come, everyone who thirsts, to the waters! Come, he who has no money, buy, and eat! Yes, come, buy wine and milk without money and without price]]. It also echoes Jesus' great invitation: [[John 7:37|If anyone is thirsty, let him come to me and drink!]]

It is fitting that the Bible ends with an invitation. After all the visions of judgment, the final word to the world is not condemnation, but welcome. God's desire is that people come to Him and live.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites everyone who is spiritually thirsty to come to Christ. Anyone who longs for meaning, forgiveness, peace, or life can come and receive the water of life freely. No one is too far gone, and nothing needs to be paid.

Believers are called to join in the invitation. The Spirit and the bride say, "Come!" and everyone who hears is invited to say it too. Believers can share this invitation with warmth and joy, telling others about the free gift of life in Christ.

Believers can also keep coming themselves. The water of life is not received only once. Believers can come to Christ daily for refreshment, renewal, and strength.

### Revelation 22:20–21 — Come, Lord Jesus

verse: Revelation 22:20-21

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus speaks His final words in the Bible: "He who testifies these things says, ‘Yes, I am coming soon.’" This is the third time in the chapter that Jesus promises to come soon. His return is certain.

John responds with a prayer: "Amen! Yes, come, Lord Jesus!" This is the heartfelt response of the church to Christ's promise. Believers long for His return.

Verse 21 closes the book with a blessing: "The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with all the saints." The last word of the Bible is grace.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The prayer "Come, Lord Jesus" is one of the earliest Christian prayers. Paul includes an Aramaic version of it, *Maranatha*, meaning Our Lord, come (1 Corinthians 16:22). The early church prayed this prayer with longing, eager for Christ's return.

The promise "I am coming soon" has been understood in different ways, as discussed at the beginning of the book. From God's perspective, a thousand years is like a day (2 Peter 3:8). "Soon" expresses the certainty and nearness of His coming. Every generation of believers is called to live as though He could come at any time.

It is deeply fitting that the Bible ends with grace. The story of Scripture is the story of God's grace: creating the world, calling a people, sending His Son, redeeming sinners, and making all things new. Revelation, with all its visions of judgment and conflict, ends not with a warning but with grace. Everything believers have, and everything they hope for, is a gift of God's grace through the Lord Jesus Christ.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can make John's prayer their own: "Come, Lord Jesus." This prayer expresses longing for Christ's return, for the end of suffering, and for the fulfillment of God's promises. It is a prayer of hope.

Praying for Christ's coming also shapes how believers live now. Those who long for His return will want to live in a way that honors Him, to be ready to meet Him, and to invite others to know Him.

Finally, believers can live by grace. Just as the Bible ends with grace, their lives can be marked by grace: receiving God's grace with gratitude, resting in it when they fail, and extending it to others. The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ is with all who belong to Him, today and forever.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Revelation 22 brings the story of the Bible to its glorious conclusion. What was lost in Eden is restored and surpassed. The river of life flows from God's throne, the tree of life is available again, the curse is gone, and God's people see His face. This is the hope of every believer, the future toward which all of history is moving.

This hope can transform how believers live now. They can face suffering with courage, knowing that the curse will one day be lifted. They can long for God above all else, knowing that the greatest joy of eternity is seeing His face. And they can work for healing and reconciliation among people, as a foretaste of the healing of the nations.

The chapter also calls believers to faithfulness. Jesus promises three times that He is coming soon. Those who keep the words of this prophecy are blessed. Believers are called to live in readiness, worshiping God alone, persevering in righteousness and holiness, and receiving God's word without adding to it or taking away from it.

The book ends with an invitation. The Spirit and the bride say, "Come!" Anyone who is thirsty can come and receive the water of life freely. Believers can come to Christ themselves and invite others to come.

Finally, the book ends with prayer and grace. Believers can join John in praying, "Come, Lord Jesus," longing for His return. And they can rest in the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, which is with all His people. From the first page of Scripture to the last, God's story is a story of grace, and it ends with the promise that His people will be with Him forever.

Thank You that one day I will see Your face. Make that my deepest longing. Help me to keep the words of this prophecy, to worship You alone, and to persevere in righteousness and holiness. When I am thirsty, help me to come to You for the water of life, and give me joy in inviting others to come. Lord Jesus, You are the Root and Offspring of David, the bright Morning Star, the Alpha and the Omega. You have promised that You are coming soon. I long for that day; come, Lord Jesus. Until You come, let Your grace be with me and with all Your people. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
