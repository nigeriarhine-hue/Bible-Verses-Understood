# Romans 3
theme: All Have Sinned, and All Who Believe Are Justified Freely by Grace
main: Paul answers objections about God's faithfulness to Israel and then draws his conclusion: Jews and Gentiles alike are under sin, and no one is righteous before God. The Law cannot justify anyone; it reveals sin. But now God has revealed His righteousness apart from the Law, through faith in Jesus Christ, for all who believe. All have sinned and fall short of God's glory, yet they are justified freely by His grace through the redemption in Christ Jesus, whom God presented as an atoning sacrifice. In this way God shows Himself both just and the One who justifies those who have faith in Jesus, leaving no room for boasting.

## Chapter Background and Story

Romans 3 contains the turning point of Paul's argument and some of the most important verses in the Bible. Since 1:18, Paul has been showing that all humanity, Gentiles and Jews alike, stand guilty before God. In 3:1–20 he completes this argument, answering objections and declaring the universal guilt of humanity. Then, in 3:21–26, comes the great announcement of the gospel: God's righteousness has been revealed through faith in Jesus Christ. Many have called this passage the heart of the whole letter, and even of the whole Bible.

### Objections Answered (3:1–8)

If being Jewish and circumcised does not guarantee righteousness (2:25–29), a reader might ask, [[v1|Then what advantage does the Jew have? Or what is the profit of circumcision?]] Paul answers, [[v2|Much in every way! Because first of all, they were entrusted with the revelations of God]]. Israel received the Scriptures, God's covenants, and His promises. This was a great privilege.

But what if some Jews were unfaithful? Does their unfaithfulness cancel God's faithfulness? [[v4|May it never be! Yes, let God be found true, but every man a liar]]. Even if every human being proved false, God would remain true. Paul quotes Psalm 51:4, David's prayer of repentance, in which David acknowledges that God is right to judge him.

Paul then addresses a twisted argument: if human sin makes God's righteousness shine more brightly, is it unfair for God to judge sinners? Some even slanderously accused Paul of teaching, [[v8|Let’s do evil, that good may come]]. Paul rejects this completely: [[v8|Those who say so are justly condemned]]. God's grace never excuses sin.

### No One Is Righteous (3:9–20)

Paul then asks, [[v9|What then? Are we better than they? No, in no way. For we previously warned both Jews and Greeks that they are all under sin]]. "Under sin" means under sin's power and guilt.

He supports this with a collection of Old Testament passages, mostly from the Psalms, studied below in part. They describe the corruption of the whole person: [[v13|Their throat is an open tomb. With their tongues they have used deceit.]] (Psalm 5:9) [[v14|Their mouth is full of cursing and bitterness.]] (Psalm 10:7) [[v15|Their feet are swift to shed blood.]] (Isaiah 59:7) The list moves from speech to violence to the root of it all: [[v18|There is no fear of God before their eyes.]] (Psalm 36:1)

Paul explains the purpose of the Law: [[v19|that every mouth may be closed, and all the world may be brought under the judgment of God]]. Like a defendant who has no answer, every person stands silent before God's judgment. [[v20|Because by the works of the law, no flesh will be justified in his sight; for through the law comes the knowledge of sin]]. The Law is like a mirror: it shows people their sin, but it cannot wash it away. Keeping the Law cannot make anyone right with God, because no one keeps it perfectly.

### The Righteousness of God Revealed (3:21–26)

Then come two of the most important words in Romans: [[v21|But now]]. After the long, dark description of human sin, Paul turns to God's answer. [[v21|apart from the law, a righteousness of God has been revealed, being testified by the law and the prophets]]. God has provided a righteousness that does not come by keeping the Law. Yet it is not a departure from the Old Testament; the Law and the Prophets point to it.

[[v22|even the righteousness of God through faith in Jesus Christ to all and on all those who believe. For there is no distinction]]. This righteousness comes through faith in Jesus. It is available to all who believe, without distinction between Jew and Gentile. (Some interpreters translate the phrase "through the faithfulness of Jesus Christ," emphasizing Christ's own faithful obedience; both readings affirm that salvation rests on Christ and is received by faith.) The rest of this passage is studied below.

### Boasting Excluded (3:27–31)

[[v27|Where then is the boasting? It is excluded]]. If salvation is God's free gift, received by faith, no one can boast. Paul's conclusion is studied below. Paul then shows that this truth means God is the God of Gentiles as well as Jews: [[v30|since indeed there is one God who will justify the circumcised by faith and the uncircumcised through faith]]. Because there is one God, there is one way of salvation for all people.

Finally, Paul anticipates an objection: [[v31|Do we then nullify the law through faith? May it never be! No, we establish the law]]. Faith does not destroy the Law; it fulfills its true purpose. The Law pointed to Christ and revealed humanity's need for Him. In Romans 4, Paul will show that the Law itself teaches justification by faith through the example of Abraham. And in chapter 8, he will show that those who live by the Spirit fulfill the righteous requirement of the Law (8:4).

## Important Verses Explained

### Romans 3:10–12 — There Is No One Righteous

verse: Romans 3:10-12

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been building his case since Romans 1:18 that all people, both Gentiles and Jews, are guilty before God. He had shown that the pagan world rejected God and fell into every kind of sin, and that those who judge others, including those who have God's Law, are guilty too. Now he brings his argument to a conclusion with a series of quotations from the Old Testament.

[[v10|As it is written, “There is no one righteous; no, not one.]] This quotation comes from Psalm 14:1–3 and Psalm 53:1–3. No one, not a single person, is righteous before God by their own merit. Paul emphasizes "not one." There are no exceptions.

[[v11|There is no one who understands. There is no one who seeks after God.]] Sin affects the mind ("no one who understands") and the will ("no one who seeks"). People do not naturally seek God on their own. When people do seek God, it is because God has first drawn them (John 6:44).

[[v12|They have all turned away. They have together become unprofitable. There is no one who does good, no, not so much as one.]] All people have turned from God's way. The word "unprofitable" was used of milk that has gone sour, or of fruit that has become rotten. Humanity has become spoiled, unable to fulfill its purpose.

These are strong words. They do not mean that people never do anything kind or good in a human sense. People can be generous, brave, and loving. But no one does good in the full sense God requires, with a perfect heart, for God's glory, and consistently. Measured against God's perfect standard, everyone falls short.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses describe what theologians often call the universal sinfulness of humanity. Sin is not merely a problem for some especially bad people; it is the condition of every human heart. The prophet Isaiah wrote, [[Isaiah 64:6|For we have all become like one who is unclean, and all our righteousness is like a polluted garment]].

They also show that sin affects the whole person: mind, will, desires, words, and actions. This does not mean that people are as bad as they could possibly be, but that every part of human nature has been affected by sin.

Finally, these verses prepare the way for grace. Paul does not describe human sinfulness to make people despair, but to show them their need for a Savior. Only when people recognize that they cannot save themselves are they ready to receive God's free gift.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses humble believers. No one can stand before God on the basis of their own goodness. Believers can lay down any pride in their own righteousness and depend completely on God's grace.

They also help believers understand the world. The brokenness, violence, and injustice in the world are not surprising; they flow from the human heart. Believers can respond to evil with sorrow and with the hope of the gospel, which alone can change hearts.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to share the gospel with compassion. Everyone stands in the same need. Believers do not approach others as superior people, but as fellow sinners who have found mercy and want to share it.

### Romans 3:23–24 — Justified Freely by His Grace

verse: Romans 3:23-24

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had just announced that God's righteousness has been revealed through faith in Jesus Christ for all who believe, with no distinction between Jews and Gentiles. Now he explains why there is no distinction.

[[v23|for all have sinned, and fall short of the glory of God]]. Everyone has sinned. Paul uses two verbs. "Have sinned" looks back at the past: everyone has committed sin. "Fall short" is in the present tense: everyone continues to fall short of God's glory. "The glory of God" may refer to the perfect standard of God's character, or to the glory God intended humans to share as His image bearers. Either way, everyone misses the mark.

[[v24|being justified freely by his grace]]. Then comes the good news. The same "all" who have sinned can be justified. "Justified" is a legal term meaning "declared righteous." It is the verdict a judge gives when he declares someone not guilty. God declares sinners righteous.

How? "Freely by his grace." The word "freely" means as a gift, without cost to the receiver. The same word is used in John 15:25, "They hated me without a cause." Justification is given without any cause in the person receiving it. "Grace" is God's undeserved favor. People do not earn it; God gives it.

[[v24|through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus]]. "Redemption" means release through the payment of a price. In the ancient world, it was used of buying the freedom of a slave or a prisoner. In the Old Testament, God redeemed Israel from slavery in Egypt. Through Christ, God redeems people from slavery to sin. The price was paid by Christ Himself through His death.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses summarize the gospel. All have sinned, and all who believe are justified freely by God's grace through Christ. Salvation is entirely God's gift. Paul writes, [[Ephesians 2:8|for by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God]].

They also show that justification is a legal declaration. God does not merely help people become better; He declares them righteous in His sight. This is not a legal fiction, because it rests on the real work of Christ, who bore the penalty of sin. Believers are counted righteous because they are united to Christ, the Righteous One.

Finally, these verses show that the gift is free to the receiver but costly to the Giver. Grace is free, but redemption required a price. The cost was the life of God's Son. Peter writes that believers were redeemed [[1 Peter 1:19|with precious blood, as of a lamb without blemish or spot, the blood of Christ]].

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses bring freedom from guilt. Believers who trust in Christ are justified, declared not guilty, by God Himself. No accusation can stand against them. They can live with peace and confidence before God.

They also bring freedom from the pressure to earn God's approval. Justification is free. Believers do not need to prove themselves to God. They can serve Him out of gratitude, not out of fear.

Finally, these verses level the ground among all people. Everyone has sinned, and everyone is saved in the same way. Believers can welcome others as fellow recipients of grace, regardless of their background or past.

### Romans 3:25–26 — Just and the Justifier

verse: Romans 3:25-26

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had just said that sinners are justified freely by God's grace through the redemption in Christ Jesus. Now he explains how God accomplished this.

[[v25|whom God sent to be an atoning sacrifice through faith in his blood]]. The phrase "atoning sacrifice" translates a word that refers to a sacrifice that turns away God's wrath and removes sin. In the Greek translation of the Old Testament, the same word described the "mercy seat," the cover of the Ark of the Covenant, where the high priest sprinkled blood on the Day of Atonement (Leviticus 16:14–15). Jesus is the true place where God's mercy meets human sin. His blood, His death on the cross, makes atonement. This atonement is received "through faith."

[[v25|for a demonstration of his righteousness through the passing over of prior sins, in God’s forbearance]]. Before Christ came, God had been patient with human sin. He did not punish every sin immediately. Someone might ask whether God was being unjust by letting sin go unpunished. The cross answers this question: God had not ignored sin; He was waiting to deal with it fully through Christ.

[[v26|to demonstrate his righteousness at this present time, that he might himself be just and the justifier of him who has faith in Jesus]]. This is the heart of the gospel. God faced what seemed like an impossible problem. If He is just, He must punish sin. If He is loving, He wants to forgive sinners. How can He do both? At the cross, God punished sin in Christ, so He remains just. And He forgives those who believe in Jesus, so He is the justifier. Justice and mercy meet at the cross.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal the meaning of the cross. Jesus' death was not merely an example of love or a tragic martyrdom. It was an atoning sacrifice in which God dealt with sin. Isaiah wrote of the suffering servant, [[Isaiah 53:5|But he was pierced for our transgressions. He was crushed for our iniquities. The punishment that brought our peace was on him; and by his wounds we are healed]].

They also show that God's love and justice are not in conflict. Some people imagine that God's love means He overlooks sin, or that His justice means He has no mercy. The cross shows that God is perfectly just and perfectly loving. He takes sin seriously enough to judge it, and He loves sinners enough to bear that judgment Himself in His Son.

Finally, these verses show that atonement was God's initiative. "God sent" Jesus. The Father did not have to be persuaded to love; He provided the sacrifice. John writes, [[1 John 4:10|In this is love, not that we loved God, but that he loved us, and sent his Son as the atoning sacrifice for our sins]].

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses help believers understand why the cross was necessary. God could not simply overlook sin without compromising His justice. The cross shows how seriously God takes sin and how much He loves sinners. This should lead believers to worship and gratitude.

They also assure believers that their forgiveness is secure. God's justice has been satisfied in Christ. When God forgives believers, He is not ignoring their sin; He has dealt with it fully. Believers can rest in complete forgiveness.

Finally, these verses call believers to reflect God's character. God is both just and merciful. Believers are called to love justice and show mercy (Micah 6:8), taking wrong seriously while extending grace to others.

### Romans 3:28 — Justified by Faith Apart from Works of the Law

verse: Romans 3:28

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had just explained that God justifies sinners freely through the atoning sacrifice of Jesus. He asked, "Where then is the boasting?" and answered, "It is excluded." If salvation is God's gift, no one can take credit for it.

Then Paul gave his conclusion: [[v28|We maintain therefore that a man is justified by faith apart from the works of the law]]. "We maintain" means "we conclude" or "we hold firmly." This is the summary of everything Paul has argued.

"A man is justified," declared righteous before God, "by faith," by trusting in Jesus Christ. "Apart from the works of the law," without depending on keeping the commands of the Law of Moses. Faith is the means by which a person receives God's gift.

Faith is not a work that earns salvation. It is like an empty hand that receives a gift. The value is not in the hand but in what it receives. Faith looks away from oneself to Christ.

Some readers wonder how this fits with James 2:24, which says that a person is justified by works and not by faith alone. Paul and James are addressing different issues. Paul is arguing against the idea that people can earn God's acceptance by keeping the Law. James is arguing against a "faith" that is merely intellectual agreement and produces no change in life. Both agree that genuine faith in Christ saves, and that genuine faith shows itself in good works. Paul himself writes about [[Galatians 5:6|faith working through love]].

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse expresses the doctrine of justification by faith, which was central to the Protestant Reformation of the sixteenth century. Martin Luther called it the article on which the church stands or falls. But the truth goes back to Abraham, who [[Genesis 15:6|believed in the LORD, who credited it to him for righteousness]]. Paul will develop this example in Romans 4.

It also shows that salvation is the same for all people. Since it is by faith, not by the Law, Gentiles can be saved without becoming Jews, and Jews are saved in the same way as Gentiles. Faith unites all believers on equal ground.

Finally, the verse excludes all boasting. No one can say, "I earned my salvation." All the glory belongs to God. Paul writes, [[1 Corinthians 1:31|He who boasts, let him boast in the Lord]].

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse brings rest to believers who are anxious about whether they have done enough for God. Justification is by faith, not by performance. Believers can stop striving to earn God's acceptance and rest in what Christ has done.

It also protects believers from pride. Since salvation is a gift received by faith, believers have nothing to boast about except Christ. This humility can shape how they treat others.

Finally, the verse invites anyone to come to God. No one is required to clean up their life first or achieve a certain level of goodness. Anyone can be justified by trusting in Jesus. Believers can share this open invitation with everyone.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Romans 3 brings every reader face to face with the truth: there is no one righteous, not even one. All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God. This truth humbles everyone, the religious and the irreligious alike. Believers can lay aside every claim to self-righteousness and acknowledge their complete need for God's grace.

The chapter also announces the good news: God's righteousness has been revealed through faith in Jesus Christ for all who believe. Sinners are justified freely by God's grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus. Believers can rest in this gift, free from the guilt of sin and from the burden of trying to earn God's acceptance.

Romans 3 explains the meaning of the cross. Jesus is the atoning sacrifice through whom God deals with sin. At the cross, God shows that He is both just and the justifier of those who have faith in Jesus. Believers can worship God for His perfect justice and His overwhelming mercy, and they can reflect both in how they live.

Finally, the chapter excludes all boasting. Since salvation is by faith apart from works, no one can take credit for it. Jews and Gentiles, people of every background, are saved in the same way. Believers can welcome one another as fellow recipients of grace, share the gospel freely with everyone, and give all the glory to God.

## Chapter Prayer

Holy God, I confess that there is no one righteous, not even one, and that I too have sinned and fallen short of Your glory. Thank You that You have not left me in my sin. Thank You for revealing Your righteousness through faith in Jesus Christ, and for justifying me freely by Your grace through the redemption that is in Him. Lord Jesus, thank You for being the atoning sacrifice for my sins, bearing the judgment I deserved. Father, I praise You because You are both just and the One who justifies those who believe in Jesus. Take away every trace of pride in my heart, and help me to boast only in You. Help me to rest in Your grace and to share this good news with everyone, for there is no distinction. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
