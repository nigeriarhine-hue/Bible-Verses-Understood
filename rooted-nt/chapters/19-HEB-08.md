# Hebrews 8
theme: The Mediator of a Better Covenant
main: The main point of all that has been said is this: believers have a high priest who has taken His seat at the right hand of God's throne in heaven, where He serves in the true sanctuary set up by the Lord, not by human hands. Earthly priests served in a sanctuary that was only a copy and shadow of the heavenly one, as God showed when He told Moses to build the tabernacle according to the pattern shown on the mountain. Jesus' ministry is far superior, because He mediates a better covenant founded on better promises. If the first covenant had been without fault, there would have been no need for a second. Quoting Jeremiah 31, the author shows that God Himself promised a new covenant, different from the one made at the exodus, in which He would put His laws in His people's minds and write them on their hearts, be their God, make Himself known to all of them from the least to the greatest, and forgive their sins and remember them no more. By calling this covenant "new," God made the first one obsolete, and what is obsolete is ready to disappear.

## Chapter Background and Story

The author begins this chapter by summarizing his argument: "the main point is this." Jesus is a high priest, and He serves not on earth but in heaven. The rest of the chapter develops two connected ideas: Jesus serves in a better sanctuary, and He mediates a better covenant.

A covenant is a binding relationship established by God with His people, with promises and commitments. The "first covenant" in Hebrews is the covenant God made with Israel at Mount Sinai after the exodus, often called the Mosaic or old covenant (Exodus 19–24). It included the Law, the priesthood, the tabernacle, and the sacrifices. It was a gracious gift of God, but Israel repeatedly broke it.

Centuries later, during the dark days before the Babylonian exile, God spoke through the prophet Jeremiah. Though Israel had broken the covenant, God promised a new covenant that would succeed where the old had failed, because God Himself would change His people's hearts (Jeremiah 31:31–34). Jesus announced at the Last Supper that this new covenant was established in His blood (Luke 22:20). Hebrews 8 contains the longest Old Testament quotation in the New Testament, and it is this promise.

### The Main Point (8:1–2)

The author's summary is studied below.

### A Better Ministry (8:3–6)

[[v3|For every high priest is appointed to offer both gifts and sacrifices. Therefore it is necessary that this high priest also have something to offer]]. A priest must offer something. Jesus' offering, as the author has already said and will explain, is Himself (Hebrews 7:27).

[[v4|For if he were on earth, he would not be a priest at all, seeing there are priests who offer the gifts according to the law]]. Jesus was from the tribe of Judah, so under the Law He could not serve as a priest in the earthly temple (Hebrews 7:13–14). His priesthood belongs to a different realm. The heavenly sanctuary and the better covenant are studied below.

### The Promise of a New Covenant (8:7–13)

[[v7|For if that first covenant had been faultless, then no place would have been sought for a second]]. The first covenant was not evil, but it was inadequate to accomplish everything God intended, because it depended on a sinful people who could not keep it.

[[v8|For finding fault with them, he said, “Behold, the days are coming”, says the Lord, “that I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah]]. Notice the words "with them." The fault was not mainly in the covenant itself but in the people who broke it. God promised a new covenant to the whole of His people, both the northern kingdom (Israel) and the southern kingdom (Judah).

[[v9|not according to the covenant that I made with their fathers in the day that I took them by the hand to lead them out of the land of Egypt; for they didn’t continue in my covenant, and I disregarded them,” says the Lord]]. The new covenant would be different from the Sinai covenant, which Israel did not keep. The image of God taking Israel "by the hand" is tender, like a father leading a child. Yet the people turned away. The content of the new covenant is studied below.

[[v13|In that he says, “A new covenant”, he has made the first obsolete. But that which is becoming obsolete and grows aged is near to vanishing away]]. By promising a new covenant, God showed that the old one was temporary. If Hebrews was written before A.D. 70, the author may have been anticipating the destruction of the temple, which ended the sacrificial system. The old covenant's role as a system of priesthood and sacrifice was fulfilled and passing away, because its purpose was complete in Christ.

It is important to read this carefully. The author is not saying that God has rejected the Jewish people. The new covenant was promised "with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah," and Jesus and His first followers were Jewish. Gentiles are welcomed into this covenant through Christ (Ephesians 2:11–13). Christians understand the relationship between Israel and the church in different ways, but Paul insists that God has not rejected His people (Romans 11:1–2, 29). What has become obsolete is the old covenant's system of priesthood and sacrifice as the way of relating to God, not God's love for Israel.

## Important Verses Explained

### Hebrews 8:1–2 — The Main Point

verse: Hebrews 8:1-2

#### Simple Explanation

"Now in the things which we are saying, the main point is this." The author pauses to summarize. Everything he has said about Melchizedek, the priesthood, and the oath comes down to one central truth.

"We have such a high priest." Believers have a high priest, the one the author has described: eternal, sinless, sympathetic, able to save completely.

"Who sat down on the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens." He has taken His seat in the place of supreme honor and authority, beside God the Father. Sitting down shows that His sacrificial work is finished (Hebrews 10:11–12). "The Majesty" is a reverent way of referring to God.

Verse 2: "a servant of the sanctuary and of the true tabernacle which the Lord pitched, not man." Jesus ministers in the heavenly sanctuary, the true tabernacle, which God Himself set up. The earthly tabernacle was built by human hands according to God's instructions. The heavenly one is the reality it pictured.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The heart of Hebrews is not a doctrine but a person: Jesus, our high priest at God's right hand. Everything else serves this main point. When believers are weary, confused, or tempted to drift, the author directs them to this central truth.

Jesus is both seated and serving. He is seated because His sacrifice is complete. He is serving because He continues to minister on behalf of His people, interceding for them (Hebrews 7:25) and applying the benefits of His sacrifice to them.

"The true tabernacle" does not mean the earthly one was false. "True" here means real, genuine, the ultimate reality. The earthly tabernacle was a true copy; the heavenly one is the original. Jesus serves in the very presence of God.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

When the Christian life feels complicated, believers can return to the main point: they have a high priest at God's right hand. Everything rests on Him.

This truth brings confidence. Believers' access to God does not depend on an earthly building, ritual, or human priest. Jesus represents them in God's very presence.

Finally, the image of Jesus seated at God's right hand invites rest. His work is finished. Believers do not need to complete it; they can trust it.

### Hebrews 8:5 — A Copy and Shadow of Heavenly Things

verse: Hebrews 8:5

#### Simple Explanation

The earthly priests "serve a copy and shadow of the heavenly things." The tabernacle and its worship were a copy, a sketch or model, of heavenly realities. And they were a shadow, an outline that indicates something real but is not the real thing itself.

"Even as Moses was warned by God when he was about to make the tabernacle." When God gave Moses instructions for the tabernacle, He told him to follow a specific design.

"For he said, 'See, you shall make everything according to the pattern that was shown to you on the mountain.'" This quotation comes from Exodus 25:40. God showed Moses a pattern on Mount Sinai, and the tabernacle was built according to it. The pattern pointed to heavenly reality.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that the Old Testament system of worship was not an end in itself. It was designed by God to point beyond itself. Every detail, the altar, the lampstand, the table of bread, the curtain, the ark, the sacrifices, was a picture of spiritual truths that would be fulfilled in Christ.

Colossians 2:17 uses similar language: the festivals and Sabbaths were "a shadow of the things to come; but the body is Christ’s." The shadow is real and meaningful, but it gives way to the substance when the substance arrives.

This understanding helps believers read the Old Testament with appreciation. The tabernacle and sacrifices are not irrelevant. They teach about God's holiness, the seriousness of sin, the need for atonement, and the way God provides access to Himself. And they all point to Jesus.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can read the Old Testament descriptions of the tabernacle and sacrifices not as tedious details but as rich pictures of Christ. Each element teaches something about what Jesus has done.

This verse also reminds believers that physical buildings, rituals, and religious practices are not the ultimate reality. Churches and worship practices can be valuable, but their purpose is to point to God, not to replace Him.

Finally, believers can live with their eyes on heavenly realities. The things of this world are, in a sense, shadows. What is lasting is found in God's presence, where Christ is.

### Hebrews 8:6 — A Better Covenant on Better Promises

verse: Hebrews 8:6

#### Simple Explanation

"But now he has obtained a more excellent ministry." Jesus' priestly work is superior to that of the earthly priests.

"By as much as he is also the mediator of a better covenant." A mediator brings two parties together and establishes an agreement between them. Moses was the mediator of the old covenant (Galatians 3:19). Jesus is the mediator of the new covenant, which is "better."

"Which on better promises has been given as law." The new covenant is established on better promises. These promises are listed in verses 10–12: an inner transformation of the heart, a personal relationship with God, a universal knowledge of God, and complete forgiveness of sins.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Why is the new covenant better? Not because the old covenant was bad, but because the new covenant accomplishes what the old could only point toward. The old covenant gave God's Law on tablets of stone; the new covenant writes it on the heart. The old covenant had repeated sacrifices that could not fully remove sin; the new covenant brings a once-for-all sacrifice that cleanses completely. The old covenant had a human mediator; the new has the Son of God.

Jesus is the mediator of this covenant in a unique way. He not only announces it; He establishes it through His own blood (Hebrews 9:15; Luke 22:20). And He guarantees it (Hebrews 7:22). The new covenant is secure because it rests on Him.

The word "better" is a key word in Hebrews. Every comparison points to Christ as the fulfillment of what came before. Believers have something better than the greatest privileges of the old covenant.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can appreciate the privileges they have under the new covenant: direct access to God, the Spirit's work in their hearts, and complete forgiveness. These are not small blessings but the fulfillment of centuries of hope.

This verse also reminds believers that their relationship with God rests on Jesus, the mediator. Their confidence is not in their own faithfulness but in His.

Finally, the "better promises" invite trust. God's promises in the new covenant are sure, because they rest on Christ's finished work. Believers can build their lives on them.

### Hebrews 8:10–12 — Written on the Heart

verse: Hebrews 8:10-12

#### Simple Explanation

These verses describe the content of the new covenant promised in Jeremiah 31:33–34. There are four great promises.

First, inner transformation. "I will put my laws into their mind; I will also write them on their heart." Under the old covenant, the Law was written on stone tablets, outside the people. Under the new covenant, God writes His law inside, on the heart. People do not merely know what God commands; they are changed so that they desire to do His will. This is the work of the Holy Spirit (Ezekiel 36:26–27; 2 Corinthians 3:3).

Second, a personal relationship. "I will be their God, and they will be my people." This was always God's goal (Exodus 6:7; Leviticus 26:12). In the new covenant, it is fully realized.

Third, universal knowledge of God. "They will not teach every man his fellow citizen and every man his brother, saying, 'Know the Lord,' for all will know me, from their least to their greatest." Under the old covenant, knowledge of God was often mediated through priests and prophets. Under the new covenant, every member of God's people knows Him personally, regardless of status or age. This does not mean teachers are no longer needed (Hebrews 5:12), but that every believer has a direct, personal relationship with God.

Fourth, complete forgiveness. "For I will be merciful to their unrighteousness. I will remember their sins and lawless deeds no more." This is the foundation of the other promises. God forgives completely and permanently. When He says He will [[v12|remember their sins and lawless deeds no more]], it does not mean He forgets in the sense of losing information, but that He will never hold those sins against His people again.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These promises describe the heart of the gospel. God does not simply give people better rules; He gives them new hearts. He does not merely forgive some sins temporarily; He forgives all sins permanently. He does not keep His people at a distance; He makes Himself known to each one.

The promise of forgiveness is placed last in the list, but it is the foundation of everything. The word "for" at the beginning of verse 12 shows that the inner transformation and the personal knowledge of God are possible because God has forgiven sins. Chapters 9 and 10 will explain how this forgiveness is secured through Christ's sacrifice.

These promises were made to Israel, and they have begun to be fulfilled through Jesus, the Messiah of Israel. Through Him, Gentiles also share in these blessings (Romans 11:17; Ephesians 3:6). Many Christians believe the promises also look forward to a future fulfillment for the people of Israel (Romans 11:25–27).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can thank God that their obedience is not merely an external effort to follow rules. God has written His law on their hearts, giving them new desires to love and obey Him. When they struggle, they can ask the Spirit to strengthen what He has begun in them.

The promise that "all will know me" assures every believer, no matter how young, uneducated, or new to the faith, that they can know God personally. They do not need to go through a special class of people to have a relationship with Him.

Finally, the promise "I will remember their sins and lawless deeds no more" brings deep peace. Believers who are haunted by past sins can rest in God's complete forgiveness. If God does not hold their sins against them, they can stop holding those sins against themselves.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Hebrews 8 summarizes the main point of the letter's teaching about Christ: believers have a high priest who has sat down at the right hand of God's throne in heaven and serves in the true sanctuary that the Lord Himself set up. When the Christian life feels complicated or discouraging, believers can return to this central truth. Everything rests on Jesus, their high priest in God's presence.

The chapter explains that the earthly tabernacle and its worship were a copy and shadow of heavenly realities. They were good and meaningful, designed by God to point to Christ. Believers can read the Old Testament with fresh eyes, seeing how its details reveal the work of Jesus.

Jesus is the mediator of a better covenant, established on better promises. God foretold this new covenant through Jeremiah, promising to write His laws on His people's hearts, to be their God, to make Himself known to all of them, and to forgive their sins and remember them no more. These promises describe the heart of the gospel: a new heart, a personal relationship with God, and complete forgiveness.

Believers can live in the joy and confidence of these promises. They can rely on the Spirit's work within them, approach God personally, and rest in the assurance that their sins are forgiven and forgotten. The old covenant system has given way to the new, not because God's love for Israel has ended, but because His promises have been fulfilled in Jesus, the Messiah of Israel and the Savior of the world.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, You are my high priest, seated at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in heaven. Thank You that You serve in the true sanctuary, in the very presence of God, on my behalf. Thank You for the tabernacle and sacrifices of the old covenant, which pointed to You, and for fulfilling all they foreshadowed. Thank You for being the mediator of a better covenant, established on better promises. Write Your law on my heart, and give me a desire to love and obey You. Thank You that You are my God and I am Yours, and that I can know You personally. Thank You that You have forgiven my sins and will remember them no more. Help me to live in the freedom and joy of that forgiveness. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
