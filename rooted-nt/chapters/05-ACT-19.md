# Acts 19
theme: The Word of the Lord Grows Mighty in Ephesus
main: In Ephesus, Paul meets about twelve disciples who knew only John's baptism; he explains that John pointed to Jesus, and they are baptized in Jesus' name and receive the Holy Spirit. Paul teaches for over two years, so that the whole province of Asia hears the word, and God works extraordinary miracles. When seven sons of Sceva try to use Jesus' name like a magic spell, an evil spirit overpowers them, and fear spreads through the city. Many believers confess their practices and burn their valuable magic books. A silversmith named Demetrius, alarmed at losing business from shrines of Artemis, stirs up a city-wide riot, until the town clerk calms the crowd.

## Chapter Background and Story

Acts 19 describes Paul's ministry in Ephesus, the longest he stayed in any city during his missionary journeys, nearly three years (20:31). Ephesus was the capital of the Roman province of Asia and one of the largest cities in the empire, with perhaps 250,000 people. It was a major port and commercial center.

Ephesus was famous for the Temple of Artemis (called Diana by the Romans), one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World. It was four times the size of the Parthenon in Athens. The Artemis of Ephesus was a fertility goddess, and her worship drew pilgrims from across the region. The city was also known as a center of magic and the occult. "Ephesian letters" were magical formulas sold as charms.

Paul's ministry in Ephesus shows the gospel confronting this world of idolatry and magic, and the power of Jesus' name overcoming spiritual darkness. Paul later wrote his letter to the Ephesians, and the church in Ephesus is addressed in Revelation 2:1–7.

### Disciples Who Knew Only John's Baptism (19:1–7)

Paul arrived in Ephesus and found about twelve disciples. He asked them a question, and their conversation is studied below. When Paul laid his hands on them, [[v6|the Holy Spirit came on them and they spoke with other languages and prophesied]]. This experience recalls Pentecost (Acts 2), Samaria (Acts 8), and Cornelius's household (Acts 10). In each case, a new group of people was clearly welcomed into the church through the gift of the Spirit.

### Two Years of Teaching (19:8–10)

As usual, Paul began in the synagogue, where [[v8|he entered into the synagogue and spoke boldly for a period of three months, reasoning and persuading about the things concerning God’s Kingdom]]. But when some became stubborn and spoke evil of the Way publicly, Paul withdrew with the disciples and taught daily in [[v9|the school of Tyrannus]], probably a lecture hall rented out at certain hours. One early manuscript adds that Paul taught there from about eleven in the morning to four in the afternoon, the hottest part of the day when most people rested.

[[v10|This continued for two years, so that all those who lived in Asia heard the word of the Lord Jesus, both Jews and Greeks]]. People from all over the province came to Ephesus for business, and many heard the gospel. Paul's co-workers also traveled to nearby cities. Churches were founded in places like Colossae, Laodicea, and Hierapolis (Colossians 1:7; 4:13), probably during this time. The seven churches of Asia addressed in Revelation 2–3 may have their roots in this period.

### Extraordinary Miracles (19:11–12)

[[v11|God worked special miracles by the hands of Paul]]. Notice that it was God who worked the miracles. Even [[v12|handkerchiefs or aprons were carried away from his body to the sick, and the diseases departed from them, and the evil spirits went out]]. These were probably the sweat cloths and work aprons Paul used as a tentmaker. Luke describes these miracles as "special" or "extraordinary." They were not the usual pattern even in Paul's ministry. In a city obsessed with magic, God demonstrated His power in a way that showed that Jesus is greater than any sorcery. (Compare the woman healed by touching the edge of Jesus' garment in Luke 8:44, and the people healed by Peter's shadow in Acts 5:15.)

### The Sons of Sceva (19:13–17)

Some traveling Jewish exorcists tried to use the name of Jesus as a magic formula. This incident is studied below.

### Burning the Magic Books (19:18–20)

The incident with the sons of Sceva led to deep repentance among believers, studied below.

### Paul's Plans (19:21–22)

[[v21|Paul determined in the Spirit, when he had passed through Macedonia and Achaia, to go to Jerusalem, saying, “After I have been there, I must also see Rome.”]] This is a turning point in Acts. From here on, Paul's journey moves toward Jerusalem and ultimately Rome, the capital of the empire. The word "must" suggests a divine necessity. Paul sent Timothy and Erastus ahead to Macedonia while he stayed in Asia a little longer.

### The Riot in Ephesus (19:23–41)

[[v23|About that time there arose no small disturbance concerning the Way]]. A silversmith named Demetrius made silver shrines of Artemis, small models of the temple or the goddess that pilgrims bought as souvenirs or for worship. His concerns are studied below.

The craftsmen were enraged and began shouting, [[v28|Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!]] The city filled with confusion. The crowd rushed into the great theater of Ephesus, which could hold about 25,000 people and whose ruins can still be seen today. They dragged Gaius and Aristarchus, Paul's traveling companions from Macedonia, with them.

Paul wanted to go into the theater to address the crowd, but the disciples would not let him. Even some of the [[v31|Asiarchs]], wealthy officials of the province who were his friends, sent word urging him not to go. That Paul had friends among the province's leading citizens shows how widely his influence had spread.

[[v32|Some therefore cried one thing, and some another, for the assembly was in confusion. Most of them didn’t know why they had come together]]. Luke paints a picture of mob mentality. Some Jews pushed forward a man named Alexander, perhaps to explain that the Jews were not responsible for Paul's teaching. But when the crowd realized he was a Jew, they shouted for about two hours, [[v34|Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!]]

Finally, the town clerk, the chief civic official, calmed the crowd. He reminded them that Ephesus was the guardian of Artemis's temple and of the image [[v35|which fell down from Zeus]] (probably a meteorite believed to be sacred). Since no one could deny this, they should not act rashly. Paul's companions were [[v37|neither robbers of temples nor blasphemers of your goddess]]. Paul had not insulted Artemis; he had simply preached the truth about the living God. If Demetrius had a complaint, the courts were open. The town clerk warned that the city could be accused of rioting by the Roman authorities. Then he dismissed the assembly.

## Important Verses Explained

### Acts 19:4–5 — John Pointed to Jesus

verse: Acts 19:4-5

#### Simple Explanation

When Paul arrived in Ephesus, he met about twelve disciples. Something about them seemed incomplete, so he asked, [[v2|Did you receive the Holy Spirit when you believed?]] They answered, [[v2|No, we haven’t even heard that there is a Holy Spirit]]. They probably meant that they had not heard that the Holy Spirit had been given, since John the Baptist had spoken of the coming Spirit (Luke 3:16).

Paul asked, [[v3|Into what then were you baptized?]] They said, [[v3|Into John’s baptism]]. These were disciples of John the Baptist, or people who had been taught by his followers, perhaps like Apollos before Priscilla and Aquila instructed him (18:25). They had repented and prepared for the Messiah, but they had not yet heard the full message about Jesus.

Paul explained: [[v4|John indeed baptized with the baptism of repentance, saying to the people that they should believe in the one who would come after him, that is, in Christ Jesus]]. John's baptism was a baptism of repentance, preparing people for the coming Messiah. But John always pointed beyond himself. He told people to believe in the One coming after him. That One was Jesus.

[[v5|When they heard this, they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus]]. When they understood that Jesus was the One John had pointed to, they believed and were baptized in His name. This is the only place in the New Testament where people are recorded as being rebaptized. They had received John's baptism, which looked forward to Jesus; now they received Christian baptism, which identified them with Jesus who had come.

Then Paul laid his hands on them, and the Holy Spirit came on them.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that John the Baptist's ministry was always meant to lead to Jesus. John said, [[John 3:30|He must increase, but I must decrease]]. Repentance prepares the way, but faith in Jesus is the goal.

They also show the importance of a complete understanding of the gospel. These disciples were sincere, but their faith was incomplete. They needed to hear about Jesus, His death and resurrection, and the gift of the Spirit. Paul did not dismiss them; he patiently explained and led them into the fullness of faith.

Finally, these verses show the close connection between faith in Jesus, baptism in His name, and the gift of the Holy Spirit. These belong together in the Christian life. While Christians have different views about the exact timing and relationship of these elements, as discussed at Acts 8:14–17, all agree that every believer is meant to know Jesus, be baptized into Him, and live by His Spirit.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to make sure their faith is centered on Jesus. Some people have a religious background or a sincere desire to be good, but have not yet come to know Jesus personally. Believers can gently help them, as Paul did, to see that all true faith points to Christ.

They also encourage believers to seek the fullness of the Christian life. The Holy Spirit is given to all who believe in Jesus. Believers can depend on the Spirit, ask for His filling, and grow in His fruit.

Finally, these verses model patient, respectful teaching. Paul did not criticize the Ephesian disciples for what they lacked. He asked questions, listened, and explained. Believers can follow this approach when helping others grow in their understanding.

### Acts 19:15 — [[Acts 19:15|Jesus I Know, and Paul I Know, but Who Are You?]]

verse: Acts 19:15

#### Simple Explanation

God was working extraordinary miracles through Paul in Ephesus. Even cloths that had touched Paul were taken to the sick, and they were healed and evil spirits left them. Ephesus was a city full of magic and exorcists, and some people noticed Paul's power and wanted to copy it.

[[v13|But some of the itinerant Jews, exorcists, took on themselves to invoke over those who had the evil spirits the name of the Lord Jesus, saying, “We adjure you by Jesus whom Paul preaches.”]] These traveling exorcists did not know Jesus personally. They treated His name like a magic formula, a word of power that would work if spoken correctly. Notice their words: "Jesus whom Paul preaches." They did not know Jesus for themselves; they only knew about Him through Paul.

Among them were [[v14|seven sons of one Sceva, a Jewish chief priest]]. Sceva is not known from other sources; he may have been from a priestly family, or he may have claimed the title to impress people.

[[v15|The evil spirit answered, “Jesus I know, and Paul I know, but who are you?”]] The evil spirit recognized Jesus' authority and knew Paul as His servant. But it did not recognize these men. They had no relationship with Jesus and therefore no authority from Him.

The man with the evil spirit then attacked them, overpowered all seven, and beat them so badly that they ran out of the house naked and wounded.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that the power of Jesus' name is not magic. The name of Jesus represents His person and authority. It cannot be used as a formula by those who do not know Him. Jesus warned that on the day of judgment many will say, [[Matthew 7:22|Lord, Lord, didn’t we prophesy in your name, in your name cast out demons, and in your name do many mighty works?]] and He will say, [[Matthew 7:23|I never knew you]].

It also shows that spiritual authority comes from relationship with Jesus. Paul had authority because he belonged to Jesus and served Him. The sons of Sceva had no such relationship. Believers have authority in Jesus' name because they are united to Him by faith.

Finally, the verse shows the reality of spiritual forces. The evil spirit recognized Jesus and Paul. Scripture teaches that there is a real spiritual battle (Ephesians 6:12), and the forces of darkness must submit to Jesus' authority, but only Jesus and those who truly belong to Him have power over them.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse warns against treating faith as a formula. Some people use Jesus' name or religious practices as if they were magic, hoping to get results without a genuine relationship with Him. Prayer, worship, and the name of Jesus are not tools to manipulate God. They are expressions of trust in the One we know.

It also invites believers to examine whether they know Jesus personally or only know about Him. The sons of Sceva knew about "Jesus whom Paul preaches," but they did not know Him. Believers can ask whether their faith is secondhand, based only on what others say, or whether they have a living relationship with Christ.

Finally, the verse gives believers confidence. Believers who truly belong to Jesus have nothing to fear from evil powers. Jesus has defeated them, and His people are under His protection.

### Acts 19:18–20 — Burning the Books of Magic

verse: Acts 19:18-20

#### Simple Explanation

The story of the sons of Sceva spread throughout Ephesus. [[v17|Fear fell on them all, and the name of the Lord Jesus was magnified]]. People realized that the name of Jesus was not something to be used lightly. It was holy and powerful.

This led to a powerful response among believers: [[v18|Many also of those who had believed came, confessing and declaring their deeds]]. These were people who had already believed in Jesus, but had continued in some of their old practices, including magic. Now they came forward publicly, confessing what they had done.

[[v19|Many of those who practiced magical arts brought their books together and burned them in the sight of all]]. Ephesus was famous for its books of spells and charms. These new believers did not sell their magic books or hide them; they burned them publicly, making a clean break from their old way of life.

[[v19|They counted their price, and found it to be fifty thousand pieces of silver]]. A silver coin (a drachma) was about a day's wage for a laborer. Fifty thousand days' wages represented an enormous sum. These believers were willing to destroy something very valuable because they recognized it was incompatible with following Jesus.

[[v20|So the word of the Lord was growing and becoming mighty]]. The result was that the gospel spread even more powerfully. Genuine repentance strengthened the church's witness.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that true faith leads to repentance from sin, including secret practices. These believers had believed in Jesus but had not fully left their old ways. When they saw the holiness of Jesus' name, they brought their hidden practices into the light. John writes, [[1 John 1:9|If we confess our sins, he is faithful and righteous to forgive us the sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness]].

They also show that following Jesus may involve costly decisions. The believers burned books worth a fortune. Jesus taught that following Him means being willing to give up anything that stands in the way (Mark 9:43–47). Some things must be destroyed, not merely set aside.

Finally, these verses show that repentance strengthens the church's witness. When believers turned decisively from sin, the word of the Lord grew mightily. A church that takes holiness seriously is a powerful testimony to the world.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to bring hidden sins into the light. Many believers have areas of their lives they keep secret, such as habits, practices, or attitudes that do not honor God. Confession to God, and where appropriate to trusted believers, brings freedom and healing (James 5:16).

They also challenge believers to make clean breaks with sin. It is not enough to reduce or hide sinful practices. Believers may need to remove things from their lives completely, such as certain media, habits, relationships, or possessions that draw them away from God. This may be costly, but freedom is worth it.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to take spiritual matters seriously. Involvement in the occult, such as fortune-telling, spells, or séances, is incompatible with following Jesus (Deuteronomy 18:10–12). Believers who have been involved in such practices can renounce them and trust in the power of Christ.

### Acts 19:26–27 — When the Gospel Threatens Profits

verse: Acts 19:26-27

#### Simple Explanation

Paul's ministry in Ephesus had been so successful that it began to affect the city's economy. A silversmith named Demetrius made silver models of the temple of Artemis, which he sold to worshipers and pilgrims. His business, and the business of many other craftsmen, depended on the worship of Artemis.

Demetrius called a meeting of the craftsmen and said, [[v25|Sirs, you know that by this business we have our wealth]]. He went straight to the issue: money.

[[v26|You see and hear that not at Ephesus alone, but almost throughout all Asia, this Paul has persuaded and turned away many people, saying that they are no gods that are made with hands]]. Paul had taught that idols made by human hands are not gods. As people turned to the living God, they stopped buying silver shrines. Demetrius's words are a remarkable testimony to the spread of the gospel throughout the region.

[[v27|Not only is there danger that this our trade come into disrepute, but also that the temple of the great goddess Artemis will be counted as nothing and her majesty destroyed, whom all Asia and the world worships]]. Demetrius combined economic concerns with religious and civic pride. He presented the threat to his business as a threat to the goddess and to the city's honor.

His speech worked. The craftsmen were enraged and shouted, "Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!" Soon the whole city was in an uproar.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that the gospel can threaten economic systems built on sin or idolatry. When people turn to Christ, their spending, priorities, and values change. Businesses that profit from things contrary to God's ways may lose income. This happened in Philippi too, where the owners of the slave girl lost their profits (16:19).

They also show how financial interests can disguise themselves as religious or civic concerns. Demetrius's real concern was money, but he framed it as devotion to Artemis and pride in the city. People often justify opposing what is right by appealing to tradition, identity, or patriotism when their real motive is self-interest.

Finally, these verses show that the gospel calls into question every idol. Paul taught that gods made with hands are not gods at all. This message challenged not only religious practices but the whole way of life built around them. Paul later wrote that believers had [[1 Thessalonians 1:9|turned to God from idols, to serve a living and true God]].

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses invite believers to consider how their faith shapes their economic lives. Following Jesus may mean changing how believers earn, spend, and invest money, avoiding businesses or practices that profit from harm to others.

They also encourage believers to recognize when opposition to the gospel is driven by self-interest. Sometimes people oppose Christian values because those values threaten their profits or comfort. Believers can respond with wisdom and integrity, continuing to do what is right.

Finally, these verses challenge believers to identify the idols of their own culture. Modern idols may not be silver shrines, but they can include wealth, success, pleasure, or status. The gospel calls believers to turn from every idol to serve the living and true God.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Acts 19 shows the power of the gospel in a city full of idolatry and magic. Through Paul's patient teaching over two years, the word of the Lord spread throughout the province of Asia. Believers today can trust that faithful, consistent teaching and witness bear fruit, even in places that seem resistant.

The chapter also shows that the name of Jesus is not a magic formula. The sons of Sceva tried to use Jesus' name without knowing Him, and they were overpowered. Believers are called to know Jesus personally, not merely to know about Him. Their authority comes from belonging to Him.

Acts 19 calls believers to genuine repentance. When believers in Ephesus saw the holiness of Jesus' name, they confessed their practices and burned their costly books of magic. Believers today can bring hidden sins into the light, make clean breaks with anything that draws them away from God, and experience the freedom that comes through confession and forgiveness.

Finally, the chapter shows that the gospel can challenge economic and cultural systems. Demetrius saw his profits threatened as people turned from idols to the living God. Believers can let their faith shape how they use money and how they engage with their culture, recognizing and turning from the idols of their own time, while trusting that God's word will continue to grow and prevail.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You that John the Baptist pointed to You, and that all true faith leads to You. Thank You for the gift of Your Holy Spirit. Help me to know You personally, not only to know about You, and keep me from treating Your name or Your ways as a formula. Your name is holy and powerful. Bring into the light any hidden sin in my life, and give me courage to confess it and to make a clean break, whatever it costs. Show me the idols of my own heart and culture, and help me to turn from them to serve You, the living and true God. Let my use of money and possessions reflect my faith in You. Let Your word grow and become mighty in my life, my church, and my city. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
