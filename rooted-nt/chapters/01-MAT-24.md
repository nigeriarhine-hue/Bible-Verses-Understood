# Matthew 24
theme: Watchful and Faithful Until the Son of Man Comes
main: As Jesus leaves the temple, He predicts its destruction, and His disciples ask when this will happen and what will signal His coming and the end of the age. Jesus warns them not to be deceived, prepares them for wars, persecution, and the coming devastation of Jerusalem, and describes His glorious return. Because no one knows the day or hour, His followers must stay alert and remain faithful in their responsibilities until He comes.

## Chapter Background and Story

Matthew 24–25 make up the fifth and final major teaching section in Matthew, often called the Olivet Discourse because Jesus delivered it on the Mount of Olives. It follows directly from chapter 23, where Jesus ended His public ministry by lamenting over Jerusalem and declaring, [[Matthew 23:38|Behold, your house is left to you desolate]]. Now He leaves the temple for the last time.

This is one of the most debated chapters in the Bible, and readers should approach it with care and humility. Jesus' words address two connected events: the destruction of the Jerusalem temple, which happened in A.D. 70 when Roman armies under Titus besieged and burned the city, and His own return at the end of the age. Christians interpret the relationship between these two events in different ways. Some (often called preterists) believe that most of the chapter, through verse 34, was fulfilled in the events of A.D. 70. Others (futurists) believe that much of it describes events still to come before Christ's return. Many interpreters hold a combined view: that Jesus' words had a first fulfillment in A.D. 70 and point forward to a final fulfillment at the end of history. Whatever view one takes, the chapter's main practical teaching is clear: do not be deceived, endure faithfully, and be ready.

### The Prediction and the Question (24:1–3)

As Jesus left the temple, His disciples pointed out its buildings. Herod the Great had begun a massive renovation of the temple around 20 B.C., and work was still continuing. The temple complex was one of the wonders of the ancient world, built with enormous white stones, some over forty feet long, and decorated with gold. The Jewish historian Josephus wrote that from a distance it looked like a mountain covered with snow.

Jesus answered, [[v2|there will not be left here one stone on another, that will not be thrown down]]. This was a shocking prediction. About forty years later, the Romans destroyed the temple so completely that only parts of its retaining walls remained, including the section known today as the Western Wall.

Later, as Jesus sat on the Mount of Olives overlooking the temple, the disciples asked privately, [[v3|Tell us, when will these things be? What is the sign of your coming, and of the end of the age?]] They may have assumed that the destruction of the temple and the end of the world would happen together. Jesus' answer addresses both.

### Do Not Be Deceived (24:4–14)

Jesus began with a warning: [[v4|Be careful that no one leads you astray]]. Many would come claiming to be the Messiah and would deceive many. They would hear of wars and rumors of wars, but these should not alarm them; such things must happen, but [[v6|the end is not yet]]. Nations would fight, and there would be famines and earthquakes. These were only [[v8|the beginning of birth pains]]. In Jewish thought, the "birth pains of the Messiah" were troubles expected before the arrival of the new age. Jesus' point was that disasters are not signs that the end has immediately arrived; they are part of the long, painful process leading to it.

His followers would be persecuted, hated by all nations because of His name, and some would be killed. Many would fall away, betray one another, and hate each other. False prophets would mislead many. Because lawlessness would increase, [[v12|the love of many will grow cold]]. But [[v13|he who endures to the end will be saved]]. Meanwhile, the gospel of the kingdom would be preached throughout the whole world as a testimony to all nations, and then the end would come.

### The Abomination of Desolation and the Great Distress (24:15–28)

Jesus then spoke of [[v15|the abomination of desolation]], a phrase from Daniel (Daniel 9:27; 11:31; 12:11). In Daniel, it referred to a desecration of the temple. Many Jews saw an early fulfillment in 167 B.C., when the Greek king Antiochus IV Epiphanes set up an altar to Zeus in the temple and sacrificed a pig on it. Jesus said that something similar would happen again. Luke's parallel says, [[Luke 21:20|But when you see Jerusalem surrounded by armies, then know that its desolation is at hand]], which suggests that the Roman siege of Jerusalem was in view. The phrase "let the reader understand" calls readers to pay attention.

When this happened, those in Judea should flee to the mountains immediately. Someone on a housetop (flat roofs were used as living space) should not go inside to get possessions; someone in the field should not go back for a cloak. Jesus expressed special compassion for pregnant women and nursing mothers, for whom flight would be especially difficult, and told His followers to pray that their flight would not be in winter, when travel was harder, or on a Sabbath, when traveling was restricted and gates might be closed. According to the early church historian Eusebius, Christians in Jerusalem heeded such a warning and fled to Pella, east of the Jordan, before the city's destruction.

Jesus described [[v21|great suffering, such as has not been from the beginning of the world until now]]. Josephus records the horrors of the siege of Jerusalem, including starvation, internal violence, and the deaths of hundreds of thousands. Many interpreters believe Jesus' words also point beyond A.D. 70 to a final time of tribulation before His return. For the sake of the chosen, those days would be shortened.

He warned again about false messiahs and false prophets who would perform great signs to deceive even the chosen, if possible. If anyone said the Messiah was in the wilderness or in an inner room, they should not believe it. His coming would not be secret: [[v27|For as the lightning flashes from the east, and is seen even to the west, so will the coming of the Son of Man be]]. It would be unmistakable and visible to all.

### The Coming of the Son of Man (24:29–31)

[[v29|But immediately after the suffering of those days]], the sun would be darkened, the moon would not give light, the stars would fall, and the powers of heaven would be shaken. This language comes from Old Testament prophecies of God's judgment (Isaiah 13:10; 34:4; Joel 2:10). Some interpreters understand it as symbolic language for the collapse of earthly powers; others take it as describing literal cosmic events at the end of history.

Then [[v30|the sign of the Son of Man will appear in the sky]], and all the peoples of the earth would mourn. They would see the Son of Man coming on the clouds with power and great glory, language from Daniel 7:13–14. He would send His angels with a loud trumpet call to gather His chosen ones from the four winds. This describes Jesus' return in glory to gather His people.

### The Lesson of the Fig Tree (24:32–35)

Jesus told His disciples to learn from the fig tree. When its branches become tender and its leaves appear, summer is near. In the same way, when they saw "all these things," they would know that He was near, right at the door. He added, [[v34|this generation will not pass away until all these things are accomplished]]. This verse is part of the debate about the chapter. Those who see much of it as fulfilled in A.D. 70 note that this happened within a generation of Jesus' words. Others suggest "generation" means the kind of people or the generation that sees the final signs. Jesus then affirmed, [[v35|Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will not pass away]].

### No One Knows the Day or Hour (24:36–51)

Jesus then emphasized that the exact time of His coming is unknown: [[v36|But no one knows of that day and hour, not even the angels of heaven, but my Father only]]. This is studied below.

He compared His coming to the days of Noah. People were going about ordinary life, eating, drinking, and marrying, until the flood came and swept them away. They did not expect it. So it will be when the Son of Man comes. Two men will be in the field; one will be taken and one left. Two women will be grinding grain; one will be taken and one left. These images show that His coming will divide people suddenly. (Interpreters differ on whether "taken" means taken in judgment, as the flood "took them all away," or taken to be with Christ.)

Therefore, Jesus said, keep watch. If a homeowner knew when a thief was coming, he would stay awake. Since no one knows when Christ will come, His followers must always be ready.

Finally, Jesus told a parable about a servant put in charge of a household. If the master returns and finds him faithfully caring for the household, the master will reward him. But if the servant thinks, "My master is delayed," and begins to abuse his fellow servants and live carelessly, the master will come unexpectedly and punish him severely. The final words describe the fate of hypocrites.

## Important Verses Explained

### Matthew 24:4 — Do Not Be Led Astray

verse: Matthew 24:4

#### Simple Explanation

These are Jesus' first words in response to His disciples' question about when the temple would be destroyed and what would be the sign of His coming and the end of the age. The disciples wanted information about timing and signs. Jesus began not with a timetable but with a warning: [[v4|Be careful that no one leads you astray]].

To be "led astray" means to be deceived, misled, or caused to wander from the truth. Jesus immediately explained the danger: [[v5|For many will come in my name, saying, ‘I am the Christ,’ and will lead many astray]]. In the decades after Jesus' ascension, several men did claim to be messiahs or prophets and led followers into failed revolts against Rome. The book of Acts mentions Theudas and Judas the Galilean (Acts 5:36–37) and an Egyptian who led thousands into the wilderness (Acts 21:38).

Jesus repeated this warning throughout the chapter. False prophets would arise and deceive many (v. 11). False messiahs would perform great signs (v. 24). People would claim that Christ was in the wilderness or in secret rooms (v. 26). Deception is the danger Jesus mentions most often in this discourse.

He also warned against misinterpreting world events. Wars, famines, and earthquakes would happen, but they would not mean the end had come. People in every generation have been tempted to see current events as proof that the end is immediately at hand. Jesus told His disciples not to be alarmed or misled by such interpretations.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that one of the greatest threats to faith is deception. Persecution can harm the body, but deception can lead people away from Christ. Paul similarly warned about those who would come in with false teaching (Acts 20:29–30), and John wrote that many antichrists had already appeared (1 John 2:18).

The verse also shows that Jesus cared more about His disciples' faithfulness than about satisfying their curiosity. He did give information about future events, but His main concern was that they would remain faithful and not be misled. The purpose of biblical prophecy is not to provide material for speculation but to prepare God's people to live faithfully.

Finally, Jesus' warning reveals that deception often comes "in my name." False teachers and false messiahs use Jesus' name and Christian language. This means believers need discernment, testing teaching and claims by Scripture and by the character of Christ.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse calls believers to be discerning. Many voices claim to speak for God, offer new revelations, predict the date of Christ's return, or promise special knowledge. Believers can test such claims by comparing them with Scripture and with the teaching of Jesus and the apostles. Any teaching that contradicts the clear message of the Bible, or that sets dates for Christ's return, should be rejected.

It also calls believers not to be alarmed by world events. Wars, disasters, and crises are painful and real, and believers should respond to them with compassion and prayer. But they should not let fear or speculation take over. Jesus said, [[v6|See that you aren’t troubled]].

Finally, the best protection against deception is knowing Jesus and His Word well. Regular reading of Scripture, participation in a faithful church, and humility before God help believers recognize what is true and resist what is false.

### Matthew 24:36 — No One Knows the Day or Hour

verse: Matthew 24:36

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus speaks these words after describing His glorious return and telling His disciples to learn from the fig tree. He has spoken of signs and events that would precede the end. Now He makes clear that, despite these signs, the exact time of His coming cannot be known.

[[v36|But no one knows of that day and hour]]. "That day" refers to the day of His return. Jesus says plainly that no human being knows when it will be. Then He adds, [[v36|not even the angels of heaven]]. Even the angels, who stand in God's presence, do not know. Mark's Gospel also adds [[Mark 13:32|nor the Son]], and many manuscripts of Matthew include those words too. Christians have understood this to mean that during His earthly life, in His humanity, Jesus voluntarily limited His knowledge, so that this particular knowledge belonged to the Father alone. [[v36|but my Father only]].

Jesus followed this statement with the example of Noah. People before the flood were living normal lives, eating, drinking, and marrying, with no awareness of the coming judgment until it arrived. Christ's return will come in the same unexpected way.

This verse would have been surprising to disciples who wanted a timeline. Jesus did not give one. Instead, He told them to be ready at all times.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse teaches that the timing of Christ's return belongs to God alone. Throughout history, many people have tried to calculate the date of Jesus' return, often causing confusion, disappointment, and damage to the church's witness. Jesus' clear words rule out such date-setting. Before His ascension, Jesus told His disciples again, [[Acts 1:7|It isn’t for you to know times or seasons which the Father has set within his own authority]].

The verse also reveals the purpose of not knowing. If people knew the exact day, they might live carelessly until shortly before it. Because the time is unknown, believers are called to live faithfully every day. The uncertainty of the timing is meant to produce constant readiness, not anxiety.

Finally, this verse points to the trust believers can place in the Father's wisdom. God knows the right time. His timing is perfect, and believers can rest in His knowledge rather than trying to know what He has not revealed.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse calls believers to reject speculation and date-setting about Christ's return. When people claim to know when Jesus will come back, believers can respond with Jesus' clear words: no one knows. This protects them from being misled or discouraged.

It also calls believers to live in readiness. Since Christ could return at any time, believers can live each day as if it might be the day: faithful in their responsibilities, at peace with God, reconciled with others, and actively serving. This is not a call to fear but to faithfulness.

Finally, this verse encourages believers to trust God with the future. Many people feel anxious about what is coming. Believers can rest in knowing that the Father holds the timing in His hands. They do not need to know when Christ will come; they need to know Him and be found faithful.

### Matthew 24:45–46 — The Faithful Servant

verse: Matthew 24:45-46

#### Simple Explanation

These verses begin a short parable that concludes Jesus' teaching in this chapter. He has just told His disciples to stay alert because they do not know when He will come, and He compared His coming to a thief arriving unexpectedly. Now He describes what readiness looks like.

[[v45|Who then is the faithful and wise servant, whom his lord has set over his household, to give them their food in due season?]] In wealthy households of that time, a trusted servant or slave was often put in charge of the household's affairs while the master was away, including distributing food to the other servants. This was a position of responsibility and trust.

The servant is described as "faithful," meaning reliable and trustworthy, and "wise," meaning sensible and prudent. His wisdom shows in his understanding that the master will return and that his responsibility is to care for others in the meantime.

[[v46|Blessed is that servant whom his lord finds doing so when he comes]]. The servant is blessed because the master finds him carrying out his responsibilities. He is not idle, not careless, and not taking advantage of the master's absence. He is faithfully doing his job. The next verse says the master will put him in charge of all his possessions, a great reward.

In contrast, Jesus describes a wicked servant who thinks, "My master is staying away a long time," and begins to beat his fellow servants and eat and drink with drunkards. The master will return unexpectedly and punish him severely.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This parable shows that readiness for Christ's return is not about calculating dates or withdrawing from ordinary life. It is about faithfully doing what Christ has entrusted to us. Being ready means being found at work: caring for others, serving well, and living with integrity.

The parable also highlights how believers treat other people. The faithful servant feeds the household; the wicked servant abuses his fellow servants. The way believers treat others, especially those under their care, reveals whether they are truly ready for the Lord's return. This anticipates the parable of the sheep and the goats in Matthew 25, where the Son of Man judges people according to how they treated the least of His brothers and sisters.

Finally, the parable warns about the danger of delay. The wicked servant reasoned that the master was delayed and used that as an excuse for careless living. Believers can be tempted to think that because Christ has not yet returned, it does not matter how they live. Jesus warns that such thinking is dangerous.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses invite believers to consider what Christ has entrusted to them. This might include responsibilities in their families, workplaces, churches, and communities. Faithfulness means carrying out these responsibilities well: caring for children, working honestly, serving others, and using their gifts for God's purposes.

They also call believers, especially those in positions of authority, to treat others with care. Parents, employers, church leaders, teachers, and anyone with influence over others are like the servant in charge of the household. They are called to provide for and protect those under their care, never to abuse them.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to live with a long-term perspective. Christ's return may seem delayed, but believers can remain faithful day after day, year after year. The reward for faithfulness is great, and the Master will surely come.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Matthew 24 deals with the future, but its purpose is to shape how believers live in the present. Jesus did not give His disciples a detailed timetable; He prepared them to remain faithful in a world marked by deception, conflict, persecution, and uncertainty.

The first application is discernment. Jesus warned repeatedly about false messiahs and false prophets. Believers today encounter many voices claiming to speak for God, offering new revelations, or predicting the end. They can test these claims by Scripture, refuse to follow anyone who sets dates for Christ's return, and hold firmly to the teaching of Jesus.

The second is endurance. Jesus warned that His followers would face hatred, persecution, betrayal, and the temptation to let their love grow cold. Believers in many parts of the world experience this directly. Others face subtler pressures. In every setting, Jesus calls His followers to endure to the end, keeping their love warm and their faith firm, while continuing to share the gospel with all nations.

The third is calm trust. Wars, disasters, and crises are real, but Jesus told His followers not to be alarmed. They can respond to world events with compassion and prayer without being controlled by fear or speculation, trusting that the Father holds the timing of history in His hands.

The fourth is readiness through faithfulness. Because no one knows the day or hour, believers are called to live faithfully every day. This means doing what Christ has entrusted to them, caring for those in their charge, and refusing to live carelessly because His return seems delayed. The best way to be ready for Christ's coming is to be found faithfully serving Him when He comes.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for warning Your followers about the dangers ahead and for promising that You will return in power and glory. Guard me from deception. Give me discernment to recognize false teaching, and keep me anchored in Your Word, which will never pass away.

When I hear of wars, disasters, and troubles, keep me from fear and give me compassion and trust. Strengthen me to endure to the end and keep my love from growing cold. Thank You that the Father knows the day and hour of Your coming. Help me to trust Him with the future and to live each day ready to meet You. Make me a faithful and wise servant, caring well for the people and responsibilities You have given me, so that when You come, You will find me doing Your will. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
