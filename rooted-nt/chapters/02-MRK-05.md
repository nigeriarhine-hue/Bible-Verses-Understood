# Mark 5
theme: Restored, Healed, and Raised: Jesus and the Desperate
main: Jesus meets three people in situations no one else could change: a man tormented by many demons living among the tombs, a woman who has suffered from bleeding for twelve years, and a synagogue leader whose daughter dies before Jesus arrives. He frees the man and sends him home to tell what God has done, calls the trembling woman "daughter" and declares her healed, and raises the girl from death with a tender command. Jesus' power reaches into places of uncleanness, shame, and death.

## Chapter Background and Story

Mark 5 follows directly from the end of Mark 4, where Jesus calmed a violent storm on the Sea of Galilee and the disciples asked, [[Mark 4:41|Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?]] Mark 5 continues to answer that question. Having shown Jesus' authority over nature, Mark now shows His authority over demonic powers, chronic disease, and death itself.

All three stories in this chapter involve people who were considered ritually unclean under the law of Moses. The man lived among tombs, in Gentile territory, near pigs, and was possessed by unclean spirits. The woman had a bleeding condition that made her unclean according to Leviticus 15:25–27. The girl died, and touching a corpse made a person unclean (Numbers 19:11). In each case, Jesus does not become unclean by contact; instead, His holiness and power restore what was broken.

### The Man With a Legion of Demons (5:1–20)

Jesus and His disciples crossed to the eastern shore of the lake, into the region of the Gadarenes (the manuscripts vary between Gerasenes, Gadarenes, and Gergesenes, names connected with towns in the area). This was the Decapolis, a league of largely Gentile cities with Greek culture. As soon as Jesus stepped out of the boat, a man with an unclean spirit came out of the tombs to meet Him.

Mark describes the man's condition in painful detail. He lived among the burial caves. People had tried to restrain him with chains and shackles, but he tore the chains apart and broke the shackles. No one was strong enough to subdue him. Night and day, among the tombs and in the hills, he cried out and cut himself with stones. He was isolated, tormented, self-destructive, and feared by everyone. (The Gospels distinguish demonic oppression from ordinary illness; readers today should not assume that mental illness or self-harm is always caused by demons. Mark presents this specific case as spiritual bondage, and he shows Jesus' compassion for a man everyone else had given up on.)

When the man saw Jesus from a distance, he ran and fell on his knees before Him. The demon shouted, [[v7|What have I to do with you, Jesus, you Son of the Most High God? I adjure you by God, don’t torment me]]. "Most High God" was a title often used by Gentiles for the God of Israel. Ironically, the demon tried to use a solemn oath to control Jesus, but it had no power over Him. Jesus had already commanded the spirit to come out.

Jesus asked its name. The answer was, [[v9|My name is Legion, for we are many]]. A Roman legion contained about six thousand soldiers. The name suggested a vast number of spirits and evoked the image of an occupying army, a powerful picture for people living under Roman rule. The demons begged Jesus not to send them out of the area. A large herd of about two thousand pigs was feeding on the nearby hillside. The demons begged to be sent into the pigs. Jesus gave them permission, and the whole herd rushed down the steep bank into the lake and drowned.

Readers sometimes wonder why Jesus allowed the destruction of the pigs. The text does not explain fully, but it shows the destructive nature of the demons and the reality of the man's deliverance. The pigs' fate made visible what the demons intended for the man. It also shows that one human being was worth more to Jesus than a large herd of animals.

The herdsmen ran and reported what had happened in the town and countryside. People came out and found the man [[v15|sitting, clothed, and in his right mind]]. This description is a complete reversal of his former condition: instead of wandering, he was sitting; instead of naked (Luke 8:27), he was clothed; instead of tormented, he was sane. Yet the people were afraid. When they heard what had happened, including the loss of the pigs, they begged Jesus to leave their region. They valued their economic security and their comfort over the presence of the One who had freed a tormented man.

As Jesus was getting into the boat, the man who had been freed begged to go with Him. Jesus did not allow it but gave him a different mission, studied below. The man went away and began to proclaim in the Decapolis how much Jesus had done for him, and everyone was amazed.

### Jairus and the Woman (5:21–43)

Jesus crossed back to the western shore, and a large crowd gathered. One of the synagogue leaders, named Jairus, came and fell at His feet. A synagogue leader was a respected layman responsible for organizing worship services. Jairus pleaded earnestly, [[v23|My little daughter is at the point of death. Please come and lay your hands on her, that she may be made healthy, and live]]. Jesus went with him, and the crowd pressed in around Him.

Mark now uses his "sandwich" technique, interrupting one story with another so that each helps interpret the other. In the crowd was a woman who had suffered from bleeding for twelve years, the same number of years Jairus's daughter had been alive. Mark describes her suffering vividly: she [[v26|had suffered many things by many physicians, and had spent all that she had, and was no better, but rather grew worse]]. Her condition made her ritually unclean, so she would have been excluded from worship and close contact with others. Anyone she touched would become unclean. She had lost her health, her money, her community, and likely her hope.

She had heard about Jesus. She came up behind Him in the crowd and touched His clothes, thinking, [[v28|If I just touch his clothes, I will be made well]]. Immediately her bleeding stopped, and she felt in her body that she was healed.

Jesus immediately realized that power had gone out from Him. He turned around and asked, [[v30|Who touched my clothes?]] The disciples thought the question was absurd, since the crowd was pressing in on Him from every side. But Jesus kept looking around to see who had done it. The woman, trembling with fear, came and fell at His feet and told Him the whole truth. She may have feared rebuke for touching Him while unclean. Instead, Jesus spoke words of blessing, studied below.

While Jesus was still speaking, messengers arrived from Jairus's house: [[v35|Your daughter is dead. Why bother the Teacher any more?]] The delay caused by the woman seemed to have cost the girl her life. But Jesus overheard and told Jairus, [[v36|Don’t be afraid, only believe]]. This is studied below.

Jesus allowed only Peter, James, and John to follow Him. At the house, there was a commotion of people weeping and wailing loudly. Professional mourners were common in that culture, and even poor families hired flute players and wailing women. Jesus said, [[v39|Why do you make an uproar and weep? The child is not dead, but is asleep]]. They laughed at Him, because they knew she was dead. Jesus was not denying that she had died; He was saying that in His presence, death is like sleep, from which He can awaken her.

He put them all outside and took only the girl's parents and His three disciples into the room. He took her by the hand and said, [[v41|Talitha cumi!]] Mark preserves the Aramaic words Jesus actually spoke, likely as Peter remembered them, and translates them: [[v41|Girl, I tell you, get up!]] "Talitha" was a tender word, like "little girl" or "little lamb." Immediately she stood up and began to walk. Mark adds, [[v42|for she was twelve years old]]. Everyone was completely astonished.

Jesus gave strict orders that no one should know about this, and He told them to give her something to eat. This practical instruction shows His tender care, and it also confirms that she was truly alive and well.

## Important Verses Explained

### Mark 5:19 — Go Home and Tell What the Lord Has Done

verse: Mark 5:19

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus speaks these words to the man He had just freed from a legion of demons. Only hours earlier, this man had been living among the tombs, screaming day and night, cutting himself with stones, and breaking every chain people used to restrain him. Now he was sitting quietly, clothed, and in his right mind. The people of the region, frightened by what had happened and upset about the loss of their pigs, had begged Jesus to leave.

As Jesus was getting into the boat, the man begged to go with Him. This was a natural desire. Jesus had rescued him, and the people of his town were afraid. He wanted to stay with the One who had set him free and become one of His followers.

[[v19|He didn’t allow him]]. This is surprising. Jesus often called people to follow Him. But for this man, Jesus had a different calling: [[v19|Go to your house, to your friends, and tell them what great things the Lord has done for you and how he had mercy on you]].

Jesus sent him back to the very people who had known him in his tormented state. His family and friends had probably given up on him. Now they would see the change in his life and hear his story. Jesus told him to tell them what "the Lord" had done, pointing to God. Notice also that Jesus had told others to keep quiet about His miracles, but He told this man to speak. In this largely Gentile region, there was less danger of people misunderstanding Jesus as a political messiah, and the man's testimony would prepare the way for Jesus' later ministry there (Mark 7:31).

Verse 20 tells us the man obeyed. He went throughout the Decapolis, a region of ten cities, proclaiming how much Jesus had done for him, and everyone was amazed. Mark notes that he spoke of what "Jesus" had done, showing that he understood that God's mercy had come through Jesus.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that every believer has a story of God's mercy to tell. The man did not have theological training. He had not been with Jesus long. But he had experienced Jesus' power and mercy, and that was enough to make him a witness. Personal testimony, describing what God has done, is one of the most powerful ways the gospel spreads.

It also shows that Jesus' calling looks different for different people. Some are called to leave their homes and travel with Him. Others are called to stay and witness among the people who know them best. Both callings are valuable. Faithfulness means accepting the calling Jesus gives, even if it differs from what we would choose.

Finally, the verse highlights mercy. The man's freedom was not something he earned; it was mercy. Testimony about God is ultimately testimony about His mercy toward people who could not help themselves.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to tell others what God has done for them. They do not need to have all the answers or be skilled speakers. They can share honestly how Jesus has shown them mercy, forgiven them, changed them, or helped them through difficulty. Personal stories often open doors that arguments cannot.

It also reminds believers that home is often the first mission field. Family members, friends, neighbors, and coworkers are the people who know us best. They can see the difference Christ makes in our lives over time. Living faithfully among them and sharing our story with them is an important part of following Jesus.

Finally, this verse teaches believers to accept God's calling even when it differs from their desires. Some may wish for a dramatic ministry elsewhere, but God may call them to serve faithfully where they are. Obedience in the place God puts us is a true form of discipleship.

### Mark 5:34 — [[Mark 5:34|Daughter, Your Faith Has Made You Well]]

verse: Mark 5:34

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus speaks these words to the woman who had suffered from bleeding for twelve years. She had spent all her money on doctors and only grown worse. Under the law of Moses, her condition made her ritually unclean (Leviticus 15:25–27), which meant she could not participate fully in worship, and anyone she touched would become unclean. For twelve years she had lived with physical weakness, poverty, and social isolation.

She came up behind Jesus in the crowd, believing that if she could just touch His clothes, she would be healed. When she did, her bleeding stopped immediately. Jesus felt that power had gone out from Him and asked who had touched Him. Although the disciples thought the question was strange, Jesus kept looking for her. The woman came forward, trembling with fear, fell at His feet, and told Him the whole truth.

Jesus then said, [[v34|Daughter, your faith has made you well. Go in peace, and be cured of your disease]]. Every part of this response is significant.

"Daughter" is the only time in the Gospels that Jesus addresses a woman this way. For someone who had been isolated for years, this word welcomed her into a relationship of belonging and affection.

[[v34|your faith has made you well]]. The word translated "made well" can also mean "saved." Jesus credits her faith, not the touch itself, as the means of her healing. Her faith had reached out to Him, and His power had responded. Calling attention to her faith publicly also restored her reputation. Everyone now knew she was healed and could be welcomed back into community life.

[[v34|Go in peace]]. This was a common Jewish blessing ("Go in shalom"), wishing someone wholeness and well-being.

[[v34|and be cured of your disease]]. Jesus confirmed that her healing was complete and lasting.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that Jesus cares about the whole person: body, relationships, and soul. He could have let the woman slip away healed but unnoticed. Instead, He sought her out to give her more than physical healing. He gave her a public declaration of restoration, a new identity as a daughter, and a blessing of peace.

It also shows the nature of faith. The woman's faith was imperfect; she may have thought of Jesus' clothes almost as having magical power. Yet Jesus honored her faith because it was directed toward Him. Faith does not need to be perfectly informed to be real. What matters is that it reaches out to Jesus.

Finally, Jesus' encounter with an "unclean" woman shows that His holiness is contagious in a new way. Instead of becoming unclean by her touch, He made her clean. This anticipates the deeper cleansing He brings through His death, removing the barriers of sin and shame that separate people from God.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse offers hope to anyone who has suffered for a long time or exhausted every other option. Like the woman, they can bring their need to Jesus. He sees them, cares about their suffering, and responds to faith that reaches out to Him.

It also encourages those who feel ashamed or excluded. Jesus did not shame the woman; He called her "daughter." Believers who carry shame because of illness, past sin, or circumstances can find in Jesus a welcome that restores dignity and belonging.

Finally, this verse invites believers to see people as Jesus does. In a crowded world, it is easy to overlook those who are suffering quietly. Jesus stopped for one hidden, ashamed woman. Believers can slow down, notice people others ignore, and offer them dignity, kindness, and a place to belong.

### Mark 5:36 — [[Mark 5:36|Don't Be Afraid, Only Believe]]

verse: Mark 5:36

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus speaks these words to Jairus, a synagogue leader whose twelve-year-old daughter had been dying. Jairus had come to Jesus, fallen at His feet, and begged Him to come and lay His hands on her so she would live. Jesus agreed and went with him. But on the way, the crowd slowed them down, and Jesus stopped to speak with the woman who had been healed of bleeding.

While Jesus was still speaking to her, messengers came from Jairus's house with terrible news: [[v35|Your daughter is dead. Why bother the Teacher any more?]] From their point of view, it was over. Jesus could heal the sick, but there was nothing to be done for the dead.

Jesus heard what they said and immediately spoke to Jairus: [[v36|Don’t be afraid, only believe]]. The Greek suggests "stop being afraid" and "keep on believing." Jairus had already shown faith by coming to Jesus. Now Jesus called him to continue trusting even in the face of death.

Imagine Jairus's emotions. He had just watched Jesus stop for someone else while his own daughter was dying. Now he heard that she was dead. He may have felt despair, grief, perhaps even anger. Jesus did not ask him to stop grieving or to pretend the news was false. He asked him to keep believing in Jesus' power, even when all hope seemed gone.

Jesus went with Jairus to his house. When the mourners laughed at Jesus' statement that the child was only sleeping, He put them out, took the parents and three disciples into the room, took the girl's hand, and raised her to life.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that faith in Jesus can persist even when circumstances seem hopeless. Death seemed to have the final word, but Jesus had power over death. This scene anticipates Jesus' own resurrection and His promise to raise all who belong to Him. For those who trust in Jesus, death is not the end.

The verse also shows the relationship between fear and faith. Fear is natural when facing devastating news. But Jesus calls His followers not to let fear rule them. The opposite of fear here is not courage based on circumstances, but trust in Jesus' power and character.

Finally, the delay in this story teaches something about God's timing. Jesus stopped to help the woman, and the delay seemed to cost the girl her life. But the delay led to an even greater miracle. Sometimes God's delays, which feel painful at the time, serve purposes we cannot see.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse speaks to believers facing frightening news, whether about health, family, finances, or the future. Jesus' words, "Don't be afraid, only believe," invite them to keep trusting Him even when the situation seems beyond hope. This does not mean denying grief or pretending everything is fine. It means holding onto Jesus in the midst of fear.

It also offers comfort to those who have lost loved ones. Jesus raised Jairus's daughter as a sign of His power over death. While not every loved one is restored to life in this world, believers can trust that those who die in Christ will be raised to eternal life. Grief can be held together with hope.

Finally, this verse encourages believers to trust God's timing when He seems to delay. When prayers seem unanswered or help seems late, believers can keep believing, trusting that Jesus is still at work and that His purposes are good.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Mark 5 shows Jesus meeting people at their lowest points: a man tormented beyond human help, a woman exhausted by years of illness and isolation, and a father whose child had died. In each case, Jesus brought restoration that no one else could provide. This chapter invites believers to bring their most desperate situations to Him, trusting that no condition is beyond His reach.

The chapter also shows how differently people respond to Jesus' power. The townspeople begged Him to leave because His presence disrupted their economy and frightened them. The freed man begged to go with Him. Jairus fell at His feet, and the woman reached out in faith. Believers can examine whether they welcome Jesus' work in their lives, even when it disrupts their comfort, or whether they try to keep Him at a distance.

Each person in this chapter received not only healing but restored relationships. The man was sent home to his family and community. The woman was called "daughter" and restored to her community. The girl was returned to her parents. Jesus cares about belonging as well as healing. Churches can reflect this by welcoming people who have been isolated, ashamed, or excluded, and by helping them find a place in God's family.

Finally, the chapter calls believers to persistent faith and bold witness. Jairus was told to keep believing even after hearing that his daughter had died. The freed man was told to go and tell what the Lord had done. Believers can trust Jesus in the face of fear and loss, and they can share their own stories of His mercy with the people who know them best.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You that no one is beyond the reach of Your power and compassion. You freed the man whom everyone feared, You healed the woman whom everyone avoided, and You raised the girl when everyone said it was too late. I bring You the situations in my life and in the lives of those I love that seem hopeless. Meet us there with Your power and mercy.

Keep me from asking You to leave when Your work disrupts my comfort. Thank You for calling me Your child and for removing my shame. When I hear frightening news, help me to hear Your voice saying, "Don't be afraid, only believe." Give me courage to tell my family and friends what great things You have done for me and how You have had mercy on me. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
