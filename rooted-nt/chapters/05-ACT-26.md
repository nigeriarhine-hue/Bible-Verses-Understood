# Acts 26
theme: From Darkness to Light: Paul's Testimony before King Agrippa
main: Before King Agrippa, Bernice, Festus, and the leaders of Caesarea, Paul tells his story once more. He explains that he stands on trial for Israel's hope in the resurrection, describes his former fury against the followers of Jesus, and recounts how the risen Lord confronted him and sent him to open the eyes of the nations, turning them from darkness to light and from Satan's power to God. Festus shouts that Paul is out of his mind, but Paul appeals to Agrippa's belief in the prophets. Agrippa asks whether Paul thinks he can make him a Christian so quickly, and Paul answers that he prays all his hearers would become as he is, except for his chains. The rulers agree that he has done nothing deserving death.

## Chapter Background and Story

Acts 26 contains Paul's final and fullest defense in Acts, and the third account of his conversion (see also Acts 9 and 22). This telling is shaped for a royal audience. King Agrippa II was knowledgeable about Jewish customs and the Scriptures, so Paul speaks to him as someone who could understand. Paul also emphasizes his commission to the Gentiles and the content of his message more fully than in the earlier accounts.

The scene fulfills Jesus' words that Paul would bear His name "before the nations and kings, and the children of Israel" (9:15), and that His followers would be brought before kings and governors for His name's sake, as a testimony to them (Luke 21:12–13). Although Paul is technically defending himself, his speech is really a presentation of the gospel. He ends not by asking for release, but by inviting the king to believe.

The events of the Damascus road are studied at Acts 9 and Acts 22. The studies below focus on what this telling adds.

### Paul's Introduction (26:1–3)

Agrippa gave Paul permission to speak. Paul [[v1|stretched out his hand]], a gesture used by orators to begin a speech. He said that he considered himself fortunate to make his defense before Agrippa, [[v3|especially because you are expert in all customs and questions which are among the Jews]]. He asked the king to listen patiently.

### Paul's Life as a Pharisee and the Hope of Israel (26:4–8)

Paul reminded his audience that his life had been well known from his youth in Jerusalem. He had lived as a Pharisee, [[v5|after the strictest sect of our religion]]. Now he was on trial [[v6|for the hope of the promise made by God to our fathers]], the hope that the twelve tribes served God day and night to attain. That hope was the coming of the Messiah and the resurrection of the dead. Paul's question about the resurrection is studied below.

### Paul's Former Hostility (26:9–11)

Paul confessed his past openly: [[v9|I myself most certainly thought that I ought to do many things contrary to the name of Jesus of Nazareth]]. He imprisoned many believers on the authority of the chief priests, and [[v10|when they were put to death I gave my vote against them]]. This may mean that Paul was a member of a council that voted for executions, or it may be a way of saying that he approved of them. He punished believers in synagogues and tried to force them to blaspheme, that is, to deny or curse Jesus. [[v11|Being exceedingly enraged against them, I persecuted them even to foreign cities]].

### The Heavenly Vision (26:12–18)

Paul again described the light on the road to Damascus, [[v13|brighter than the sun]], and the voice speaking in Hebrew (Aramaic). This account adds a phrase, studied below. Jesus' commission to Paul, recorded here more fully than in the other accounts, is also studied below.

### Paul's Obedience and Message (26:19–23)

[[v19|Therefore, King Agrippa, I was not disobedient to the heavenly vision]]. Paul had obeyed. He preached first in Damascus, then in Jerusalem, throughout Judea, and to the Gentiles, [[v20|that they should repent and turn to God, doing works worthy of repentance]]. Repentance is shown in a changed life. John the Baptist had taught the same: [[Luke 3:8|Therefore produce fruits worthy of repentance]].

For this reason, Paul said, the Jews had seized him in the temple and tried to kill him. But [[v22|Having therefore obtained the help that is from God, I stand to this day testifying both to small and great]]. Paul had spoken to the humble and the powerful alike, and now he was speaking to a king. His message was nothing new; it was [[v22|nothing but what the prophets and Moses said would happen]]: [[v23|how the Christ must suffer, and how, by the resurrection of the dead, he would be first to proclaim light both to these people and to the Gentiles]].

### Festus Interrupts (26:24–27)

At this point, Festus interrupted loudly: [[v24|Paul, you are crazy! Your great learning is driving you insane!]] To a Roman, the idea of a crucified Messiah who rose from the dead and brought light to the nations sounded like madness. Paul wrote that the message of the cross is [[1 Corinthians 1:23|foolishness to Greeks]].

Paul answered respectfully: [[v25|I am not crazy, most excellent Festus, but boldly declare words of truth and reasonableness]]. The gospel is not irrational; it is true and reasonable. Paul then turned to Agrippa: [[v26|For the king knows of these things, to whom also I speak freely. For I am persuaded that none of these things is hidden from him, for this has not been done in a corner]]. The events of Jesus' life, death, and resurrection, and the growth of the church, were public knowledge. They had not happened in secret. Christianity rests on historical events that could be investigated.

Then Paul asked the king directly, [[v27|King Agrippa, do you believe the prophets? I know that you believe]]. Agrippa's response, and Paul's reply, are studied below.

### The Verdict (26:30–32)

The king, the governor, Bernice, and those with them rose and withdrew. They agreed: [[v31|This man does nothing worthy of death or of bonds]]. Agrippa said to Festus, [[v32|This man might have been set free if he had not appealed to Caesar]]. Paul's innocence was confirmed again by the highest authorities. But because he had appealed to Caesar, he would go to Rome, exactly as Jesus had promised.

## Important Verses Explained

### Acts 26:8 — Why Is It Incredible That God Raises the Dead?

verse: Acts 26:8

#### Simple Explanation

Paul was making his defense before King Agrippa, the Roman governor Festus, and the leading people of Caesarea. He explained that he had lived as a strict Pharisee and that he was on trial because of the hope God had promised to Israel's ancestors, the hope that all twelve tribes longed to see fulfilled. That hope was the coming of the Messiah and the resurrection of the dead.

Then Paul asked a direct question: [[v8|Why is it judged incredible with you if God does raise the dead?]] The word "you" is plural; Paul was addressing the whole audience, perhaps especially the Gentiles who found the idea of resurrection hard to believe.

Paul's question went to the heart of the matter. Why should it seem impossible that God could raise the dead? If God created the universe and gave life to every living thing, why could He not give life again? The question is not whether resurrection is possible for humans, but whether it is possible for God.

Paul's audience included people with different views. Agrippa was Jewish and familiar with the hope of resurrection. The Romans generally did not believe in bodily resurrection; many thought the soul might live on, but the body was gone forever. Paul challenged them all to consider the power of God.

This question prepared the way for Paul's testimony. He had met Jesus, who had been raised from the dead. The resurrection was not a theory to him; it was the reason his whole life had changed.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that the resurrection is a matter of God's power. Jesus told the Sadducees, who denied the resurrection, [[Mark 12:24|Isn’t this because you are mistaken, not knowing the Scriptures nor the power of God?]] Those who believe in a Creator God have every reason to believe He can raise the dead.

It also shows that the resurrection is the fulfillment of Israel's hope. Paul did not present it as a new idea. God had promised it through the prophets (Isaiah 26:19; Daniel 12:2; Ezekiel 37). Jesus' resurrection was the first fruits of the resurrection of all God's people (1 Corinthians 15:20).

Finally, the verse invites honest reflection. Many people dismiss the resurrection as impossible without considering who God is. Paul's question challenges them to think again. If God exists, the resurrection is not incredible; it is exactly what we might expect from the Author of life.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to have confidence in the resurrection. In a world that often doubts the supernatural, believers can rest in the power of God, who raised Jesus and will raise His people.

It also gives believers a helpful question to ask others. When people say the resurrection is impossible, believers can gently ask Paul's question: Why is it incredible that God could raise the dead? This can open a conversation about who God is.

Finally, the verse offers hope to those facing death or grieving loved ones. The God who raised Jesus has the power to raise all who belong to Him. Death is not the end.

### Acts 26:14 — Kicking Against the Goads

verse: Acts 26:14

#### Simple Explanation

Paul was retelling the story of his conversion to King Agrippa. He described the blinding light on the road to Damascus at noon, brighter than the sun. He and his companions fell to the ground.

[[v14|I heard a voice saying to me in the Hebrew language, ‘Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me?]] Paul noted that the voice spoke in Hebrew (likely Aramaic), his native language. Jesus spoke to him personally.

Then Paul added something not recorded in the earlier accounts: [[v14|It is hard for you to kick against the goads.]] A "goad" was a sharp pointed stick used by farmers to drive oxen. If an ox kicked against the goad, it would only hurt itself. This was a common proverb in the Greek world, meaning that it is useless and painful to resist a power greater than oneself.

What did Jesus mean? He was telling Paul that by persecuting the church, Paul was fighting against God, and it was only hurting himself. Some interpreters also think the phrase suggests that God had been working on Paul's conscience before this moment. Perhaps Paul had been troubled by Stephen's death, by the courage of the believers he persecuted, or by the Scriptures that pointed to a suffering Messiah. He may have been resisting these "goads" as he raged against the church.

Paul had been "exceedingly enraged" against the believers (26:11). Sometimes, the fiercer a person's opposition, the more they are fighting against an inner conviction.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that resisting God is ultimately futile and painful. God is sovereign, and His purposes will prevail. Gamaliel had warned the council that if this movement was from God, they would find themselves fighting against God (5:39). Paul was doing exactly that, and it was harming him.

It also shows God's patient pursuit of those who resist Him. God was not merely punishing Paul; He was calling him. The goads were meant to direct him, not to destroy him. God pursues people even when they run from Him, as He pursued Jonah and as the father waited for the prodigal son.

Finally, the verse reveals the inner struggle that can lie behind hostility to faith. Paul's violent persecution may have been partly a way of silencing his own doubts. People who oppose the gospel most fiercely may sometimes be closest to the truth.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites everyone to consider whether they are resisting God. Some people sense God's call, the conviction of their conscience, or the truth of the gospel, but they push back against it. Jesus' words apply: resisting God only brings pain. Surrendering to Him brings freedom.

It also encourages believers to pray for those who oppose the faith. Paul was the fiercest opponent of the early church, yet God was at work in him. Believers can pray with hope for those who seem hostile, trusting that God may be "goading" their hearts.

Finally, the verse encourages believers to respond to God's guidance in their own lives. Even believers can resist God's direction in areas such as forgiveness, obedience, or calling. Believers can ask God to show them where they are resisting Him and give them grace to yield.

### Acts 26:18 — From Darkness to Light

verse: Acts 26:18

#### Simple Explanation

Paul was telling King Agrippa what Jesus said to him on the road to Damascus. Jesus told him to stand up, because He had appeared to appoint him as a servant and a witness. Jesus promised to rescue him from both his own people and the Gentiles, to whom He was sending him. Then Jesus described Paul's mission.

[[v18|to open their eyes]]. People without Christ are spiritually blind. They cannot see the truth about God. Paul's message would open their eyes. Paul himself had been physically blinded and then received his sight, a picture of what happens spiritually when someone comes to Christ.

[[v18|that they may turn from darkness to light]]. Darkness represents sin, ignorance, and separation from God. Light represents truth, holiness, and life with God. Conversion is a turning from one realm to the other. Jesus is the light of the world (John 8:12).

[[v18|and from the power of Satan to God]]. Before coming to Christ, people are under the power of Satan, the enemy of God. Through the gospel, they are rescued and brought under the loving rule of God. Paul later wrote that God [[Colossians 1:13|delivered us out of the power of darkness, and translated us into the Kingdom of the Son of his love]].

[[v18|that they may receive remission of sins]]. Those who turn to God receive forgiveness. Their sins are released and cancelled.

[[v18|and an inheritance among those who are sanctified by faith in me]]. They also receive a share in the inheritance of God's people, all who have been made holy by faith in Jesus. This inheritance includes eternal life and every blessing God has promised.

Notice the final words: "by faith in me." All of this comes through faith in Jesus.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse is one of the most complete descriptions of salvation in the New Testament. It includes illumination (eyes opened), conversion (turning from darkness to light), deliverance (from Satan's power to God), forgiveness (remission of sins), and inheritance (a place among God's people). All of it comes by faith in Jesus.

It also echoes the mission of God's Servant in Isaiah, who was to be [[Isaiah 42:6|a light for the nations]] and [[Isaiah 42:7|to open the blind eyes, to bring the prisoners out of the dungeon]]. Paul's mission was a participation in the mission of Jesus, the true Servant.

Finally, the verse shows the reality of spiritual warfare. Salvation is not merely a change of opinion but a rescue from one kingdom to another. The gospel has the power to set people free from the power of Satan.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse helps believers understand what God has done for them. They were once in darkness, under Satan's power, guilty of sin. Now their eyes have been opened, they live in the light, they belong to God, they are forgiven, and they have an inheritance. This should fill believers with gratitude and joy.

It also clarifies the mission of the church. Believers are called to share the gospel so that others' eyes may be opened and they may turn from darkness to light. This is the most important work believers can do.

Finally, the verse gives hope to anyone who feels trapped in darkness. No one is too far gone. Through faith in Jesus, anyone can be brought from darkness to light, from Satan's power to God, and receive forgiveness and a place among God's people.

### Acts 26:28–29 — "Become Such as I Am, Except for These Bonds"

verse: Acts 26:28-29

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had just asked King Agrippa directly, "Do you believe the prophets? I know that you believe." This put Agrippa in a difficult position. If he said yes, Paul would argue that the prophets pointed to Jesus. If he said no, he would lose credibility with his Jewish subjects.

Agrippa avoided a direct answer: [[v28|With a little persuasion are you trying to make me a Christian?]] The Greek can also be read as "In a short time you would persuade me to become a Christian?" Agrippa may have been brushing off Paul's appeal with a joke, or expressing surprise at Paul's boldness, or perhaps showing that he was more moved than he wanted to admit. Notice that he used the word "Christian," one of only three times it appears in the New Testament.

Paul's response was gracious and heartfelt: [[v29|I pray to God, that whether with little or with much, not only you, but also all that hear me today, might become such as I am]]. Paul did not care whether it took a short time or a long time. He prayed that the king, and everyone present, would become a follower of Jesus as he was.

Then Paul added, [[v29|except for these bonds]]. Paul was in chains. He wished his hearers to share his faith, his joy, his hope, and his forgiveness, but not his chains. Even as a prisoner, Paul considered himself more blessed than the king.

This was the high point of Paul's defense. He was not asking for his own release. He was asking for the salvation of the people judging him.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show the heart of a true witness. Paul's greatest desire was not his own freedom but others' salvation. He wrote to the Romans, [[Romans 10:1|Brothers, my heart’s desire and my prayer to God is for Israel, that they may be saved]]. Paul's love for people drove his witness, even in chains.

They also show that true freedom is found in Christ. Paul was physically bound, yet spiritually free. Agrippa was physically free and powerful, yet spiritually needy. Paul knew that knowing Christ was worth more than any earthly position. He wrote, [[Philippians 3:8|I count all things to be a loss for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus, my Lord]].

Finally, Agrippa's response shows how people can be close to faith but not come to it. He was familiar with the Scriptures and had heard the gospel clearly. Yet he deflected. Being "almost persuaded" is not the same as believing.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to love others enough to share Christ with them. Paul wanted everyone who heard him to become a Christian. Believers can ask God for the same desire for others' salvation, praying for and speaking to people of every status.

They also remind believers of the treasure they have in Christ. Even when believers face hardship, illness, or loss, they possess something more valuable than anything the world can offer. Believers can rejoice in their faith even in difficult circumstances.

Finally, these verses challenge anyone who is "almost persuaded." Agrippa heard the truth but did not respond. Those who have heard the gospel and are close to believing are invited not to delay, but to trust Christ fully.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Acts 26 shows the power of a changed life. Paul told the king how he had once raged against Jesus' followers, and how the risen Lord had met him and transformed him. Every believer has a story of what God has done. Believers can share their testimony honestly, as Paul did, pointing to Jesus as the One who changed them.

The chapter also challenges people to take the resurrection seriously. Paul asked, "Why is it judged incredible with you if God does raise the dead?" The God who created life can give life again. Believers can rest in this hope and invite others to consider it.

Acts 26 describes salvation in rich terms: eyes opened, darkness turned to light, freedom from Satan's power, forgiveness of sins, and an inheritance among God's people, all by faith in Jesus. Believers can rejoice in what God has done for them and join in His mission to bring others from darkness to light, calling them to repent and live lives that show their repentance.

Finally, the chapter shows the heart of true witness. Paul did not ask for his own release; he prayed that everyone who heard him would become as he was, except for his chains. Believers can ask God for this kind of love, sharing the gospel with people of every status, and inviting those who are "almost persuaded" to trust Christ fully.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for meeting Paul on the road to Damascus and for meeting me. Thank You for opening my eyes, turning me from darkness to light and from the power of Satan to God, forgiving my sins, and giving me an inheritance among Your people. I believe that God raises the dead, because You are risen. Where I have been kicking against the goads and resisting You, help me to yield to Your will. Give me courage to tell my story of what You have done, and to speak the truth boldly and reasonably. Fill my heart with Paul's love for others, so that I long for everyone I meet to know You as I do. For those who are almost persuaded, I pray that they would come to You fully. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
