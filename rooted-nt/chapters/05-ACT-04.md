# Acts 4
theme: Bold Witness Before the Council and a Church United in Prayer and Generosity
main: Peter and John are arrested for proclaiming the resurrection of Jesus. Filled with the Holy Spirit, Peter tells the council that the lame man was healed in the name of Jesus, whom they crucified and God raised, and declares that there is salvation in no other name. The leaders, astonished at the boldness of these ordinary men, order them to stop speaking about Jesus, but they answer that they cannot help telling what they have seen and heard. The church responds with prayer for boldness, the place is shaken, and the believers share everything so that no one among them is in need.

## Chapter Background and Story

Acts 4 records the first official opposition to the church. The same council that condemned Jesus now confronts His apostles. Jesus had warned His disciples that they would be brought before rulers and authorities, and He had promised that the Holy Spirit would give them words to speak (Luke 12:11–12; 21:12–15). In this chapter, that promise is fulfilled.

### Arrested for Preaching the Resurrection (4:1–4)

While Peter was still speaking to the crowd in Solomon's Porch, the priests, the captain of the temple (the officer responsible for temple security), and the Sadducees arrived. They were [[v2|upset because they taught the people and proclaimed in Jesus the resurrection from the dead]]. The Sadducees were the priestly aristocracy who controlled the temple. They did not believe in the resurrection of the dead (Luke 20:27). The apostles' message that Jesus had risen challenged both their beliefs and their authority.

They arrested Peter and John and held them overnight. But Luke notes that the message could not be locked up: [[v4|many of those who heard the word believed, and the number of the men came to be about five thousand]]. Opposition did not stop the growth of the church.

### Before the Council (4:5–12)

The next morning, the Sanhedrin gathered: the rulers, elders, and scribes. Luke names Annas, Caiaphas, John, Alexander, and other members of the high priest's family. These were the same men who had examined Jesus only weeks earlier (John 18:13, 24). Now Peter and John stood before them.

They asked, [[v7|By what power, or in what name, have you done this?]] Then [[v8|Peter, filled with the Holy Spirit]], answered. The man who had denied Jesus before a servant girl now stood boldly before the highest court in the land. Peter pointed out that they were being questioned about [[v9|a good deed done to a crippled man]]. Then he declared that the healing had been done [[v10|in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom you crucified, whom God raised from the dead]].

Peter quoted Psalm 118:22: [[v11|the stone which was regarded as worthless by you, the builders, which has become the head of the corner]]. Jesus had applied this verse to Himself after the parable of the wicked tenants (Luke 20:17). The religious leaders, the "builders" of Israel, had rejected Jesus as useless, but God had made Him the cornerstone of His new temple, the foundation of everything. Peter's conclusion is studied below.

### The Council's Dilemma (4:13–22)

The leaders were astonished. Their reaction is studied below. They could not deny the miracle, since the healed man was standing right there. They sent the apostles out and discussed what to do. They admitted, [[v16|a notable miracle has been done through them, as can be plainly seen by all who dwell in Jerusalem, and we can’t deny it]]. Yet instead of considering whether the apostles' message might be true, they decided to stop it from spreading: [[v17|let’s threaten them, that from now on they don’t speak to anyone in this name]].

They called Peter and John back and ordered them not to speak or teach at all in the name of Jesus. The apostles' answer is studied below. The council threatened them further but let them go, because the people were praising God for the healing of a man who had been lame for over forty years.

### The Church Prays (4:23–31)

When Peter and John were released, they went to the other believers and reported what had happened. The church's response was to pray. Their prayer is studied below. They began by acknowledging God as the Creator: [[v24|O Lord, you are God, who made the sky, the earth, the sea, and all that is in them]]. Then they quoted Psalm 2:1–2, which speaks of the nations raging and the rulers plotting [[v26|against the Lord, and against his Christ]]. They saw this fulfilled in what had happened to Jesus: Herod, Pilate, the Gentiles, and the people of Israel had gathered against Him, [[v28|to do whatever your hand and your counsel foreordained to happen]]. Even the worst human rebellion could not overturn God's plan.

### One Heart and Soul (4:32–37)

Luke describes the life of the church again: [[v32|The multitude of those who believed were of one heart and soul. Not one of them claimed that anything of the things which he possessed was his own, but they had all things in common]]. Their unity was not only spiritual but practical. They did not hold on to their possessions as if they belonged only to themselves.

[[v33|With great power, the apostles gave their testimony of the resurrection of the Lord Jesus. Great grace was on them all]]. The resurrection remained the center of the apostles' message, and God's grace was evident in the whole community.

[[v34|For neither was there among them any who lacked]]. This fulfilled God's intention for Israel: "there will be no poor with you" (Deuteronomy 15:4). Property owners sold land or houses and brought the money to the apostles, who distributed it to those in need. This was voluntary and based on need, not a system that abolished private property; Peter later reminds Ananias that his property was his own to keep (5:4).

Luke introduces one man by name as an example: [[v36|Joses, who by the apostles was also called Barnabas (which is, being interpreted, Son of Encouragement), a Levite, a man of Cyprus by race]]. He sold a field and brought the money to the apostles. Barnabas will become one of the most important figures in Acts. He will vouch for Paul when others are afraid of him (9:27), encourage the new church in Antioch (11:22–24), and travel with Paul on his first missionary journey (13:2). His nickname fit him perfectly.

## Important Verses Explained

### Acts 4:12 — No Other Name

verse: Acts 4:12

#### Simple Explanation

Peter and John stood before the Sanhedrin, the same council that had condemned Jesus. They were being questioned about how they had healed a man who had been lame from birth. Peter explained that the man had been healed by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom the council had crucified and God had raised from the dead.

Then Peter moved from physical healing to spiritual salvation: [[v12|There is salvation in no one else]]. The Greek word for "salvation" is related to the word for being "made whole" or "healed" (4:9). The lame man had been made whole through Jesus' name. In the same way, salvation, being made whole spiritually and rescued from sin and death, is found only in Jesus.

[[v12|for there is no other name under heaven that is given among men, by which we must be saved!]] A "name" in Scripture represents a person's identity, character, and authority. Peter declared that no other person, religion, or power in all the world can save. God has given only one name for salvation: Jesus.

Notice the word "given." Salvation through Jesus is God's gift to humanity. It is not a human invention or achievement. And notice the word "must." Salvation is not optional; all people need it, and there is only one way to receive it.

Peter spoke these words to the most powerful religious leaders in Israel. He did not soften his message to avoid offense. He told them plainly that the Jesus they had rejected was the only Savior.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse expresses one of the central convictions of the Christian faith: Jesus is the only Savior. Jesus Himself said, [[John 14:6|No one comes to the Father, except through me]]. Paul writes, [[1 Timothy 2:5|For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus]]. The uniqueness of Christ flows from who He is, the Son of God, and what He has done, dying for sins and rising again.

It also shows that this exclusive claim is the foundation of a universal invitation. Because there is only one Savior, His salvation is offered to all. The name of Jesus is "given among men," meaning it is available to all people everywhere. No one is excluded because of nationality, background, or past.

Finally, this verse shows the courage of the early church. Claiming that Jesus is the only Savior was unpopular then, as it often is now. The apostles proclaimed it anyway, because they believed it was true and because they loved those who needed to hear it.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse gives believers confidence in the gospel. In a world of many beliefs and spiritual options, believers can be sure that Jesus is the Savior. They do not need to search elsewhere for salvation.

It also calls believers to share this message with love and humility. The claim that Jesus is the only Savior can be offensive if spoken with arrogance. Believers are not better than others; they have simply received a gift that is offered to everyone. They can share it with gentleness and respect (1 Peter 3:15), honoring the people they speak with even when they disagree.

Finally, the verse encourages believers to pray and work for the spread of the gospel. If salvation is found only in Jesus, then sharing His name is the most loving thing believers can do. This motivates mission, evangelism, Bible translation, and support for those who carry the gospel to places where His name is not yet known.

### Acts 4:13 — They Had Been with Jesus

verse: Acts 4:13

#### Simple Explanation

The members of the Sanhedrin were among the most educated and powerful men in Israel. They were experts in the Law, trained in rabbinic schools, and accustomed to respect. Standing before them were Peter and John, two former fishermen from Galilee.

[[v13|Now when they saw the boldness of Peter and John]]. "Boldness" means courage and freedom in speaking, especially in the face of danger. Peter and John did not cower before the council. They spoke clearly and confidently about Jesus.

[[v13|and had perceived that they were unlearned and ignorant men, they marveled]]. The words "unlearned and ignorant" do not mean that the apostles were foolish or illiterate. They mean that they had not received formal training in the rabbinic schools. They were laymen, not professional scholars. Yet they spoke with wisdom and power and used Scripture skillfully.

[[v13|They recognized that they had been with Jesus]]. The council remembered Jesus, who had also taught with authority without formal rabbinic training (John 7:15). They saw in Peter and John the same boldness and authority they had seen in Jesus. The apostles' time with Jesus had shaped them.

The source of their boldness was also the Holy Spirit, who had filled Peter as he spoke (4:8). Their time with Jesus and the presence of His Spirit had transformed these ordinary men.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that God uses ordinary people. The apostles had no special credentials, yet God used them to turn the world upside down. Paul writes that God chose [[1 Corinthians 1:27|the foolish things of the world that he might put to shame those who are wise]]. God's power is displayed through human weakness.

It also shows that time with Jesus transforms people. The apostles had spent three years with Jesus, listening to His teaching, watching His life, and learning His ways. That relationship changed them so deeply that others could see it. Paul describes believers being [[2 Corinthians 3:18|transformed into the same image from glory to glory]] as they behold the Lord's glory.

Finally, the verse shows the work of the Spirit in giving boldness. The same Peter who had denied Jesus in fear was now fearless. The difference was not merely his own resolve but the Spirit's power.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers who feel unqualified. Many believers think they cannot share their faith or serve God because they lack education, training, or special gifts. God does not require credentials. He uses ordinary people who have been with Jesus and are filled with His Spirit.

It also invites believers to spend time with Jesus. Today, believers spend time with Him through prayer, reading Scripture, worship, and fellowship with other believers. As they do, they are shaped by Him, and others may notice the difference.

Finally, the verse challenges believers to consider what others see in them. The council recognized that Peter and John had been with Jesus. Believers can ask God to make their lives, words, and character reflect Him so clearly that others are drawn to Christ.

### Acts 4:19–20 — We Can't Help Telling What We Saw and Heard

verse: Acts 4:19-20

#### Simple Explanation

The Sanhedrin could not deny that a miracle had happened. Everyone in Jerusalem knew about it. But the leaders were determined to stop the message about Jesus from spreading. They called Peter and John back and [[v18|commanded them not to speak at all nor teach in the name of Jesus]].

This was a serious command. The Sanhedrin had religious and civil authority, and disobeying it could lead to punishment. Peter and John had to choose between obeying human authorities and obeying God.

They answered, [[v19|Whether it is right in the sight of God to listen to you rather than to God, judge for yourselves]]. They respectfully but firmly pointed out that the council was asking them to disobey God. Jesus had commanded them to be His witnesses (1:8). They could not obey both.

[[v20|for we can’t help telling the things which we saw and heard]]. The apostles had seen Jesus' life, death, and resurrection. They had heard His teaching. What they had experienced was so real and so important that they could not keep silent. It was not merely that they would not stop; they could not stop.

Notice the respectful tone of their answer. They did not insult the council or call for rebellion. They simply stated that they must obey God. Later, when the council again ordered them to stop, Peter said, [[Acts 5:29|We must obey God rather than men]].

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses establish an important principle for believers: when human authorities command what God forbids, or forbid what God commands, believers must obey God. Scripture generally calls believers to respect and obey governing authorities (Romans 13:1–7; 1 Peter 2:13–17). But this obedience has limits. God's authority is higher than any human authority.

They also reveal the nature of true witness. The apostles spoke because they had encountered Jesus. Their witness was rooted in personal experience of real events. Christian witness is not repeating secondhand ideas but telling what God has done.

Finally, these verses show the inner compulsion of the gospel. The prophet Jeremiah once said that God's word was like [[Jeremiah 20:9|a burning fire shut up in my bones]], and he could not hold it in. The apostles felt the same. When people truly encounter Christ, they cannot help sharing Him.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to remain faithful to God even under pressure. In many parts of the world, believers face laws or threats that forbid them from sharing their faith. In other places, the pressure is social rather than legal. In every setting, believers are called to obey God first, while treating authorities with respect.

They also call believers to share what they have experienced. Every believer has a story of what Jesus has done in their life. Sharing this story is a natural and powerful form of witness.

Finally, these verses invite believers to examine their passion for Christ. The apostles could not help speaking about Him. If believers find it easy to stay silent about Jesus, they can ask God to renew their love for Him and their awareness of what He has done, so that their witness flows from a full heart.

### Acts 4:29–31 — Grant Your Servants Boldness

verse: Acts 4:29-31

#### Simple Explanation

After Peter and John were released and told the other believers about the council's threats, the church gathered to pray. They did not complain, panic, or plan revenge. They lifted their voices to God together.

Their prayer began by acknowledging God's sovereignty over creation and history. They quoted Psalm 2, which describes the nations and rulers raging against God and His anointed King, and they recognized that this had been fulfilled in the opposition to Jesus. Yet even this opposition had accomplished only what God had planned.

Then they made their request: [[v29|Now, Lord, look at their threats]]. They brought the threats before God, trusting Him to see and respond.

[[v29|and grant to your servants to speak your word with all boldness]]. Remarkably, they did not ask God to remove the danger or punish their enemies. They asked for boldness to keep speaking His word. Their concern was faithfulness, not safety.

[[v30|while you stretch out your hand to heal; and that signs and wonders may be done through the name of your holy Servant Jesus]]. They asked God to continue confirming His word through miracles, as He had done with the lame man.

God answered immediately: [[v31|When they had prayed, the place was shaken where they were gathered together. They were all filled with the Holy Spirit, and they spoke the word of God with boldness]]. The shaking of the place was a sign of God's presence and power, recalling the shaking of Mount Sinai (Exodus 19:18). The believers were filled with the Spirit again, and God gave exactly what they had asked for: boldness.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This prayer shows how believers can respond to persecution. They turned first to God, acknowledging His sovereignty. They saw their situation in light of Scripture. They asked not for comfort but for courage to continue God's work. This kind of prayer reflects deep trust in God's purposes.

It also shows that the filling of the Spirit is not a one-time event. The believers had been filled at Pentecost, and they were filled again here. Paul later commands believers to [[Ephesians 5:18|be filled with the Spirit]], using a verb that suggests continual filling. Believers need the Spirit's fresh empowering for each new challenge.

Finally, the passage reveals the power of united prayer. The believers prayed together with one voice, and God answered powerfully. Throughout Acts, prayer precedes and accompanies the advance of the gospel.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This prayer offers a model for believers facing opposition or pressure. Instead of asking only for relief, believers can ask for boldness to remain faithful. They can bring their fears to God, remember His sovereignty, and ask for courage to keep speaking His word.

It also encourages believers to pray together. Many churches pray mainly for physical needs, which is good and right. The early church also prayed for boldness in witness. Believers can pray for one another's courage and faithfulness, especially for those who face persecution.

Finally, the passage reminds believers that God answers prayer. The early church asked for boldness, and God gave it. Believers can pray with confidence, trusting that God hears and responds according to His will.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Acts 4 shows that faithful witness may bring opposition. Peter and John were arrested and threatened for proclaiming Jesus. Believers today may face resistance as well, whether through laws, social pressure, ridicule, or rejection. This chapter reminds them that opposition is not a sign that God has abandoned them; it is part of following a rejected and risen Lord.

The chapter also shows the source of the apostles' courage. They had been with Jesus, and they were filled with the Holy Spirit. Believers today can grow in boldness by spending time with Jesus and depending on His Spirit. God uses ordinary people, not because of their credentials, but because of His presence in them.

Acts 4 proclaims a clear message: there is salvation in no other name than Jesus. This truth gives believers confidence and urgency. They are called to share the name of Jesus with humility and love, trusting that He alone can save.

Finally, the chapter shows a church that responds to threats with prayer and to need with generosity. The believers prayed for boldness rather than safety, and God filled them with His Spirit. They shared their possessions so that no one was in need. Believers today can follow their example, praying together for courage, caring for one another practically, and encouraging one another like Barnabas.

## Chapter Prayer

Sovereign Lord, You made the heavens, the earth, the sea, and everything in them. Nothing happens outside Your hand. Thank You that there is salvation in Jesus, the name given among us by which we must be saved. Make me bold to speak of Him, and help me to share His name with humility and love. Let my life show that I have been with Jesus. When I face pressure to be silent, help me to obey You rather than people, and to speak respectfully but faithfully. Look at the threats Your people face around the world, and grant Your servants boldness. Fill me again with Your Holy Spirit. Make Your church one in heart and soul, generous with what we have, so that no one among us is in need. Raise up encouragers like Barnabas, and make me one of them. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
