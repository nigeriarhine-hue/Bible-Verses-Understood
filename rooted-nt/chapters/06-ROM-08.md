# Romans 8
theme: Life in the Spirit: No Condemnation and No Separation
main: There is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus, because the Spirit of life has set them free from the power of sin and death. God has done what the Law could not do by sending His Son as a sin offering, so that those who live by the Spirit fulfill the Law's righteous requirement. The Spirit dwells in believers, gives life, leads them as God's adopted children, and assures them that they can call God "Abba, Father." Though they groan with all creation while awaiting final redemption, present sufferings cannot compare with the coming glory, and the Spirit helps them pray. God works all things for good for those who love Him, and nothing in all creation can separate them from His love in Christ Jesus.

## Chapter Background and Story

Romans 8 is often called the summit of Paul's letter, and many consider it one of the greatest chapters in the Bible. It completes the argument Paul began in chapter 5 about the new life of those who have been justified by faith. In chapter 6, Paul showed that believers have died to sin. In chapter 7, he showed that the Law, though holy, cannot give power over sin. Now, in chapter 8, he shows the answer: the Holy Spirit. The Spirit is mentioned only once in chapters 1–7 after the opening (5:5), but about twenty times in chapter 8.

The chapter begins with "no condemnation" (8:1) and ends with "no separation" (8:39). Between these two great statements, Paul describes the Spirit's work, believers' identity as God's children, the hope of glory, and the unbreakable love of God.

### No Condemnation (8:1–4)

The chapter's opening declaration is studied below. Paul explains how God accomplished this: [[v3|For what the law couldn’t do, in that it was weak through the flesh, God did, sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh and for sin, he condemned sin in the flesh]]. The Law could not save because sinful human nature could not obey it. So God sent His Son in a real human body, "in the likeness of sinful flesh," though without sin Himself (Hebrews 4:15). The phrase "for sin" can be translated "as a sin offering." On the cross, God condemned sin in Christ's body. Sin was judged and its power broken.

The purpose: [[v4|that the ordinance of the law might be fulfilled in us who don’t walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit]]. Those who live by the Spirit fulfill what the Law truly required: love for God and neighbor (Romans 13:8–10).

### Flesh and Spirit (8:5–13)

Paul contrasts two ways of living. [[v5|For those who live according to the flesh set their minds on the things of the flesh, but those who live according to the Spirit, the things of the Spirit]]. The "flesh" refers to human nature apart from God, dominated by sin. The "Spirit" refers to God's Holy Spirit. What people set their minds on reveals which way they live. [[v6|For the mind of the flesh is death, but the mind of the Spirit is life and peace]].

[[v9|But you are not in the flesh but in the Spirit, if it is so that the Spirit of God dwells in you. But if any man doesn’t have the Spirit of Christ, he is not his]]. Every true believer has the Holy Spirit. The Spirit is not an extra blessing for a few; He is the mark of belonging to Christ. Notice that Paul uses "the Spirit of God" and "the Spirit of Christ" interchangeably, pointing to the unity of Father, Son, and Spirit.

[[v11|But if the Spirit of him who raised up Jesus from the dead dwells in you, he who raised up Christ Jesus from the dead will also give life to your mortal bodies through his Spirit who dwells in you]]. The same Spirit who raised Jesus will one day raise believers' bodies. This is a promise of the future resurrection.

Therefore, believers have an obligation, not to the flesh but to the Spirit. [[v13|For if you live after the flesh, you must die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live]]. Believers are called to "put to death" sinful practices, but they do this "by the Spirit," not by willpower alone.

### Children of God (8:14–17)

[[v14|For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are children of God]]. Being led by the Spirit is the mark of God's children. The Spirit's witness to believers' adoption is studied below.

[[v17|and if children, then heirs—heirs of God and joint heirs with Christ, if indeed we suffer with him, that we may also be glorified with him]]. As God's children, believers will inherit everything God has promised, sharing in Christ's inheritance. But the path to glory goes through suffering, just as it did for Christ.

### Future Glory (8:18–25)

[[v18|For I consider that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which will be revealed toward us]]. Paul, who suffered more than most (2 Corinthians 11:23–28), weighed his sufferings against the coming glory and concluded that there is no comparison.

Paul then describes creation itself waiting for this glory. [[v19|For the creation waits with eager expectation for the children of God to be revealed]]. The whole created world was [[v20|subjected to vanity]], frustration and futility, because of human sin (Genesis 3:17–19). But it was subjected [[v20|in hope]], [[v21|that the creation itself also will be delivered from the bondage of decay into the liberty of the glory of the children of God]]. God's plan includes the renewal of the whole creation, not only the salvation of individual souls. [[v22|For we know that the whole creation groans and travails in pain together until now]], like a woman in labor, whose pain leads to new life.

Believers groan too: [[v23|Not only so, but ourselves also, who have the first fruits of the Spirit, even we ourselves groan within ourselves, waiting for adoption, the redemption of our body]]. Believers already have the Spirit as "first fruits," the first part of the harvest, which guarantees that the rest will come. But they still wait for the full experience of adoption, when their bodies will be raised and transformed. [[v24|For we were saved in hope, but hope that is seen is not hope]]. [[v25|But if we hope for that which we don’t see, we wait for it with patience]].

### The Spirit's Help and God's Purpose (8:26–30)

The Spirit's help in prayer and God's purpose in all things are studied below. Paul then describes the unbreakable chain of God's saving work: [[v29|For whom he foreknew, he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son, that he might be the firstborn among many brothers]]. [[v30|Whom he predestined, those he also called. Whom he called, those he also justified. Whom he justified, those he also glorified]]. God's purpose is to make believers like Jesus. Notice that "glorified" is in the past tense, even though glorification is still future. It is so certain that Paul speaks of it as already done.

Christians have understood "foreknew" and "predestined" in different ways. Some, especially in the Reformed tradition, understand that God chose specific people for salvation according to His sovereign will. Others, especially in Wesleyan and Arminian traditions, understand that God predestined those whom He foreknew would believe, or that He predestined the church as a whole to be conformed to Christ. Many note that "foreknew" in Scripture often means to know in a personal, loving relationship. All agree that salvation is God's work from beginning to end, and that it gives believers deep security.

### More Than Conquerors (8:31–39)

Paul then asks a series of questions. [[v31|What then shall we say about these things? If God is for us, who can be against us?]] No opposition can succeed against those whom God is for. [[v32|He who didn’t spare his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how would he not also with him freely give us all things?]] If God gave His greatest gift, His Son, He will surely give everything else believers need.

[[v33|Who could bring a charge against God’s chosen ones? It is God who justifies]]. The judge has already declared them righteous. [[v34|Who is he who condemns? It is Christ who died, yes rather, who was raised from the dead, who is at the right hand of God, who also makes intercession for us]]. Christ, the only one who has the right to condemn, is the one who died for believers and now prays for them.

[[v35|Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? Could oppression, or anguish, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or sword?]] Paul lists hardships he himself had experienced. He quotes Psalm 44:22: [[v36|For your sake we are killed all day long. We were accounted as sheep for the slaughter]]. Believers may suffer terribly. [[v37|No, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him who loved us]]. Not "in spite of" these things, but "in" them, believers overcome through Christ's love. The chapter's conclusion is studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### Romans 8:1–2 — No Condemnation

verse: Romans 8:1-2

#### Simple Explanation

Romans 7 ended with Paul describing the struggle with sin and crying out, "What a wretched man I am! Who will deliver me?" He then gave thanks to God through Jesus Christ. Now, in Romans 8, Paul explains the deliverance believers have in Christ.

[[v1|There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus]]. "Condemnation" is a legal term meaning the verdict of guilty and the punishment that follows. For those in Christ, there is "no condemnation," none at all. God will never condemn them. The word "now" emphasizes that this is a present reality, not only a future hope. Believers do not have to wait until the end of their lives to find out whether they will be condemned. The verdict has already been given.

Who receives this? "Those who are in Christ Jesus." Being "in Christ" means being united to Him by faith. Everything that belongs to Christ, including His righteousness, belongs to those who are in Him. Since Christ cannot be condemned, those in Him cannot be condemned.

(Some later manuscripts add the words "who don't walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit" to verse 1, as the World English Bible includes; the earliest manuscripts end the verse with "in Christ Jesus." The phrase appears in all manuscripts at the end of verse 4.)

[[v2|For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus made me free from the law of sin and of death]]. Paul explains why there is no condemnation. Two "laws" or powers are contrasted. "The law of sin and of death" is the power of sin that Paul described in chapter 7, which leads to death. "The law of the Spirit of life" is the power of the Holy Spirit, who gives life. The Spirit has set believers free from sin's power.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses express the heart of the gospel's comfort. Believers are completely free from condemnation because Christ has taken their condemnation on Himself. Paul writes later in this chapter, [[Romans 8:34|Who is he who condemns? It is Christ who died]]. The one who could condemn is the one who died for them.

They also show that freedom from condemnation and freedom from sin's power go together. God not only declares believers not guilty; He also gives them His Spirit to free them from sin's control. Justification and transformation are both part of salvation.

Finally, these verses show the centrality of union with Christ. Everything depends on being "in Christ Jesus." Paul uses this phrase repeatedly in his letters. It describes the deep, personal union between believers and their Savior.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses bring deep comfort to believers who struggle with guilt. Many believers continue to feel condemned even after trusting Christ. Paul declares that there is no condemnation for those in Christ. Believers can rest in this truth, even when their feelings say otherwise.

They also encourage believers who feel trapped by sin. The Spirit of life has set them free from the law of sin and death. They are no longer slaves. They can rely on the Spirit's power to resist sin and live a new life.

Finally, these verses invite believers to find their identity in Christ. They are not defined by their failures but by their union with Him. When believers are tempted to see themselves as condemned, they can remember who they are in Christ.

### Romans 8:15–16 — [[Romans 8:15|Abba! Father!]]

verse: Romans 8:15-16

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had just said that those who are led by the Spirit of God are God's children. Now he explains the nature of this relationship.

[[v15|For you didn’t receive the spirit of bondage again to fear]]. Before coming to Christ, people lived as slaves, under the power of sin and the fear of judgment. Some may have related to God mainly through fear, like slaves afraid of a harsh master. The Spirit does not bring believers back into that kind of fearful slavery.

[[v15|but you received the Spirit of adoption]]. Instead, believers received "the Spirit of adoption." In the Roman world, adoption was a legal act that gave an adopted child all the rights of a natural-born child, including the right to inherit. Adopted children received a new name, a new family, and a new status. Their old debts were canceled. Believers have been adopted into God's family through the Spirit.

[[v15|by whom we cry, “Abba! Father!”]] "Abba" is an Aramaic word for "father," used by children to address their fathers with affection and trust. Jesus Himself used this word when He prayed in Gethsemane: [[Mark 14:36|Abba, Father]]. Believers can address God with the same intimate trust that Jesus had. The word "cry" suggests a heartfelt, sometimes urgent call, especially in times of need.

[[v16|The Spirit himself testifies with our spirit that we are children of God]]. The Holy Spirit confirms in believers' hearts that they truly belong to God. This inner assurance is a gift of the Spirit. Believers do not have to wonder whether God accepts them; the Spirit bears witness that they are His children.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal the intimacy of the relationship between believers and God. God is not a distant ruler but a loving Father. Through Christ, believers share in the relationship that Jesus has with the Father. Paul writes in Galatians, [[Galatians 4:6|And because you are children, God sent out the Spirit of his Son into your hearts, crying, “Abba, Father!”]]

They also show that fear is not the right foundation for a relationship with God. While believers rightly revere God, they do not need to live in terror of His rejection. John writes, [[1 John 4:18|There is no fear in love; but perfect love casts out fear]].

Finally, these verses show the work of the Spirit in giving assurance. Assurance of salvation is not merely a conclusion drawn from evidence; it is also the inner witness of the Spirit. God wants His children to know that they belong to Him.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses invite believers to approach God as their loving Father. Believers can pray with confidence and trust, bringing their needs, fears, and joys to Him as children bring them to a caring parent.

They also bring comfort to those who have had difficult relationships with earthly fathers. Some people find it hard to think of God as a father because of painful experiences. God is the perfect Father, whose love is faithful, patient, and good. Believers can ask the Spirit to help them know God's fatherly love.

Finally, these verses encourage believers who struggle with assurance. When doubts arise, believers can ask the Spirit to testify to their hearts that they are God's children, and they can rest in the promises of God's word.

### Romans 8:26–28 — The Spirit Helps, and God Works for Good

verse: Romans 8:26-28

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been describing the groaning of creation and of believers as they wait for the full redemption God has promised. Life in this world involves weakness, suffering, and longing. Now Paul describes how God helps believers in this condition.

[[v26|In the same way, the Spirit also helps our weaknesses, for we don’t know how to pray as we ought]]. Believers are weak, and sometimes they do not even know what to pray for. In times of confusion, grief, or suffering, words may fail. [[v26|But the Spirit himself makes intercession for us with groanings which can’t be uttered]]. The Holy Spirit prays on believers' behalf, with deep longings that cannot be put into words. Believers are not alone in prayer; the Spirit carries their prayers to God.

[[v27|He who searches the hearts knows what is on the Spirit’s mind, because he makes intercession for the saints according to God]]. God the Father, who knows every heart, understands the Spirit's prayers, because the Spirit always prays in line with God's will.

Then comes one of the most beloved verses in the Bible: [[v28|We know that all things work together for good for those who love God, for those who are called according to his purpose]]. Paul does not say that all things are good. Suffering, loss, and sin are not good in themselves. But God works in all things, even painful ones, to bring about good for His people.

What is this "good"? The next verse explains: God's purpose is that believers would be [[v29|conformed to the image of his Son]]. The ultimate good is becoming like Jesus and sharing His glory. Everything God allows in believers' lives serves this purpose.

The promise is for "those who love God," who are "called according to his purpose." It describes believers, those who have responded to God's call with love.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show the deep care of God for believers in their weakness. The Spirit prays for them, and the Father works in every circumstance for their good. Believers are surrounded by God's care. Jesus also intercedes for them (8:34; Hebrews 7:25).

They also show that God's purposes are larger than believers' immediate comfort. The "good" God works toward is not necessarily ease or success, but conformity to Christ. Sometimes God uses hardship to make believers more like Jesus. Joseph told his brothers, [[Genesis 50:20|As for you, you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good]].

Finally, these verses must be read with care. Romans 8:28 is sometimes quoted too quickly to people who are suffering, as if it explains away their pain. Paul wrote these words in a passage about groaning and suffering. The verse is not a slogan to silence grief but a deep comfort that God is at work even in what is broken. Believers can grieve honestly while trusting God's purposes.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers who do not know how to pray. In times of overwhelming pain or confusion, believers can come to God with sighs, tears, or silence, trusting that the Spirit is praying for them.

They also bring hope to believers in difficult circumstances. Whatever believers face, God is working for their good. They may not see how, and they may not understand why, but they can trust that God has not abandoned them.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to be gentle with those who are suffering. Rather than quickly quoting Romans 8:28 to someone in pain, believers can first weep with them (Romans 12:15), pray with them, and, in time, gently share the hope that God is at work.

### Romans 8:38–39 — Nothing Can Separate Us from God's Love

verse: Romans 8:38-39

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been asking a series of questions: If God is for us, who can be against us? Who can bring a charge against God's chosen ones? Who can condemn? Who can separate us from the love of Christ? He had listed many hardships: trouble, hardship, persecution, famine, nakedness, danger, and sword. And he had declared that in all these things, believers are more than conquerors through Christ who loved them.

Now Paul gives his final, triumphant answer: [[v38|For I am persuaded]]. Paul is completely convinced. This is not wishful thinking but deep certainty.

[[v38|that neither death, nor life]]. Neither dying nor the challenges of living can separate believers from God's love.

[[v38|nor angels, nor principalities]]. No spiritual powers, whether good or evil, can separate them. "Principalities" probably refers to spiritual rulers, including evil forces (Ephesians 6:12).

[[v38|nor things present, nor things to come, nor powers]]. Nothing happening now and nothing that will happen in the future, no power of any kind, can separate them.

[[v39|nor height, nor depth]]. These words may refer to the farthest reaches of the universe, or to astrological powers that people in Paul's day feared. Nothing in all of space can separate believers from God's love.

[[v39|nor any other created thing]]. To make sure nothing is left out, Paul adds "any other created thing." Everything except God is created, and nothing created can separate believers from Him.

[[v39|will be able to separate us from God’s love which is in Christ Jesus our Lord]]. God's love is found "in Christ Jesus our Lord." Because believers are in Christ, they are held in God's love forever.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses express the ultimate security of believers. Their salvation does not depend on their own strength, but on God's love, which cannot fail. Jesus said, [[John 10:28|I give eternal life to them. They will never perish, and no one will snatch them out of my hand]].

They also show the comprehensive nature of God's love. Paul's list covers every category: life and death, spiritual powers, time, space, and all creation. There is no situation, place, or power that lies outside the reach of God's love.

Finally, these verses show that God's love is found in Christ. It is not a general sentiment but a specific love revealed and secured through Jesus' death and resurrection. Believers are held in this love because they are united to Christ.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses bring comfort to believers facing fear. Whether they fear death, illness, the future, spiritual attack, or any other danger, they can be sure that nothing will separate them from God's love.

They also encourage believers in suffering. Paul wrote these words knowing that believers face trouble, persecution, and even death. Suffering does not mean that God's love has failed. Believers can hold on to His love in their darkest moments.

Finally, these verses invite believers to rest in God's love. Many believers live with anxiety about whether God still loves them, especially after failure. Paul's words are clear: nothing can separate them from His love. Believers can live with confidence, joy, and peace.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Romans 8 begins with the declaration that there is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus. Believers who struggle with guilt can rest in this truth. God has condemned sin in Christ, and the Spirit of life has set believers free from the power of sin and death. They can live without fear of God's judgment.

The chapter also describes life in the Spirit. Every believer has the Spirit of God, who gives life, leads them, helps them put sinful practices to death, and assures them that they are God's children. Believers can depend on the Spirit daily, setting their minds on the things of the Spirit and approaching God with the trust of children who cry, "Abba, Father."

Romans 8 gives believers hope in suffering. Present sufferings are not worth comparing with the glory to come. All creation groans, waiting for renewal, and believers groan too as they await the redemption of their bodies. Yet the Spirit helps them pray when they do not know how, and God works in all things for the good of those who love Him, shaping them into the image of His Son.

Finally, the chapter ends with the triumphant assurance that nothing can separate believers from God's love in Christ. If God is for them, no one can stand against them. If God gave His own Son, He will give them everything they need. Believers can face every fear, hardship, and uncertainty with confidence that they are held forever in the love of God.

## Chapter Prayer

Heavenly Father, thank You that there is now no condemnation for me because I am in Christ Jesus. Thank You for sending Your Son to condemn sin and for giving me Your Spirit to set me free. Holy Spirit, lead me, help me to put to death what is sinful, and fill my heart with the assurance that I am a child of God. Abba, Father, thank You for adopting me into Your family. When I suffer, lift my eyes to the glory that is coming. When I do not know how to pray, thank You that Your Spirit prays for me. Help me to trust that You are working in all things for my good, making me more like Jesus. Thank You that nothing in all creation, not death or life, not present or future, not any power, will ever be able to separate me from Your love in Christ Jesus my Lord. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
