# 2 Timothy 4
theme: Finishing the Race and Keeping the Faith
main: In the presence of God and of Christ Jesus, who will judge the living and the dead, Paul solemnly charges Timothy to preach the word, to be ready in season and out of season, and to correct, rebuke, and encourage with great patience and careful teaching, because a time is coming when people will not tolerate sound teaching but will gather teachers to say what their itching ears want to hear. Timothy must stay clear-minded, endure hardship, do the work of an evangelist, and fulfill his ministry. Paul, aware that his death is near, declares that he has fought the good fight, finished the race, and kept the faith, and that the crown of righteousness awaits him and all who have longed for Christ's appearing. He asks Timothy to come quickly, bringing Mark, his cloak, and his scrolls, and tells how everyone deserted him at his first defense, yet the Lord stood by him and strengthened him. He closes with greetings and confidence that the Lord will bring him safely into His heavenly kingdom.

## Chapter Background and Story

These are likely the last words Paul ever wrote. He was in a Roman prison, probably the harsh Mamertine prison or another place of severe confinement, awaiting execution. Winter was approaching, and he was cold (v13, 21). Most of his companions were gone. Some had left for ministry elsewhere; others had abandoned him. He had already faced a preliminary hearing, and no one had stood with him (v16).

Yet the chapter is not one of despair. It overflows with faith, hope, and purpose. Paul gives Timothy a final, solemn charge to preach the word. He looks back on his life with gratitude and looks forward with confidence to his reward. And he shows, in his personal requests and greetings, that even at the end, he cared deeply about people, about his friends, his work, and the mission of the gospel.

This chapter is one of the most moving in the New Testament. It shows how a faithful servant of Christ faces death: not with fear, but with confidence in the Lord who stood by him.

### The Charge to Preach (4:1–5)

[[v1|I command you therefore before God and the Lord Jesus Christ, who will judge the living and the dead at his appearing and his Kingdom]]. Paul's charge is given in the presence of God and Christ, the judge of all. Timothy's ministry will be evaluated by the Lord at His return. The content of the charge, and the warning about itching ears, are studied below.

### Paul's Farewell (4:6–8)

[[v6|For I am already being offered, and the time of my departure has come]]. "Being offered" uses the image of a drink offering, wine poured out on the altar as part of a sacrifice (Numbers 15:5–10). Paul sees his life, and soon his death, as an offering poured out to God (see Philippians 2:17). "Departure" was used for loosening a ship from its moorings, or for soldiers striking their tents to move on. Death, for Paul, is not an end but a departure to a new destination. His reflection on his life and his hope are studied below.

### Personal Requests (4:9–15)

[[v9|Be diligent to come to me soon]], [[v10|for Demas left me, having loved this present world, and went to Thessalonica; Crescens to Galatia; and Titus to Dalmatia]]. Demas had once been Paul's coworker (Colossians 4:14; Philemon 1:24). But he loved "this present world" and abandoned Paul. Crescens and Titus had gone to other places, probably for ministry. Dalmatia was on the eastern coast of the Adriatic Sea.

[[v11|Only Luke is with me. Take Mark and bring him with you, for he is useful to me for service]]. Luke, the beloved physician, remained faithful to the end. And Mark, who had once deserted Paul and Barnabas (Acts 13:13; 15:37–39), is now "useful" for ministry. This is a beautiful picture of restoration. The young man who failed has become a valued coworker, and Paul, who once refused to take him, now asks for him.

[[v12|But I sent Tychicus to Ephesus]]. Tychicus may have carried this letter and taken Timothy's place in Ephesus so that Timothy could come.

[[v13|Bring the cloak that I left at Troas with Carpus when you come—and the books, especially the parchments]]. Paul needed his cloak for the cold winter in prison. He also wanted his books and parchments, possibly copies of the Scriptures, his own notes, or legal documents. Even near death, Paul wanted to keep reading and studying. These small human details show that Paul was a real person with real needs.

[[v14|Alexander the coppersmith did much evil to me. The Lord will repay him according to his deeds]]. [[v15|Beware of him, for he greatly opposed our words]]. Alexander may be the same person mentioned in 1 Timothy 1:20. He had caused Paul serious harm, perhaps by testifying against him. Paul does not seek revenge; he entrusts justice to the Lord, echoing Psalm 62:12. But he warns Timothy to be careful.

### The Lord Stood by Me (4:16–18)

The story of Paul's first defense is studied below. [[v18|And the Lord will deliver me from every evil work and will preserve me for his heavenly Kingdom. To him be the glory forever and ever. Amen]]. Paul does not expect to be rescued from death. He expects to be rescued through death into Christ's heavenly kingdom. Even facing execution, he ends with praise.

### Final Greetings (4:19–22)

[[v19|Greet Prisca and Aquila, and the house of Onesiphorus]]. Prisca (Priscilla) and Aquila were longtime coworkers who had risked their lives for Paul (Romans 16:3–4). Onesiphorus had refreshed Paul in prison (2 Timothy 1:16–18).

[[v20|Erastus remained at Corinth, but I left Trophimus at Miletus sick]]. Paul had seen God work miracles of healing, yet he left Trophimus sick. Not every illness was healed, even in the apostolic age. This shows that God's healing is according to His will, and that sickness is not necessarily a sign of lack of faith.

[[v21|Be diligent to come before winter. Eubulus salutes you, as do Pudens, Linus, Claudia, and all the brothers]]. "Before winter" is urgent. Once winter came, sea travel stopped, and Timothy would not be able to reach Rome until spring, possibly too late. Linus may be the same Linus who, according to early church tradition, later became a leader of the church in Rome.

[[v22|The Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit. Grace be with you. Amen]]. Paul's final written words are a blessing of the Lord's presence and grace. The "you" in the last sentence is plural, extending the blessing to the whole church.

## Important Verses Explained

### 2 Timothy 4:2 — Preach the Word

verse: 2 Timothy 4:2

#### Simple Explanation

This is the heart of Paul's charge to Timothy. "Preach the word." The "word" is the message of the gospel and the Scriptures, which Paul has just described as God-breathed (2 Timothy 3:16). "Preach" means to proclaim publicly, like a herald announcing a king's message.

"Be urgent in season and out of season." Timothy must be ready at all times, whether the moment seems convenient or not, whether people are receptive or not. He should not wait for ideal circumstances.

Paul then describes three ways of applying the word. "Reprove": convince people of the truth and expose error. "Rebuke": confront sin directly and call for repentance. "Exhort": encourage, comfort, and urge people to follow God.

"With all patience and teaching." This ministry must be done with great patience, not giving up on people, and with careful teaching, explaining the truth clearly. Correction without patience can become harsh; correction without teaching can become empty.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse summarizes the calling of every minister of the gospel and, in a broader sense, the calling of the whole church. The church exists to proclaim God's word. Romans 10:14–17 asks how people can believe without hearing and how they can hear without someone preaching. Faith comes from hearing the message about Christ.

The phrase "in season and out of season" acknowledges that there will be times when preaching the truth is unpopular. Faithful ministry does not depend on popularity. It depends on faithfulness to God, who will judge the living and the dead (v1).

The balance of reproof, rebuke, and exhortation reflects the full ministry of God's word. Scripture comforts the afflicted and challenges the comfortable. Faithful preaching does both, always with patience and careful teaching.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Pastors and teachers can take this charge to heart, making the preaching and teaching of God's word central to their ministry, being faithful whether or not it is welcomed, and combining truth with patience.

Every believer can also "preach the word" in a broader sense: sharing the gospel with others, encouraging fellow believers with Scripture, and speaking truth with love when needed. Being ready "in season and out of season" means being prepared to speak about Christ whenever opportunities arise.

Churches can value faithful biblical preaching and support those who give their lives to it, recognizing that God's word is the source of spiritual life and growth.

### 2 Timothy 4:3–5 — Itching Ears and a Faithful Ministry

verse: 2 Timothy 4:3-5

#### Simple Explanation

Paul explains why preaching the word is urgent. "For the time will come when they will not listen to the sound doctrine." "Sound doctrine" means healthy teaching that nourishes spiritual life. People will reject it.

"But having itching ears, will heap up for themselves teachers after their own lusts." "Itching ears" describes a craving to hear something pleasing, new, or exciting. Instead of seeking the truth, people will gather teachers who tell them what they want to hear, teachers who match their own desires.

Verse 4: "and will turn away their ears from the truth, and turn away to fables." They will reject the truth in favor of myths, appealing stories with no basis in reality.

Verse 5: "But you be sober in all things." In contrast, Timothy must keep a clear head, not swayed by popular trends. "Suffer hardship": he must endure difficulty. "Do the work of an evangelist": he must keep proclaiming the gospel to those who have not believed. "Fulfill your ministry": he must complete the task God has given him.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage describes a temptation that exists in every age: preferring comfortable lies to uncomfortable truth. People naturally gravitate toward messages that affirm their desires and avoid messages that challenge them. The prophets of Israel faced this: the people said to the seers, "Don't see!" and to the prophets, [[Isaiah 30:10|Don't prophesy to us right things. Tell us pleasant things]].

The phrase "heap up for themselves teachers" suggests that people will not lack teachers. There will be many, offering a variety of appealing messages. The problem is not a lack of preaching but a lack of truth.

Paul's response is not to compete by offering more pleasing messages. It is to remain faithful, clear-minded, willing to suffer, and committed to the gospel. Faithfulness is measured not by popularity but by obedience to God.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers today have access to an endless supply of teachers, sermons, podcasts, books, and videos. This can be a great blessing, but it also creates the temptation to choose teachers based on what one wants to hear. This passage invites believers to examine their own hearts: Do they seek truth, even when it challenges them, or do they seek affirmation of what they already want?

Pastors and teachers can resist the pressure to tell people what they want to hear. Faithful ministry speaks the truth with love, even when it is unpopular.

The call to "do the work of an evangelist" reminds all believers that sharing the gospel is part of every Christian's calling. In a world that often prefers myths, the truth of Christ is needed more than ever.

### 2 Timothy 4:7–8 — I Have Kept the Faith

verse: 2 Timothy 4:7-8

#### Simple Explanation

Paul looks back on his life with three statements.

"I have fought the good fight." Paul's life had been a struggle against sin, error, opposition, and suffering. He had not given up. The phrase may also suggest an athletic contest, such as wrestling. It was a "good" fight, one worth fighting.

"I have finished the course." Paul uses the image of a runner who has completed the race. He did not quit halfway. He ran to the finish line.

"I have kept the faith." Paul guarded the gospel entrusted to him and remained faithful to Christ to the end. He did not abandon or distort the truth.

Verse 8 looks forward. "From now on, the crown of righteousness is stored up for me." In Greek athletic games, winners received a crown, or wreath, of leaves that soon withered. Paul looks forward to a crown that will never fade. "The crown of righteousness" may mean the crown that is righteousness itself, or the crown given as a reward for a righteous life.

"Which the Lord, the righteous judge, will give to me on that day." Earthly judges had condemned Paul, but the righteous Judge would vindicate him. "That day" is the day of Christ's return.

"And not to me only, but also to all those who have loved his appearing." This crown is not only for apostles. It is for all who have longed for Christ's return and lived in light of it.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses express the hope of every believer: to finish well. Paul did not claim to be perfect (Philippians 3:12). But he had remained faithful. His confidence was not in his own achievements but in the Lord, the righteous judge, who would reward faithfulness.

The phrase "those who have loved his appearing" is beautiful. It describes believers whose hearts are set on Christ's return, who long to see Him. This love shapes how they live. Those who love His appearing live in a way that prepares for it.

Jesus also spoke of crowns and rewards for faithful servants (Revelation 2:10; 3:11). These rewards are not earned by merit in a way that replaces grace; they are God's gracious acknowledgment of the fruit His grace has produced in believers' lives. James 1:12 speaks of the crown of life promised to those who love God.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses invite every believer to consider how they want to finish their life. Will they be able to say, "I have fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith"? Finishing well requires daily faithfulness over a lifetime.

For those near the end of life, or facing serious illness, these verses offer hope. Death is not the end but the finish line, and the crown of righteousness awaits all who love Christ's appearing.

For those discouraged in the middle of the race, these verses offer encouragement to keep going. The fight is good, the race is worth finishing, and the reward is sure.

### 2 Timothy 4:16–17 — The Lord Stood by Me

verse: 2 Timothy 4:16-17

#### Simple Explanation

Paul describes his "first defense," probably a preliminary hearing before a Roman court. "At my first defense, no one came to help me, but all left me." In Roman courts, the accused could have supporters or advocates stand with them. When Paul stood trial, no one stood beside him. Perhaps they were afraid of being associated with a condemned man.

"May it not be held against them." Like Jesus on the cross (Luke 23:34) and Stephen at his death (Acts 7:60), Paul prays for those who abandoned him. He does not hold their failure against them.

Verse 17: "But the Lord stood by me and strengthened me." When everyone else left, the Lord was present. He gave Paul strength.

"That through me the message might be fully proclaimed, and that all the Gentiles might hear." Even at his trial, Paul preached the gospel. His defense became an opportunity to proclaim Christ before Roman officials and possibly a large audience.

"So I was delivered out of the mouth of the lion." This may be a figure of speech for being saved from great danger, echoing Psalm 22:21 or Daniel 6, or it may refer to escaping immediate execution at that first hearing. Paul was spared, for the moment.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses capture one of the most painful and most comforting experiences in the Christian life: being abandoned by people but upheld by God. Jesus experienced this when His disciples fled at His arrest (Mark 14:50). Paul experienced it at his trial. Believers may experience it when friends, family, or fellow church members fail them.

Paul's response is a model of grace. He does not become bitter. He prays for those who failed him. He focuses on the Lord's faithfulness rather than human failure.

The promise that the Lord stands by His people is found throughout Scripture. God told Joshua, [[Joshua 1:5|I will not fail you nor forsake you]]. Jesus promised, [[Matthew 28:20|I am with you always, even to the end of the age]]. Hebrews 13:5–6 echoes this: God will never leave His people, so they can say, "The Lord is my helper."

Even in his darkest moment, Paul's concern was the gospel. He saw his trial as an opportunity for the message to be "fully proclaimed." This shows the depth of his devotion.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many believers have experienced being let down or abandoned by others in their time of need. This passage offers comfort: the Lord stands by His people, even when everyone else leaves.

Paul's example also shows how to respond to those who fail us. Forgiveness, rather than bitterness, frees the heart. This does not mean ignoring real harm or trusting people who are unsafe, but it does mean releasing resentment and entrusting the situation to God.

Finally, Paul's focus on the gospel, even at his trial, invites believers to see their own difficult moments as opportunities to testify about Christ. God can use even the hardest circumstances for His purposes.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Second Timothy 4 contains Paul's final charge and farewell. In the presence of God and Christ, who will judge the living and the dead, Paul urges Timothy to preach the word, ready in season and out of season, correcting, rebuking, and encouraging with great patience and careful teaching. This charge applies to pastors and teachers, and in a broader sense to every believer called to share God's truth.

Paul warns that people will turn away from sound teaching and gather teachers who tell them what they want to hear. In a world full of appealing messages, believers are called to seek the truth even when it challenges them, and leaders are called to speak it faithfully even when it is unpopular.

Facing death, Paul could say, "I have fought the good fight. I have finished the course. I have kept the faith." He looked forward to the crown of righteousness, which the Lord will give to all who love His appearing. His example invites every believer to finish well, staying faithful through daily obedience until the end.

The chapter also shows Paul's humanity. He was cold, lonely, abandoned by many, and still eager to read and to see his friends. He celebrated Mark's restoration, warned Timothy about a dangerous opponent, and acknowledged that not every sick friend was healed. Most of all, he testified that when everyone else deserted him, the Lord stood by him and strengthened him. Believers can find comfort in that same faithful presence, forgive those who fail them, and trust that the Lord will bring them safely into His heavenly kingdom. To Him be the glory forever and ever.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, You will judge the living and the dead at Your appearing. Help me to be faithful to Your word, ready in season and out of season, speaking truth with patience and love. Guard me from itching ears that want to hear only what pleases me, and give me a love for sound teaching. Help me to endure hardship, to share the gospel, and to fulfill the calling You have given me. I want to finish well. Give me strength to fight the good fight, to finish the race, and to keep the faith. Fill me with love for Your appearing. When others fail me or leave me, stand by me and strengthen me, as You did for Paul. Help me to forgive those who let me down. Thank You for restoring those who have failed, as You restored Mark. Rescue me from every evil and bring me safely into Your heavenly kingdom. To You be the glory forever and ever. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
