# Revelation 13
theme: The Beast from the Sea and the Beast from the Earth
main: The dragon stands on the shore, and John sees a beast rising out of the sea with ten horns and seven heads, ten crowns on its horns and blasphemous names on its heads. It resembles a leopard, with feet like a bear's and a mouth like a lion's, and the dragon gives it his power, throne, and great authority. One of its heads seems to have had a fatal wound that has been healed, and the whole world is filled with wonder and follows the beast. People worship the dragon and the beast, saying, Who is like the beast? Who can wage war against it? The beast is given a mouth to utter proud words and blasphemies and authority to act for forty-two months. It blasphemes God, His name, His dwelling place, and those who live in heaven. It is permitted to wage war against God's people and to conquer them, and it is given authority over every tribe, people, language, and nation. All inhabitants of the earth will worship it, all whose names are not written in the Lamb's book of life. John calls for those with ears to hear: captivity and the sword will come, and this calls for patient endurance and faithfulness from God's people. Then John sees another beast, coming out of the earth, with two horns like a lamb but speaking like a dragon. It exercises the authority of the first beast and makes the world worship it. It performs great signs, even calling fire down from heaven, and deceives the inhabitants of the earth, ordering them to set up an image of the first beast. It is given power to give breath to the image so that it can speak and cause all who refuse to worship it to be killed. It forces everyone, small and great, rich and poor, free and slave, to receive a mark on the right hand or forehead, so that no one can buy or sell without the mark, which is the beast's name or the number of its name. This calls for wisdom: the number of the beast is the number of a man, and his number is 666.

## Chapter Background and Story

Revelation 13 continues the unveiling of the spiritual conflict that began in chapter 12. The dragon, Satan, has been thrown down to earth and is waging war against the people of God. In this chapter, he calls up two allies: a beast from the sea and a beast from the earth. Together, the dragon and the two beasts form an unholy trinity, a counterfeit of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

The beast from the sea represents political power that demands worship and persecutes God's people. The beast from the earth, later called "the false prophet" (16:13; 19:20; 20:10), represents religious and ideological power that promotes the worship of the first beast through deception and signs.

For John's first readers, these images would have had clear connections to the Roman Empire. Rome came from across the sea to Asia. It demanded loyalty, and in the province of Asia local leaders and priests promoted the worship of the emperor, built temples and statues in his honor, and pressured people to participate. Those who refused could face economic hardship and even death. The images also reach beyond Rome, describing every power in history that sets itself up in God's place and demands the loyalty that belongs to God alone.

### How the Beasts Are Understood

Interpreters understand the beasts in several ways. **Preterists** identify the first beast with the Roman Empire and often with a specific emperor, especially Nero, and the second beast with the local priesthood of emperor worship. **Futurists** generally see the first beast as a future world ruler, often called the antichrist, who will rise in the last days, and the second as a religious figure who supports him. **Historicists** have identified the beasts with various powers in church history. **Idealists** see the beasts as symbols of the recurring realities of oppressive political power and deceptive religion that oppose God's people in every age.

Many readers combine these perspectives: the beasts described the powers that threatened John's readers, they represent forces that recur throughout history, and they point to a final climax of evil before Christ returns. As John wrote in his letter, [[1 John 2:18|Little children, these are the end times, and as you heard that the Antichrist is coming, even now many antichrists have arisen]].

### The Beast from the Sea (13:1–10)

The beast's rise and the world's worship of it are studied below.

[[v5|A mouth speaking great things and blasphemy was given to him. Authority to make war for forty-two months was given to him]]. [[v6|He opened his mouth for blasphemy against God, to blaspheme his name, his dwelling, and those who dwell in heaven]]. These verses echo Daniel 7:8, 25, where a boastful horn speaks against the Most High. The repeated phrase "was given" is significant. The beast's power is permitted, not independent. Its time is limited to forty-two months, the same symbolic period of persecution seen in chapters 11 and 12.

[[v7|It was given to him to make war with the saints and to overcome them. Authority over every tribe, people, language, and nation was given to him]]. The beast appears to triumph over God's people. Yet Revelation has already shown that believers overcome through the blood of the Lamb and their testimony, even when they die (12:11). What looks like defeat on earth is victory in heaven. The beast's claim to authority over every tribe and nation is a counterfeit of the Lamb, who purchased people from every tribe and nation (5:9). The call for endurance is studied below.

### The Beast from the Earth (13:11–18)

[[v11|I saw another beast coming up out of the earth. He had two horns like a lamb and it spoke like a dragon]]. This beast looks harmless and even lamb-like, but its voice reveals its true nature. Jesus warned about false prophets who come in sheep's clothing but inwardly are ravenous wolves (Matthew 7:15). The beast imitates the Lamb but speaks for the dragon.

[[v12|He exercises all the authority of the first beast in his presence. He makes the earth and those who dwell in it to worship the first beast, whose fatal wound was healed]]. The second beast serves the first, promoting its worship. As the Holy Spirit glorifies Christ, this counterfeit spirit glorifies the beast.

[[v13|He performs great signs, even making fire come down out of the sky to the earth in the sight of people]]. [[v14|He deceives my own people who dwell on the earth because of the signs he was granted to do in front of the beast, saying to those who dwell on the earth that they should make an image to the beast who had the sword wound and lived]]. The second beast imitates the miracles of God's prophets, such as Elijah calling down fire from heaven (1 Kings 18:38). Jesus warned that false christs and false prophets would perform signs to deceive (Mark 13:22). Miracles alone are not proof of God's work; what matters is whether they lead people to worship the true God.

[[v15|It was given to him to give breath to the image of the beast, that the image of the beast should both speak, and cause as many as wouldn’t worship the image of the beast to be killed]]. This recalls Daniel 3, where King Nebuchadnezzar set up a golden image and commanded everyone to worship it on pain of death. Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego refused and were delivered. In John's world, statues of the emperor were set up in temples, and loyalty to the emperor was demonstrated by offering incense before them. Ancient priests sometimes used tricks to make statues seem to speak. The mark and number of the beast are studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### Revelation 13:1–4 — The Beast Rises from the Sea

verse: Revelation 13:1-4

#### Simple Explanation

John sees "a beast coming up out of the sea, having ten horns and seven heads." In the ancient world, the sea often symbolized chaos and evil. In Daniel 7, four beasts rise out of the sea, representing four empires. The beast John sees combines features of all four of Daniel's beasts: it is "like a leopard," with feet "like those of a bear," and a mouth "like the mouth of a lion." It is like all the oppressive empires of history rolled into one.

The beast has "ten crowns" on its horns and "blasphemous names" on its heads. It claims royal power and divine titles. Roman emperors were given titles like "divine," "son of god," and "lord."

Verse 2 reveals the source of its power: "The dragon gave him his power, his throne, and great authority." The beast is Satan's agent.

Verse 3 describes something astonishing: "One of his heads looked like it had been wounded fatally. His fatal wound was healed." The whole earth marvels and follows the beast. In verse 4, people worship both the dragon and the beast, saying, "Who is like the beast? Who is able to make war with him?"

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The beast is a counterfeit of Christ. Christ has many crowns (19:12); the beast has crowns. Christ received authority from the Father; the beast receives authority from the dragon. Christ was slain and rose again; the beast has a fatal wound that is healed. The world worships the beast as Christ's followers worship the Lamb.

The fatal wound that was healed has been interpreted in several ways. Some connect it with a legend that circulated after the death of Nero in A.D. 68, claiming that he would return from the dead or from hiding. Others see it as the Roman Empire's recovery after the chaotic year of civil war following Nero's death. Futurists often see it as a future event involving the antichrist. Many interpreters see a broader truth: evil empires and ideologies often seem to be defeated, only to rise again in new forms.

The cry "Who is like the beast?" is a parody of Israel's praise of God: [[Exodus 15:11|Who is like you, LORD, among the gods? Who is like you, glorious in holiness]]? The world gives to political power the worship that belongs to God alone. This happens whenever people place ultimate trust and loyalty in a nation, a leader, or a system.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage calls believers to be discerning about political and social power. Governments and leaders can do much good, and Scripture calls believers to pray for them and respect legitimate authority (Romans 13:1–7; 1 Timothy 2:1–2). But when any power demands ultimate loyalty, claims divine authority, or persecutes God's people, it takes on the character of the beast.

Believers can guard their hearts against giving any human leader, nation, or movement the devotion that belongs to God. They can ask whether their hopes and fears are shaped more by political power than by Christ's kingdom.

This passage also reminds believers that the world is often impressed by power. The question "Who is able to make war with him?" reflects the belief that might makes right. Believers know a different answer: the Lamb, who conquered through sacrificial love, is the true King.

### Revelation 13:8–10 — The Endurance and Faith of the Saints

verse: Revelation 13:8-10

#### Simple Explanation

Verse 8 describes the extent of the beast's influence: "All who dwell on the earth will worship him." But there is an important exception. Those who worship the beast are "everyone whose name has not been written from the foundation of the world in the book of life of the Lamb who has been killed."

God's people are not among the worshipers of the beast. Their names are written in the Lamb's book of life. The Lamb who was killed has secured them.

Verse 9 calls for attention: "If anyone has an ear, let him hear." This echoes the words of Christ to the seven churches.

Verse 10 gives a sober message: "If anyone is to go into captivity, he will go into captivity. If anyone is to be killed with the sword, he must be killed." Some believers will be imprisoned; some will be killed. Then comes the application: "Here is the endurance and the faith of the saints."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The phrase "from the foundation of the world" can be connected either with the writing of names in the book of life or with the Lamb who was slain. Both ideas are true in Scripture. God chose His people in Christ before the foundation of the world (Ephesians 1:4), and Christ's sacrifice was planned from before creation (1 Peter 1:19–20). The security of believers rests on God's eternal purpose and on the Lamb's sacrifice.

Verse 10 echoes Jeremiah 15:2, where God says that those destined for captivity will go into captivity and those destined for the sword will go to the sword. In Revelation, the point is that believers should not resist persecution by violence or panic, but should accept suffering with faithful endurance. Some manuscripts read the second line as a warning that whoever kills with the sword will be killed by the sword, echoing Jesus' words to Peter (Matthew 26:52). Either way, the message calls believers to patient, nonviolent faithfulness rather than taking up the beast's weapons.

"Here is the endurance and the faith of the saints" is a key phrase in Revelation (compare 14:12). In the face of overwhelming opposition, God's people are not called to fight back with worldly power, but to endure faithfully, trusting God.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can take deep comfort in knowing that their names are written in the Lamb's book of life. Their security does not depend on the outcome of political struggles or on their own strength. It rests on God's eternal love and Christ's sacrifice.

This passage calls believers to patient endurance. In a world where some will face imprisonment or death for their faith, believers are called to remain faithful rather than to compromise or retaliate. This endurance is not passive resignation, but active trust in God.

Believers can also pray for and support those who are suffering for their faith around the world, standing with them in their endurance.

### Revelation 13:16–18 — The Mark and the Number of the Beast

verse: Revelation 13:16-18

#### Simple Explanation

The second beast "causes all, the small and the great, the rich and the poor, and the free and the slave, to be given marks on their right hands or on their foreheads." Everyone in society is pressured to receive this mark.

Verse 17 describes the consequence of refusing: "no one would be able to buy or to sell unless he has that mark, which is the name of the beast or the number of his name." Those who refuse are shut out of economic life.

Verse 18 invites reflection: "Here is wisdom. He who has understanding, let him calculate the number of the beast, for it is the number of a man. His number is six hundred sixty-six."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The mark of the beast is a counterfeit of the seal of God, which was placed on the foreheads of God's servants (7:3; 14:1). Just as God's people bear His name, the beast's followers bear the beast's name. The forehead may represent thought and identity, and the hand may represent action. The mark shows whom a person belongs to and serves.

Many interpreters understand the mark as a symbol of allegiance rather than a literal physical mark, just as the seal of God on believers is not a visible tattoo. In John's world, participation in trade guilds and economic life often required honoring the emperor and the gods. Those who refused could be excluded from business. Others, especially among futurists, expect a literal mark connected with economic control in the last days. Whatever form it takes, the mark represents choosing loyalty to the beast over loyalty to God. It is not something a person receives accidentally or unknowingly; it is an expression of worship and allegiance.

Over the years, many people have claimed that various modern technologies, such as credit cards, barcodes, identification systems, or medical procedures, are the mark of the beast. Such claims have caused unnecessary fear and division. Revelation's concern is not with a particular technology but with worship and allegiance. Believers should not live in fear of accidentally receiving the mark. They should live in loyal devotion to Christ.

The number 666 has also been interpreted in many ways. In both Hebrew and Greek, letters had numerical values, so names could be expressed as numbers. A widely held view is that 666 represents the name "Nero Caesar" spelled in Hebrew letters. Interestingly, some ancient manuscripts read 616, which matches the Latin spelling of the same name in Hebrew letters. Others see 666 as symbolic: if seven is the number of completeness and perfection, then six represents falling short, and 666 represents complete failure dressed up as power. The beast, for all its claims, is merely human, "the number of a man," and falls short of God's glory. Futurists often expect the number to identify a future antichrist.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

The central question of this passage is: to whom do you belong? Every person bears the mark of the one they worship. Believers are called to bear the seal of God, living lives marked by loyalty to Christ in their thinking and their actions.

This passage also recognizes that faithfulness can be costly. Refusing to compromise may lead to economic hardship or exclusion. Believers today may face situations where faithfulness to Christ costs them opportunities, income, or acceptance. Revelation encourages them to remain faithful, trusting God to provide.

Believers can also respond to fear-based speculation about the mark of the beast with wisdom and calm. Rather than being anxious about technology or conspiracy theories, they can focus on wholehearted devotion to Christ. Those who belong to the Lamb have nothing to fear.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Revelation 13 reveals how evil works in the world. Satan uses political power, represented by the beast from the sea, and deceptive religion or ideology, represented by the beast from the earth, to draw people away from God. These powers demand worship, persecute those who refuse, and use signs, propaganda, and economic pressure to enforce loyalty.

This chapter calls believers to discernment. They must recognize when any power, whether a government, a leader, a movement, or an ideology, demands the loyalty that belongs to God alone. They must also recognize deception, even when it looks lamb-like and performs impressive signs.

The chapter also calls believers to endurance. Faithfulness to Christ may bring captivity, the sword, or exclusion from economic life. Yet believers' names are written in the Lamb's book of life, and their security rests in Him. They are called not to retaliate with the beast's weapons, but to endure with faith.

Finally, the chapter reminds believers that every person bears a mark of allegiance. The question is not whether we will worship, but whom. Believers are called to bear the seal of God, belonging wholly to the Lamb who was slain, in their minds and in their actions. The beast's power is limited, its time is short, and its number falls short. The Lamb will reign forever.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord God, You alone are worthy of worship. Give me discernment to recognize every power that demands the loyalty that belongs only to You. Guard my heart from placing my ultimate trust in any leader, nation, or system. Protect me from deception, even when it looks harmless or impressive. Thank You that my name is written in the Lamb's book of life, and that my security rests in Your eternal love. Give me patient endurance and faith when faithfulness is costly. Help me not to fight with the world's weapons but to trust You. I pray for all who suffer for Your name; strengthen and sustain them. Let my thoughts and my actions bear the mark of my loyalty to You, and free me from fear, for I belong to the Lamb. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
