# Colossians 1
theme: The Supremacy of Christ
main: Paul thanks God for the Colossians' faith, love, and hope, which grew from the gospel that Epaphras brought them, and prays that they will be filled with the knowledge of God's will, live in a way that pleases Him, and be strengthened with patience and joy. He celebrates what the Father has done: rescuing believers from the domain of darkness and transferring them into the kingdom of His beloved Son. Then, in a majestic hymn, Paul proclaims that Christ is the image of the invisible God, the one through whom and for whom all things were created, the one who holds all things together, the head of the church, and the one through whom God reconciled all things by the blood of His cross. Paul rejoices to serve this gospel and to proclaim the mystery now revealed: Christ in you, the hope of glory.

## Chapter Background and Story

Paul had never visited Colossae, but he had heard about the church through Epaphras, who had planted it. The report was largely encouraging: the Colossians had genuine faith and love. But there was also a danger. Some teachers were pressing a mixture of ideas that, whatever their details, made Christ seem insufficient. They suggested that believers needed special knowledge, visions, strict rules, or the help of angels and spiritual powers to reach spiritual fullness.

Paul's response begins not with an attack on the false teaching but with a glorious portrait of Christ. If the Colossians truly understood who Christ is, they would see that nothing else is needed. Verses 15–20 are often called the "Christ hymn" of Colossians. They may have been an early Christian hymn or confession that Paul quotes or composed. They contain some of the highest statements about Christ in the New Testament.

### Thanksgiving (1:3–8)

[[v3|We give thanks to God the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, praying always for you]], [[v4|having heard of your faith in Christ Jesus and of the love which you have toward all the saints]], [[v5|because of the hope which is laid up for you in the heavens, of which you heard before in the word of the truth of the Good News]]. Faith, love, and hope appear together here, as they do in 1 Corinthians 13:13 and 1 Thessalonians 1:3. Faith is directed toward Christ, love toward fellow believers, and hope toward what God has prepared. Notice that hope comes first in Paul's reasoning: their faith and love spring from the secure hope "laid up" for them in heaven.

[[v6|which has come to you, even as it is in all the world and is bearing fruit and growing, as it does in you also, since the day you heard and knew the grace of God in truth]]. The gospel is alive. Like a healthy plant, it bears fruit and grows wherever it goes. The Colossians were part of a worldwide movement.

[[v7|even as you learned from Epaphras our beloved fellow servant, who is a faithful servant of Christ on your behalf]], [[v8|who also declared to us your love in the Spirit]]. Paul affirms Epaphras as a trustworthy teacher. The gospel the Colossians had received from him was the true gospel. They did not need a different one.

### Paul's Prayer (1:9–12)

[[v9|For this cause, we also, since the day we heard this, don’t cease praying and making requests for you, that you may be filled with the knowledge of his will in all spiritual wisdom and understanding]]. The false teachers offered special knowledge. Paul prays for true knowledge: understanding God's will, with spiritual wisdom. This knowledge is practical; its purpose is a changed life.

[[v10|that you may walk worthily of the Lord, to please him in all respects, bearing fruit in every good work and increasing in the knowledge of God]]. Knowledge leads to living in a way that pleases the Lord, which leads to good works and to deeper knowledge of God. It is a growing cycle.

[[v11|strengthened with all power, according to the might of his glory, for all endurance and perseverance with joy]]. God's mighty power is given not only for dramatic deeds but for endurance and patience. And endurance is to be accompanied by joy.

[[v12|giving thanks to the Father, who made us fit to be partakers of the inheritance of the saints in light]]. Believers did not qualify themselves. The Father made them fit to share in the inheritance of God's people. The rescue that made this possible is studied below.

### The Supremacy of Christ (1:15–20)

The great hymn about Christ is studied below in two parts.

### Reconciled and Presented Holy (1:21–23)

[[v21|You, being in past times alienated and enemies in your mind in your evil deeds]], [[v22|yet now he has reconciled in the body of his flesh through death, to present you holy and without defect and blameless before him]]. The cosmic reconciliation described in the hymn becomes personal. The Colossians were once far from God and hostile toward Him. Now, through Christ's death in His real, physical body, they have been reconciled. The goal is that they will stand before God holy, without defect, and free from accusation.

[[v23|if it is so that you continue in the faith, grounded and steadfast, and not moved away from the hope of the Good News which you heard, which was proclaimed in all creation under heaven, of which I, Paul, was made a servant]]. Paul encourages perseverance. Those who are truly reconciled will continue in the faith, firmly established like a building on a foundation, not shifting away to other teachings. Christian traditions have different views on whether a true believer can finally fall away, but all agree on the practical call here: hold fast to the gospel you received.

### Paul's Ministry and the Mystery (1:24–29)

[[v24|Now I rejoice in my sufferings for your sake, and fill up on my part that which is lacking of the afflictions of Christ in my flesh for his body’s sake, which is the assembly]]. This is a difficult verse. Paul does not mean that Christ's sacrifice for sin was incomplete; he has just said that Christ reconciled all things through His cross. Most interpreters understand Paul to mean that Christ's people continue to suffer in the world as they carry the gospel, and that these sufferings are in some sense Christ's own, since He is united with His body. Jesus asked Paul on the Damascus road, [[Acts 9:4|why do you persecute me?]]. There is a measure of suffering appointed for the church before the end, and Paul gladly bore his share for others' sake.

[[v25|of which I was made a servant according to the stewardship of God which was given me toward you to fulfill the word of God]], [[v26|the mystery which has been hidden for ages and generations. But now it has been revealed to his saints]]. The content of the mystery, and Paul's goal in ministry, are studied below.

[[v29|for which I also labor, striving according to his working, which works in me mightily]]. Paul worked hard, but the energy came from God working powerfully in him.

## Important Verses Explained

### Colossians 1:13–14 — Rescued from Darkness into the Kingdom

verse: Colossians 1:13-14

#### Simple Explanation

Paul describes what God the Father has done for believers. "Who delivered us out of the power of darkness." "The power of darkness" refers to the realm where sin, evil, and spiritual forces opposed to God hold people captive. God has rescued believers from that realm. The word "delivered" suggests a rescue from danger, like being pulled out of a burning building.

"And translated us into the Kingdom of the Son of his love." "Translated" means transferred, moved from one place to another. In the ancient world, conquering kings sometimes moved whole populations from one land to another. God has moved believers out of the kingdom of darkness and into the kingdom of His beloved Son. They have a new King and a new home.

Verse 14 explains what this means: "in whom we have our redemption, the forgiveness of our sins." Redemption means being set free by the payment of a price. In Christ, believers have been set free, and the heart of that freedom is the forgiveness of their sins.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses describe salvation as a change of kingdoms. Before Christ, people lived under the dominion of darkness. They were not merely making bad choices; they were under a power they could not escape on their own. Jesus described His mission in similar terms: He came to bind the strong man and plunder his house (Mark 3:27), and to set the captives free (Luke 4:18).

The phrase "the Son of his love" emphasizes the Father's love for the Son. Believers are brought into the kingdom of the one the Father loves supremely, and in Him they too are loved.

Notice that both verbs are in the past tense. The rescue and transfer have already happened. Believers do not need to fear the powers of darkness or seek special rituals to protect themselves from spiritual forces, as the false teachers in Colossae may have suggested. They already belong to Christ's kingdom.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many people live in fear of spiritual forces, bad luck, curses, or unseen powers. These verses declare that believers have been rescued from the dominion of darkness and now belong to Christ. They do not need to live in fear.

This truth also changes identity. Believers are citizens of Christ's kingdom. Their loyalty, values, and hope belong to Him. When old patterns of sin or fear try to pull them back, they can remember that they no longer live under the old ruler.

Finally, these verses remind believers of the heart of salvation: the forgiveness of sins. Whatever burdens of guilt a person carries, they can be lifted in Christ.

### Colossians 1:15–17 — The Image of the Invisible God

verse: Colossians 1:15-17

#### Simple Explanation

Paul begins the great hymn with breathtaking statements about Christ.

"He is the image of the invisible God." God is spirit and cannot be seen with human eyes. But in Christ, the invisible God has been made visible. An image is an exact representation. To see Christ is to see what God is like. Jesus said, [[John 14:9|He who has seen me has seen the Father]].

"The firstborn of all creation." This phrase does not mean that Christ was the first being created. In the Bible, "firstborn" often refers to rank and inheritance, the one who holds the place of honor and authority. God said of David, who was the youngest son, [[Psalms 89:27|I will also appoint him my firstborn, the highest of the kings of the earth]]. Christ holds supreme rank over all creation.

Verse 16 explains why: "For by him all things were created." Christ is not part of creation; He is the Creator. "In the heavens and on the earth, visible things and invisible things, whether thrones or dominions or principalities or powers." Everything, including every spiritual power the false teachers feared or honored, was made by Christ. "All things have been created through him and for him." Christ is the agent of creation and its goal. Everything exists for His glory.

Verse 17: "He is before all things, and in him all things are held together." Christ existed before creation. And He continues to hold the universe together. Without Him, everything would fall apart.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses make some of the highest claims about Christ anywhere in Scripture. They echo John 1:1–3, which says that the Word was with God and was God, and that all things were made through Him. They also echo Hebrews 1:2–3, which says that God made the universe through the Son and that the Son upholds all things by the word of His power.

In the early centuries of the church, some teachers, such as Arius in the fourth century, argued that "firstborn of all creation" meant that Christ was a created being, the first and highest of God's creatures. The church rejected this view at the Council of Nicaea in A.D. 325, affirming that the Son is "begotten, not made," fully God, of one being with the Father. The very next verse supports this, because it says all things were created by Him; He cannot be both the Creator of all things and one of the created things.

For the Colossians, these verses answered the false teaching directly. Angels and spiritual powers are not rivals to Christ or necessary mediators. They are His creations, under His authority. Believers who have Christ have the Creator of all things.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses invite believers to see Christ in His full glory. He is not merely a great teacher or a good example. He is the Creator of the universe, the one who holds all things together, and the one for whom everything exists. That vision of Christ should lead to worship.

It also brings security. The one who holds the universe together holds believers' lives together too. When life feels chaotic or falling apart, believers can remember that "in him all things are held together."

Finally, these verses give meaning to all of life. If all things were created for Christ, then work, relationships, creativity, and the beauty of the natural world all find their purpose in Him. Every part of life can be lived for His glory.

### Colossians 1:18–20 — Head of the Church and Reconciler of All Things

verse: Colossians 1:18-20

#### Simple Explanation

The second part of the hymn moves from creation to new creation.

"He is the head of the body, the assembly." Christ is the head of the church, which is His body. As the head directs and gives life to the body, Christ leads and sustains His people.

"Who is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead." Christ is the beginning of the new creation. He is the "firstborn from the dead," the first to rise from the dead with a resurrection body, never to die again. His resurrection is the first of many; all who belong to Him will follow (1 Corinthians 15:20–23).

"That in all things he might have the preeminence." Christ is first in everything, in creation and in redemption.

Verse 19: "For all the fullness was pleased to dwell in him." God, in all His fullness, chose to dwell in Christ. The false teachers may have used the word "fullness" for a series of spiritual beings or levels between God and humanity. Paul says the fullness of God is found entirely in Christ.

Verse 20: "and through him to reconcile all things to himself by him, whether things on the earth or things in the heavens, having made peace through the blood of his cross." God's purpose is to reconcile all things to Himself through Christ. The means of reconciliation is "the blood of his cross," Christ's sacrificial death.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The hymn presents Christ as supreme in two creations: the original creation (vv15–17) and the new creation (vv18–20). He is the firstborn of all creation and the firstborn from the dead. He is the Creator and the Redeemer.

The reconciliation of "all things" is a vast vision. Sin did not only break the relationship between humanity and God; it disordered the whole creation (Romans 8:19–22). Through the cross, God is setting everything right. Christians have understood this in different ways. Most understand it to mean that God will restore creation and bring all things under Christ's rightful rule, with every hostile power defeated and subdued (Colossians 2:15). Some hope it points to the eventual salvation of all people, though most Christian traditions have held that Scripture also teaches a final judgment for those who reject Christ. All agree that the cross is the center of God's plan to heal a broken world.

The phrase "made peace through the blood of his cross" is remarkable. Peace with God and the healing of creation come not through human effort, special knowledge, or spiritual rituals, but through Christ's sacrifice.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses call believers to let Christ have first place in everything: their priorities, their affections, their decisions, and their worship. If He is preeminent in the universe, He should be preeminent in their lives.

They also bring hope in a broken world. Injustice, suffering, environmental damage, and broken relationships are not the final word. Through the cross, God is reconciling all things. Believers can work for peace, justice, and the care of creation as signs of the reconciliation Christ is bringing.

Finally, these verses remind believers that Christ's resurrection guarantees their own. He is the firstborn from the dead, and those who belong to Him will share His resurrection life.

### Colossians 1:27–28 — Christ in You, the Hope of Glory

verse: Colossians 1:27-28

#### Simple Explanation

Paul describes the mystery God has now revealed. In Paul's letters, a "mystery" is a truth once hidden in God's plan but now made known. God was "pleased to make known what are the riches of the glory of this mystery among the Gentiles."

What is the mystery? "Christ in you, the hope of glory." The astonishing truth is that Christ Himself lives in His people, including Gentiles who were once far from God. Through the Holy Spirit, the Lord of all creation dwells in ordinary believers. And His presence within them is the guarantee of future glory. Because Christ is in them now, they can be sure they will share His glory when He returns.

Verse 28 describes Paul's response: "We proclaim him." Paul's message is not a philosophy, a set of rules, or a spiritual technique. It is a person: Christ. "Admonishing every man and teaching every man in all wisdom." Admonishing means warning and correcting; teaching means instructing in truth. Both are needed.

"That we may present every man perfect in Christ Jesus." "Perfect" here means mature, complete. Paul's goal is that every believer would grow to full maturity in Christ. Notice the repetition of "every man," three times in this verse. The false teachers may have offered special knowledge to a select few. Paul's gospel is for everyone.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

"Christ in you" is one of the most profound truths of the Christian faith. Believers are not only forgiven and declared righteous; they are indwelt by Christ Himself. Jesus promised that He and the Father would come and make their home with those who love Him (John 14:23). Paul wrote, [[Galatians 2:20|Christ lives in me]]. This indwelling presence is the source of transformation, strength, and hope.

The phrase "the hope of glory" links the present and the future. Christ's presence in believers now is the down payment of the glory they will share with Him forever. Colossians 3:4 says, [[Colossians 3:4|When Christ, our life, is revealed, then you will also be revealed with him in glory]].

Paul's goal of presenting "every man perfect in Christ" shows that spiritual maturity is not for an elite few but for all believers. Every person who belongs to Christ can grow toward maturity, and the church's ministry exists to help them.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers who feel spiritually inadequate or unimportant can take great comfort in this truth: Christ Himself lives in them. They are not ordinary in God's eyes. They carry the presence of the Lord of the universe.

This truth also gives hope. Whatever struggles, weaknesses, or sufferings believers face, Christ in them is the guarantee of future glory. The present is not the final chapter.

Finally, Paul's ministry goal can shape the way believers serve one another. In families, friendships, and churches, the aim is to help every person grow to maturity in Christ, through teaching, encouragement, and loving correction. No one is beyond growth, and no one should be left behind.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Colossians 1 lifts believers' eyes to see the greatness of Jesus Christ. Paul begins by thanking God for the Colossians' faith, love, and hope, and by praying that they will grow in knowledge, fruitfulness, endurance, and joy. These prayers remind believers what matters most: knowing God's will, living in a way that pleases Him, and finding strength for patient endurance.

The chapter then reveals what God has done. He has rescued believers from the domain of darkness and transferred them into the kingdom of His beloved Son, where they have redemption and forgiveness. They do not need to fear spiritual powers or seek special rituals for protection. They belong to Christ.

At the heart of the chapter is a majestic portrait of Christ. He is the image of the invisible God, the Creator of all things, the one who holds the universe together, the head of the church, the firstborn from the dead, and the one in whom all the fullness of God dwells. Through His cross, God is reconciling all things to Himself. This vision of Christ answers every claim that believers need something more. In Christ, they have everything.

Finally, Paul celebrates the mystery now revealed: Christ in you, the hope of glory. The Lord of all creation lives in His people and guarantees their future glory. This truth gives every believer dignity, hope, and purpose. Like Paul, believers can proclaim Christ, help one another grow to maturity, and labor with the strength that God supplies. Christ is supreme, Christ is sufficient, and Christ is in His people.

## Chapter Prayer

Father, thank You for rescuing me from the domain of darkness and bringing me into the kingdom of Your beloved Son, in whom I have redemption and the forgiveness of my sins. Fill me with the knowledge of Your will in all spiritual wisdom and understanding, so that I may live in a way that pleases You, bear fruit in every good work, and grow in knowing You. Strengthen me with all power for endurance and patience with joy. Lord Jesus, You are the image of the invisible God, the Creator of all things, and the one who holds all things together. You are the head of the church and the firstborn from the dead. Take first place in every part of my life. Thank You for making peace through the blood of Your cross. Thank You that You live in me, my hope of glory. Help me to proclaim You and to help others grow to maturity in You. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
