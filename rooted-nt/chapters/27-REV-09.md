# Revelation 9
theme: The Locusts from the Abyss and the Armies from the Euphrates
main: When the fifth angel sounds his trumpet, John sees a star that had fallen from heaven to earth, and it is given the key to the shaft of the abyss. When the abyss is opened, smoke rises like a great furnace, darkening the sun and the air, and out of the smoke come locusts given power like scorpions. They are told not to harm the grass or trees, but only the people who do not have the seal of God on their foreheads. They are not allowed to kill, only to torment for five months, and in those days people will seek death but not find it. The locusts look like horses prepared for battle, with crowns like gold, human faces, women's hair, lions' teeth, iron breastplates, and wings that roar like chariots; their king is the angel of the abyss, called Abaddon in Hebrew and Apollyon in Greek, meaning Destroyer. This is the first woe. When the sixth angel sounds, a voice from the golden altar commands that the four angels bound at the great river Euphrates be released. They had been prepared for that very hour to kill a third of mankind. John hears that the mounted troops number two hundred million. The horses have heads like lions, and from their mouths come fire, smoke, and sulfur, which kill a third of mankind; their tails are like snakes with heads that cause harm. Yet the rest of mankind who survive these plagues do not repent of worshiping demons and idols, nor of their murders, sorceries, sexual immorality, and thefts.

## Chapter Background and Story

Revelation 9 describes the fifth and sixth trumpets, which are also the first two of the three "woes" announced by the eagle in 8:13. These judgments are more intense than the first four. While the first four trumpets affected the natural world, the fifth and sixth strike human beings directly, and they involve demonic forces.

The imagery in this chapter is among the most vivid and strange in the whole book. Locusts with human faces and scorpion tails, armies of two hundred million, horses breathing fire: these pictures are meant to be terrifying. They draw heavily on the Old Testament, especially the locust plague of Joel 1–2, where an army of locusts is described like horses and chariots, and the plague of locusts in Egypt (Exodus 10:12–15).

### How the Fifth and Sixth Trumpets Are Understood

These images have been interpreted in many ways. Some **futurist** interpreters take them as literal future events, either supernatural demonic creatures or, in some modern readings, descriptions of modern warfare seen through ancient eyes. **Historicist** interpreters have linked the locusts and the armies with various invasions that struck the Roman Empire and Christendom from the east over the centuries. **Preterist** interpreters connect them with first-century events, such as the threat of the Parthian armies beyond the Euphrates or the turmoil surrounding the Jewish war with Rome. **Idealist** interpreters understand them as symbols of the demonic torment and destructive violence that sin unleashes in every age, and of God's permitting such forces to judge a world that has rejected Him.

All of these approaches share important common ground. The forces described are real and destructive, they are demonic in character, and yet they act only within limits set by God. And the purpose of these judgments is to call people to repentance, a purpose the world tragically refuses.

### The Locusts from the Abyss (9:1–12)

The opening of the abyss and the limits placed on the locusts are studied below.

[[v5|They were given power, not to kill them, but to torment them for five months. Their torment was like the torment of a scorpion when it strikes a person]]. The torment is limited in both severity and time. "Five months" may correspond to the natural life span of a locust, or the season in which locusts typically appear, suggesting a limited period.

[[v6|In those days people will seek death, and will in no way find it. They will desire to die, and death will flee from them]]. The torment is so severe that people long for death. This is a picture of the misery that comes when people are handed over to the destructive forces they have chosen to serve.

[[v7|The shapes of the locusts were like horses prepared for war. On their heads were something like golden crowns, and their faces were like people’s faces]]. [[v8|They had hair like women’s hair, and their teeth were like those of lions]]. [[v9|They had breastplates like breastplates of iron. The sound of their wings was like the sound of many chariots and horses rushing to war]]. [[v10|They have tails like those of scorpions, with stingers. In their tails they have power to harm men for five months]]. The description combines features from different creatures into a nightmarish image. Joel had described locusts that look like horses and sound like chariots (Joel 2:4–5), with teeth like a lion's (Joel 1:6). Each feature adds to the sense of menace: they are organized like an army, they claim authority (crowns), they have an intelligent and almost human quality (faces), they are fierce (lions' teeth), they are protected (iron breastplates), and they are overwhelming (the roar of chariots).

[[v11|They have over them as king the angel of the abyss. His name in Hebrew is “Abaddon”, but in Greek, he has the name “Apollyon”.]] Natural locusts have no king (Proverbs 30:27), but these do. "Abaddon" in Hebrew and "Apollyon" in Greek both mean "Destruction" or "Destroyer." Some identify this angel with Satan; others see him as a powerful demonic being under Satan. Some scholars note that "Apollyon" may also be a play on the name of the god Apollo, whom the emperor Domitian liked to be associated with. If so, John may be hinting that the powers worshiped by the empire are actually destructive.

[[v12|The first woe is past. Behold, there are still two woes coming after this]].

### The Armies from the Euphrates (9:13–19)

[[v13|The sixth angel sounded. I heard a voice from the horns of the golden altar which is before God,]] The voice comes from the golden altar where the prayers of the saints were offered (8:3–5). This judgment, too, is connected with God's response to the prayers of His people.

[[v14|saying to the sixth angel who had the trumpet, “Free the four angels who are bound at the great river Euphrates!”]] The Euphrates River marked the eastern boundary of the Roman Empire and, in the Old Testament, the boundary of the land promised to Israel. Beyond it lay the lands of Israel's great enemies, Assyria and Babylon, and in John's day the feared Parthian Empire. Invasion from beyond the Euphrates was a symbol of overwhelming judgment.

[[v15|The four angels were freed who had been prepared for that hour and day and month and year, so that they might kill one third of mankind]]. The four angels are bound until released. They are evil powers held in restraint by God. The precise timing, "that hour and day and month and year," shows that God is sovereign over the exact moment of judgment.

[[v16|The number of the armies of the horsemen was two hundred million. I heard the number of them]]. The Greek literally speaks of two myriads of myriads, a vast army beyond imagining. In John's world, such a number was almost unthinkable and likely symbolizes an overwhelming, innumerable force.

[[v17|Thus I saw the horses in the vision and those who sat on them, having breastplates of fiery red, hyacinth blue, and sulfur yellow; and the horses’ heads resembled lions’ heads. Out of their mouths proceed fire, smoke, and sulfur]]. [[v18|By these three plagues, one third of mankind was killed: by the fire, the smoke, and the sulfur, which proceeded out of their mouths]]. [[v19|For the power of the horses is in their mouths and in their tails. For their tails are like serpents, and have heads; and with them they harm]]. Fire and sulfur recall the destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah (Genesis 19:24) and point to the fiery judgment of the lake of fire (Revelation 19:20; 20:10). Like the locusts, these horses are monstrous, combining lion and serpent. They are demonic powers of death. The world's refusal to repent is studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### Revelation 9:1–4 — The Abyss Opened, God's People Protected

verse: Revelation 9:1-4

#### Simple Explanation

When the fifth angel sounds, John sees "a star from the sky which had fallen to the earth." In Revelation, stars sometimes represent angels (1:20). This fallen star is given "the key to the pit of the abyss." The abyss, or bottomless pit, is the place where evil spirits are confined (Luke 8:31; Revelation 20:1–3).

Notice that the key "was given to him." He does not seize it. Even the opening of the abyss happens only by God's permission.

When the abyss is opened, "smoke went up out of the pit, like the smoke from a burning furnace," darkening the sun and the air. Out of the smoke come locusts with the power of scorpions.

Verse 4 contains a crucial limit: "They were told that they should not hurt the grass of the earth, neither any green thing, neither any tree, but only those people who don’t have God’s seal on their foreheads." The locusts are not ordinary insects; they leave plants alone. And they cannot harm those who belong to God.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The fallen star has been understood in different ways. Some see it as Satan, who fell from heaven (Luke 10:18; Isaiah 14:12). Others see it as an angel sent by God to carry out His command, since an angel later holds the key to the abyss and binds Satan (Revelation 20:1). Either way, the key is given by God. Evil can only act within the boundaries He sets.

The most important truth in this passage is the protection of God's people. The servants of God were sealed in chapter 7 precisely so that they would be protected from these judgments. The demonic forces released from the abyss cannot touch those who bear God's seal. This echoes the Exodus, where the plagues struck Egypt but God protected His people in Goshen (Exodus 8:22–23; 9:4, 26).

This does not mean believers will never suffer. Revelation is clear that many will suffer persecution and even death. But it means that the demonic torment unleashed on a rebellious world has no power over those who belong to God. Spiritual darkness cannot claim them, because they belong to Christ.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can take comfort in knowing that evil spiritual forces are real but limited. They act only within the boundaries God allows, and they cannot ultimately harm those who belong to Him. As John wrote, [[1 John 4:4|greater is he who is in you than he who is in the world]].

This passage also reminds believers that life apart from God is not neutral. When people reject God, they open themselves to forces that torment and destroy. Sin promises freedom but leads to bondage and misery. This can move believers to compassion for those trapped in destructive patterns and to pray for their freedom.

Finally, believers can rest in the seal of God. Their security does not depend on their own strength but on the One who has marked them as His own.

### Revelation 9:20–21 — They Did Not Repent

verse: Revelation 9:20-21

#### Simple Explanation

After all the devastation of the six trumpets, John records a tragic response: "The rest of mankind, who were not killed with these plagues, didn’t repent of the works of their hands."

They continued to worship "demons, and the idols of gold, and of silver, and of brass, and of stone, and of wood, which can’t see, hear, or walk." They kept trusting in lifeless idols that could not help them, and behind those idols stood demonic powers.

Verse 21 lists their other sins: "They didn’t repent of their murders, their sorceries, their sexual immorality, or their thefts." The worship of false gods led to the breakdown of moral life: violence, occult practices, immorality, and dishonesty.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal the purpose of the trumpet judgments: to bring people to repentance. God's judgments in history are warnings, calls to turn back before final judgment comes. But the hardened human heart refuses to turn.

This echoes the story of Pharaoh, who hardened his heart again and again in spite of the plagues (Exodus 7–11). It also shows a pattern described by Paul in Romans 1: when people exchange the glory of God for idols, they are given over to every kind of wrongdoing (Romans 1:21–32). Idolatry and immorality are closely connected. What people worship shapes how they live.

The description of idols that "can’t see, hear, or walk" echoes Psalm 115:4–8 and Daniel 5:23, which mock idols as powerless. The prophets pointed out the foolishness of trusting in things made by human hands. Yet behind idolatry lies something more sinister: demonic powers that deceive and enslave (1 Corinthians 10:19–20).

The word "sorceries" translates a Greek word related to "pharmakeia," which referred to magic, witchcraft, and the use of drugs or potions in occult practices. It represents any attempt to manipulate spiritual powers apart from God.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage is a sobering reminder of the hardness of the human heart. Suffering alone does not always lead people to God. Many people, even in the face of great hardship, continue to cling to what cannot save them. Believers can pray earnestly for hearts to be softened, knowing that only God's grace can bring true repentance.

The list of idols may seem ancient, but idolatry takes many forms today. Money, success, pleasure, power, technology, and even good things can become idols when they take God's place. Believers can examine their own hearts and turn from anything that competes with God for their trust and devotion.

Finally, this passage highlights the urgency of repentance. God's patience is great, but it is not endless. Today is the day to turn to Him. Believers can share this message with love, urging others to turn to the God who forgives and restores.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Revelation 9 reveals the destructive power of evil when it is unleashed on a world that has rejected God. The locusts from the abyss and the armies from the Euphrates are terrifying images of demonic torment and violent destruction. These are not merely ancient fantasies but pictures of what happens when people turn from God and open themselves to the powers of darkness.

Yet the chapter also shows that these powers are limited. The key to the abyss is given by God. The locusts are told whom they may and may not harm. Their torment lasts for a limited time. The four angels are released only at the precise hour God has set. Evil is real, but it is never in control.

Most comforting of all, those who bear God's seal are protected. The demonic forces cannot harm them. Believers can face spiritual darkness with confidence, knowing that they belong to Christ and that He is greater than every power of evil.

The chapter ends with a tragic warning. In spite of all these judgments, people refuse to repent. They cling to idols and continue in violence, sorcery, immorality, and theft. Believers can learn from this to take sin seriously, to examine their own hearts for idols, and to respond quickly to God's call to repentance. They can also pray for those who are hardened, asking God to open their eyes and draw them to Himself.

Lord God, thank You that You are sovereign over every power, and that even the forces of darkness act only within the limits You set. Thank You that I bear Your seal and belong to You, and that no evil power can take me from Your hand. Protect me from the deceptions of the enemy, and keep me close to You. Search my heart for every idol, anything that takes Your place, and give me grace to turn from it. Soften my heart so that I respond quickly when You call me to repent. I pray for those who are trapped in destructive patterns and who cling to things that cannot save them; open their eyes, break their chains, and draw them to Yourself. Give me compassion, courage, and urgency to share Your hope. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
