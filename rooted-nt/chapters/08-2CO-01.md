# 2 Corinthians 1
theme: The God of All Comfort and the Yes of His Promises
main: Paul opens his letter by praising the Father of mercies and God of all comfort, who comforts His people in every trouble so that they can comfort others. He tells of a crushing affliction in Asia that made him despair of life, which taught him to rely not on himself but on God who raises the dead, and he thanks the Corinthians for their prayers. Paul then explains his changed travel plans, insisting that he did not act carelessly, because the gospel he preached is not "yes and no." In Christ, all God's promises are "Yes," and God has anointed, sealed, and given His Spirit as a guarantee to those who belong to Him. Paul delayed his visit to spare them pain, for he is not a ruler over their faith but a worker for their joy.

## Chapter Background and Story

2 Corinthians is Paul's most personal letter. Between 1 and 2 Corinthians, Paul's relationship with the church had gone through a painful time. He had made a brief, sorrowful visit to Corinth that did not go well (2:1). He had written a severe letter "out of much affliction and anguish of heart... with many tears" (2:4). He had changed his travel plans, and some Corinthians accused him of being unreliable. Meanwhile, rival teachers were questioning his authority.

Paul begins not by defending himself but by praising God. The theme of comfort in suffering runs through the opening chapter. The word "comfort" (paraklesis, related to the Holy Spirit's title "Counselor" or "Comforter") appears ten times in verses 3–7 alone.

### Greeting (1:1–2)

Paul writes as [[v1|an apostle of Christ Jesus through the will of God]], together with [[v1|Timothy our brother]], to the church in Corinth and [[v1|all the saints who are in the whole of Achaia]], the whole Roman province of southern Greece. [[v2|Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ]].

### The God of All Comfort (1:3–7)

Paul's praise is studied below. He continues: [[v5|For as the sufferings of Christ abound to us, even so our comfort also abounds through Christ]]. Believers share in Christ's sufferings as they follow Him in a world that opposed Him. But in the same way, they share abundantly in His comfort. [[v6|But if we are afflicted, it is for your comfort and salvation. If we are comforted, it is for your comfort, which produces in you the patient enduring of the same sufferings which we also suffer]]. Paul's sufferings and comforts were not only for himself; they benefited the Corinthians. [[v7|Our hope for you is steadfast, knowing that, since you are partakers of the sufferings, so you are also of the comfort]].

### Delivered from Deadly Peril (1:8–11)

Paul's account of his affliction in Asia is studied below. He adds, [[v11|you also helping together on our behalf by your supplication; that, for the gift given to us by means of many, thanks may be given by many persons on your behalf]]. Paul valued the Corinthians' prayers. When many people pray, God's answer leads many people to give thanks.

### Paul's Integrity and Changed Plans (1:12–24)

[[v12|For our boasting is this: the testimony of our conscience that in holiness and sincerity of God, not in fleshly wisdom but in the grace of God, we behaved ourselves in the world, and more abundantly toward you]]. Paul's conscience was clear. He had acted with integrity, by God's grace, not by worldly scheming.

Paul had planned to visit Corinth twice, once on his way to Macedonia and again on his way back (1:15–16). But he changed his plans. Some accused him of being fickle: [[v17|When I therefore planned this, did I show fickleness? Or the things that I plan, do I plan according to the flesh, that with me there should be the “Yes, yes” and the “No, no?”]] They suggested Paul said "yes" and "no" at the same time, making promises he did not keep.

Paul answers by pointing to God's faithfulness: [[v18|But as God is faithful, our word toward you was not “Yes and no.”]] [[v19|For the Son of God, Jesus Christ, who was preached among you by us—by me, Silvanus, and Timothy—was not “Yes and no,” but in him is “Yes.”]] The message Paul preached was completely reliable, because Christ Himself is God's "Yes." This is studied below, together with God's work of establishing believers.

[[v23|But I call God for a witness to my soul, that to spare you, I didn’t come to Corinth]]. Paul explains the real reason for his changed plans: he wanted to spare them another painful visit. He did not want to come with harshness. [[v24|We don’t control your faith, but are fellow workers with you for your joy. For you stand firm in faith]]. Paul did not see himself as a ruler over their faith. He was a co-worker for their joy. Leaders in the church are not meant to dominate believers but to serve their growth and joy.

## Important Verses Explained

### 2 Corinthians 1:3–4 — The God of All Comfort

verse: 2 Corinthians 1:3-4

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been through great suffering. He had faced deadly danger in Asia, conflict with the Corinthian church, and opposition from false teachers. Yet he begins his letter not with complaints but with praise.

[[v3|Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ]]. "Blessed" means praised. Paul praises God, describing Him in relation to Jesus. God is not a distant deity; He is the Father of the Lord Jesus Christ.

[[v3|the Father of mercies and God of all comfort]]. Paul gives God two beautiful titles. He is "the Father of mercies," the source of all compassion. And He is the "God of all comfort," the source of every kind of comfort. Every comfort believers receive ultimately comes from Him.

[[v4|who comforts us in all our affliction]]. God does not always remove affliction, but He comforts His people in it. The word "comfort" means more than sympathy. It includes encouragement, strength, and help. And God comforts "in all" afflictions. No trouble is too great or too small for His comfort.

[[v4|that we may be able to comfort those who are in any affliction, through the comfort with which we ourselves are comforted by God]]. God's comfort has a purpose beyond the one who receives it. Those whom God comforts become able to comfort others. Believers who have been through suffering and received God's comfort can share that comfort with others who are hurting. They understand, and they can point others to the same God.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses reveal the compassionate heart of God. He is the Father of mercies, who cares about His children's pain. Isaiah writes, [[Isaiah 66:13|As one whom his mother comforts, so I will comfort you]]. God's comfort is personal and tender.

They also show that suffering can have purpose. God does not waste His people's pain. Through suffering, they receive comfort that equips them to help others. A person who has grieved, been ill, or faced loss can often comfort others in a way that no one else can.

Finally, these verses show the connection between receiving and giving. Believers receive comfort from God and pass it on. The church becomes a community of comfort, in which people who have been comforted comfort others.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to turn to God in their suffering. Whatever believers face, God is the God of all comfort. They can bring their pain to Him in prayer, through Scripture, and through the support of other believers.

They also encourage believers to use their experiences to comfort others. Believers who have walked through grief, illness, divorce, loss, depression, or other hardships can reach out to others facing similar trials, sharing the comfort they received.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to see that their pain can have meaning. While suffering is never good in itself, God can use it to make believers more compassionate and more able to help others.

### 2 Corinthians 1:8–9 — Trusting God Who Raises the Dead

verse: 2 Corinthians 1:8-9

#### Simple Explanation

Paul wanted the Corinthians to know about a terrible experience he had gone through. [[v8|For we don’t desire to have you uninformed, brothers, concerning our affliction which happened to us in Asia]]. "Asia" refers to the Roman province in western Asia Minor, where Ephesus was located. Paul does not say exactly what happened. It may have been the riot in Ephesus (Acts 19), a serious illness, imprisonment, or severe persecution.

[[v8|that we were weighed down exceedingly, beyond our power, so much that we despaired even of life]]. The pressure was crushing, far beyond Paul's ability to endure. He expected to die. This is remarkable honesty from a great apostle. Paul did not pretend to be unshakable. He admitted that he reached the point of despair.

[[v9|Yes, we ourselves have had the sentence of death within ourselves]]. Paul felt as though he had received a death sentence. Humanly speaking, there was no way out.

[[v9|that we should not trust in ourselves, but in God who raises the dead]]. But Paul saw a purpose in this experience. It taught him not to rely on himself, his strength, wisdom, or resources, but on God. And not just any idea of God, but the God "who raises the dead." If God can raise the dead, He can deliver from any danger. And even if death comes, God will raise His people.

[[v10|who delivered us out of so great a death, and does deliver, on whom we have set our hope that he will also still deliver us]]. God rescued Paul. Paul speaks of God's deliverance in the past, present, and future: He delivered, He does deliver, and He will deliver.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that even strong believers can reach the end of their own resources. Paul, who had faced so much, was overwhelmed. This honesty gives permission to believers who feel crushed. Despair is a human experience, even for those with great faith.

They also show that God can use extreme hardship to teach dependence on Him. When people are strong, they are tempted to rely on themselves. When they are brought to the end of their strength, they learn to rely on God. Paul later wrote, [[2 Corinthians 12:10|For when I am weak, then am I strong]].

Finally, these verses show that the resurrection is the foundation of hope. God raises the dead. This truth gives believers hope in every situation, because no situation is beyond the power of the God who raised Jesus.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses comfort believers who feel overwhelmed. Paul despaired of life itself, yet God delivered him. Believers who feel crushed can be honest with God about their despair and turn to Him for help. They can also reach out to others for support, including trusted believers, counselors, or medical professionals when needed.

They also encourage believers to rely on God rather than themselves. When believers face situations beyond their strength, they can see them as opportunities to trust God more deeply.

Finally, these verses give believers hope for the future. God has delivered in the past, He delivers now, and He will deliver again. Believers can set their hope on Him.

### 2 Corinthians 1:20 — All God's Promises Are "Yes" in Christ

verse: 2 Corinthians 1:20

#### Simple Explanation

Some Corinthians had accused Paul of being unreliable because he changed his travel plans. They suggested that his word could not be trusted. Paul responded by pointing to the reliability of the gospel he preached and of Jesus Christ Himself.

He had said that Jesus Christ, whom he preached, was not "Yes and no," but in Him is "Yes." Now he explains: [[v20|For however many are the promises of God, in him is the “Yes.”]] God made many promises throughout Scripture: to Abraham, to David, through the prophets. He promised a Savior, forgiveness of sins, the gift of the Spirit, a new covenant, eternal life, and the renewal of all things. Every one of these promises finds its fulfillment in Jesus Christ. In Him, God says "Yes" to all His promises.

[[v20|Therefore also through him is the “Amen”, to the glory of God through us]]. "Amen" means "so be it" or "it is true." When believers say "Amen" in worship, they affirm God's promises. Because Christ has fulfilled God's promises, believers respond with "Amen," giving glory to God. In the early church, believers would say "Amen" together when prayers were offered and Scripture was read.

Paul's point is that the message he preached is completely reliable, because it is about Jesus, in whom God's promises are fulfilled. Paul himself might change travel plans, but the gospel never changes.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that Jesus is the fulfillment of all God's promises. The whole Old Testament points to Him. Jesus said, [[Luke 24:44|that all things which are written in the law of Moses, the prophets, and the psalms concerning me must be fulfilled]].

It also shows the faithfulness of God. God keeps His promises. Believers can trust every promise in Scripture because God has proven His faithfulness in Christ. Paul writes, [[Romans 8:32|He who didn’t spare his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how would he not also with him freely give us all things?]]

Finally, the verse shows that worship is a response to God's faithfulness. Believers say "Amen" to God's promises, affirming their trust and giving Him glory.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to trust God's promises. When believers read promises in Scripture, they can know that God will keep them, because in Christ, His answer is "Yes."

It also encourages believers to be people of their word. Paul defended his integrity. Believers, who follow a faithful God, can strive to be reliable and honest in their own promises.

Finally, the verse invites believers to say "Amen" with faith. When believers hear God's promises, they can respond with heartfelt agreement and praise.

### 2 Corinthians 1:21–22 — Anointed, Sealed, and Given the Spirit

verse: 2 Corinthians 1:21-22

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been explaining that God's promises are "Yes" in Christ. Now he describes what God has done for believers, using four images.

[[v21|Now he who establishes us with you in Christ and anointed us is God]]. First, God "establishes" believers. The word means to make firm, secure, and stable. God strengthens believers in their relationship with Christ. Notice "us with you": Paul and the Corinthians were established together.

Second, God "anointed" believers. In the Old Testament, prophets, priests, and kings were anointed with oil as a sign of being set apart for God's service. The title "Christ" means "Anointed One." Believers share in Christ's anointing through the Holy Spirit. John writes that believers [[1 John 2:20|have an anointing from the Holy One]].

[[v22|who also sealed us]]. Third, God has "sealed" believers. In the ancient world, a seal was a mark of ownership and authenticity, pressed into wax on a document or package. A seal showed who owned something and protected it from tampering. Believers are marked as belonging to God. Paul writes elsewhere that believers [[Ephesians 1:13|were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit]].

[[v22|and gave us the down payment of the Spirit in our hearts]]. Fourth, God has given the Holy Spirit as a "down payment." The Greek word (arrabon) was used in business for a deposit or first installment that guaranteed the rest of the payment would come. The Holy Spirit in believers' hearts is God's guarantee that He will complete everything He has promised, including the resurrection and eternal life.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that salvation is God's work. God establishes, anoints, seals, and gives the Spirit. Believers' security rests on what God has done, not on their own strength.

They also show the work of the Holy Spirit. The Spirit anoints, seals, and guarantees. The Spirit is God's presence in believers, the mark of belonging to Him, and the foretaste of the glory to come.

Finally, these verses show the Trinity at work. God (the Father) establishes believers in Christ (the Son) and gives them the Spirit. Salvation involves all three Persons of the Trinity.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses bring assurance to believers who doubt. God has established them, anointed them, sealed them as His own, and given them the Spirit as a guarantee. Their salvation is secure in His hands.

They also encourage believers to live as those set apart for God. They have been anointed for His service and sealed as His possession. Believers can live in a way that reflects whose they are.

Finally, these verses give believers hope. The Spirit is a down payment, a foretaste of what is to come. The joy, peace, and presence of God that believers experience now are just the beginning of what they will experience in eternity.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

2 Corinthians 1 reveals God as the Father of mercies and the God of all comfort. He comforts His people in every affliction, so that they can comfort others. Believers can bring their pain to God and receive His comfort, and they can use their experiences of suffering and comfort to help others who are hurting.

The chapter also shows that even strong believers can reach the point of despair. Paul was crushed beyond his strength and expected to die, yet God delivered him. Through this, Paul learned not to rely on himself but on God who raises the dead. Believers who feel overwhelmed can be honest with God and others, and can set their hope on the God who delivers, past, present, and future.

2 Corinthians 1 declares that all God's promises are "Yes" in Christ. Every promise God has made is fulfilled in Jesus. Believers can trust God's promises, respond with "Amen," and strive to be people of integrity whose word can be trusted.

Finally, the chapter assures believers that God has established them, anointed them, sealed them, and given them the Spirit as a guarantee. Their security rests in God's work. And like Paul, those who serve in the church can do so not as rulers over others' faith, but as fellow workers for their joy.

## Chapter Prayer

Blessed be You, God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies and God of all comfort. Thank You for comforting me in all my troubles. Help me to comfort others with the comfort I have received from You. When I am crushed beyond my strength, teach me not to rely on myself but on You, the God who raises the dead. Thank You for delivering me in the past; I trust You to deliver me now and in the future. Thank You that all Your promises are "Yes" in Christ. I gladly agree with Your promises and give You glory. Thank You for establishing me in Christ, anointing me, sealing me as Your own, and giving me Your Spirit as a guarantee. Make me a person of integrity and a fellow worker for the joy of others. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
