# Revelation 12
theme: The Woman, the Child, and the Dragon
main: A great sign appears in heaven: a woman clothed with the sun, with the moon under her feet and a crown of twelve stars on her head. She is pregnant and cries out in the pain of childbirth. Another sign appears: an enormous red dragon with seven heads, ten horns, and seven crowns, whose tail sweeps a third of the stars from the sky and flings them to the earth. The dragon stands before the woman, ready to devour her child the moment He is born. She gives birth to a son, a male child, who will rule all the nations with an iron rod, and her child is snatched up to God and His throne. The woman flees into the wilderness to a place prepared by God, where she is cared for 1,260 days. Then war breaks out in heaven: Michael and his angels fight against the dragon and his angels, and the dragon is defeated. The great dragon, the ancient serpent called the devil and Satan, who leads the whole world astray, is hurled down to the earth with his angels. A loud voice in heaven proclaims that the salvation, power, and kingdom of God and the authority of His Messiah have come, for the accuser of the believers has been thrown down. They triumphed over him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony, and they did not love their lives so much as to shrink from death. Heaven is told to rejoice, but woe to the earth and sea, because the devil has come down in great fury, knowing his time is short. The dragon pursues the woman, but she is given the wings of a great eagle to fly to her place in the wilderness, where she is cared for a time, times, and half a time. The serpent spews water like a river to sweep her away, but the earth helps her by swallowing the flood. Enraged, the dragon goes off to wage war against the rest of her offspring, those who keep God's commands and hold fast their testimony about Jesus.

## Chapter Background and Story

Revelation 12 marks a turning point in the book. The seven trumpets have sounded, and the kingdom of God has been proclaimed (11:15). Now John steps back to reveal the deeper spiritual conflict that lies behind all the events of history. Chapters 12–14 pull back the curtain on the cosmic struggle between God and Satan, between the Lamb and the dragon, and explain why God's people suffer in the world.

The chapter tells the story of Christ's birth, victory, and ascension, and Satan's defeat, in symbolic form. It explains that the hostility believers experience is ultimately the rage of a defeated enemy who knows his time is short. This would have been profound encouragement for persecuted believers. Their suffering was not a sign of God's absence or Satan's victory, but of Satan's desperation.

The imagery draws on Genesis 3, where God promised that the offspring of the woman would crush the serpent's head (Genesis 3:15). Revelation 12 shows the fulfillment of that promise.

### The Woman and the Dragon (12:1–6)

The signs of the woman, the dragon, and the child are studied below.

[[v6|The woman fled into the wilderness, where she has a place prepared by God, that there they may nourish her one thousand two hundred sixty days]]. The wilderness in Scripture is a place of both testing and divine provision. Israel was cared for in the wilderness after the exodus, fed with manna and protected by God. Elijah was fed in the wilderness (1 Kings 17:2–6; 19:4–8). The woman is protected and nourished by God for 1,260 days, the same period as the witness of the two witnesses (Revelation 11:3). Many interpreters see this as the period of the church's existence in the world, a time of persecution but also of God's sustaining care.

### War in Heaven (12:7–12)

[[v7|There was war in the sky. Michael and his angels made war on the dragon. The dragon and his angels made war]]. [[v8|They didn’t prevail. No place was found for them any more in heaven]]. Michael is the archangel who protects God's people (Daniel 10:13, 21; 12:1; Jude 1:9). The war in heaven results in the dragon's defeat and expulsion.

When does this war happen? Some see it as the original fall of Satan before creation. But the context connects it closely with the ascension of Christ (v5) and with the salvation won by "the Lamb’s blood" (v11). Many interpreters therefore see it as the heavenly side of Christ's victory at the cross and resurrection. Jesus said, as His death approached, [[John 12:31|Now is the judgment of this world. Now the prince of this world will be cast out]]. Others see it as a future event in the end times. The text emphasizes that the victory has been won and Satan has been thrown down.

The casting down of the dragon and the song of victory are studied below. [[v12|Therefore rejoice, heavens, and you who dwell in them. Woe to the earth and to the sea, because the devil has gone down to you, having great wrath, knowing that he has but a short time.”]] Heaven rejoices because the accuser has been expelled. But the earth faces his fury. The devil is angry precisely because he is defeated and knows that his time is short. This explains the intensity of persecution: it is the rage of a losing enemy.

### The Dragon Pursues the Woman (12:13–17)

[[v13|When the dragon saw that he was thrown down to the earth, he persecuted the woman who gave birth to the male child]]. Unable to defeat the Child, now enthroned with God, the dragon turns his anger on the woman.

[[v14|Two wings of the great eagle were given to the woman, that she might fly into the wilderness to her place, so that she might be nourished for a time, times, and half a time, from the face of the serpent]]. The wings of the eagle recall God's words to Israel after the exodus: [[Exodus 19:4|You have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I bore you on eagles’ wings, and brought you to myself]]. God carries His people to safety. "A time, times, and half a time" equals three and a half years, the same period as the 1,260 days (v6).

[[v15|The serpent spewed water out of his mouth after the woman like a river, that he might cause her to be carried away by the stream]]. [[v16|The earth helped the woman, and the earth opened its mouth and swallowed up the river which the dragon spewed out of his mouth]]. The flood may represent persecution, lies, or overwhelming attacks intended to destroy God's people. In the Old Testament, floods often symbolize overwhelming trouble (Psalm 124:4–5; Isaiah 43:2). The earth swallowing the river may recall the earth swallowing Korah's rebels (Numbers 16:32) or the parting of the Red Sea. God uses even creation to protect His people.

The dragon's war against the woman's offspring is studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### Revelation 12:1–5 — The Woman, the Dragon, and the Child

verse: Revelation 12:1-5

#### Simple Explanation

John sees "a great sign" in heaven. A sign is a symbol that points to a deeper reality. The first sign is "a woman clothed with the sun, and the moon under her feet, and on her head a crown of twelve stars." She is glorious, and she is about to give birth. She cries out "in pain, laboring to give birth."

Then John sees "another sign": "a great red dragon, having seven heads and ten horns, and on his heads seven crowns." The dragon is later identified as Satan (v9). His red color suggests violence and bloodshed. His seven heads, ten horns, and crowns represent great power and authority, which he claims for himself. His tail sweeps "one third of the stars of the sky" to the earth, perhaps representing his power or the angels who fell with him.

The dragon stands before the woman, waiting to "devour her child" as soon as He is born. But the woman "gave birth to a son, a male child, who is to rule all the nations with a rod of iron." This describes the Messiah, quoting Psalm 2:9. Before the dragon can harm Him, "her child was caught up to God and to his throne."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The child is clearly Jesus Christ, the Messiah who will rule the nations. The phrase "caught up to God and to his throne" summarizes His ascension and enthronement after His death and resurrection. The whole earthly life of Jesus is compressed into His birth and exaltation, because the point is that the dragon failed. He could not destroy the Messiah.

The woman has been understood in several ways. Many interpreters see her as the faithful people of God, Israel from whom the Messiah came, who continues as the community of God's people, the church. The twelve stars recall the twelve tribes of Israel; the imagery of sun, moon, and stars echoes Joseph's dream about Jacob's family (Genesis 37:9). Israel is pictured in the prophets as a woman in labor (Isaiah 26:17; 66:7–9; Micah 4:10). After the child's ascension, the woman continues to be persecuted, and her "offspring" are believers who keep God's commands (v17), which fits the people of God. Others, especially in Catholic and Orthodox traditions, see the woman also as Mary, the mother of Jesus, who literally gave birth to the Messiah. Many interpreters hold that the image can include both: Mary as the one through whom the Messiah came, and the people of God as the larger community she represents.

The dragon's attempt to devour the child recalls Herod's attempt to kill the infant Jesus (Matthew 2:16), and more broadly, Satan's constant opposition to God's plan of salvation, from the serpent in Eden onward. The scene reveals the spiritual reality behind history: a cosmic struggle in which Satan seeks to destroy God's Messiah and God's people, but fails.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage helps believers understand that their lives take place within a larger spiritual story. Behind the visible events of history lies a conflict between God and the forces of evil. Believers do not need to fear this conflict, because the decisive victory has already been won by Christ.

The failure of the dragon to destroy the child gives believers confidence. Satan is powerful, but he is not all-powerful. He could not stop God's plan of salvation, and he cannot stop God's purposes for His people.

This passage also reminds believers that Christ now reigns from God's throne. The One who was born in Bethlehem, who died and rose again, now sits enthroned, ruling over all the nations. Believers can trust Him in every circumstance.

### Revelation 12:9–10 — The Accuser Thrown Down

verse: Revelation 12:9-10

#### Simple Explanation

Verse 9 identifies the dragon with several names: "the great dragon," "the old serpent," "the devil," "Satan," and "the deceiver of the whole world." Each name reveals something about him.

"The old serpent" connects him with the serpent in Eden who deceived Eve (Genesis 3). "The devil" means "slanderer." "Satan" means "adversary" or "accuser." And he is "the deceiver of the whole world," leading people away from God through lies.

He "was thrown down to the earth, and his angels were thrown down with him." He has lost his place in heaven.

In verse 10, a loud voice in heaven proclaims: "Now the salvation, the power, and the Kingdom of our God, and the authority of his Christ has come." Satan's defeat means the arrival of God's salvation and kingdom.

The reason is given: "for the accuser of our brothers has been thrown down, who accuses them before our God day and night."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

One of Satan's chief activities is accusation. In the Old Testament, Satan accused Job before God (Job 1:6–11) and stood to accuse Joshua the high priest (Zechariah 3:1). He points to the sins and failures of God's people and claims that they deserve condemnation.

But his accusations have lost their power. Through the death of Christ, the sins of God's people have been paid for. Paul asks, [[Romans 8:33|Who could bring a charge against God’s chosen ones? It is God who justifies]]. Because Christ has taken the penalty for sin, there is no longer any ground for accusation. The accuser has been thrown down.

The proclamation that salvation has now come shows that Satan's defeat is not merely future. It has happened through Christ's victory. Believers live in the time after the decisive battle. Satan still rages on earth, but he has been cast out of heaven and his accusations have been silenced before God.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many believers struggle with guilt and shame, sometimes long after they have confessed their sins. They may hear accusing thoughts: you are not good enough, God could never forgive you, you have failed too many times. This passage reminds them that the accuser has been thrown down. In Christ, there is no condemnation (Romans 8:1).

When accusing thoughts come, believers can answer them with the truth of the gospel: Christ has died for their sins, God has justified them, and nothing can separate them from His love.

This passage also helps believers recognize Satan's tactics. He deceives and accuses. Believers can resist him by holding to God's truth and resting in God's forgiveness.

### Revelation 12:11 — They Overcame Him

verse: Revelation 12:11

#### Simple Explanation

This verse explains how believers overcome Satan: "They overcame him because of the Lamb’s blood, and because of the word of their testimony. They didn’t love their life, even to death."

There are three parts to their victory. First, "the Lamb’s blood." Their victory rests on the sacrifice of Jesus. His death defeated Satan and paid for their sins.

Second, "the word of their testimony." They bore faithful witness to Jesus, speaking the truth about Him even under pressure.

Third, "They didn’t love their life, even to death." They were willing to give up their lives rather than deny Christ. Their loyalty to Him was greater than their desire to live.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse reveals the surprising way God's people win. They do not overcome Satan through political power, violence, or worldly strength. They overcome through the cross of Christ, faithful witness, and willingness to suffer. This is the way of the Lamb, who conquered through His death (5:5–6).

The foundation is the blood of the Lamb. Without it, believers would have no defense against Satan's accusations. But because Christ has died, they stand forgiven and free.

Faithful testimony is the believer's active participation in Christ's victory. Each time believers bear witness to Jesus, they declare that Satan's lies are false and that Christ is Lord.

The willingness to die echoes Jesus' teaching: [[Mark 8:35|For whoever wants to save his life will lose it; and whoever will lose his life for my sake and the sake of the Good News will save it]]. Satan's greatest weapon is the threat of death. But those who do not cling to life cannot be controlled by that fear. Their death becomes a victory, not a defeat.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers today can overcome evil in the same way. They can stand on the finished work of Christ, trusting His blood for forgiveness and freedom. They can bear faithful witness to Him in word and deed. And they can hold their lives loosely, valuing faithfulness to Christ above comfort, reputation, or safety.

For most believers, this may not mean literal martyrdom, though for many around the world it does. But it does mean being willing to sacrifice convenience, approval, and even relationships for the sake of Christ.

This verse also encourages believers that ordinary faithfulness is powerful. Every time they trust Christ's sacrifice, speak about Him, or choose obedience over self-protection, they share in His victory over the enemy.

### Revelation 12:17 — War Against the Rest of Her Offspring

verse: Revelation 12:17

#### Simple Explanation

When the dragon fails to destroy the woman, "The dragon grew angry with the woman, and went away to make war with the rest of her offspring." Unable to defeat the Messiah or destroy the people of God as a whole, Satan turns his attack on individual believers.

These offspring are described as those "who keep God’s commandments and hold Jesus’ testimony." They are faithful believers who obey God and remain loyal to Jesus.

This verse sets the stage for chapter 13, where the dragon calls up two beasts to carry out his war against God's people.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse explains why believers face opposition in the world. They are targets of Satan's anger because they belong to Christ. Their suffering is not a sign that God has abandoned them, but a sign that they are on the right side of the conflict.

The description of believers is significant. They "keep God’s commandments," living in obedience, and they "hold Jesus’ testimony," remaining faithful to the truth about Him. These two marks, obedience and faithful witness, characterize God's people throughout Revelation (14:12).

The phrase "the rest of her offspring" echoes Genesis 3:15, where God promised enmity between the serpent's offspring and the woman's offspring. Jesus is the ultimate Offspring who crushes the serpent's head. Believers, united with Him, share in this conflict and in His victory.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers should not be surprised when they face spiritual opposition. Peter wrote, [[1 Peter 4:12|Beloved, don’t be astonished at the fiery trial which has come upon you, to test you, as though a strange thing happened to you]]. Opposition is part of following Jesus in a fallen world.

Believers can also take comfort that the enemy who attacks them is already defeated and angry because his time is short. His rage is a sign of his weakness, not his strength.

The best response to spiritual warfare is the faithfulness described in this verse: keeping God's commands and holding fast to the testimony about Jesus. Believers do not need special techniques. They need steady obedience, trust, and loyalty to Christ.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Revelation 12 pulls back the curtain on the spiritual conflict behind history. It shows that the opposition believers face in the world is part of a larger struggle between God and Satan. But it also shows that the decisive battle has already been won. The Child was born, ascended to God's throne, and now reigns. The dragon has been thrown down from heaven.

This chapter offers believers deep comfort. The accuser who points to their sins has been silenced, because Christ's blood has paid for them. Believers do not need to live under condemnation. They can rest in God's forgiveness and answer every accusation with the truth of the gospel.

The chapter also shows how believers overcome. They overcome through the blood of the Lamb, the word of their testimony, and a love for Christ greater than their love for their own lives. This is the way of the cross, the way Jesus Himself conquered.

Finally, the chapter explains why believers face opposition. Satan is angry because he knows his time is short. He wages war against those who keep God's commands and hold to the testimony of Jesus. Yet God protects and nourishes His people in the wilderness, carrying them on eagles' wings. Believers can face whatever comes with confidence, knowing that their enemy is defeated and their Lord reigns.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You that You came into the world, defeated the enemy, and now reign at the right hand of the Father. Thank You that the accuser has been thrown down, and that because of Your blood there is no condemnation for me. When accusing thoughts rise against me, help me to stand on the truth of Your forgiveness. Teach me to overcome as Your people do: through Your blood, through faithful testimony, and through a love for You greater than my love for my own life. When I face opposition, remind me that the enemy is defeated and his time is short. Carry me on eagles' wings, nourish me in the wilderness, and help me to keep God's commands and hold fast to Your testimony. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
