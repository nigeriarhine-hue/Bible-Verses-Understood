# Ephesians 6
theme: Faithful at Home and Strong in the Lord
main: Paul continues his teaching on Christian households, calling children to obey and honor their parents and fathers not to provoke their children but to bring them up in the Lord's training and instruction. He addresses servants and masters, reminding both that they serve one Master in heaven who shows no favoritism. Then Paul calls believers to be strong in the Lord and to put on the full armor of God, because their struggle is not against flesh and blood but against spiritual forces of evil. Truth, righteousness, the gospel of peace, faith, salvation, and the word of God equip them to stand firm, all undergirded by persistent prayer. The letter closes with a request for prayer, news of Tychicus, and a blessing of peace and grace.

## Chapter Background and Story

The final chapter of Ephesians completes Paul's teaching on household relationships, which began in Ephesians 5:22, and then turns to the spiritual battle every believer faces.

In the Roman world, a household included not only parents and children but often servants or slaves as well. Roman fathers had near-absolute legal authority over their children, and slaves had very few rights. Paul writes within that world, but he addresses each member of the household as a responsible person before God, and he places demands on those with power (fathers and masters) that the culture rarely placed on them. Every relationship is brought under the lordship of Christ.

The section on spiritual armor (6:10–20) is one of the most vivid passages in Paul's letters. Paul was writing as a prisoner, probably chained to a Roman soldier (v20; Acts 28:16, 20). He may have looked at that soldier's equipment and seen in it a picture of the spiritual resources God provides. For the Ephesian believers, many of whom had once practiced magic and lived in fear of spiritual powers (Acts 19:18–19), this passage offered both a sober warning and great encouragement. The powers of evil are real, but Christ has already triumphed over them (Ephesians 1:20–22), and believers can stand firm in His strength.

### Children and Parents (6:1–4)

[[v1|Children, obey your parents in the Lord, for this is right]]. Paul addresses children directly, which shows that they were present when the letter was read in the church gathering. Children were members of the community, not mere bystanders. "In the Lord" frames their obedience as part of their relationship with Christ.

[[v2|“Honor your father and mother,” which is the first commandment with a promise]]: [[v3|“that it may be well with you, and you may live long on the earth.”]] Paul quotes the fifth commandment (Exodus 20:12; Deuteronomy 5:16). Honoring parents is a lifelong calling that continues into adulthood, even when obedience in the childhood sense no longer applies. It involves respect, gratitude, and care for parents, especially in their old age.

Obedience "in the Lord" never requires a child to do what is wrong, and this verse never justifies abuse by parents. Children who are being harmed need protection, and adults who know of abuse should act to protect them and report it to the proper authorities. Honoring parents who have caused deep harm may look very different, involving forgiveness and respect for their humanity while keeping healthy boundaries. Paul's instruction to fathers is studied below.

### Servants and Masters (6:5–9)

[[v5|Servants, be obedient to those who according to the flesh are your masters, with fear and trembling, in singleness of your heart, as to Christ]]. Slavery was woven into the structure of the Roman Empire. A large portion of people in cities like Ephesus were enslaved. Roman slavery was not identical to the race-based chattel slavery of later centuries; people became slaves through war, debt, birth, or abandonment, and some could gain freedom. But it was still a system of ownership in which human beings could be bought, sold, beaten, and exploited.

Paul did not have the power to abolish this system, and his instructions address believers living within it. He calls enslaved believers to serve faithfully, not as people-pleasers, [[v6|but as servants of Christ, doing the will of God from the heart]], [[v7|with good will doing service as to the Lord and not to men]]. Their ultimate identity is not "slave" but "servant of Christ." [[v8|knowing that whatever good thing each one does, he will receive the same good again from the Lord, whether he is bound or free]]. Christ sees and rewards their faithful work, even when earthly masters do not.

[[v9|You masters, do the same things to them, and give up threatening, knowing that he who is both their Master and yours is in heaven, and there is no partiality with him]]. This was radical. Masters were to treat slaves with the same good will, stop using threats, and remember that they themselves had a Master in heaven who would judge them impartially. In God's eyes, master and slave stood on equal ground.

It is important to be clear: this passage does not endorse slavery as God's design. Scripture condemns kidnapping and trading in human beings (Exodus 21:16; 1 Timothy 1:10), and Paul encouraged slaves to gain their freedom if they could (1 Corinthians 7:21). The gospel's declaration that all are one in Christ (Galatians 3:28) and that every person bears God's image planted seeds that, in time, led many Christians to oppose and work to end slavery. Tragically, others in later centuries misused passages like this to defend the enslavement of Africans and others, a terrible distortion of Scripture that Christians must reject and repent of. Today, the principles Paul gives apply especially to work: employees can serve faithfully as to the Lord, and employers must treat workers justly, without threats or exploitation, remembering that they too answer to God.

### Prayer (6:18–20)

The call to persistent prayer is studied below. [[v19|Pray for me, that utterance may be given to me in opening my mouth, to make known with boldness the mystery of the Good News]], [[v20|for which I am an ambassador in chains; that in it I may speak boldly, as I ought to speak]]. Paul does not ask for release from prison. He asks for boldness to proclaim the gospel. An ambassador normally enjoyed honor and immunity; Paul, Christ's ambassador, was in chains. Yet he saw even his imprisonment as an opportunity to make Christ known (Philippians 1:12–14).

### Closing Greetings (6:21–24)

[[v21|But that you also may know my affairs, how I am doing, Tychicus, the beloved brother and faithful servant in the Lord, will make known to you all things]]. Tychicus was a trusted coworker from the province of Asia (Acts 20:4) who also carried the letter to the Colossians (Colossians 4:7–8). [[v22|I have sent him to you for this very purpose, that you may know our state and that he may comfort your hearts]].

[[v23|Peace be to the brothers, and love with faith, from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ]]. [[v24|Grace be with all those who love our Lord Jesus Christ with incorruptible love. Amen]]. The letter ends as it began, with grace and peace (Ephesians 1:2). "Incorruptible love" means love that is sincere, undying, and unfailing.

## Important Verses Explained

### Ephesians 6:4 — Bringing Up Children in the Lord

verse: Ephesians 6:4

#### Simple Explanation

After addressing children, Paul turns to fathers. In the Roman world, fathers had nearly unlimited legal power over their children. They could discipline harshly, arrange marriages, and in some circumstances even reject newborn children. Paul's instruction would have been surprising.

"You fathers, don’t provoke your children to wrath." Fathers are not to exasperate their children through harshness, unfairness, constant criticism, favoritism, excessive demands, or inconsistency. Such treatment produces anger, discouragement, and resentment. Colossians 3:21 adds the reason: so that children will not lose heart.

"But nurture them in the discipline and instruction of the Lord." "Nurture" means to bring up, to feed and care for, to help grow. "Discipline" refers to training, including correction when needed. "Instruction" means teaching and guidance through words. Both are to be "of the Lord," shaped by His character and His ways.

Though Paul addresses fathers, who held the most authority in the household, the principle applies to mothers and all who raise children.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse reflects God's own way of parenting His children. Hebrews 12:5–11 describes God's discipline as an expression of love, aimed at our good, so that we may share His holiness. God does not provoke His children with cruelty; He corrects, teaches, and patiently nurtures them.

The Old Testament also emphasizes parents' responsibility to teach children about God. Deuteronomy 6:6–7 instructs parents to impress God's commands on their children, talking about them at home and on the road, when they lie down and when they rise. Faith is meant to be woven into the fabric of family life.

This verse also limits parental authority. Parents are accountable to God for how they treat their children. Authority is given for nurture, not for domination. Harsh, abusive, or neglectful treatment contradicts this command and grieves God, who has a special concern for the vulnerable, including children (Mark 9:36–37; 10:13–16).

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Parents can examine their own patterns: Do they listen to their children? Do they discipline fairly and consistently, explaining the reasons? Do they encourage more than they criticize? Do they apologize when they are wrong? These practices help children feel loved and secure.

Bringing children up in the Lord's instruction includes reading Scripture together, praying together, talking openly about faith, and modeling a genuine relationship with God. Children learn as much from what they see as from what they hear.

For those who were raised by harsh or absent parents, this verse affirms that such treatment was not God's design. God can bring healing, and He offers Himself as the perfect Father. For those who are raising children now, God offers grace for mistakes and wisdom for the journey.

### Ephesians 6:10–12 — Our Struggle Is Not Against Flesh and Blood

verse: Ephesians 6:10-12

#### Simple Explanation

"Finally, be strong in the Lord and in the strength of his might." Paul does not tell believers to be strong in themselves. Their strength comes from the Lord, the same mighty power that raised Christ from the dead (Ephesians 1:19–20).

"Put on the whole armor of God, that you may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil." "Wiles" means schemes, strategies, or tricks. The devil does not usually attack openly. He works through deception, temptation, discouragement, and division. God provides the armor believers need to stand against these schemes.

Verse 12 explains the nature of the struggle: "For our wrestling is not against flesh and blood." The ultimate enemies of believers are not other human beings. They are "the principalities... the powers... the world’s rulers of the darkness of this age, and... the spiritual forces of wickedness in the heavenly places." These terms describe spiritual beings opposed to God, who work behind the scenes of human evil.

"Wrestling" suggests close, personal struggle. The Christian life involves real spiritual conflict.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This passage teaches that evil in the world is not merely the result of human choices or social systems, though those are real. Behind them are spiritual forces that oppose God and His people. Jesus confronted these forces throughout His ministry, casting out demons and resisting Satan's temptations. Through His death and resurrection, He disarmed the powers and triumphed over them (Colossians 2:15).

Christians in different traditions understand spiritual warfare in somewhat different ways. Some emphasize direct encounters with demonic forces; others emphasize the ways evil works through temptation, deception, and unjust structures. Both perspectives recognize that evil is real and personal, and that believers must rely on God's strength rather than their own.

The statement "our wrestling is not against flesh and blood" has an important implication: people are not the ultimate enemy. Even those who oppose or harm believers are not the true foe. This should shape how believers treat others, with love, prayer, and a desire for their redemption, even while standing firm against evil and seeking justice for harm done.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers face real spiritual struggles: temptations that seem to come at the worst moments, discouragement that whispers lies, divisions that tear relationships apart. This passage helps believers recognize these struggles for what they are and respond with God's strength rather than their own.

It also changes how believers view conflict with others. When tension arises in a family, church, or community, it helps to remember that the real enemy is not the other person. This can lead to more patience, prayer, and grace, while still addressing wrongs honestly.

Finally, this passage brings hope. The powers of evil are real, but they are not equal to God. Christ reigns far above them all, and believers stand in His strength.

### Ephesians 6:14–17 — The Whole Armor of God

verse: Ephesians 6:14-17

#### Simple Explanation

Paul describes six pieces of armor, drawing on the equipment of a Roman soldier and on images from Isaiah, where God Himself is pictured as a warrior wearing righteousness as a breastplate and salvation as a helmet (Isaiah 59:17).

"The utility belt of truth." A soldier's belt held his clothing in place and secured other equipment. Truth, both the truth of God's word and a life of honesty, holds everything else together.

"The breastplate of righteousness." The breastplate protected the heart and vital organs. Righteousness includes the right standing believers have through Christ and the righteous living that flows from it. It guards the heart against accusation and corruption.

"Having fitted your feet with the preparation of the Good News of peace." Roman soldiers wore sturdy sandals with hobnails for grip. The gospel of peace gives believers firm footing and readiness to move forward with the message of Christ.

"The shield of faith, with which you will be able to quench all the fiery darts of the evil one." Roman shields were large and could be soaked in water to put out flaming arrows. Faith, trust in God's promises, extinguishes the attacks of the evil one, such as lies, doubts, accusations, and temptations.

"The helmet of salvation." The helmet protected the head. The assurance of salvation guards the mind against despair and doubt.

"The sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God." This is the only offensive weapon in the list. The word translated "word" often refers to a specific spoken word. When Jesus was tempted in the wilderness, He answered each temptation with Scripture (Matthew 4:1–11). The Spirit enables believers to use God's word against the enemy.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Every piece of armor is ultimately found in Christ. He is the truth (John 14:6), our righteousness (1 Corinthians 1:30), our peace (Ephesians 2:14), the object of our faith, our salvation, and the living Word (John 1:1). To put on the armor of God is, in a sense, to put on Christ (Romans 13:14).

The armor is primarily defensive. Paul's repeated command is to "stand" (vv11, 13, 14). Believers are not called to go looking for battles with spiritual forces but to stand firm in the victory Christ has already won. Their task is to hold their ground, not to win the war, which Christ has already won.

The armor is also communal. The commands are plural, addressed to the whole church. Roman soldiers fought in formation, their shields locked together. Believers stand together, supporting and protecting one another.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can "put on" the armor daily through practical habits: committing to truth and honesty; resting in Christ's righteousness rather than their own performance; being ready to share the gospel of peace; trusting God's promises when doubts or accusations come; remembering their secure salvation; and filling their minds with Scripture so that the Spirit can bring it to mind when needed.

When temptation or discouragement strikes, believers can respond as Jesus did, with God's word. Memorizing key passages of Scripture equips the heart for such moments.

Believers also need one another. Standing firm is easier in community, where others pray, encourage, and help carry the shield of faith when one is weak.

### Ephesians 6:18 — Praying at All Times in the Spirit

verse: Ephesians 6:18

#### Simple Explanation

Prayer is not listed as a piece of armor, but it is woven through the whole passage. It is how believers put on the armor and stay alert in the battle.

Paul uses the word "all" four times: "all prayer and requests," "at all times," "all perseverance," and "all the saints." Prayer is to be comprehensive and constant.

"With all prayer and requests" includes every kind of prayer: praise, thanksgiving, confession, and specific requests.

"Praying at all times in the Spirit." Prayer is not limited to set times but can be a continual conversation with God throughout the day. "In the Spirit" means prayer guided and empowered by the Holy Spirit (Romans 8:26–27).

"Being watchful to this end in all perseverance." Believers must stay alert and keep praying, not giving up. Jesus told His disciples to watch and pray (Mark 14:38).

"And requests for all the saints." Prayer is not only for oneself but for all of God's people. Believers support one another in the spiritual battle by praying for each other.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Prayer is the posture of dependence. In the spiritual battle, believers do not rely on their own strength, wisdom, or effort. They rely on God. Prayer acknowledges that dependence and draws on His power.

Praying "in the Spirit" reminds believers that even prayer is a gift of grace. The Spirit helps them pray when they do not know what to say, interceding with groans too deep for words (Romans 8:26). Prayer is not a technique to master but a relationship to enjoy.

Praying for "all the saints" reflects the communal nature of the Christian life. Believers are members of one body. When one suffers, all suffer. Through prayer, they carry one another's burdens and stand together against the enemy.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many believers struggle to pray consistently. This verse encourages them to see prayer not as an occasional duty but as an ongoing conversation with God throughout the day: in the car, at work, while doing chores, in moments of worry or joy.

Setting aside regular times for focused prayer is also valuable. Some find it helpful to keep a list of people and needs to pray for, including fellow believers in their church and Christians around the world who face persecution.

Perseverance matters. Some prayers take years to be answered. This verse encourages believers to keep praying, staying alert and trusting God's timing.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Ephesians 6 completes Paul's picture of the Christian life by showing how faith shapes the home, the workplace, and the unseen spiritual battle.

In the home, children are called to obey and honor their parents, and parents are called to nurture their children with patient discipline and instruction in the Lord, without provoking them to anger. Parental authority is a trust from God, given for the children's good, never a license for harshness or abuse. Honoring parents remains a lifelong calling, shaped by wisdom where there has been harm.

In the workplace, Paul's words to servants and masters, written within the unjust system of Roman slavery, are not an endorsement of that system. Their lasting principles apply to every form of work: serve faithfully, as to the Lord, whether anyone is watching or not; and if you have authority over others, treat them justly, without threats or exploitation, knowing that you too answer to a Master in heaven who shows no favoritism.

Above all, the chapter reminds believers that they face a real spiritual battle. Their ultimate enemies are not other people but spiritual forces of evil. They cannot win this struggle in their own strength, but they can stand firm in the Lord's mighty power, clothed in the armor He provides: truth, righteousness, the gospel of peace, faith, salvation, and the word of God. All of this is sustained by persistent prayer, for themselves and for all of God's people.

The letter ends with grace and peace. Whatever believers face at home, at work, or in the spiritual realm, they are held in the love of God, strengthened by His Spirit, and secure in Christ, who reigns above every power.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord, I want my home, my work, and my whole life to honor You. Help me to honor my parents and to treat the children in my care with patience, kindness, and faithful instruction in Your ways. Protect every child who is in danger, and heal those who carry wounds from childhood. In my work, help me to serve faithfully as unto You, and if I have authority over others, help me to treat them with justice and respect. Make me strong in You and in the strength of Your might. Clothe me with the whole armor of God: truth, righteousness, the gospel of peace, faith, salvation, and Your word. When the enemy attacks with lies and temptations, help me to stand firm. Teach me to pray at all times in the Spirit, for myself and for all Your people, and give boldness to all who proclaim the gospel. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
