# 2 Corinthians 4
theme: Treasure in Clay Jars and an Eternal Weight of Glory
main: Because God in His mercy has given him this ministry, Paul does not lose heart. He refuses deceit and openly proclaims the truth, knowing that the god of this age has blinded unbelievers' minds, but that the God who said [[v6|Light will shine out of darkness]] has shone in believers' hearts to reveal His glory in the face of Christ. This treasure is held in fragile clay jars, so that its surpassing power is clearly from God. Though hard-pressed, perplexed, persecuted, and struck down, Paul is never crushed, abandoned, or destroyed, carrying Jesus' death in his body so that Jesus' life may be revealed. Though his outer self is wasting away, his inner self is renewed daily, and his momentary troubles are achieving an eternal glory, so he fixes his eyes on what is unseen and eternal.

## Chapter Background and Story

2 Corinthians 4 continues Paul's description of Christian ministry under the new covenant. In chapter 3, he described its surpassing glory. Now he explains how that glorious ministry is carried out by weak, suffering people. Paul's opponents in Corinth may have pointed to his sufferings and unimpressive appearance as evidence that he was not a true apostle. Paul turns this around: his weakness shows that the power at work in his ministry comes from God.

This chapter is one of the most encouraging passages in the New Testament for believers who are suffering, weary, or growing old.

### Not Losing Heart (4:1–6)

[[v1|Therefore, seeing we have this ministry, even as we obtained mercy, we don’t faint]]. Paul received his ministry as an act of mercy. He did not earn it. Because it came from God's mercy, he did not give up, despite the difficulties.

[[v2|But we have renounced the hidden things of shame, not walking in craftiness nor handling the word of God deceitfully, but by the manifestation of the truth commending ourselves to every man’s conscience in the sight of God]]. Paul refused to use deceptive methods or to twist God's word. He simply proclaimed the truth openly, before God and every person's conscience.

[[v3|Even if our Good News is veiled, it is veiled in those who are dying]]. Some did not believe. But this was not because Paul hid the gospel. [[v4|in whom the god of this world has blinded the minds of the unbelieving, that the light of the Good News of the glory of Christ, who is the image of God, should not dawn on them]]. "The god of this world" refers to Satan, who has influence over the present evil age (John 12:31; Ephesians 2:2). He blinds people so that they cannot see the glory of Christ. Christ is "the image of God," the perfect revelation of who God is (Colossians 1:15). Paul's message and God's light are studied below.

### Treasure in Clay Jars (4:7–15)

The treasure in fragile vessels, and Paul's description of his sufferings, are studied below. [[v11|For we who live are always delivered to death for Jesus’ sake, that the life also of Jesus may be revealed in our mortal flesh]]. [[v12|So then death works in us, but life in you]]. Paul's sufferings brought life to others. His willingness to endure hardship for the gospel resulted in the Corinthians' salvation.

[[v13|But having the same spirit of faith, according to that which is written, “I believed, and therefore I spoke.” We also believe, and therefore we also speak]]. Paul quotes Psalm 116:10, a psalm of someone who trusted God in great distress and was delivered. Like the psalmist, Paul believed, and so he spoke. Faith leads to proclamation, even in suffering.

[[v14|knowing that he who raised the Lord Jesus will raise us also through Jesus, and will present us with you]]. Paul's confidence rested on the resurrection. The God who raised Jesus will raise believers and bring them together into His presence.

[[v15|For all things are for your sakes, that the grace, being multiplied through the many, may cause the thanksgiving to abound to the glory of God]]. Everything Paul endured was for the good of others, so that God's grace would reach more people, resulting in more thanksgiving and more glory to God.

## Important Verses Explained

### 2 Corinthians 4:5–6 — Light Shining Out of Darkness

verse: 2 Corinthians 4:5-6

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had explained that Satan blinds the minds of unbelievers so that they cannot see the glory of Christ. Now he describes what he preaches and how God opens blind eyes.

[[v5|For we don’t preach ourselves, but Christ Jesus as Lord]]. Paul did not promote himself. His message was not about his own importance, wisdom, or achievements. He proclaimed Jesus Christ as Lord, the One who rules over all.

[[v5|and ourselves as your servants for Jesus’ sake]]. Paul saw himself as a servant (literally "slave") of the Corinthians, for Jesus' sake. He served them because he served Jesus. Ministry is not about status but service.

[[v6|seeing it is God who said, “Light will shine out of darkness,”]]. Paul refers to creation. In Genesis 1:3, God said, [[Genesis 1:3|Let there be light]], and light shone in the darkness. The same God who created light at the beginning of the world now creates light in human hearts.

[[v6|who has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ]]. Just as God spoke light into existence at creation, He shines light into the hearts of those who believe. This light gives "the knowledge of the glory of God." And this glory is seen "in the face of Jesus Christ." To know God, people must look at Jesus. In Jesus, God's glory is fully revealed.

Paul may have been thinking of his own conversion. On the road to Damascus, a blinding light from heaven shone around him, and he saw the glory of the risen Christ (Acts 9:3). God shone light into his darkened heart.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that conversion is a new creation. Just as God created light out of darkness, He creates spiritual light in darkened hearts. Paul writes, [[2 Corinthians 5:17|Therefore if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation]].

They also show that God's glory is revealed in Jesus. Jesus said, [[John 14:9|He who has seen me has seen the Father]]. People who want to know what God is like can look at Jesus.

Finally, these verses show the right focus of ministry. Ministry is not about promoting oneself but proclaiming Jesus as Lord and serving others for His sake.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to keep Jesus at the center of their witness. When believers share their faith, the focus should be on Jesus, not on themselves.

They also encourage believers to pray for those who are spiritually blind. Only God can shine light into darkened hearts. Believers can pray for God to open the eyes of those they love.

Finally, these verses invite believers to keep looking at Jesus. The knowledge of God's glory is found in the face of Jesus Christ. Believers can grow in knowing God by studying the life, words, and work of Jesus in the Gospels.

### 2 Corinthians 4:7 — Treasure in Clay Jars

verse: 2 Corinthians 4:7

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had just described the glorious light of the knowledge of God in the face of Jesus Christ, which God has shone into believers' hearts. This is an incredible treasure. Now he describes the containers that hold it.

[[v7|But we have this treasure in clay vessels]]. In the ancient world, clay jars were ordinary, inexpensive, and fragile. They were used for everyday purposes, such as storing water, oil, or grain. Sometimes people hid valuable things like coins in clay jars, since no one would expect treasure in such ordinary containers. The Dead Sea Scrolls, discovered in 1947, had been preserved in clay jars for nearly two thousand years.

Paul says that the treasure of the gospel is held in "clay vessels," meaning ordinary, weak human beings. Paul himself was not physically impressive (10:10). He suffered illness, persecution, and hardship. He was a fragile container for a priceless treasure.

[[v7|that the exceeding greatness of the power may be of God and not from ourselves]]. Why would God put such a great treasure in such weak containers? So that it is clear that the power comes from God, not from the human messengers. If the messengers were impressive, people might think the power came from them. Because they are weak, the power is obviously God's.

This was an important answer to Paul's critics. They pointed to his weakness as evidence that he was not a true apostle. Paul says his weakness actually proves that God is at work.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that God works through weakness. Throughout Scripture, God used weak and unlikely people: Moses, who could not speak well; Gideon, the least in his family; David, the youngest son; and the fishermen who became apostles. Paul writes, [[1 Corinthians 1:27|God chose the weak things of the world that he might put to shame the things that are strong]].

It also shows the value of the gospel. The gospel is a "treasure," more valuable than anything the world can offer. Jesus compared the kingdom of heaven to treasure hidden in a field (Matthew 13:44).

Finally, the verse keeps believers humble. Whatever good is done through them comes from God's power, not their own. All the glory belongs to Him.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers who feel weak or ordinary. God does not need impressive people; He uses ordinary "clay jars." Believers' weaknesses do not disqualify them from serving God. They may even make God's power more visible.

It also encourages believers to value the gospel. The good news of Jesus is a priceless treasure. Believers can treasure it, guard it, and share it.

Finally, the verse encourages humility. When God works through believers, they can give Him the credit. The power belongs to God, not to the clay jar.

### 2 Corinthians 4:8–10 — Pressed but Not Crushed

verse: 2 Corinthians 4:8-10

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had just said that believers hold the treasure of the gospel in fragile clay jars, so that the power is clearly God's. Now he describes what life in a clay jar is like, using four contrasts.

[[v8|We are pressed on every side, yet not crushed]]. "Pressed" means squeezed, under pressure from all sides. Paul faced pressure constantly. But he was not "crushed," completely flattened.

[[v8|perplexed, yet not to despair]]. Paul was sometimes confused, not knowing what to do or why things were happening. But he did not give up hope. In Greek, there is a play on words: "at a loss, but not utterly at a loss."

[[v9|pursued, yet not forsaken]]. Paul was hunted by enemies. But he was never abandoned by God.

[[v9|struck down, yet not destroyed]]. Paul was knocked down, as in a fight. But he was not destroyed. He got back up. This was literally true; he had been stoned and left for dead, then got up and went back into the city (Acts 14:19–20).

[[v10|always carrying in the body the putting to death of the Lord Jesus, that the life of Jesus may also be revealed in our body]]. Paul's sufferings were a way of sharing in Jesus' suffering and death. But through these sufferings, the life of the risen Jesus was revealed. God's power sustained Paul, showing that Jesus is alive.

Notice the pattern: in each pair, the first part describes real hardship, and the second part describes God's sustaining power. Paul does not deny the hardships. But he declares that they do not have the final word.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that the Christian life involves real suffering. Paul does not pretend that following Jesus is easy. Believers may be pressed, perplexed, persecuted, and struck down.

They also show that God's power sustains believers in suffering. God does not always remove hardship, but He keeps His people from being destroyed by it. Jesus promised, [[Hebrews 13:5|I will in no way leave you, neither will I in any way forsake you]].

Finally, these verses show the pattern of death and resurrection in the Christian life. Believers share in Jesus' sufferings, and they also share in His life. Through their weakness, the life of the risen Jesus is displayed.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers in hard times. Believers may feel pressed, confused, attacked, or knocked down. But God has not abandoned them. They are not crushed, not in despair, not forsaken, and not destroyed.

They also encourage honesty about struggles. Paul admitted that he was perplexed and struck down. Believers do not need to pretend everything is fine. They can be honest about their difficulties while holding on to God's faithfulness.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to see their sufferings as opportunities for Christ's life to be revealed. When believers endure hardship with faith, others can see the power of the risen Jesus at work in them.

### 2 Corinthians 4:16–18 — Renewed Day by Day

verse: 2 Corinthians 4:16-18

#### Simple Explanation

Paul had been describing his sufferings and the hope of resurrection. Now he draws a conclusion.

[[v16|Therefore we don’t faint]]. Paul repeats what he said at the beginning of the chapter (4:1). Despite everything, he does not give up.

[[v16|but though our outward person is decaying, yet our inward person is renewed day by day]]. Paul contrasts two parts of his life. The "outward person," his physical body and earthly life, was "decaying," wearing out. Suffering, persecution, illness, and age were taking their toll. But the "inward person," his spirit, his life in God, was being "renewed day by day." While his body weakened, his inner life grew stronger.

[[v17|For our light affliction, which is for the moment, works for us more and more exceedingly an eternal weight of glory]]. Paul calls his afflictions "light" and "for the moment." This is remarkable, considering what he endured (11:23–28). His sufferings were not light in themselves; they seem light only when compared to the glory to come. And they are "for the moment," temporary compared to eternity. These afflictions are producing "an eternal weight of glory." The word "weight" contrasts with "light." The glory to come is weighty, substantial, and far greater than the suffering.

[[v18|while we don’t look at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen]]. Paul's secret was where he fixed his eyes. He did not focus on visible circumstances, his suffering and problems. He focused on the unseen realities: God, Christ, and the eternal glory to come.

[[v18|For the things which are seen are temporal, but the things which are not seen are eternal]]. Visible things, including suffering, possessions, and even physical bodies, are temporary. Invisible things, God and eternal life, last forever.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that God renews believers inwardly even as they age or suffer outwardly. The psalmist says that God renews strength like the eagle's (Psalm 103:5). Isaiah writes, [[Isaiah 40:31|But those who wait for the LORD will renew their strength]].

They also show that present suffering produces future glory. Paul writes in Romans, [[Romans 8:18|For I consider that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which will be revealed toward us]]. Suffering is not meaningless; God uses it to prepare believers for glory.

Finally, these verses show the importance of perspective. What believers focus on shapes how they experience life. Those who focus only on visible circumstances may lose heart. Those who fix their eyes on eternal realities find strength to endure.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses bring hope to believers who are aging, ill, or suffering. Even as the body weakens, God renews the inner person day by day. Believers can seek this daily renewal through prayer, Scripture, and fellowship with God.

They also encourage believers to keep an eternal perspective. When troubles feel heavy, believers can remember that they are temporary, and that God is using them to prepare an eternal glory far greater.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to fix their eyes on the unseen. Believers can focus on God's presence, His promises, and the hope of eternal life, rather than being overwhelmed by visible circumstances. This does not mean ignoring real problems or pain, but seeing them in the light of eternity.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

2 Corinthians 4 encourages believers not to lose heart. Paul faced constant hardship, yet he did not give up, because his ministry came from God's mercy and his hope rested in God's power. Believers who feel discouraged can remember that they have received mercy and that God is at work.

The chapter also shows that the treasure of the gospel is held in fragile clay jars. God uses weak, ordinary people so that it is clear the power comes from Him. Believers who feel inadequate can trust that God works through their weakness, and they can keep Jesus, not themselves, at the center of their witness.

2 Corinthians 4 describes the reality of suffering and the faithfulness of God. Believers may be pressed, perplexed, persecuted, and struck down, but they are not crushed, in despair, forsaken, or destroyed. Through their sufferings, the life of Jesus is revealed. Believers can be honest about their struggles while trusting God's sustaining power.

Finally, the chapter offers deep hope to those who are suffering or aging. Though the outer person is wasting away, the inner person is being renewed day by day. Present troubles, though real, are light and momentary compared to the eternal weight of glory they are producing. Believers can fix their eyes on what is unseen and eternal, finding strength to endure.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord God, thank You for shining Your light into my heart and showing me Your glory in the face of Jesus Christ. Thank You for entrusting the treasure of the gospel to an ordinary clay jar like me. Let Your power be seen in my weakness. When I am pressed on every side, keep me from being crushed. When I am perplexed, keep me from despair. When I feel attacked, remind me that You will never forsake me. When I am struck down, raise me up. Let the life of Jesus be revealed in me. Though my outer self is wasting away, renew my inner self day by day. Help me to see my troubles in the light of eternity, and to fix my eyes not on what is seen, but on what is unseen and eternal. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
