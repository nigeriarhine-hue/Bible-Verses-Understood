# Colossians 2
theme: Complete in Christ
main: Paul tells the Colossians how he struggles in prayer for them, longing that they be encouraged, united in love, and given full understanding of Christ, in whom all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden. He urges them, as they received Christ Jesus the Lord, to keep walking in Him, rooted and built up in Him, and to beware of empty philosophy based on human tradition rather than on Christ. In Christ the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily, and in Him believers have been made full. They have been spiritually circumcised, buried and raised with Him, made alive, and forgiven, because God canceled the record of debt against them by nailing it to the cross and disarmed the spiritual powers. So they must not let anyone judge them by food laws and festivals, which were shadows of Christ, or disqualify them through false humility, angel worship, and harsh rules that cannot restrain the flesh.

## Chapter Background and Story

In chapter 1, Paul presented a majestic portrait of Christ as supreme over all creation and the one through whom God reconciled all things. In chapter 2, he applies that truth to the danger facing the Colossian church. If Christ is supreme and sufficient, then believers do not need anything else to reach spiritual fullness.

The teaching Paul warns against is not described in full, and scholars have reconstructed it in different ways. It seems to have combined elements of Jewish practice (circumcision, food laws, Sabbaths, festivals), ascetic discipline (harsh treatment of the body), mystical experiences (visions), and reverence for angels or spiritual powers. Its teachers presented it as a "philosophy," a path to deeper wisdom and spiritual maturity. Its underlying message was that faith in Christ was a good start but not enough.

Paul's answer is clear and powerful. In Christ, believers already have everything. They do not need to add rules, rituals, or experiences to be complete. They need to stay rooted in the Christ they first received.

### Paul's Struggle for Them (2:1–5)

[[v1|For I desire to have you know how greatly I struggle for you and for those at Laodicea, and for as many as have not seen my face in the flesh]]. Paul had never met these believers, but he struggled for them, likely in intense prayer (see Colossians 4:12). Laodicea was a nearby city; Paul wanted this letter shared there too (4:16).

[[v2|that their hearts may be comforted, they being knit together in love, and gaining all riches of the full assurance of understanding, that they may know the mystery of God, both of the Father and of Christ]], [[v3|in whom all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden]]. The false teachers offered hidden knowledge. Paul declares that all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are already hidden in Christ. "Hidden" does not mean inaccessible, but stored up, like treasure in a vault, available to all who are in Him.

[[v4|Now I say this that no one may delude you with persuasiveness of speech]]. The false teachers were persuasive. Paul warns the Colossians not to be fooled by impressive arguments. [[v5|For though I am absent in the flesh, yet I am with you in the spirit, rejoicing and seeing your order, and the steadfastness of your faith in Christ]]. Paul uses military language: "order" and "steadfastness" describe soldiers in disciplined ranks, standing firm.

### Rooted in Christ (2:6–7)

These verses, at the heart of this chapter, are studied below.

### Beware of Empty Philosophy (2:8)

[[v8|Be careful that you don’t let anyone rob you through his philosophy and vain deceit, after the tradition of men, after the elemental spirits of the world, and not after Christ]]. "Rob" means to take captive, to carry off as plunder. The false teachers would make the Colossians their captives. Paul is not condemning all careful thinking or philosophy as such. He condemns "vain deceit," empty and deceptive teaching rooted in human tradition and "the elemental spirits of the world," rather than in Christ. As in Galatians 4:3, "elemental spirits" may mean the basic principles of religion or the spiritual powers thought to govern the world.

### Fullness and Freedom in Christ (2:9–15)

The fullness of deity in Christ, and the believer's fullness in Him, are studied below. [[v11|In him you were also circumcised with a circumcision not made with hands, in the putting off of the body of the sins of the flesh, in the circumcision of Christ]]. The false teachers may have urged physical circumcision. Paul says believers have already received a spiritual circumcision performed by Christ Himself: the cutting away of the old sinful nature.

[[v12|having been buried with him in baptism, in which you were also raised with him through faith in the working of God, who raised him from the dead]]. Baptism pictures union with Christ in His burial and resurrection (Romans 6:3–4). Believers have died to the old life and been raised to new life, through faith in the God who raised Jesus. What God has done in canceling their debt and defeating the powers is studied below.

### Do Not Be Judged by Shadows (2:16–19)

The issue of food laws and festivals is studied below. [[v18|Let no one rob you of your prize by self-abasement and worshiping of the angels, dwelling in the things which he has not seen, vainly puffed up by his fleshly mind]]. "Rob you of your prize" uses an athletic image: an umpire disqualifying a runner. The false teachers claimed spiritual superiority based on extreme self-denial, angel worship, and visions. "Worshiping of the angels" may mean worshiping angels directly or claiming to join in the worship angels offer. Either way, it shifted attention from Christ. Paul notes the irony: their supposed humility actually made them proud.

[[v19|and not holding firmly to the Head, from whom all the body, being supplied and knit together through the joints and ligaments, grows with God’s growth]]. The fundamental problem was that they had lost connection with Christ, the Head. True spiritual growth comes only from Him, as the body is nourished and held together through its connection to the Head.

### Dead to Human Rules (2:20–23)

[[v20|If you died with Christ from the elemental spirits of the world, why, as though living in the world, do you subject yourselves to ordinances]], [[v21|“Don’t handle, nor taste, nor touch”]]. Paul mimics the rules of the false teachers. Believers have died with Christ and been freed from such regulations. Why would they submit to them again?

[[v22|(all of which perish with use), according to the precepts and doctrines of men?]] These rules concern things that perish, like food. Jesus taught that what goes into the body does not defile a person, but what comes out of the heart (Mark 7:14–23). These are human commands, not God's.

[[v23|These things indeed appear like wisdom in self-imposed worship, humility, and severity to the body, but aren’t of any value against the indulgence of the flesh]]. This is an important insight. Harsh rules and self-punishment may look impressive and spiritual. But they cannot change the heart or truly restrain sinful desires. Only union with Christ and the power of His Spirit can do that, as Paul will show in chapter 3.

## Important Verses Explained

### Colossians 2:6–7 — Rooted and Built Up in Him

verse: Colossians 2:6-7

#### Simple Explanation

These verses summarize Paul's counsel to the Colossians. "As therefore you received Christ Jesus the Lord, walk in him." When they first believed, they received Christ, not just a teaching about Him, but Christ Himself as Lord. Now they are to "walk" in Him, living each day in union with Him. The way they began is the way they should continue. They did not receive Christ through rules or rituals, and they should not try to grow that way either.

Paul then uses several images. "Rooted": like a tree whose roots go deep into the soil, drawing nourishment and stability. The tense in Greek suggests something already done: they have been rooted in Christ once for all. "Built up in him": like a building rising on a firm foundation. This is ongoing: they are being built up continually. "Established in the faith": made firm, stable, and secure in the truth of the gospel. "Even as you were taught": the gospel they learned from Epaphras was sufficient. They did not need new teachers with new doctrines.

"Abounding in it in thanksgiving." The result of being rooted in Christ is overflowing gratitude. Thanksgiving is a recurring theme in Colossians, appearing in every chapter.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses describe the whole Christian life in a few words. It begins with receiving Christ, continues by walking in Him, grows by being rooted and built up in Him, stands firm through being established in the faith, and overflows in thanksgiving.

The image of roots is especially rich. Psalm 1:3 describes the righteous person as [[Psalms 1:3|like a tree planted by the streams of water, that produces its fruit in its season]]. Jeremiah 17:8 says the one who trusts in the LORD is like a tree whose roots reach the stream, not fearing when heat comes. Jesus spoke of seed that fell on rocky ground and withered because it had no root (Mark 4:5–6, 16–17). Deep roots in Christ provide stability in the storms of life and nourishment for growth.

The combination of being "rooted" (a past, completed action) and "built up" (an ongoing process) reflects the shape of the Christian life. Believers are already fully united with Christ, and they are continually growing in Him. Both are true at once.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers grow deep roots in Christ through regular practices that keep them connected to Him: reading and meditating on Scripture, prayer, worship, fellowship with other believers, and obedience. These are not ways of earning God's favor, but ways of drawing nourishment from Christ, just as roots draw from the soil.

When new teachings, trends, or spiritual fads come along, promising something more than Christ, these verses call believers back to the gospel they first received. The way forward is not adding something new, but going deeper into Christ.

Finally, thanksgiving is both a sign and a source of healthy roots. Believers who regularly give thanks find their faith strengthened and their hearts steadied. Gratitude keeps attention focused on what God has already given in Christ.

### Colossians 2:9–10 — The Fullness of Deity Dwells in Him

verse: Colossians 2:9-10

#### Simple Explanation

Paul gives the reason believers should not look beyond Christ. "For in him all the fullness of the Deity dwells bodily." Everything that God is, all of His divine nature, lives in Christ. Not a portion, not a reflection, but all the fullness. And it dwells in Him "bodily," in His real, physical human body. In Jesus, God became fully present in human form.

"And in him you are made full." Because the fullness of God dwells in Christ, and believers are in Christ, they share in that fullness. They are complete in Him. They do not lack anything they need for spiritual life and growth.

"Who is the head of all principality and power." Christ rules over every spiritual power and authority. The false teachers may have suggested that believers needed to honor or appease these powers. Paul says Christ is their head. Believers who have Christ do not need to look to any other power.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse is one of the clearest statements of the full divinity of Christ in the New Testament. It goes beyond saying that Christ reveals God or represents God. It says that the full divine nature dwells in Him. Combined with the word "bodily," it affirms that Jesus is both fully God and fully human, the truth the church later expressed in its creeds.

This truth answered the false teaching directly. Some thinkers of the time believed that God's "fullness" was spread out among many spiritual beings, and that people needed to climb a ladder of spiritual experiences or intermediaries to reach God. Paul says no: all of God's fullness is in Christ. There is no higher level to reach, no other mediator needed (1 Timothy 2:5).

"In him you are made full" is a great comfort. Believers are not spiritually second-class or incomplete. They are not waiting for some extra experience or special knowledge to become truly spiritual. They have Christ, and in Him they have all they need. This does not mean they have reached maturity; growth continues. But they have the source of everything.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many believers feel that something is missing in their spiritual lives. They may be tempted to chase new experiences, techniques, or teachings that promise to take them to a higher level. This verse assures them that in Christ, they are already complete. Growth comes not from looking elsewhere but from going deeper into Him.

This truth also brings freedom from fear. Christ is the head over every spiritual power. Believers do not need to fear unseen forces, curses, or spiritual threats. They belong to the one who rules over all.

Finally, this verse invites worship. In Jesus, the fullness of God has come near. To know Jesus is to know God.

### Colossians 2:13–15 — Nailed to the Cross

verse: Colossians 2:13-15

#### Simple Explanation

Paul describes what God has done for believers through Christ.

"You were dead through your trespasses and the uncircumcision of your flesh." Before Christ, the Colossians, mostly Gentiles, were spiritually dead and outside God's covenant people.

"He made you alive together with him, having forgiven us all our trespasses." God gave them new life together with Christ and forgave all their sins. Notice Paul's shift from "you" to "us": he includes himself among the forgiven.

Verse 14: "wiping out the handwriting in ordinances which was against us." The "handwriting" was a term for a handwritten certificate of debt, like an IOU. Humanity owed God a debt of obedience that it could not pay. The record of that debt, with its legal demands, stood against us. God wiped it out. "He has taken it out of the way, nailing it to the cross." When Jesus was crucified, the charge against Him was nailed to the cross above His head (John 19:19). Paul pictures God nailing humanity's record of debt to the cross. Christ paid it in full.

Verse 15: "Having stripped the principalities and the powers, he made a show of them openly, triumphing over them in it." The spiritual powers that held humanity captive were disarmed. The image is of a Roman triumphal procession, in which a victorious general paraded defeated enemies through the streets, stripped of their weapons. The cross, which looked like Christ's defeat, was actually His victory over every hostile power.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show the cross from two angles. First, it is the place of forgiveness. The debt of sin has been canceled completely. There is no record left against those who are in Christ (Romans 8:1). Second, it is the place of victory. The powers of evil, which used sin and accusation to hold people captive, have been disarmed and defeated.

The two are connected. The spiritual powers' main weapon against humanity is accusation: pointing to the record of sin. When that record is canceled, the powers lose their grip. Revelation 12:10–11 describes Satan as [[Revelation 12:10|the accuser of our brothers]], who is overcome [[Revelation 12:11|because of the Lamb’s blood]].

This passage directly addresses the fear of spiritual powers that may have troubled the Colossians. They did not need to appease or worship angels or other spiritual beings. Those powers had already been publicly defeated by Christ at the cross.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many believers live under a sense of unpaid debt, feeling they must somehow make up for their failures. This passage declares that the debt has been canceled and nailed to the cross. Nothing remains to be paid. Believers can live in the freedom of complete forgiveness.

When feelings of accusation or condemnation arise, whether from one's own conscience, from others, or from the enemy, believers can remember the cross. The record has been wiped out. That does not mean ignoring wrongdoing or the need to make things right with people who have been harmed, but it does mean that guilt before God has been fully dealt with.

This passage also brings courage. Whatever spiritual opposition believers face, Christ has already triumphed. They stand in His victory.

### Colossians 2:16–17 — The Shadow and the Substance

verse: Colossians 2:16-17

#### Simple Explanation

Paul draws a practical conclusion. "Let no one therefore judge you in eating or drinking, or with respect to a feast day or a new moon or a Sabbath day." The false teachers were pressuring the Colossians to follow food laws and to observe the Jewish religious calendar, including annual festivals, monthly new moon celebrations, and the weekly Sabbath, as requirements for spiritual maturity. Paul says believers should not let anyone condemn them for not keeping these practices.

Verse 17 explains why: "which are a shadow of the things to come; but the body is Christ’s." A shadow is cast by a real object. It has an outline of the real thing but no substance of its own. The Old Testament food laws and festivals were shadows that pointed forward to Christ. Now that the reality has come, believers do not need to cling to the shadows as a means of standing with God.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The idea of shadow and reality appears also in Hebrews 8:5 and 10:1, which describe the Old Testament priesthood and sacrifices as shadows of heavenly realities fulfilled in Christ. The Passover pointed to Christ, our Passover Lamb (1 Corinthians 5:7). The Sabbath rest pointed to the true rest found in Him (Matthew 11:28; Hebrews 4:9–10). The food laws pointed to the holiness that Christ provides.

Christians hold different views about the Sabbath today. Some believe the Sabbath principle continues, observed on Sunday as the Lord's Day in honor of the resurrection. Others believe a weekly day of rest is a wise practice but not a binding law. Others keep the seventh-day Sabbath as an act of faithfulness. Paul's point in this passage is not to forbid observing special days, which he allows elsewhere (Romans 14:5–6), but to forbid anyone from judging others or making these observances requirements for being right with God or spiritually mature.

The key phrase is "the body is Christ's," meaning the substance, the reality, belongs to Christ. Everything the shadows pointed to is fulfilled in Him.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers today may face pressure to follow rules that are not required by the gospel: particular diets, religious observances, styles of worship, or spiritual disciplines presented as necessary for real Christianity. This passage frees believers from being judged by such standards. Their standing with God rests on Christ alone.

At the same time, believers are free to practice disciplines and observances that help them grow, such as setting aside a day for rest and worship, fasting, or following the church calendar, as long as they do not treat these as requirements for God's acceptance or use them to judge others.

The image of shadow and substance also invites believers to read the Old Testament with fresh eyes, seeing how its laws, festivals, and sacrifices point to Christ. Every shadow finds its substance in Him.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Colossians 2 calls believers to find their completeness in Christ alone. All the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden in Him. All the fullness of God dwells in Him bodily. And in Him, believers have been made full. They do not need to add special knowledge, rituals, rules, or spiritual experiences to become complete.

The way forward in the Christian life is the same as the way it began. As believers received Christ Jesus the Lord, so they are to walk in Him, rooted and built up in Him, established in the faith, and overflowing with thanksgiving. Deep roots in Christ provide stability when new teachings and spiritual fads come along promising something more.

This chapter also celebrates the completeness of Christ's work on the cross. Believers have been spiritually circumcised, buried and raised with Christ, made alive, and forgiven. The record of debt against them has been wiped out and nailed to the cross. The spiritual powers have been disarmed and publicly defeated. Believers can live free from guilt, accusation, and fear.

Finally, Paul warns against those who would judge believers by food laws, festivals, or harsh disciplines, or who claim spiritual superiority through visions and angel worship. These things are shadows, and the substance is Christ. Human rules may look wise, but they have no power to change the heart. True growth comes only from holding fast to Christ, the Head, from whom the whole body is nourished and grows with the growth that God gives.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden in You, and all the fullness of God dwells in You bodily. Thank You that in You I have been made complete. As I received You as Lord, help me to walk in You day by day, rooted and built up in You, established in the faith, and overflowing with thanksgiving. Guard me from empty philosophy and deceptive teaching that would draw me away from You. Thank You that I was buried with You and raised with You, that You made me alive and forgave all my sins, and that You canceled the record of debt against me by nailing it to the cross. Thank You for triumphing over every power of darkness. Free me from the judgments of others and from rules that cannot change my heart. Help me to hold fast to You, my Head, and to grow with the growth that comes from God. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
