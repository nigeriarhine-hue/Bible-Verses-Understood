# Romans 16
theme: A Family of Faithful Servants and a Final Warning
main: Paul closes his letter by commending Phoebe, a servant of the church in Cenchreae who likely carried the letter, and by greeting a remarkable list of believers in Rome: men and women, Jews and Gentiles, slaves and free, many of whom labored hard in the Lord. Among them are Prisca and Aquila, who risked their lives for Paul, and Andronicus and Junia, notable among the apostles. Paul warns the church to watch out for those who cause divisions with smooth talk, urges them to be wise about good and innocent about evil, and promises that the God of peace will soon crush Satan under their feet. Greetings from Paul's companions and a blessing of grace complete the letter.

## Chapter Background and Story

Many readers skip Romans 16 because it seems to be just a list of names. But this chapter offers a rare and beautiful window into the life of the early church. After fifteen chapters of profound theology, Paul shows that the gospel creates a real community of people who love and serve one another. The names reveal a church that was diverse in background, gender, and social status, united in Christ.

Paul mentions about twenty-six people by name, plus several households and groups. Many of the names are Greek or Latin; some are common names for slaves or freed slaves in Rome. Some are Jewish. About a third are women, and Paul praises several of them for their hard work in ministry. Paul had never visited Rome, yet he knew many people there, probably because believers traveled widely in the Roman world, and some, like Prisca and Aquila, had lived elsewhere and returned to Rome after Claudius's expulsion of the Jews ended.

### Commendation of Phoebe (16:1–2)

Paul's commendation of Phoebe is studied below.

### Greetings to Believers in Rome (16:3–16)

[[v3|Greet Prisca and Aquila, my fellow workers in Christ Jesus]]. Prisca (Priscilla is a diminutive form) and her husband Aquila were tentmakers whom Paul had met in Corinth (Acts 18:2–3). They had taught Apollos the way of God more accurately (Acts 18:26). Paul says they [[v4|risked their own necks for my life]]. We do not know exactly when this happened, perhaps during the riot in Ephesus (Acts 19). Paul and all the Gentile churches owed them thanks. Prisca is often named before her husband, which may suggest her prominence in ministry.

[[v5|Greet the assembly that is in their house]]. The early church had no special buildings; believers met in homes. Prisca and Aquila hosted a house church in Rome, as they had done in Ephesus (1 Corinthians 16:19).

Paul greets Epaenetus, [[v5|the first fruits of Achaia to Christ]] (or of Asia, in some manuscripts), the first convert in his region. He greets Mary, [[v6|who labored much for us]]. Andronicus and Junia are studied below.

Many of the greetings are warm and personal. Paul calls several people "beloved." He greets those [[v10|who are of the household of Aristobulus]] and [[v11|them of the household of Narcissus, who are in the Lord]]. These may have been slaves or servants in the households of prominent men, possibly including Narcissus, a powerful freed slave in the emperor's household. The gospel had reached even into the households of the powerful.

[[v12|Greet Tryphaena and Tryphosa, who labor in the Lord. Greet Persis, the beloved, who labored much in the Lord]]. Tryphaena and Tryphosa may have been sisters, perhaps twins. Their names mean "dainty" and "delicate," yet Paul describes them as hard workers for the Lord.

[[v13|Greet Rufus, the chosen in the Lord, and his mother and mine]]. Mark's Gospel mentions that Simon of Cyrene, who carried Jesus' cross, was [[Mark 15:21|the father of Alexander and Rufus]]. Mark, writing probably for believers in Rome, may have mentioned Rufus because he was known to them. If this is the same Rufus, his father carried Jesus' cross. Rufus's mother had cared for Paul like a mother.

[[v16|Greet one another with a holy kiss]]. The kiss was a common greeting in the ancient world among family and friends. Among believers, it expressed their family relationship in Christ. Today, believers in different cultures express this with a handshake, an embrace, or another warm greeting. [[v16|The assemblies of Christ greet you]].

### Greetings from Paul's Companions (16:21–24)

Paul includes greetings from his companions in Corinth. [[v21|Timothy, my fellow worker, greets you]], along with Lucius, Jason, and Sosipater. Then a surprise: [[v22|I, Tertius, who write the letter, greet you in the Lord]]. Tertius was Paul's scribe or secretary, who wrote down the letter as Paul dictated it. This was common practice in the ancient world.

[[v23|Gaius, my host and host of the whole assembly, greets you]]. Gaius hosted Paul and the whole church in Corinth, probably the Gaius whom Paul had baptized (1 Corinthians 1:14). [[v23|Erastus, the treasurer of the city, greets you]]. Archaeologists in Corinth have found a first-century inscription reading, "Erastus, in return for his aedileship, laid this pavement at his own expense." Many scholars believe this may be the same Erastus, a city official who became a believer.

[[v24|The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all! Amen]]. (In the World English Bible, the closing doxology that many Bibles place here as verses 25–27 appears at the end of chapter 14; see the note there.)

## Important Verses Explained

### Romans 16:1–2 — Phoebe, Servant and Helper

verse: Romans 16:1-2

#### Simple Explanation

Paul begins the final chapter of Romans by introducing a woman named Phoebe. She was probably the person who carried this letter to Rome. In the ancient world, there was no postal service for private letters. Letters were carried by trusted travelers. Phoebe likely traveled from Corinth to Rome, a long and difficult journey, and may have read or explained the letter to the believers there.

[[v1|I commend to you Phoebe, our sister]]. Paul calls her "our sister," a member of God's family. He "commends" her, giving her a letter of recommendation so that the Roman believers would welcome and trust her.

[[v1|who is a servant of the assembly that is at Cenchreae]]. Cenchreae was the eastern port of Corinth. The word translated "servant" is diakonos, the same word that is sometimes translated "deacon" (Philippians 1:1; 1 Timothy 3:8–13). Christians have understood this description of Phoebe in different ways. Some traditions understand that Phoebe held the recognized office of deacon in her church. Others understand the word in its general sense of "servant" or "minister," describing her faithful service without implying a formal office. Either way, Paul clearly honors her as a valued servant of the church.

[[v2|that you receive her in the Lord in a way worthy of the saints]]. Paul asks the Roman believers to welcome her as fellow believers should welcome one another, with love and honor.

[[v2|and that you assist her in whatever matter she may need from you]]. They should help her with whatever she needed while in Rome.

[[v2|for she herself also has been a helper of many, and of my own self]]. The word "helper" can mean "patron" or "benefactor." Phoebe had likely used her resources to support Paul and many others. She may have been a woman of some means, who provided hospitality, financial support, or legal protection.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show the important role of women in the early church. Phoebe was trusted with carrying the most theologically significant letter in the New Testament. She served the church and supported Paul's ministry. Throughout this chapter, Paul greets many women who worked hard for the Lord.

They also show the value of generous service. Phoebe used what she had to help many people. Paul honored her for it. Jesus taught that greatness in His kingdom comes through service (Mark 10:43–45).

Finally, these verses show the importance of hospitality and mutual support. Paul asks the Roman believers to welcome and help Phoebe, just as she had helped others. The church is a network of mutual care, where believers support those who serve.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to honor those who serve in the church, both men and women. Many people serve faithfully behind the scenes, providing hospitality, financial support, and practical help. Believers can recognize and thank them.

They also encourage believers to use their resources to support God's work. Phoebe was a helper of many. Believers can ask how they might use their homes, money, time, and skills to support others.

Finally, these verses encourage believers to welcome those who come to them in the Lord's name. Missionaries, traveling ministers, and believers from other churches deserve warm welcome and practical help.

### Romans 16:7 — Andronicus and Junia

verse: Romans 16:7

#### Simple Explanation

In his long list of greetings, Paul mentions a pair of believers with special honor: [[v7|Greet Andronicus and Junia, my relatives and my fellow prisoners]]. "Relatives" may mean that they were Paul's actual family members, or more likely that they were fellow Jews, since Paul uses this word for several people in this chapter (16:11, 21). They had been in prison with Paul at some point, suffering for the gospel.

[[v7|who are notable among the apostles]]. This phrase has been understood in two ways. Many interpreters understand it to mean that Andronicus and Junia were outstanding apostles themselves, in the broader sense of the word "apostle" as a commissioned messenger or missionary, not one of the Twelve. Others understand it to mean that they were well known to the apostles, respected by them. The Greek can be read either way, and scholars continue to discuss it.

Junia is a female name, and most ancient Christian writers understood her to be a woman. The early church leader John Chrysostom (around A.D. 400) praised her: "How great the devotion of this woman must have been, that she should be even counted worthy of the title of apostle!" Andronicus and Junia were likely a married couple who served together, like Prisca and Aquila.

[[v7|who were also in Christ before me]]. They became believers before Paul did. They may have been among the early believers in Jerusalem, perhaps even witnesses of the risen Christ, since Paul says that Jesus appeared to [[1 Corinthians 15:7|all the apostles]].

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows the honor given to faithful servants who suffer for the gospel. Andronicus and Junia had been imprisoned for Christ. Paul honored them as fellow prisoners and as notable among the apostles.

It also shows the partnership of men and women in ministry. Throughout Romans 16, Paul greets both men and women who served the Lord. Andronicus and Junia, like Prisca and Aquila, appear to have served together as a couple. Christians hold different views about the roles of men and women in church leadership, but all can recognize from this chapter that women were valued partners in the work of the gospel.

Finally, the verse shows Paul's humility. Andronicus and Junia were "in Christ before me." Paul, the great apostle, honored those who had come to faith before him. He recognized that he was part of a larger story.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse encourages believers to honor those who have suffered for Christ. Many believers around the world are imprisoned for their faith. Believers can pray for them, support them, and remember their sacrifice.

It also encourages believers to appreciate the contributions of both men and women in the church. Whatever one's view of specific leadership roles, believers can honor the faithful service of all who labor for the gospel.

Finally, the verse encourages believers to respect those who have walked with Christ longer. Paul honored those who were believers before him. Younger believers can learn from and honor those who have been faithful over many years.

### Romans 16:17–18 — Watch Out for Those Who Cause Divisions

verse: Romans 16:17-18

#### Simple Explanation

After his warm greetings, Paul suddenly gives a strong warning. He cared deeply about the Roman church and wanted to protect it from harm.

[[v17|Now I beg you, brothers, look out for those who are causing the divisions and occasions of stumbling, contrary to the doctrine which you learned]]. Believers should watch for people who cause divisions and create obstacles to faith. These people teach things "contrary to the doctrine which you learned," contradicting the gospel Paul and others had taught. This is different from the disputable matters of chapter 14, where sincere believers disagreed about food and days. These people were promoting false teaching that undermined the gospel itself.

[[v17|and turn away from them]]. Believers should avoid such people, not giving them influence in the church.

[[v18|For those who are such don’t serve our Lord Jesus Christ, but their own belly]]. These people were not truly serving Christ. They served "their own belly," their own appetites and desires. They may have been seeking money, power, recognition, or pleasure.

[[v18|and by their smooth and flattering speech they deceive the hearts of the innocent]]. They used pleasant, persuasive words and flattery to deceive people who were naive or unsuspecting. False teachers are often charming and persuasive. Their danger lies partly in how attractive they seem.

Paul does not name these people. They may not yet have arrived in Rome, but Paul knew from experience that such teachers often followed the spread of the gospel.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show that the church must guard the truth. Paul had spent fifteen chapters explaining the gospel. He knew that false teaching could undermine everything. Jesus also warned, [[Matthew 7:15|Beware of false prophets, who come to you in sheep’s clothing, but inwardly are ravening wolves]].

They also show that motives matter. False teachers serve their own desires rather than Christ. One way to recognize them is to look at their fruit: do they seek Christ's glory or their own gain? Jesus said, [[Matthew 7:16|By their fruits you will know them]].

Finally, these verses show that smooth speech is not the same as truth. Persuasiveness, charm, and flattery can deceive. Believers must test teaching by Scripture, not by how pleasing it sounds.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to be discerning. Not everyone who speaks in the name of Christ is serving Him. Believers can test teaching by comparing it with Scripture and by looking at the character and fruit of those who teach.

They also warn against being swayed by flattery and smooth speech. Some teachers today use charm, emotion, or flattery to gain followers. Believers can be wise, recognizing that truth is more important than eloquence.

Finally, these verses call believers to protect the unity of the church against those who would divide it through false teaching. At the same time, believers should be careful not to label every disagreement as false teaching. The difference between disputable matters (chapter 14) and teaching contrary to the gospel (chapter 16) is important.

### Romans 16:19–20 — Wise in Good, Innocent in Evil

verse: Romans 16:19-20

#### Simple Explanation

After warning about those who cause divisions, Paul encourages the Roman believers.

[[v19|For your obedience has become known to all. I rejoice therefore over you]]. The Roman church was known everywhere for its faithfulness to Christ. Paul rejoiced over them. He was not warning them because they were failing, but because he wanted to protect them.

[[v19|But I desire to have you wise in that which is good, but innocent in that which is evil]]. Paul wanted them to be experts in what is good, knowing God's truth well and living it out. But he wanted them to be "innocent" in evil, inexperienced and unfamiliar with evil, not drawn into it. This echoes Jesus' words: [[Matthew 10:16|Therefore be wise as serpents, and harmless as doves]].

Being "innocent in evil" does not mean being naive or unaware of danger. Paul has just warned them about deceivers. It means not participating in evil, not being stained by it, while being wise enough to recognize it.

[[v20|And the God of peace will quickly crush Satan under your feet]]. This is a powerful promise. Behind the false teachers and divisions stands Satan, the enemy of God's people. But God will defeat him. The language recalls Genesis 3:15, where God promised that the woman's offspring would crush the serpent's head. Christ has already won the decisive victory over Satan through His death and resurrection (Colossians 2:15). The final crushing will come at His return. And believers will share in that victory: Satan will be crushed "under your feet."

Notice that the one who crushes Satan is "the God of peace." God brings peace by defeating the enemy who causes division and destruction.

[[v20|The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you]]. Paul closes this section with a blessing of grace. Grace was the theme of his whole letter, and it remains believers' strength and hope.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses show the balance of Christian wisdom. Believers are called to be discerning without becoming cynical, knowledgeable about good without being fascinated by evil. Paul writes elsewhere, [[1 Corinthians 14:20|Brothers, don’t be children in thoughts, yet in malice be babies, but in thoughts be mature]].

They also show that the battle against evil is ultimately God's. Believers are called to resist evil, but the final victory belongs to God. The church can face opposition with confidence, knowing that Satan is a defeated enemy.

Finally, these verses connect the promise of victory with the first promise of the gospel in Genesis 3:15. From the beginning, God planned to defeat the serpent through the offspring of the woman. Christ fulfilled this promise, and believers share in His triumph.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses encourage believers to grow in wisdom about what is good. Believers can fill their minds with God's truth and learn to recognize what pleases Him.

They also encourage believers to avoid unnecessary exposure to evil. While believers must be aware of the world around them, they do not need to immerse themselves in evil to understand it. Believers can be careful about what they watch, read, and participate in.

Finally, these verses give believers hope in spiritual battle. When believers face temptation, opposition, or the work of the enemy, they can trust that the God of peace will crush Satan under their feet. The victory is certain.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Romans 16 shows that the gospel creates a real community. After fifteen chapters of profound teaching, Paul greets a long list of real people: men and women, Jews and Gentiles, slaves and free, all serving the Lord together. Believers can see their own churches as families of brothers and sisters, valuing each person and their contributions.

The chapter also honors faithful service. Phoebe supported many, Prisca and Aquila risked their lives, Mary and Persis labored hard, Andronicus and Junia suffered imprisonment. Many served in ways that would otherwise have been forgotten. Believers can serve faithfully, knowing that God sees their work, and they can honor those who serve, including those whose work is often unnoticed.

Romans 16 warns believers to watch out for those who cause divisions through teaching contrary to the gospel. Such people often use smooth words and flattery, serving their own desires rather than Christ. Believers can be discerning, testing teaching by Scripture, while still distinguishing between false teaching and disputable matters on which sincere believers differ.

Finally, the chapter calls believers to be wise in what is good and innocent in what is evil, and it promises that the God of peace will soon crush Satan under their feet. Believers can live with confidence that the battle belongs to God, that Christ has already won the victory, and that His grace will be with them always.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for building Your church as a family of faithful servants from every background. Thank You for Phoebe, Prisca and Aquila, Andronicus and Junia, and all whose names are written here, and for the many whose names are known only to You. Help me to serve faithfully in Your church, and to honor and support those who labor for Your gospel. Give me discernment to recognize teaching that is contrary to Your truth, and protect me from smooth words and flattery. Make me wise in what is good and innocent in what is evil. Thank You that the God of peace will crush Satan under our feet, and that Your victory is certain. May Your grace be with me and with all Your people. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
