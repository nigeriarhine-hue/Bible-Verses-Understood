# Revelation 10
theme: The Mighty Angel and the Little Scroll
main: Between the sixth and seventh trumpets, John sees another mighty angel coming down from heaven, wrapped in a cloud, with a rainbow over his head, a face like the sun, and legs like pillars of fire. He holds a little scroll lying open in his hand. Planting his right foot on the sea and his left foot on the land, he gives a loud shout like a lion's roar, and the seven thunders speak. John is about to write down what they said, but a voice from heaven tells him to seal up what the seven thunders have said and not to write it. The angel then raises his right hand to heaven and swears by the eternal Creator of heaven, earth, and sea that there will be no more delay: when the seventh angel sounds his trumpet, the mystery of God will be accomplished, just as He announced to His servants the prophets. The voice from heaven tells John to take the open scroll from the angel's hand. When John asks for it, the angel tells him to take it and eat it: it will be sweet as honey in his mouth but will turn his stomach sour. John eats it, and it is just as the angel said. Then John is told that he must prophesy again about many peoples, nations, languages, and kings.

## Chapter Background and Story

Revelation 10 is an interlude between the sixth and seventh trumpets, just as chapter 7 was an interlude between the sixth and seventh seals. After the terrifying judgments of chapter 9 and the tragic refusal of humanity to repent, the action pauses. This pause focuses on John's calling as a prophet and on the message God's people are to proclaim.

The chapter draws heavily on the call of the prophet Ezekiel, who was also given a scroll to eat (Ezekiel 2:8–3:3), and on Daniel 12, where an angel raises his hand and swears by the One who lives forever about the time of the end (Daniel 12:7). John is presented as standing in the line of the great prophets, receiving a fresh commission to speak God's word to the world.

This interlude continues into chapter 11, where the two witnesses are described. Together, chapters 10 and 11 show the role of God's people as prophetic witnesses in a hostile world.

### The Mighty Angel (10:1–3)

[[v1|I saw a mighty angel coming down out of the sky, clothed with a cloud. A rainbow was on his head. His face was like the sun, and his feet like pillars of fire]]. This angel is described in glorious terms. The cloud, rainbow, shining face, and pillars of fire recall the description of the risen Christ in chapter 1 and of God's throne in chapter 4. Because of this, some interpreters have suggested that this angel is Christ Himself. However, Revelation consistently calls him "another angel" and "a mighty angel" (compare 5:2), and he swears by God rather than receiving worship. Most interpreters therefore understand him as a powerful angel who reflects the glory of God, much as Moses' face shone after being in God's presence. The cloud and pillars of fire also recall the presence of God leading Israel through the wilderness.

[[v2|He had in his hand a little open book. He set his right foot on the sea, and his left on the land]]. The little scroll is open, unlike the sealed scroll of chapter 5. Some interpreters see it as the same scroll the Lamb opened, now unsealed and ready to be proclaimed; others see it as a smaller message, a portion of God's plan given to John to declare. The angel's stance on both sea and land shows that his message concerns the whole world.

[[v3|He cried with a loud voice, as a lion roars. When he cried, the seven thunders uttered their voices]]. The roar of a lion recalls the prophets' description of God's voice: [[Amos 3:8|The lion has roared. Who will not fear? The Lord GOD has spoken. Who can but prophesy?]] The seven thunders respond, perhaps echoing Psalm 29, where the voice of the Lord thunders seven times over creation.

### The Bitter-Sweet Scroll (10:8–11)

[[v8|The voice which I heard from heaven, again speaking with me, said, “Go, take the book which is open in the hand of the angel who stands on the sea and on the land.”]] John is commanded to take the scroll himself. He is not merely to observe; he is to receive the message personally.

It is worth noticing what has just happened in the story. In chapter 9, the world refused to repent in spite of severe judgments. Now, rather than immediately sounding the final trumpet, God renews the prophetic witness. Judgment alone does not soften hard hearts, so God sends His word again through His servants. This pattern reveals God's heart: even near the end, He continues to call people to Himself through the testimony of His people.

## Important Verses Explained

### Revelation 10:4 — Seal Up What the Thunders Said

verse: Revelation 10:4

#### Simple Explanation

When the seven thunders speak, John is ready to record their message: "When the seven thunders sounded, I was about to write." He has been faithfully writing everything he sees and hears, as Christ commanded (1:11, 19).

But this time he is stopped: "I heard a voice from the sky saying, ‘Seal up the things which the seven thunders said, and don’t write them.’" The message of the thunders is not to be shared. John is to keep it hidden.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This is one of the most intriguing moments in Revelation. Interpreters have offered several explanations. Some suggest that the seven thunders announced another series of judgments, which God in His mercy cancelled or chose not to reveal, perhaps in light of the delay ending (10:6). Others suggest the message was simply too sacred or too overwhelming to be written, like Paul's experience of hearing [[2 Corinthians 12:4|unspeakable words, which it is not lawful for a man to utter]].

Whatever the content, this verse teaches an important truth: God has not revealed everything. Even in a book of revelation, some things remain hidden. Moses said, [[Deuteronomy 29:29|The secret things belong to the LORD our God; but the things that are revealed belong to us and to our children forever]].

This should shape how believers read Revelation and all prophecy. There are limits to what humans know about God's plans. Those who claim to have every detail of the future figured out are going beyond what God has revealed. Humility is fitting when studying the things of God.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can rest in the fact that God knows what they do not. They do not need to understand every detail of the future to trust Him. What God has revealed is enough for faith and obedience.

This verse also gives a healthy caution against speculation. Throughout history, many people have made confident predictions about the end times that proved to be wrong. Believers can study prophecy with humility, holding firmly to what is clear and holding loosely to what is uncertain.

Finally, this verse invites believers to trust God's wisdom in what He chooses to reveal and to conceal. His silence is not neglect; it is part of His perfect wisdom and care.

### Revelation 10:5–7 — No More Delay

verse: Revelation 10:5-7

#### Simple Explanation

The mighty angel "lifted up his right hand to the sky," a gesture used when taking a solemn oath. He swears "by him who lives forever and ever, who created heaven and the things that are in it, the earth and the things that are in it, and the sea and the things that are in it." God is described as the eternal Creator of all things. The oath is made in the name of the One who has authority over everything.

The content of the oath is: "that there will no longer be delay." The time of waiting is coming to an end.

Verse 7 explains: "but in the days of the voice of the seventh angel, when he is about to sound, then the mystery of God is finished, as he declared to his servants the prophets." When the seventh trumpet sounds, God's hidden plan, His "mystery," will be brought to completion.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This oath answers the cry of the martyrs in Revelation 6:10: [[Revelation 6:10|How long, Master, the holy and true]]? They were told to wait [[Revelation 6:11|yet for a while]]. Now the angel declares that the waiting is nearly over. God has heard His people's prayers, and He will act.

The scene closely parallels Daniel 12:7, where an angel raises both hands to heaven and swears by Him who lives forever that it will be [[Daniel 12:7|for a time, times, and a half]]. In Daniel, the end was still sealed and future. In Revelation, the angel announces that its fulfillment is at hand.

"The mystery of God" in the New Testament refers to God's plan, once hidden but now revealed in Christ (Romans 16:25; Ephesians 3:3–6; Colossians 1:26–27). It includes His plan to unite all things in Christ, to bring Gentiles and Jews together in one people, and to establish His kingdom fully. This mystery was "declared to his servants the prophets," meaning that it fulfills what God promised throughout Scripture. The word translated "declared" is related to the word for preaching the good news. God's plan is gospel, good news.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers who grow weary of waiting for God to set things right can take comfort in this oath. God's timing may seem slow, but He has sworn that the delay will end. His purposes will be completed.

This passage also reminds believers that God's plan is good news. The completion of the mystery of God means the fulfillment of His promises: the defeat of evil, the vindication of His people, and the establishment of His kingdom.

Because God is the Creator of heaven, earth, and sea, nothing lies outside His authority. Believers can trust that the One who made all things will also bring all things to their intended goal.

### Revelation 10:9–11 — Sweet in the Mouth, Bitter in the Stomach

verse: Revelation 10:9-11

#### Simple Explanation

John goes to the angel and asks for the little scroll. The angel says: "Take it and eat it. It will make your stomach bitter, but in your mouth it will be as sweet as honey."

In verse 10, John obeys: "I took the little book out of the angel’s hand, and ate it. It was as sweet as honey in my mouth. When I had eaten it, my stomach was made bitter."

Then in verse 11, John receives a fresh commission: "You must prophesy again over many peoples, nations, languages, and kings." Having taken in God's message, he must now proclaim it to the whole world.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Eating the scroll is a symbolic act. It means fully taking in God's message, making it part of oneself before speaking it to others. Ezekiel had the same experience: God gave him a scroll to eat, and [[Ezekiel 3:3|it was as sweet as honey in my mouth]]. Jeremiah also said, [[Jeremiah 15:16|Your words were found, and I ate them. Your words were to me a joy and the rejoicing of my heart]].

The scroll is sweet because God's word is good. It reveals His love, His faithfulness, His victory, and His plan to make all things new. The psalmist declared that God's words are sweeter than honey (Psalm 19:10; 119:103).

But the scroll also turns bitter. God's message includes judgment on a rebellious world and suffering for His people. To carry this message is a heavy burden. Prophets throughout Scripture wept over the messages they had to deliver. Jesus Himself wept over Jerusalem. The bitterness may also point to the suffering that faithful witnesses will face, as chapter 11 goes on to describe.

The commission to prophesy "over many peoples, nations, languages, and kings" shows that God's message is for the whole world. Some translate it "about" many peoples, emphasizing that the rest of the book concerns the nations. Either way, John's message has global significance, just as the church's witness does.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This passage calls believers to take in God's word deeply, not merely to read it or study it from a distance, but to make it part of their lives. Before speaking God's word to others, they must receive it themselves.

Believers can also expect that God's word will be both sweet and bitter. It brings joy, hope, comfort, and life. But it also confronts sin, calls for sacrifice, and sometimes leads to suffering. Faithful believers embrace both, trusting that God's word is good even when it is hard.

Finally, believers share in John's commission. The church is called to bear witness to Christ among all peoples, nations, languages, and leaders. This calling is both a privilege and a responsibility. Believers can pray for courage and compassion as they speak God's truth in love.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Revelation 10 pauses the judgments to focus on God's message and the messenger who carries it. The mighty angel, glorious and powerful, stands on sea and land, showing that God's word concerns the whole world.

The sealing of the seven thunders reminds believers that God has not revealed everything. They can trust Him with what remains hidden, study prophecy with humility, and focus on what God has made clear.

The angel's oath assures believers that God's purposes will be completed. The waiting will end. The mystery of God, His plan to bring all things together in Christ, will be fulfilled. This gives hope to all who long for God to set the world right.

The eating of the scroll calls believers to receive God's word fully, to let it shape them inwardly before they speak it outwardly. God's word is sweet, full of grace and promise, but it can also be bitter, carrying hard truths and the cost of faithfulness. Like John, believers are called to bear witness to the nations, sharing God's message with courage, honesty, and love.

Lord God, Creator of heaven, earth, and sea, You live forever, and Your purposes will surely be fulfilled. Thank You that the waiting will end and that Your plan in Christ will be completed. Help me to trust You with what You have not revealed, and to hold firmly to what You have made clear. Give me a hunger for Your word. Let me take it in deeply, so that it shapes my heart and my life. Thank You that Your word is sweeter than honey. When it is hard and costly, give me grace to receive it and obey it. Give me courage and love to bear witness to Christ among the people around me and to the nations. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
