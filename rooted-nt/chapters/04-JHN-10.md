# John 10
theme: The Good Shepherd Who Lays Down His Life and Holds His Sheep Secure
main: Jesus contrasts Himself with thieves, robbers, and hired hands. He is the door through which the sheep find salvation and pasture, and He is the Good Shepherd who knows His sheep by name and lays down His life for them of His own free will. At the Feast of Dedication, He declares that His sheep hear His voice and follow Him, that He gives them eternal life and no one can snatch them from His hand or the Father's, and that He and the Father are one.

## Chapter Background and Story

John 10 follows directly from John 9. The Pharisees had just thrown out the man born blind, treating him harshly instead of caring for him. Jesus, by contrast, sought him out. Now Jesus uses the familiar picture of shepherds and sheep to show the difference between false leaders who harm the flock and the true Shepherd who cares for it.

The image was deeply rooted in Israel's Scriptures. God is called the Shepherd of Israel (Psalm 80:1), and David wrote, [[Psalm 23:1|The LORD is my shepherd]]. Israel's kings and leaders were also called shepherds. In Ezekiel 34, God condemns the shepherds of Israel who fed themselves instead of the flock, who did not strengthen the weak or seek the lost, and He promises: "I myself will search for my sheep" and "I will set up one shepherd over them, and he will feed them, even my servant David" (Ezekiel 34:11, 23). Jesus' words in John 10 claim the fulfillment of that promise.

### The Shepherd and the Door (10:1–6)

In Palestine, sheep were often kept at night in a sheepfold, a walled enclosure of stone with a single opening. Sometimes several flocks shared a fold, with a gatekeeper watching the entrance. In the morning, each shepherd came and called his own sheep, and they recognized his voice and followed him out.

Jesus said that anyone who climbs into the fold some other way is [[v1|a thief and a robber]], but the one who enters by the door is the shepherd. [[v3|He calls his own sheep by name and leads them out]]. Shepherds often gave their sheep names, and the sheep knew their shepherd's voice. [[v4|he goes before them; and the sheep follow him, for they know his voice]]. Eastern shepherds led their flocks from the front rather than driving them from behind. The sheep would not follow a stranger.

The listeners did not understand what Jesus meant, so He explained further.

### I Am the Door (10:7–10)

Jesus said, [[v7|I am the sheep’s door]]. In some shepherds' practice, especially in the open countryside, a fold had an opening but no gate, and the shepherd himself would lie across the entrance at night. Nothing could enter or leave except through him. Jesus is the only way into the safety of God's flock.

[[v8|All who came before me are thieves and robbers]]. This does not refer to the true prophets, but to false messiahs and leaders who claimed to bring salvation and instead exploited the people. [[v9|I am the door. If anyone enters in by me, he will be saved, and will go in and go out and will find pasture]]. "Going in and out" was a Hebrew expression for living safely and freely (Deuteronomy 28:6). Through Jesus, the sheep find salvation, security, freedom, and nourishment. Verse 10 is studied below.

### I Am the Good Shepherd (10:11–18)

Jesus declared, [[v11|I am the good shepherd]]. This is studied below. He contrasted the Good Shepherd with the hired hand, who works for wages and does not own the sheep. When the wolf comes, the hired hand runs away, because he does not truly care for the sheep.

[[v14|I am the good shepherd. I know my own, and I’m known by my own]]. Jesus' relationship with His sheep is personal and intimate, reflecting His relationship with the Father: [[v15|even as the Father knows me, and I know the Father]].

Then He spoke of a wider flock: [[v16|I have other sheep which are not of this fold. I must bring them also, and they will hear my voice. They will become one flock with one shepherd]]. The "other sheep" are generally understood as Gentiles, people outside Israel, who would come to faith and join with Jewish believers in one people of God.

Jesus emphasized that His death would be voluntary: [[v18|No one takes it away from me, but I lay it down by myself. I have power to lay it down, and I have power to take it again]]. His death would not be a tragic accident or a defeat. He would give His life freely, in obedience to the Father, and He would take it up again in resurrection.

The crowd was divided again. Some said He was demon-possessed and insane. Others said, [[v21|It isn’t possible for a demon to open the eyes of the blind, is it?]]

### At the Feast of Dedication (10:22–42)

Some time later, perhaps two months, Jesus was in Jerusalem for [[v22|the Feast of the Dedication]], which is known today as Hanukkah. It remembered the rededication of the temple in 164 B.C., after the Jewish leader Judas Maccabeus drove out the Syrian king Antiochus IV Epiphanes, who had desecrated the temple. The feast celebrated deliverance from foreign oppression, and it naturally stirred messianic hopes.

It was winter, and Jesus was walking in Solomon's porch, a covered colonnade on the eastern side of the temple. The leaders surrounded Him and said, [[v24|How long will you hold us in suspense? If you are the Christ, tell us plainly]]. Jesus answered that He had told them, and that His works testified about Him, but they did not believe because they were not His sheep. Then He spoke of the security of His sheep, studied below, and declared, [[v30|I and the Father are one]], also studied below.

The leaders picked up stones again. Jesus asked for which of His many good works they were stoning Him. They answered, [[v33|We don’t stone you for a good work, but for blasphemy, because you, being a man, make yourself God]].

Jesus replied with an argument from Psalm 82:6: [[v34|Isn’t it written in your law, ‘I said, you are gods’?]] In that psalm, God addresses human rulers and judges as "gods" because they were given authority to judge on God's behalf, though they would die like men. Jesus reasons from the lesser to the greater: if Scripture, which [[v35|can’t be broken]], could call such people "gods," how could they accuse Him of blasphemy for calling Himself the Son of God, when He is the One the Father consecrated and sent into the world? Jesus was not reducing His claim to that of an ordinary human judge. He was showing that their charge of blasphemy did not take Scripture seriously, and He pointed again to His works: [[v38|believe the works, that you may know and believe that the Father is in me, and I in the Father]].

They tried to seize Him, but He escaped. He went back across the Jordan to the place where John the Baptist had first baptized, where His ministry had begun. Many came to Him and said, [[v41|John indeed did no sign, but everything that John said about this man is true]]. [[v42|Many believed in him there]].

## Important Verses Explained

### John 10:10 — Life to the Full

verse: John 10:10

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus had been describing how a shepherd cares for his sheep. In the morning, the shepherd comes to the sheepfold through the gate, calls his sheep by name, and leads them out to pasture. Then Jesus said that He is the door of the sheep: anyone who enters through Him will be saved and find pasture.

In contrast, He described the thief: [[v10|The thief only comes to steal, kill, and destroy]]. In context, the "thief" refers to false leaders who use people for their own purposes. The religious leaders who had just thrown out the healed blind man (John 9) were acting like thieves, harming the very sheep they were supposed to protect. Behind all such destructive influence stands the enemy of souls, whom Jesus called [[John 8:44|a murderer from the beginning]].

Then Jesus said, [[v10|I came that they may have life, and may have it abundantly]]. Jesus' purpose is the opposite of the thief's. The thief takes; Jesus gives. The thief destroys; Jesus brings life.

The word "abundantly" means "more than enough," overflowing, beyond what is necessary. Jesus does not offer a barely-surviving existence but life in its fullness. In John's Gospel, this life is eternal life, the life of God shared with His people, which begins now and continues forever.

It is important to understand what Jesus means by "abundant life." He does not promise wealth, comfort, or freedom from trouble. In this same Gospel, He tells His disciples that in the world they will have trouble (John 16:33). Abundant life is the richness of knowing God, being forgiven, being cared for by the Good Shepherd, and having a life filled with purpose, love, and hope.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse summarizes the purpose of Jesus' coming. He came not to burden people but to give them life. Psalm 23 describes the life of a sheep under the Lord's care: green pastures, still waters, a restored soul, protection in the valley of the shadow of death, and a table prepared even in the presence of enemies. Jesus is the Shepherd who brings that life to reality.

The verse also helps believers discern between true and false voices. Any message, teacher, or influence that steals, kills, and destroys does not come from Christ. Jesus' voice leads to life, freedom, and wholeness. This does not mean the Christian life is always easy, but its direction is always toward life.

Finally, the abundance Jesus offers flows from His sacrifice. In the very next verse He says that the Good Shepherd lays down His life for the sheep. The fullness of life believers receive was purchased at the cost of His death.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites people to consider what is truly giving them life. Many things promise fulfillment but leave people empty, enslaved, or harmed. Jesus offers life that is real and lasting. Believers can bring their whole lives to Him and receive the life He gives.

It also corrects distorted ideas about the Christian life. Some people imagine that following Jesus means a smaller, more restricted life. Jesus says the opposite: He came to give abundant life. His commands are not meant to rob people of joy but to lead them into true freedom and wholeness.

Finally, the verse calls Christian leaders to examine their leadership. Leaders in families, churches, and workplaces can either give life or take it. Those who follow the Good Shepherd are called to care for others in ways that nourish and protect, never to use people for their own gain. Leadership that harms, manipulates, or exploits the vulnerable is the work of a thief, not a shepherd.

### John 10:11 — The Good Shepherd Lays Down His Life

verse: John 10:11

#### Simple Explanation

After describing Himself as the door of the sheep, Jesus used a second image: [[v11|I am the good shepherd]]. The word "good" here carries the sense of noble, excellent, or beautiful. Jesus is not only adequate as a shepherd; He is the ideal Shepherd, the one in whom all the qualities of a true shepherd are perfectly found.

[[v11|The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep]]. In Palestine, shepherding could be dangerous. Wolves, lions, bears, and thieves threatened the flock. David told King Saul that as a shepherd he had fought lions and bears to rescue his sheep (1 Samuel 17:34–35). A good shepherd was willing to put himself in danger to protect his flock.

Jesus goes further. He does not merely risk His life; He lays it down. This points directly to His death on the cross. The phrase "for the sheep" means on behalf of them, in their place. He would die so that they could live.

Jesus contrasts Himself with the hired hand, who works only for wages. When the wolf comes, the hired hand runs away, because the sheep are not his own and he does not truly care about them. Jesus owns His sheep, knows them, and loves them. He will not abandon them when danger comes.

Jesus says that His death is voluntary: [[v18|No one takes it away from me, but I lay it down by myself]]. He would not be a helpless victim of circumstances. He would give His life freely, out of love and obedience to the Father.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse is a clear statement of Jesus' substitutionary death. The Shepherd dies for the sheep. Isaiah prophesied, [[Isaiah 53:6|All we like sheep have gone astray. Everyone has turned to his own way; and the LORD has laid on him the iniquity of us all]]. Jesus is both the Shepherd who dies for the sheep and the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world (John 1:29).

It also fulfills God's promise in Ezekiel 34 to raise up a true shepherd for His people. The leaders of Israel had failed as shepherds, but God promised to shepherd His people Himself and to set over them a shepherd from David's line. Jesus fulfills both: He is God Himself shepherding His people, and He is the promised Son of David.

Finally, the verse shows the depth of Christ's love. He did not lay down His life for people who deserved it. He died for straying sheep, people who had wandered from God. This is the love Paul describes when he writes that [[Romans 5:8|God commends his own love toward us, in that while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us]].

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse offers believers deep security. The Shepherd who laid down His life for them will not abandon them now. When they face fear, loss, or danger, they can trust that their Shepherd cares for them personally and will never run away.

It also sets the pattern for Christian leadership and love. Peter later urged church leaders to [[1 Peter 5:2|shepherd the flock of God which is among you]] willingly and not for dishonest gain. Pastors, parents, and anyone responsible for others are called to sacrificial care, putting the good of those in their care above their own comfort.

Finally, this verse calls people to come to the Shepherd. Sheep that wander are in danger. Jesus has given His life to bring them back. No matter how far someone has strayed, the Good Shepherd invites them to return to His care.

### John 10:27–28 — No One Will Snatch Them

verse: John 10:27-28

#### Simple Explanation

These words were spoken during the Feast of Dedication in winter, as Jesus walked in Solomon's porch in the temple. The religious leaders surrounded Him and demanded that He tell them plainly whether He was the Christ. Jesus answered that He had already told them, and His works testified about Him, but they did not believe because they were not His sheep.

Then He described His true sheep: [[v27|My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me]]. There are three marks here. They hear His voice, listening to and receiving His words. He knows them, with a personal knowledge that includes love and care. And they follow Him, trusting and obeying Him as their Shepherd.

Jesus then made a promise: [[v28|I give eternal life to them]]. Eternal life is His gift, not something His sheep earn. [[v28|They will never perish]]. The Greek uses a strong double negative: they will certainly not perish, ever.

[[v28|and no one will snatch them out of my hand]]. The word "snatch" is the same word used for the wolf snatching the sheep in verse 12. Wolves, thieves, and enemies of every kind may try, but they will not succeed. Jesus holds His sheep securely.

He added in the next verse: [[v29|My Father who has given them to me is greater than all. No one is able to snatch them out of my Father’s hand]]. The sheep are held in two hands, the Son's and the Father's. They are doubly secure.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses are among the strongest statements in Scripture of the security of those who belong to Christ. Their eternal life does not depend on their own ability to hold on, but on the power of the Son and the Father who hold them. Paul echoes this when he writes that nothing in all creation [[Romans 8:39|will be able to separate us from God’s love, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord]].

The verses also connect security with relationship. The sheep who are secure are those who hear His voice and follow Him. Christians have discussed how these truths fit together. Some emphasize that true believers will never finally fall away, because God keeps them. Others emphasize the importance of continuing to follow Christ and warn against presuming on God's grace. Both perspectives agree that the sheep who belong to Jesus are known by their following Him, and that their safety rests in His hands.

Finally, these verses reveal the tenderness of the Shepherd. "I know them" speaks of a personal, intimate relationship. Believers are not anonymous members of a crowd. The Shepherd knows each one by name.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

These verses bring comfort to believers who fear losing their salvation or being overwhelmed by temptation, doubt, or suffering. Their security rests not on their strength but on Christ's. When they feel weak, they can remember that they are held in the hands of the Son and the Father.

They also encourage believers to listen for the Shepherd's voice. Jesus speaks through His word, by His Spirit, and through the fellowship of His people. Sheep learn to recognize their shepherd's voice by spending time with him. Believers grow in recognizing Christ's voice as they spend time in Scripture and prayer.

Finally, these verses call believers to follow. Hearing is not enough; Jesus' sheep follow Him. Following means trusting Him with each step, going where He leads, and staying close to Him through every season.

### John 10:30 — I and the Father Are One

verse: John 10:30

#### Simple Explanation

Jesus had just promised that no one could snatch His sheep out of His hand, and no one could snatch them out of the Father's hand. The sheep are held securely by both the Son and the Father. Then He made this short but profound statement: [[v30|I and the Father are one]].

The word "one" in Greek is neuter, not masculine. Jesus is not saying that He and the Father are one person; they are distinct, as the whole chapter shows (the Father gives, the Son receives; the Father sends, the Son is sent). Rather, He is saying that they are one in nature, purpose, and power. What the Son does, the Father does. To be held by the Son is to be held by the Father.

The religious leaders understood the meaning. They immediately picked up stones again to stone Him. When Jesus asked why, they answered, [[v33|because you, being a man, make yourself God]]. They understood that He was claiming divine equality.

Jesus did not withdraw His claim. He argued from Scripture that their charge of blasphemy was unjustified and pointed to His works as evidence that [[v38|the Father is in me, and I in the Father]].

This verse summarizes what John's Gospel teaches throughout: Jesus is fully one with the Father, yet distinct from Him. This is the foundation of what Christians later expressed in the doctrine of the Trinity.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse is central to the Christian understanding of who Jesus is. Jesus does not claim to be a second god alongside the Father, nor merely a good man in agreement with God. He claims a unity with the Father that only God could claim. The early church, reflecting on texts like this one, confessed that the Son is of one being with the Father, true God from true God.

It also shows that the security of believers rests on the unity of the Father and the Son. Because they are one, the believer held by the Son is held by the Father. There is no division in God's purpose to save and keep His people.

Finally, the verse invites worship. If Jesus and the Father are one, then to know Jesus is to know God, and to honor Jesus is to honor God. As Jesus said earlier, [[John 5:23|He who doesn’t honor the Son doesn’t honor the Father who sent him]].

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse assures believers that when they look at Jesus, they see the heart of God. His compassion, His truth, His mercy toward sinners, and His willingness to lay down His life all reveal what God is like. There is no hidden God behind Jesus who is different from Him.

It also gives believers a firm foundation for trust. The Shepherd who holds them is one with the all-powerful Father. Nothing can overpower Him. When believers face uncertainty, they can rest in the united love and power of the Father and the Son.

Finally, the verse calls believers to give Jesus the worship and trust that belong to God. He is not merely a teacher to admire but the Lord to adore, the One who is one with the Father.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

John 10 presents Jesus as the Good Shepherd who knows His sheep by name, leads them, protects them, and lays down His life for them. Believers can take deep comfort in this picture. They are not alone or forgotten. Their Shepherd knows them personally, cares for them more than any hired hand ever could, and has already proven His love by dying for them.

The chapter also helps believers recognize true and false shepherds. Some leaders and influences steal, kill, and destroy; Jesus gives life to the full. Believers can learn to listen for His voice, to follow Him, and to be wary of any voice that leads them away from Him. Those who lead others, whether in the church, the home, or elsewhere, are called to imitate the Good Shepherd: caring for the weak, seeking the lost, protecting the vulnerable, and never exploiting those in their care.

John 10 offers a powerful assurance of security. Jesus' sheep are held in His hand and in the Father's hand, and no one can snatch them away. This truth gives believers peace when they face fear, temptation, or suffering. Their safety rests on the power and faithfulness of God, not on their own strength.

Finally, the chapter calls readers to respond to Jesus' claim to be one with the Father. Many listeners rejected Him and picked up stones. Others believed. Jesus' works and words still testify about Him. Each person is invited to hear His voice, come through Him as the door, and follow Him as the Good Shepherd who gives eternal life.

## Chapter Prayer

Lord Jesus, You are the Good Shepherd. Thank You for knowing me by name and for laying down Your life for me. Thank You that You came not to steal and destroy, but to give life to the full. Teach me to recognize Your voice above all the other voices around me, and give me a heart that follows You wherever You lead. When I am afraid, remind me that I am held in Your hand and in the Father's hand, and that no one can snatch me away. Guard me from false shepherds and from anything that would lead me away from You. If I lead or care for others in any way, make me a shepherd like You, gentle, faithful, and willing to sacrifice. Thank You that You and the Father are one, and that in You I see the heart of God. Bring Your other sheep into Your fold, so that there may be one flock and one Shepherd. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
