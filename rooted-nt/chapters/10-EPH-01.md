# Ephesians 1
theme: Every Spiritual Blessing in Christ
main: Paul opens with a great hymn of praise to God, who has blessed believers with every spiritual blessing in Christ. Before the foundation of the world, God chose them to be holy and destined them for adoption as His children. In Christ they have redemption through His blood and the forgiveness of sins, and God has made known His plan to unite all things in Christ. Believers have been sealed with the promised Holy Spirit as the guarantee of their inheritance. Paul then prays that they will have the eyes of their hearts enlightened to know the hope of their calling, the riches of God's inheritance, and the immense power that raised Christ from the dead and seated Him above every authority as head over all things for the church.

## Chapter Background and Story

Ephesians begins differently from many of Paul's letters. There is no crisis to address, no false teaching to correct in the opening lines. Instead, after a short greeting, Paul bursts into praise. Verses 3–14 form a single, long sentence in the original Greek, overflowing with one blessing after another. It reads like a hymn, celebrating the work of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.

The believers who first received this letter lived in the shadow of the great temple of Artemis in Ephesus, in a culture full of magic, astrology, and fear of spiritual powers (Acts 19:18–19). Many of them had once lived in dread of forces they could not control. Paul's opening chapter lifts their eyes to see that God's purposes stretch from before the creation of the world to the end of the ages, and that the risen Christ reigns far above every power. Their lives are held securely in the plan of a loving God.

The chapter has two parts: the great blessing (1:3–14) and Paul's prayer for his readers (1:15–23).

### Greeting (1:1–2)

[[v1|Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus through the will of God, to the saints who are at Ephesus, and the faithful in Christ Jesus]]. "Saints" means holy ones, those set apart for God. Paul uses it for all believers, not just a special few. They are "faithful," or believing, and they are "in Christ Jesus," a phrase that will echo throughout the letter. [[v2|Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ]].

### The Father's Blessing: Chosen and Adopted (1:3–6)

The opening blessing and God's choice of His people are studied below. [[v5|having predestined us for adoption as children through Jesus Christ to himself, according to the good pleasure of his desire]]. God planned beforehand that believers would be adopted as His children. This was not a reluctant decision but "the good pleasure of his desire." God delighted to bring people into His family. [[v6|to the praise of the glory of his grace, by which he freely gave us favor in the Beloved]]. The purpose of it all is the praise of God's grace. "The Beloved" is Christ, the Father's beloved Son (Matthew 3:17). Believers are accepted because they are in Him.

### The Son's Work: Redemption and God's Plan (1:7–12)

Redemption through Christ's blood is studied below. [[v8|which he made to abound toward us in all wisdom and prudence]], [[v9|making known to us the mystery of his will, according to his good pleasure which he purposed in him]]. In Paul's letters, a "mystery" is not a puzzle but a truth once hidden and now revealed by God. The mystery here is God's plan for history.

[[v10|to an administration of the fullness of the times, to sum up all things in Christ, the things in the heavens and the things on the earth, in him]]. This is one of the grandest statements in the Bible. God's purpose is to bring everything together under Christ as head. A world fractured by sin, divided between heaven and earth, between peoples and nations, will one day be reunited and set right in Christ.

[[v11|We were also assigned an inheritance in him, having been foreordained according to the purpose of him who does all things after the counsel of his will]], [[v12|to the end that we should be to the praise of his glory, we who had before hoped in Christ]]. "We who had before hoped in Christ" may refer to Jewish believers, who first hoped in the Messiah, as distinct from the Gentile believers addressed in verse 13 as "you also."

### Paul's Prayer (1:15–23)

[[v15|For this cause I also, having heard of the faith in the Lord Jesus which is among you and the love which you have toward all the saints]], [[v16|don’t cease to give thanks for you, making mention of you in my prayers]]. Faith in the Lord and love for fellow believers go together; they are the twin marks of genuine Christian life. [[v17|that the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory, may give to you a spirit of wisdom and revelation in the knowledge of him]]. Paul's first prayer is not for their circumstances but for their knowledge of God. The content of his prayer is studied below.

[[v20|which he worked in Christ when he raised him from the dead and made him to sit at his right hand in the heavenly places]], [[v21|far above all rule, authority, power, dominion, and every name that is named, not only in this age, but also in that which is to come]]. The resurrection and ascension of Christ display God's power. Christ reigns above every spiritual power, every authority, every name, now and forever. For believers who once feared spiritual forces, this was profoundly reassuring.

[[v22|He put all things in subjection under his feet, and gave him to be head over all things for the assembly]], [[v23|which is his body, the fullness of him who fills all in all]]. Paul echoes Psalm 8:6, where God put all things under humanity's feet. That psalm finds its fulfillment in Christ. And Christ's supreme authority is exercised "for the assembly," the church. The church is His body, so closely united with Him that it is called His "fullness." The one who fills the whole universe with His presence fills the church in a special way.

## Important Verses Explained

### Ephesians 1:3–4 — Blessed and Chosen in Christ

verse: Ephesians 1:3-4

#### Simple Explanation

Paul begins with praise: "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ." To "bless" God means to praise Him, to speak well of Him. Paul then explains why: God "has blessed us with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places in Christ."

"Every spiritual blessing" means every blessing that comes from the Holy Spirit and belongs to the life of God's kingdom. Nothing believers need for their spiritual life has been withheld. "In the heavenly places" refers to the spiritual realm where Christ reigns. Though believers live on earth, they already share in the blessings of heaven through their union with Christ. And all these blessings come "in Christ." They are found in Him and given through Him.

Verse 4 names the first blessing: God "chose us in him before the foundation of the world." Before God created anything, He had a plan to have a people for Himself. That choice was made "in him," in Christ.

The purpose of the choice was "that we would be holy and without defect before him in love." God chose His people so that they would belong to Him, be set apart for Him, and be made blameless in His sight. The phrase "in love" may describe God's love in choosing, or the love in which believers are to live. Both are true.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

The idea that God "chose" His people before creation has been discussed by Christians for centuries, and different traditions understand it in different ways. Some, often called Reformed or Calvinist, understand it to mean that God, in His sovereign grace, chose particular individuals for salvation, not on the basis of anything in them. Others, often called Arminian or Wesleyan, understand it to mean that God chose Christ and all who would be in Christ by faith, foreseeing their response, so that election is corporate and conditional on faith. Still others emphasize that the choice is of a people, the church, in Christ, rather than of isolated individuals. Each view seeks to honor both God's sovereign grace and human responsibility, and all agree that salvation originates in God's loving initiative, not in human merit.

What is clear in this passage is the tone. Paul does not present election as a cause for anxiety or speculation, but as a reason for praise. Believers' salvation was not an afterthought. God's love for them reaches back before time began. It is grounded "in Christ," so that believers can look to Christ for assurance rather than trying to look into God's secret plans.

The goal of election is holiness. God did not choose people so that they could live however they please, but so that they would become holy and blameless. Election and transformation go together.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many people struggle with feelings of being unwanted, overlooked, or accidental. These verses proclaim that believers were chosen in love before the world began. Their lives have meaning within God's eternal purpose. That truth can bring deep security and peace.

These verses also invite praise. Paul begins not with requests or instructions but with worship. Believers can cultivate the habit of thanking God for spiritual blessings that cannot be taken away, even when earthly circumstances are hard.

Finally, being chosen calls for holiness. Those who have been chosen in love are invited to live lives that reflect God's character, not to earn His choice, but in grateful response to it.

### Ephesians 1:7 — Redemption Through His Blood

verse: Ephesians 1:7

#### Simple Explanation

Having described the Father's choice, Paul turns to the work of the Son. "In him we have our redemption through his blood." Redemption means release by the payment of a price. In the ancient world, it was used for freeing slaves or prisoners of war. In the Old Testament, God redeemed Israel from slavery in Egypt (Exodus 6:6). Now, through Christ, believers are set free from slavery to sin.

The price was Christ's "blood," that is, His sacrificial death on the cross. His life was given in exchange for theirs.

Paul then defines this redemption as "the forgiveness of our trespasses." "Trespasses" are wrongful steps, the ways people have crossed God's boundaries. Through Christ's death, these are forgiven, completely and freely.

All of this is "according to the riches of his grace." God does not forgive grudgingly or in small amounts. He forgives out of the overflowing wealth of His grace.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Notice that Paul says "we have" redemption. It is a present possession, not merely a future hope. Believers are already forgiven and set free.

The reference to Christ's blood connects with the entire sacrificial system of the Old Testament, where the shedding of blood signified life given for life (Leviticus 17:11). Hebrews 9:22 states that without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness. Jesus is the fulfillment of every sacrifice, the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world (John 1:29).

The phrase "the riches of his grace" is characteristic of Ephesians, which speaks often of God's riches: riches of His glory (1:18; 3:16), riches of His mercy (2:4), and the unsearchable riches of Christ (3:8). God is not poor in grace. His forgiveness is not measured out sparingly but poured out abundantly.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many believers carry lingering guilt over past sins, even after confessing them. This verse declares that forgiveness is a present reality, secured by Christ's blood and given according to the riches of God's grace. No sin is too great for that grace.

Knowing the cost of redemption also deepens gratitude. Forgiveness is free to the one who receives it, but it was costly to the one who gave it. Remembering the cross can move believers to love Christ more deeply and to extend forgiveness to others, as Paul urges later in this letter (Ephesians 4:32).

### Ephesians 1:13–14 — Sealed with the Holy Spirit

verse: Ephesians 1:13-14

#### Simple Explanation

Paul now turns to the work of the Holy Spirit and to the Gentile believers ("you also"). He traces their journey. First, they "heard the word of the truth, the Good News of your salvation." Then they "believed." And when they believed, they "were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit."

In the ancient world, a seal was a mark made in wax or clay, often with a signet ring. It showed ownership, authenticity, and protection. A sealed document or package belonged to its owner and was guarded from tampering. The Holy Spirit is God's seal on believers, marking them as His own and guaranteeing their security.

The Spirit is called "the promised Holy Spirit" because God had promised through the prophets to pour out His Spirit (Ezekiel 36:27; Joel 2:28), and Jesus promised to send the Spirit to His disciples (John 14:16–17; Acts 1:4–5).

Verse 14 says the Spirit "is a pledge of our inheritance." The word "pledge" was used in business for a down payment, a first installment guaranteeing that the full payment would follow (see 2 Corinthians 1:22; 5:5). The Spirit is the first portion of the inheritance believers will fully receive in the future. His presence now guarantees the rest.

This continues "to the redemption of God’s own possession, to the praise of his glory." Believers belong to God, and He will bring them to full and final redemption.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses complete the Trinitarian pattern of the opening hymn. The Father chose and adopted (vv3–6), the Son redeemed and revealed God's plan (vv7–12), and the Spirit seals and guarantees (vv13–14). Each section ends with a refrain about the praise of God's glory (vv6, 12, 14). Salvation is the work of the one God in three persons, and its goal is His glory.

The sealing of the Spirit provides great assurance. Believers' security does not rest on the strength of their feelings or the consistency of their performance but on God's mark of ownership. Paul repeats this in Ephesians 4:30, calling believers not to grieve the Holy Spirit, "in whom you were sealed for the day of redemption."

The idea of "inheritance" points forward. What believers experience of God now, His presence, peace, and power through the Spirit, is real but partial. It is a foretaste of the fullness to come, when God's people will see Him face to face and creation itself will be renewed.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Many believers struggle with doubts about whether they truly belong to God. These verses offer assurance rooted in God's action. Those who have heard and believed the gospel have been sealed by the Spirit. God has marked them as His own.

The Spirit's presence also gives hope in difficult times. The joy, comfort, and love believers experience through the Spirit, even in hardship, are a down payment on the inheritance to come. Whatever is lost or painful now, the best is yet ahead.

Finally, because believers are God's possession, sealed by His Spirit, they are invited to live in a way that honors their Owner. This is not a burden but a privilege: they belong to the God who loves them.

### Ephesians 1:18–19 — The Eyes of Your Hearts Enlightened

verse: Ephesians 1:18-19

#### Simple Explanation

Paul's prayer for the believers is that they would see more clearly. He prays for "the eyes of your hearts" to be "enlightened." In biblical language, the "heart" is the center of a person: mind, will, and emotions together. Paul prays that God would open the inner eyes of their understanding so they can grasp spiritual realities.

He prays that they would know three things.

First, "what is the hope of his calling." God has called them, and that calling comes with a sure hope: the future glory God has prepared for His people.

Second, "what are the riches of the glory of his inheritance in the saints." This can mean either the inheritance believers will receive, or the inheritance God has in His people. Many interpreters favor the second: God regards His people as His treasured inheritance, His own possession (Deuteronomy 32:9). Believers are precious to God.

Third, "what is the exceeding greatness of his power toward us who believe." Paul piles up words for power, "working," "strength," "might," to describe how vast it is. This power is directed "toward us who believe."

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Paul's prayer shows that the great need of believers is not simply to receive new blessings but to understand the blessings they already have. They have already been blessed with every spiritual blessing (v3), but they need spiritual sight to grasp it. This kind of understanding comes only by God's revelation through the Spirit (1 Corinthians 2:9–12).

The measure of God's power at work in believers is the resurrection and ascension of Christ (vv20–21). The same power that raised Jesus from the dead and seated Him above every authority is at work in those who believe. This is not power for showing off but power for transformation, perseverance, and victory over sin and evil.

Paul's prayer also teaches believers how to pray for one another. He does not pray mainly for health, comfort, or success, though those things are good to pray for. He prays for deeper knowledge of God, hope, an awareness of being treasured, and experience of His power. These are the riches that last.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers can pray this prayer for themselves and for others. Asking God to open the eyes of the heart is a way of seeking a deeper, more personal grasp of His truth, beyond simply knowing facts about Him.

This prayer also brings encouragement. When believers feel powerless against temptation, discouragement, or fear, they can remember that the same power that raised Christ from the dead is at work in them. When they feel insignificant, they can remember that God regards His people as His glorious inheritance.

Finally, the hope of God's calling can steady believers through hard times. Their future is secure in Christ, who reigns above every power. Whatever happens now, God's purposes will not fail.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

Ephesians 1 lifts believers' eyes to see the vast scope of God's love and purpose. Before the world was made, God chose His people in Christ to be holy and blameless, and He destined them for adoption as His children. This truth gives deep security. Believers are not accidents or afterthoughts. They are loved, chosen, and welcomed into God's family, all to the praise of His glorious grace.

In Christ, believers have redemption through His blood and the forgiveness of their sins, according to the riches of God's grace. Guilt and shame no longer define them. And God has revealed His great plan: to bring all things together under Christ. This means that history is not random. A broken and divided world is moving toward healing and unity in Christ.

The Holy Spirit seals believers as God's own possession and serves as the guarantee of their future inheritance. In times of doubt or suffering, believers can rest in this assurance. God has marked them as His, and He will complete what He has begun.

Finally, Paul's prayer teaches believers what to seek most: open eyes to know God, His hope, His treasured love for His people, and His resurrection power. Christ reigns far above every authority and power, and He is head over all things for the church. Whatever believers face, they can live with confidence, gratitude, and praise, knowing that they are held in the purposes of a God whose power and love have no limit.

## Chapter Prayer

Blessed be You, God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, for blessing me with every spiritual blessing in Christ. Thank You for choosing me in love before the foundation of the world and for adopting me as Your child. Thank You, Lord Jesus, for redeeming me through Your blood and forgiving all my trespasses according to the riches of Your grace. Thank You, Holy Spirit, for sealing me as God's own and for being the guarantee of my inheritance. Open the eyes of my heart, so that I may know the hope of Your calling, the riches of Your glorious inheritance in Your people, and the immeasurable greatness of Your power toward those who believe. Help me to live with confidence that Christ reigns above every power, and let my whole life be to the praise of Your glory. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
