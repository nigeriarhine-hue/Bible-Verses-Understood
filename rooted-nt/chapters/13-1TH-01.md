# 1 Thessalonians 1
theme: Faith, Love, and Hope That Others Can See
main: Paul, Silas, and Timothy thank God for the Thessalonian believers, remembering their work produced by faith, their labor prompted by love, and their endurance inspired by hope in Christ. They are confident that God has chosen these believers, because the gospel came to them not only in words but with power, with the Holy Spirit, and with deep conviction. Though they received the message in the midst of severe suffering, they welcomed it with joy from the Holy Spirit and became an example to believers throughout Greece. Everyone was talking about how they turned to God from idols to serve the living and true God and to wait for His Son from heaven, Jesus, who rescues from the coming wrath.

## Chapter Background and Story

Paul's time in Thessalonica had been short and stormy. Acts 17 tells how he preached in the synagogue for three Sabbaths, explaining from the Scriptures that the Messiah had to suffer and rise from the dead and that Jesus is the Messiah. Some Jews were persuaded, along with a large number of God-fearing Greeks and many leading women. But jealous opponents formed a mob, dragged a believer named Jason before the city officials, and accused the missionaries as men who had [[Acts 17:6|turned the world upside down]] and who proclaimed another king, Jesus (Acts 17:7). Paul and Silas left that night for Berea.

Paul was anxious about the new believers. They had received only a few weeks of teaching before he was forced to leave, and they were facing persecution from their own neighbors. He longed to go back but was prevented (1 Thessalonians 2:17–18). So he sent Timothy to check on them. When Timothy returned with good news, Paul was overjoyed, and he wrote this letter.

Chapter 1 is a thanksgiving overflowing with joy and encouragement. Paul wants the Thessalonians to know that their faith is genuine, that God is at work among them, and that their example is inspiring believers far beyond their city.

### Greeting (1:1)

[[v1|Paul, Silvanus, and Timothy, to the assembly of the Thessalonians in God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ: Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ]]. Silvanus is the Roman form of the name Silas, Paul's companion on his second journey (Acts 15:40). Paul does not call himself an apostle here; he simply writes as one of three coworkers. The church is "in God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ." Notice how naturally Paul places the Father and Jesus side by side as the source of the church's life and of grace and peace. Even in this very early letter, Jesus is honored alongside God.

### Faith, Love, and Hope (1:2–3)

Paul's thanksgiving for their faith, love, and hope is studied below.

### Chosen and Transformed (1:4–7)

[[v4|We know, brothers loved by God, that you are chosen]]. Paul is confident that the Thessalonians belong to God. The basis of his confidence is studied below.

[[v6|You became imitators of us and of the Lord, having received the word in much affliction, with joy of the Holy Spirit]]. The Thessalonians followed the example of the missionaries and of Jesus Himself. Like Jesus, they suffered for their faith. And yet they had joy, a joy given by the Holy Spirit that did not depend on circumstances.

[[v7|so that you became an example to all who believe in Macedonia and in Achaia]]. Macedonia was northern Greece; Achaia was southern Greece, including Corinth and Athens. This young church, only months old, had already become a model for other believers. The word "example" was used for a pattern or mold that shaped something else.

### A Faith That Spreads (1:8–10)

[[v8|For from you the word of the Lord has been declared, not only in Macedonia and Achaia, but also in every place your faith toward God has gone out, so that we need not say anything]]. "Has been declared" suggests a sound ringing out, like a trumpet blast or a thunderclap echoing across the hills. Thessalonica was on a major trade route, and travelers carried the news of the Thessalonians' faith far and wide. Their conversion was the talk of the region. The content of that report, their turning from idols and their waiting for Christ, is studied below.

## Important Verses Explained

### 1 Thessalonians 1:3 — Work of Faith, Labor of Love, Perseverance of Hope

verse: 1 Thessalonians 1:3

#### Simple Explanation

When Paul prayed for the Thessalonians, he remembered three things about them. Each is a pair: a visible action and its invisible source.

"Your work of faith." Their faith was not just an idea; it produced action. Faith in Christ led them to do things they had never done before: turn from idols, gather with other believers, speak about Jesus, and live differently.

"Labor of love." The word "labor" suggests hard, wearying toil. Their love was not merely a warm feeling. It worked hard for others, even when it was exhausting.

"Perseverance of hope in our Lord Jesus Christ." Their hope in Christ, especially in His return (v10), gave them the strength to endure persecution and hardship. "Perseverance" means steadfast endurance, bearing up under pressure without giving up.

"Before our God and Father." All of this was lived out in God's presence and noticed by Him.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

Faith, love, and hope appear together throughout Paul's letters (1 Corinthians 13:13; Colossians 1:4–5; 1 Thessalonians 5:8). They form a portrait of genuine Christian life. Faith looks to what God has done in Christ. Love reaches out to others in the present. Hope looks forward to what God will do when Christ returns. Together, they embrace past, present, and future.

This verse also shows the connection between inner reality and outward action. Faith produces work, love produces labor, and hope produces endurance. James makes a similar point: faith without works is dead (James 2:17). Genuine faith, love, and hope always show themselves in life.

Notice that hope is focused on "our Lord Jesus Christ." Christian hope is not vague optimism. It is confident expectation rooted in a person: the risen Lord who will return. That hope gave the Thessalonians strength to endure suffering.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

This verse invites believers to consider how their faith, love, and hope show up in daily life. Faith might show in trusting God enough to obey Him in a difficult decision. Love might show in tiring, unglamorous service, such as caring for an aging parent, helping a struggling neighbor, or serving in a ministry that no one notices. Hope might show in steady endurance through illness, loss, or opposition.

It also encourages believers who feel worn out by serving others. Paul calls it a "labor" of love because it is hard. Such labor is not wasted; it is noticed by God.

Finally, this verse provides a pattern for encouraging others. Like Paul, believers can notice and thank God for the faith, love, and hope they see in fellow believers, and tell them so.

### 1 Thessalonians 1:5 — Not in Word Only, but in Power

verse: 1 Thessalonians 1:5

#### Simple Explanation

Paul explains how he knows the Thessalonians are chosen by God. It is because of how the gospel came to them.

"Our Good News came to you not in word only." The missionaries did speak words; they preached and taught. But the gospel was more than words.

"But also in power." God's power was at work. This may include miraculous signs, but it especially refers to the power of God changing hearts and lives. The gospel is [[Romans 1:16|the power of God for salvation]].

"And in the Holy Spirit." The Spirit was active, convicting hearts, opening eyes, and producing faith.

"And with much assurance." This could refer to the deep conviction with which the missionaries preached, or to the full assurance the Thessalonians received when they believed. Both were true.

"You know what kind of men we showed ourselves to be among you for your sake." The missionaries' lives matched their message. Their character and conduct were part of their testimony. Paul will describe this in detail in chapter 2.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

This verse shows that genuine conversion is the work of God. The missionaries did not convert the Thessalonians through persuasive speech or clever techniques. The Holy Spirit worked through their words with power and conviction. Paul says something similar in 1 Corinthians 2:4–5: his message was not with persuasive words of wisdom but with a demonstration of the Spirit and of power, so that faith would rest on God's power, not human wisdom.

The connection between election ("you are chosen," v4) and the gospel's power is important. Paul did not have a secret list of the chosen. He recognized God's choosing by its fruit: the Spirit's power in their conversion and their transformed lives (vv6–10). Election is not a cold doctrine for speculation but a warm assurance for believers, seen in the evidence of God's work.

The final phrase reminds readers that the messengers' lives matter. The gospel came "in word" and was confirmed by the lives of those who carried it.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Believers who share their faith can take comfort that the results do not depend on their eloquence. God's Spirit works through simple, honest words. At the same time, they can pray for the Spirit's power and assurance whenever they speak about Christ.

This verse also reminds believers that their lives are part of their witness. People notice whether a believer's conduct matches their words. Integrity, kindness, and humility give credibility to the message.

For those who wonder whether God is truly at work in their lives, this verse points to the evidence: a heart that responded to the gospel, a growing love for God and others, and a changed life. These are signs of God's choosing and power.

### 1 Thessalonians 1:9–10 — Turned to God from Idols

verse: 1 Thessalonians 1:9-10

#### Simple Explanation

People throughout the region were reporting two things: the warm welcome the Thessalonians gave the missionaries, and the remarkable change in their lives.

"How you turned to God from idols." The Thessalonians had worshiped the many gods of the Greek and Roman world. Thessalonica had temples to various gods, and the emperor was also honored as divine. Turning from these gods was costly. Idol worship was woven into family life, trade guilds, civic festivals, and social gatherings. Leaving it behind could mean losing friendships, business, and social standing.

"To serve a living and true God." Idols were lifeless and false. The Thessalonians now served the God who is alive and real.

"And to wait for his Son from heaven." Their new life had a forward focus. They eagerly awaited Jesus' return.

"Whom he raised from the dead: Jesus." The one they awaited is the risen Jesus, proof that God has power over death.

"Who delivers us from the wrath to come." Jesus rescues His people from the coming judgment of God against sin.

#### Deeper Spiritual Meaning

These verses give a compact summary of Christian conversion: turning, serving, and waiting. Conversion is a turning, a change of direction, away from false gods toward the true God. It leads to service, a life devoted to God. And it involves waiting, an eager expectation of Christ's return.

Notice the order: they turned "to God from idols." The movement toward God came first. It was the beauty and truth of God in Christ that drew them away from idols. People do not usually give up false gods simply because they are told to; they let go when they discover something better.

The phrase "the wrath to come" reminds readers that God will judge evil. But the good news is that Jesus delivers His people from that judgment, because He bore it Himself on the cross (Romans 5:9). This hope gave the Thessalonians courage in the face of persecution.

This passage also introduces a theme that runs through both letters to the Thessalonians: the return of Christ. Paul will develop it more fully in chapters 4 and 5.

#### Applying This Teaching to Life Today

Idols today may not be statues in temples, but they are just as real. Anything that takes God's place as the source of security, identity, or ultimate hope can become an idol: money, success, relationships, appearance, comfort, or approval. These verses invite believers to examine their hearts and to turn from anything that competes with God.

Turning from idols can be costly, as it was for the Thessalonians. It may mean changing habits, relationships, or priorities. But it leads to serving the living and true God, who gives life and joy.

Waiting for Christ's return also shapes daily life. It gives hope in suffering, perspective in success, and urgency in sharing the gospel. Believers live each day in light of the day when Jesus will return.

## How to Apply This Chapter to Life Today

First Thessalonians 1 paints a portrait of a young church whose faith was genuine and contagious. Only months old, facing persecution from their neighbors, these believers displayed faith that worked, love that labored, and hope that endured. Their example inspired believers throughout Greece and beyond.

Their story reminds believers today that the Christian life is not merely a matter of belief but of transformation. Faith, love, and hope are invisible, but they show themselves in visible ways: in obedience, in tireless service, and in steady endurance. Believers can ask how these qualities are being expressed in their own lives, and they can encourage others by noticing and celebrating the work of God in them.

The chapter also shows that conversion is God's work. The gospel came to Thessalonica not merely in words but in power, in the Holy Spirit, and with deep conviction. Those who share their faith can rely on God's power rather than their own skill, while letting their lives confirm their message.

Finally, the Thessalonians' story shows what conversion looks like: turning to God from idols, serving the living and true God, and waiting for His Son from heaven. Believers today can examine their hearts for modern idols and turn from them to the God who is alive. And they can live with eager hope, knowing that Jesus, who rose from the dead, will return and will deliver His people from the coming wrath. Even in suffering, they can have joy given by the Holy Spirit.

## Chapter Prayer

Father, thank You for the gospel that comes not in word only but in power, in the Holy Spirit, and with deep conviction. Thank You for the faith, love, and hope You have planted in Your people. Let my faith be seen in my work, my love in my labor, and my hope in my endurance. When I face hardship for following You, give me joy from Your Holy Spirit. Show me any idols in my heart that compete with You, and help me to turn from them to serve You, the living and true God. Make my life an example that encourages others in their faith, and let the message of Your grace ring out from my life to those around me. Help me to wait eagerly for Your Son from heaven, Jesus, whom You raised from the dead and who rescues us from the wrath to come. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
