# 2 Thessalonians

## About This Letter

Second Thessalonians was written to the same young church in Thessalonica, probably only a few months after 1 Thessalonians, around A.D. 50–51, while Paul, Silas, and Timothy were still in Corinth (2 Thessalonians 1:1; Acts 18:5). Most Christians through history have accepted it as Paul's letter, and Paul himself signs it with his own hand as a mark of authenticity (2 Thessalonians 3:17). Some modern scholars have questioned whether Paul wrote it because its tone and teaching about the end differ from the first letter, while many others see those differences as natural responses to a changed situation.

Since the first letter, the church's persecution had grown worse. At the same time, confusion had spread about the return of Christ. Someone had claimed, perhaps through a supposed prophecy, a spoken message, or even a letter falsely said to come from Paul, that the day of the Lord had already arrived (2:2). This alarmed the believers. Meanwhile, some members had stopped working altogether and were living in idleness, depending on others.

## The Shape of the Letter

In chapter 1, Paul thanks God for the Thessalonians' growing faith and love under persecution and assures them that God is just: He will give relief to the afflicted and repay those who afflict them when the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven. In chapter 2, he corrects the false claim that the day of the Lord had already come, explaining that certain events must happen first, including a rebellion and the revealing of "the man of sin," whom the Lord Jesus will destroy at His coming; then he gives thanks that God chose them for salvation and urges them to stand firm. In chapter 3, he asks for prayer, expresses confidence in the Lord's faithfulness, and gives firm instructions about those who refuse to work, calling the church to follow his example of honest labor and never to grow weary of doing good.

## Themes to Watch For

Watch for endurance in suffering, the justice of God, the glory of Christ's return, the danger of deception about the end times, the certainty that evil will be defeated by the Lord Jesus, God's choosing and calling of His people, standing firm in the teaching received, the faithfulness of the Lord who strengthens and protects, the dignity and duty of work, and the peace of the Lord in every way. Second Thessalonians helps believers hold together eager hope in Christ's coming and steady faithfulness in the present.
