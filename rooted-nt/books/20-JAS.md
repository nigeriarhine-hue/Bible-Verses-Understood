# James

## About This Letter

The letter of James was written by [[James 1:1|James, a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ]]. Most Christians through history have identified him as James the brother of Jesus. During Jesus' ministry, His brothers did not believe in Him (John 7:5), but after the resurrection Jesus appeared to James (1 Corinthians 15:7), and James became a leading figure in the Jerusalem church (Acts 12:17; 15:13–21; 21:18; Galatians 1:19; 2:9). He was known for his deep devotion and prayer. According to the Jewish historian Josephus, he was put to death in Jerusalem around A.D. 62.

James writes "to the twelve tribes which are in the Dispersion," most likely Jewish believers in Jesus scattered beyond the land of Israel, perhaps after the persecution that followed Stephen's death (Acts 8:1; 11:19). Many scholars believe it is one of the earliest New Testament writings, perhaps from the A.D. 40s; others date it closer to James's death. Some modern scholars think a later writer used James's name, while many others find the letter's simple Jewish-Christian setting fits the brother of Jesus well.

James reads much like the wisdom books of the Old Testament, such as Proverbs, and like the Sermon on the Mount. Its short, vivid sayings and images echo the teaching of Jesus again and again.

## The Shape of the Letter

James moves quickly from topic to topic, returning often to a few great themes. Chapter 1 teaches joy in trials, asking God for wisdom, the source of temptation, God's good gifts, and the call to be doers of the word, not hearers only. Chapter 2 condemns favoritism toward the rich and insists that faith without works is dead. Chapter 3 warns about the power of the tongue and contrasts earthly wisdom with the wisdom from above. Chapter 4 exposes the roots of conflict, calls for humble submission to God, and warns against judging others and boasting about tomorrow. Chapter 5 confronts rich oppressors, encourages patience until the Lord's coming, and closes with teaching on honest speech, prayer for the sick, confession, and restoring those who wander.

## Themes to Watch For

Watch for genuine faith that shows itself in action, endurance in trials, wisdom from God, the danger and power of words, care for the poor and the vulnerable, warnings against favoritism and the arrogance of wealth, humility before God, patience, and the power of prayer. James has sometimes been thought to conflict with Paul's teaching on faith, but the two writers address different problems: Paul insists that no one is made right with God by works, and James insists that the faith that saves is never a faith that does nothing.
