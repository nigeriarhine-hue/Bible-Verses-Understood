# 2 John

## About This Letter

Second John is one of the shortest books in the Bible, short enough to fit on a single sheet of papyrus. Its author calls himself simply [[2 John 1:1|The elder]]. From early times Christians have identified this elder with John the apostle, the author of the Gospel of John and 1 John, though some ancient writers and modern scholars suggest a respected leader named John the Elder who belonged to the same circle. The letter's language and concerns closely match 1 John: truth, love, the commandment heard from the beginning, and the danger of deceivers who deny that Jesus Christ came in the flesh. It was likely written around the same time as 1 John, toward the end of the first century, probably from Ephesus.

The letter is addressed to "the chosen lady and her children." Some readers take this as a real Christian woman and her family. Many others understand it as a warm, figurative way of addressing a local church and its members, especially since the letter closes with greetings from "the children of your chosen sister," likely the elder's own congregation. Either way, the message is meant for a community of believers.

## The Shape of the Letter

After a greeting that joins truth and love, the elder rejoices that some of the church's members are walking in truth. He reminds them of the commandment they have had from the beginning, to love one another, and explains that love means walking according to God's commandments. He then warns them about deceivers who deny that Jesus Christ came in the flesh, urges them to remain in the teaching of Christ, and tells them not to welcome or support those who bring a different teaching. He closes by expressing his hope to visit them in person.

## Themes to Watch For

Watch for the close bond between truth and love, walking in obedience, the danger of deception about Jesus' true humanity, remaining in the teaching of Christ, the importance of discernment in hospitality, and the joy of fellowship face to face. Second John shows that love without truth becomes empty, and truth without love becomes harsh; the Christian life holds both together.
