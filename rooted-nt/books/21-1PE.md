# 1 Peter

## About This Letter

First Peter presents itself as written by [[1 Peter 1:1|Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ]], with the help of Silvanus, also known as Silas (5:12). Peter was the fisherman from Galilee whom Jesus called to follow Him, who confessed Jesus as the Christ, denied Him three times on the night of His arrest, and was restored by the risen Lord to feed His sheep (John 21:15–19). He became a leading apostle in Jerusalem and later traveled widely. Early Christian tradition holds that he died as a martyr in Rome during Nero's persecution in the mid-60s A.D. Most Christians through history have accepted the letter as Peter's own; some modern scholars think it was written later by his followers, while many others point out that Silvanus's help easily explains its polished Greek.

The letter is sent to believers "living as foreigners" scattered across five Roman provinces in what is now Turkey (1:1). Many were Gentiles who had turned from their former way of life (1:14, 18; 4:3–4). Because they no longer joined in pagan festivals, idolatry, and immorality, their neighbors viewed them with suspicion, mocked them, and slandered them. They faced social rejection and the threat of wider persecution. Peter writes from "Babylon" (5:13), probably a symbolic name for Rome, to encourage them to stand firm.

## The Shape of the Letter

Chapter 1 praises God for a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus, an imperishable inheritance, and a faith refined by trials, and it calls believers to holiness and sincere love because they were redeemed by the precious blood of Christ. Chapter 2 describes believers as living stones built into a spiritual house, a chosen people and royal priesthood, and calls them to honorable conduct among unbelievers, respect for authorities, and patient endurance of unjust suffering, following the example of Christ, who bore our sins on the cross. Chapter 3 addresses wives and husbands, calls all believers to unity, compassion, and blessing in return for insult, and explains how Christ suffered for sins once for all. Chapter 4 urges believers to live for God's will, to love deeply, to use their gifts, and not to be surprised by fiery trials but to rejoice in sharing Christ's sufferings. Chapter 5 instructs elders to shepherd God's flock willingly and humbly, calls all to clothe themselves with humility, to cast their anxieties on God, and to resist the devil, and closes with greetings.

## Themes to Watch For

Watch for living hope, suffering and glory, the example of Christ's suffering, holiness, the believer's identity as God's chosen people and exiles in the world, good conduct that silences slander, submission and respect within the structures of society, humility, love that covers sins, God's care for the anxious, and the true grace of God in which believers stand. First Peter is a letter of hope for Christians who feel like outsiders in their own culture.
