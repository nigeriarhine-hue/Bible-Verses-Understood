# 1 John

## About This Letter

First John does not name its author, but from the earliest times Christians have recognized it as the work of John, the apostle and "the disciple whom Jesus loved," who also wrote the Gospel of John. Its vocabulary and themes, light and darkness, truth and lies, life, love, abiding, and believing that Jesus is the Christ, closely match the Gospel. The author writes as an eyewitness who heard, saw, and touched [[1 John 1:1|the Word of life]]. Some scholars attribute the letters to a close disciple of John in a community shaped by his teaching. According to early tradition, John spent his later years in Ephesus, and this letter may have been written there toward the end of the first century, around A.D. 85–95, to churches in the surrounding region.

The letter was written after a painful split in the community. Some members had left the fellowship (1 John 2:19) and were teaching a different message. They apparently denied that Jesus is the Christ come in the flesh (2:22; 4:2–3), perhaps claiming that the divine Christ only seemed to be human or only came upon the man Jesus temporarily. They also claimed to know God and to be without sin, while showing little love for fellow believers. Those who remained were confused and unsettled. John writes to reassure them that they truly know God and have eternal life.

## The Shape of the Letter

First John does not follow a simple outline. It moves in circles, returning again and again to three great tests of genuine faith: right belief about Jesus, obedience to God's commands, and love for one another. It opens with the testimony of eyewitnesses to the Word of life and the message that God is light. It calls believers to walk in the light, confess their sins, keep God's commands, and love one another. It warns against loving the world and against antichrists who deny the Son. It celebrates the love of the Father who calls believers His children and promises that when Christ appears they will be like Him. It urges believers to test the spirits, declares that God is love and that perfect love casts out fear, and closes with the assurance that those who believe in the Son have eternal life, that God hears their prayers, and that they should keep themselves from idols.

## Themes to Watch For

Watch for fellowship with God and with one another, light and darkness, confession and forgiveness, Jesus as our Advocate and atoning sacrifice, the commandment to love, the danger of the world, the truth about Jesus come in the flesh, the identity of believers as God's children, love shown in action, God's love as the source of ours, overcoming the world through faith, and assurance of eternal life. First John was written "that you may know that you have eternal life" (5:13), and it offers a warm and searching picture of life in God's family.
