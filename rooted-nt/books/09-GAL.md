# Galatians

## About This Letter

Paul wrote Galatians to churches in the Roman province of Galatia, in what is now central Turkey. Many scholars believe these were the churches of Pisidian Antioch, Iconium, Lystra, and Derbe, which Paul founded on his first missionary journey (Acts 13–14); others think the letter was sent to ethnic Galatians farther north, whom Paul visited later (Acts 16:6; 18:23). Depending on which view is right, the letter was written either around A.D. 48–49, making it perhaps Paul's earliest surviving letter, or in the mid-50s.

After Paul left, other teachers arrived who insisted that Gentile believers must be circumcised and keep the Law of Moses in order to belong fully to God's people. Paul saw this as a different gospel altogether, one that would make Christ's death unnecessary. Galatians is his urgent, passionate response. It is the only one of Paul's letters to churches that skips the usual words of thanksgiving and goes straight to the problem.

## The Shape of the Letter

In chapters 1–2, Paul defends the gospel he preached by telling his own story: his life in Judaism, his call by God, his independence from the Jerusalem leaders, their recognition of his ministry, and his public confrontation with Peter at Antioch. In chapters 3–4, he argues from Scripture and experience that people are made right with God by faith in Christ, as Abraham was, and not by the works of the Law; believers are God's adopted children and heirs, free rather than enslaved. In chapters 5–6, he shows what this freedom looks like in practice: not a license for selfishness but a life led by the Spirit, marked by love, the fruit of the Spirit, and bearing one another's burdens.

## Themes to Watch For

Watch for the grace of God that calls people freely, justification by faith in Christ rather than by works of the Law, the promise to Abraham fulfilled in Christ, the meaning of the Law and its purpose, adoption as God's children, the unity of all believers in Christ regardless of ethnicity, social status, or sex, freedom in Christ, life in the Spirit, and the cross as the believer's only boast. Galatians has sometimes been called the charter of Christian freedom. It shows that the gospel is not Christ plus anything else, but Christ alone, received by faith and lived out in love.
