# 2 Corinthians

## About This Letter

Paul wrote 2 Corinthians from Macedonia around A.D. 55–56, some months after 1 Corinthians (2 Corinthians 2:13; 7:5). In between, his relationship with the Corinthian church had been strained. Paul had made a "painful visit" to Corinth (2:1), and he had written a "severe letter" with many tears (2:4; 7:8), which has probably not survived. When Titus brought news that most of the church had repented and longed to see Paul, Paul was overjoyed (7:6–16). But a group of visiting teachers, whom Paul ironically calls the "very best apostles" (11:5), were still undermining his ministry, boasting of their credentials and mocking Paul's weakness.

## The Shape of the Letter

The letter has three main parts. In chapters 1–7, Paul explains his changed travel plans, celebrates the church's repentance, and describes the nature of true Christian ministry: a ministry of the new covenant, of the Spirit, of glory, and of reconciliation, carried in fragile "clay jars" so that its power is clearly from God. In chapters 8–9, he encourages the Corinthians to complete their gift for the poor believers in Jerusalem, with some of the New Testament's richest teaching on generosity. In chapters 10–13, he defends his apostleship against the false apostles, reluctantly "boasting" in his sufferings and weakness, and preparing for his third visit.

## Themes to Watch For

2 Corinthians is the most personal of Paul's letters, revealing his heart, his tears, his anxieties, and his deep love for a difficult church. Watch for God's comfort in affliction, the glory of the new covenant, treasure in clay jars, the hope of a heavenly dwelling, the new creation, the ministry of reconciliation, cheerful and generous giving, and above all, the power of God made perfect in weakness. In a world that admires strength and success, 2 Corinthians shows that God works most powerfully through humble, suffering servants who depend on His grace.
