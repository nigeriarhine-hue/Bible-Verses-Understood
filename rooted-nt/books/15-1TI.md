# 1 Timothy

## About This Letter

First Timothy is the first of three letters, together with 2 Timothy and Titus, often called the Pastoral Letters, because they were written to individual church leaders about caring for the churches in their charge. The letter presents itself as written by Paul to Timothy, his [[1 Timothy 1:2|true child in faith]], whom Paul had left in Ephesus to correct false teaching and set the church in order while Paul traveled on to Macedonia (1:3).

Timothy came from Lystra. His mother Eunice and grandmother Lois were believers who had taught him the Scriptures from childhood, and his father was a Greek (Acts 16:1–3; 2 Timothy 1:5; 3:15). He joined Paul on the second missionary journey and became one of his most trusted coworkers, sent on delicate missions to Thessalonica, Corinth, and Philippi.

The traditional view, held throughout most of church history, is that Paul wrote this letter after being released from his first Roman imprisonment, around A.D. 62–64. Many modern scholars think the Pastoral Letters were written later by a follower of Paul, pointing to differences in vocabulary, style, and church structure. Others respond that the differences fit a later stage in Paul's ministry, a different purpose, and the possible use of a secretary. The church has received these letters as Scripture from the second century onward.

## The Shape of the Letter

Chapter 1 warns against false teachers who were absorbed in myths, genealogies, and misuse of the Law, and celebrates the grace that saved Paul, "the chief of sinners." Chapter 2 calls for prayer for all people, including rulers, because God desires all to be saved through the one mediator, Christ Jesus, and gives instructions about men and women in worship. Chapter 3 describes the character required of overseers and deacons and closes with a hymn about the mystery of godliness. Chapter 4 warns against false asceticism and urges Timothy to train himself in godliness and set an example despite his youth. Chapter 5 gives guidance for caring for widows and honoring and correcting elders. Chapter 6 addresses slaves and masters, warns against false teachers and the love of money, and calls Timothy to fight the good fight of faith and to command the rich to be generous.

## Themes to Watch For

Watch for sound teaching that leads to love from a pure heart, the grace and patience of Christ toward sinners, prayer for all people, Christ the one mediator, godliness and good works, the character of church leaders, care for the vulnerable, contentment, the danger of the love of money, and the call to guard what has been entrusted. First Timothy shows that how the church lives together matters, because the church is "the pillar and ground of the truth."
