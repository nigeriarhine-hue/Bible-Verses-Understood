# Jude

## About This Letter

The author introduces himself as [[Jude 1:1|Jude, a servant of Jesus Christ, and brother of James]]. The James he mentions is almost certainly the well-known leader of the Jerusalem church, the brother of Jesus. That would make Jude, or Judas, one of the brothers of Jesus named in the Gospels (Matthew 13:55; Mark 6:3). Like James, Jude did not believe in Jesus during His earthly ministry (John 7:5), but after the resurrection he became a follower and later a traveling servant of the gospel (1 Corinthians 9:5). Remarkably, he does not call himself the brother of the Lord, but a servant of Jesus Christ. The letter is often dated somewhere between A.D. 60 and 80. Its recipients are not named, but its heavy use of Old Testament stories and Jewish traditions suggests readers who were familiar with Jewish teaching, perhaps a mostly Jewish Christian community.

Jude had hoped to write about the salvation all believers share, but news of a danger forced him to change his plans. Certain people had slipped into the church who turned God's grace into an excuse for immoral living and rejected the authority of Jesus Christ. Jude writes an urgent appeal for believers to contend for the faith. His letter shares much material with 2 Peter 2, and many scholars believe one writer drew on the other or that both drew on a common source.

## The Shape of the Letter

After a warm greeting, Jude explains his purpose: to urge believers to contend for the faith once for all delivered to the saints. He then describes the ungodly intruders and reminds his readers of examples of God's judgment from the past: Israel in the wilderness, angels who abandoned their place, and Sodom and Gomorrah. He compares the intruders to Cain, Balaam, and Korah, and uses vivid images from nature to describe their emptiness. He quotes a prophecy attributed to Enoch and reminds believers that the apostles foretold such mockers. Then he turns to the faithful with encouragement: build yourselves up in the faith, pray in the Holy Spirit, keep yourselves in God's love, and show mercy to those who waver. The letter closes with one of the most beloved doxologies in Scripture.

## Themes to Watch For

Watch for contending for the faith, the danger of turning grace into license, God's certain judgment on ungodliness, the importance of remembering Scripture, the security of believers who are kept by God, practices that sustain faith, mercy toward those who doubt, and praise to the God who is able to keep His people from stumbling. Jude's brief letter is both a sober warning and a tender reassurance.
