# Philippians

## About This Letter

Philippi was a Roman colony in Macedonia, in northern Greece, settled by retired Roman soldiers and proud of its Roman citizenship and customs. Paul founded the church there around A.D. 49–50 on his second missionary journey, after a vision of a man from Macedonia pleading for help (Acts 16:9–10). Its first members included Lydia, a businesswoman who sold purple cloth, a slave girl set free from an oppressive spirit, and a jailer who came to faith after an earthquake opened the prison where Paul and Silas had been singing hymns at midnight (Acts 16:11–40).

Paul wrote this letter from prison, most likely in Rome around A.D. 60–62, though some scholars suggest an earlier imprisonment in Ephesus or Caesarea. The Philippians had sent him a gift by the hand of Epaphroditus, who became gravely ill while with Paul and then recovered. Paul writes to thank them, to update them on his situation, to send Epaphroditus home, and to encourage them in unity and joy.

## The Shape of the Letter

In chapter 1, Paul thanks God for the Philippians' partnership in the gospel, explains how his imprisonment has advanced the gospel, and reflects on life and death: to live is Christ, and to die is gain. In chapter 2, he calls them to unity and humility, holding up the example of Christ, who emptied Himself and became obedient to death on a cross, and then commends Timothy and Epaphroditus as examples of selfless service. In chapter 3, he warns against those who trust in the flesh and tells how he counted all his former advantages as loss compared with knowing Christ, pressing on toward the goal. In chapter 4, he urges two women leaders to agree, calls the church to rejoice always and not be anxious, and thanks the Philippians for their generous gift, testifying that he has learned contentment in Christ who strengthens him.

## Themes to Watch For

Philippians is often called the letter of joy; words for joy and rejoicing appear again and again, even though Paul writes in chains. Watch for partnership in the gospel, the mind of Christ and His humility, unity in the church, the surpassing worth of knowing Christ, the hope of resurrection and heavenly citizenship, freedom from anxiety through prayer, contentment in every circumstance, and the peace of God that guards the heart. Philippians shows that joy in Christ does not depend on comfortable circumstances but on a living relationship with the Lord who is near.
