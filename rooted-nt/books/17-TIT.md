# Titus

## About This Letter

Titus was a Gentile believer, a Greek, who became one of Paul's trusted coworkers. Paul took him to Jerusalem as a living example that Gentile believers did not need to be circumcised (Galatians 2:1–3), and later sent him on delicate missions to Corinth, where Titus helped restore the strained relationship between Paul and the church and organized the collection for Jerusalem (2 Corinthians 7:6–7, 13–15; 8:6, 16–17).

According to this letter, Paul and Titus had worked together on the island of Crete, in the Mediterranean south of Greece, and Paul had left Titus there to "set in order the things that were lacking" and to appoint elders in every town (Titus 1:5). The churches were young, the culture had a reputation for dishonesty and laziness, and false teachers, especially some from a Jewish background who insisted on rules and myths, were upsetting whole households.

Like 1 and 2 Timothy, Titus is one of the Pastoral Letters. Most Christians through history have held that Paul wrote it after his release from his first Roman imprisonment, around A.D. 62–64; some modern scholars think a later follower wrote it in his name. Paul planned to spend the winter at Nicopolis, on the western coast of Greece, and asked Titus to join him there (3:12).

## The Shape of the Letter

In chapter 1, Paul greets Titus, describes the qualifications for elders, who must be blameless in character and able to teach sound doctrine, and warns against rebellious, deceptive teachers who must be silenced. In chapter 2, he instructs Titus to teach what fits sound doctrine to older men, older women, younger women, younger men, and servants, so that God's teaching may be made attractive, because the grace of God has appeared, training believers to renounce ungodliness and live self-controlled, upright, and godly lives while waiting for the appearing of Jesus Christ. In chapter 3, he urges believers to be good citizens, gentle and courteous to all, remembering that God saved them not because of their righteous works but by His mercy, through the washing of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit, and he calls them to devote themselves to good works and avoid foolish controversies.

## Themes to Watch For

Watch for the close link between sound doctrine and godly living, the character of leaders, the grace of God that both saves and trains, the hope of Christ's appearing, salvation by mercy rather than works, the renewing work of the Holy Spirit, devotion to good works, and the call to make the gospel attractive by the way believers live. Titus shows that the gospel transforms not only individuals but households, churches, and whole communities.
