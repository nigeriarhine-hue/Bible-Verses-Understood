# John

## About This Gospel

The Gospel of John is the fourth Gospel and the most reflective of the four. Its author describes himself as "the disciple whom Jesus loved" (John 21:20–24), and early Christian tradition identifies him as John the son of Zebedee, one of the Twelve. Most scholars date the book to the last decades of the first century, and tradition connects it with the church in Ephesus. John tells us plainly why he wrote: "these are written, that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that believing you may have life in his name" (John 20:31).

## The Shape of the Book

John opens with a majestic prologue about the eternal Word who became flesh (1:1–18). The first half of the book (chapters 1–12) is sometimes called the Book of Signs. It records seven miracles, beginning with water turned into wine at Cana and ending with the raising of Lazarus, and it places Jesus at the great Jewish feasts in Jerusalem. The second half (chapters 13–21) is sometimes called the Book of Glory. It slows down to cover Jesus' final evening with His disciples, His long farewell teaching and prayer (chapters 13–17), and then His arrest, crucifixion, and resurrection appearances. About ninety percent of John's material is not found in Matthew, Mark, or Luke.

## Themes to Watch For

John presents Jesus as the eternal Son who reveals the Father. Seven times Jesus says "I am" with a picture: the bread of life, the light of the world, the door, the good shepherd, the resurrection and the life, the way, the truth and the life, and the true vine. These sayings echo God's name revealed to Moses. Key words recur through the book: believe, life, light, truth, love, witness, abide, and glory. John shows that faith is a personal response to Jesus, that eternal life begins now in knowing God (17:3), and that the Holy Spirit, the Counselor, continues Jesus' presence with His people after He returns to the Father.
