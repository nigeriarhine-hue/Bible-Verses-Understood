# 3 John

## About This Letter

Third John is the shortest book in the New Testament by word count, and the most personal of John's letters. Like 2 John, it comes from [[3 John 1:1|The elder]], traditionally identified with the apostle John, writing near the end of the first century, probably from Ephesus. Unlike 2 John, it is addressed to a named individual: Gaius, a dear friend and faithful believer in a nearby church. Gaius was a common name, and we cannot be sure whether he is the same as any other Gaius mentioned in the New Testament.

The letter gives a rare glimpse into the everyday life of the early churches. Traveling missionaries went out from the elder's community to preach the gospel, depending on the hospitality of local believers. Gaius had welcomed them generously. But a church leader named Diotrephes refused to receive them, rejected the elder's authority, spread false accusations, and even expelled members who wanted to show hospitality. The elder writes to encourage Gaius, to commend a man named Demetrius, and to address the problem Diotrephes was causing.

## The Shape of the Letter

The elder greets Gaius warmly and prays for his well-being. He rejoices to hear that Gaius walks in the truth, and he praises Gaius for his faithful hospitality to traveling missionaries, urging him to continue sending them on their way in a manner worthy of God. He then describes the behavior of Diotrephes, who loves to be first, and promises to deal with it when he comes. He urges Gaius to imitate what is good rather than what is evil, commends Demetrius, and closes with his hope to see Gaius soon and speak face to face.

## Themes to Watch For

Watch for walking in the truth, the joy of seeing others grow in faith, the value of Christian hospitality, partnership in gospel work, the danger of selfish ambition in church leadership, the importance of a good testimony, and the gift of personal fellowship. Third John shows that the truth of the gospel is lived out in ordinary acts of generosity, humility, and faithfulness.
