# 2 Peter

## About This Letter

Second Peter presents itself as a letter from [[2 Peter 1:1|Simon Peter, a servant and apostle of Jesus Christ]], written near the end of his life, when he knew his death was approaching, as Jesus had told him (1:14; John 21:18–19). Peter refers to being an eyewitness of Jesus' transfiguration (1:16–18), to an earlier letter he had written (3:1), and to the letters of "our beloved brother Paul" (3:15).

Of all the New Testament books, 2 Peter's authorship has been the most debated. Some early Christians hesitated to accept it, and many modern scholars believe it was written after Peter's death by someone in his circle, pointing to its different Greek style from 1 Peter and its close similarities with the letter of Jude. Others defend Peter's authorship, noting that he may have used a different secretary, that the letter's personal claims are clear, and that the church came to recognize it as Scripture. If Peter wrote it, it dates to the mid-60s A.D., shortly before his martyrdom in Rome.

The letter was written to believers threatened by false teachers who denied the Lord's return and the coming judgment, mocked the idea that Christ would come again, and used grace as an excuse for immorality and greed.

## The Shape of the Letter

Chapter 1 reminds believers that God's divine power has given them everything needed for life and godliness, calls them to add virtue upon virtue to their faith, and grounds the faith in eyewitness testimony of Christ's glory and in the prophetic Scriptures given by the Holy Spirit. Chapter 2 vividly exposes false teachers, their greed, arrogance, and immorality, and shows from Old Testament examples that God knows how to rescue the godly and judge the wicked. Chapter 3 answers those who scoff at Christ's return, explaining that God's apparent delay is His patience, since He does not want anyone to perish, and that the day of the Lord will come like a thief, bringing new heavens and a new earth where righteousness dwells; therefore believers should live holy lives, guard against error, and grow in grace and knowledge.

## Themes to Watch For

Watch for growth in godliness, the reliability of the apostolic witness and of Scripture, the danger of false teaching that leads to immoral living, God's justice and mercy, the certainty of Christ's return, God's patience that gives time for repentance, the hope of a new creation, and the closing call to grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. Second Peter is a farewell reminder to remember what is true and to keep growing.
