# Colossians

## About This Letter

Colossae was a small town in the Lycus River valley of Phrygia, in what is now southwestern Turkey, about a hundred miles east of Ephesus and close to the larger cities of Laodicea and Hierapolis. Paul had not visited the church there himself (Colossians 2:1). It was founded by Epaphras, a native of Colossae who had probably come to faith during Paul's long ministry in Ephesus, when, as Acts 19:10 says, "all those who lived in Asia heard the word of the Lord Jesus". Epaphras later visited Paul in prison and brought news of the church (Colossians 1:7–8; 4:12–13).

The letter presents itself as written by Paul, together with Timothy, while Paul was a prisoner (4:3, 10, 18). Most Christians through history have held that Paul wrote it during his Roman imprisonment around A.D. 60–62, at the same time as Philemon and Ephesians; Tychicus and Onesimus carried it (4:7–9). Some modern scholars think a follower of Paul wrote it in his name, pointing to differences in style and emphasis, while many others find these differences fully explained by the subject matter and circumstances.

Epaphras's report included a concern. Some teachers were urging the Colossian believers toward a mix of practices: strict food and festival rules, harsh treatment of the body, the worship of angels, claims to special visions, and a "philosophy" based on human tradition and spiritual forces (2:8, 16–23). The exact nature of this teaching is debated, but its effect was to suggest that Christ alone was not enough.

## The Shape of the Letter

In chapter 1, Paul gives thanks for the Colossians' faith, prays for their growth in knowledge and fruitfulness, and celebrates the supremacy of Christ in a great hymn: He is the image of the invisible God, the one through whom and for whom all things were created, the head of the church, and the one through whom God reconciled all things by the blood of His cross. In chapter 2, he warns against empty philosophy and human regulations, declaring that in Christ the fullness of deity dwells bodily and that believers are complete in Him, having been buried and raised with Him. In chapters 3 and 4, he shows what life in Christ looks like: setting the mind on things above, putting off the old self and putting on the new, letting Christ's peace and word dwell richly, living faithfully in households and at work, devoting themselves to prayer, and speaking graciously to outsiders.

## Themes to Watch For

Watch for the supremacy and sufficiency of Christ, the fullness of God dwelling in Him, His victory over every spiritual power, reconciliation through the cross, union with Christ in His death and resurrection, the mystery of "Christ in you, the hope of glory," freedom from human rules and spiritual intimidation, the new self being renewed in the image of its Creator, thankfulness, and doing everything in the name of the Lord Jesus. Colossians answers every claim that believers need something more than Christ: in Him they have been made full.
