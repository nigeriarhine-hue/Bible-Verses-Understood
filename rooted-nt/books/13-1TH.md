# 1 Thessalonians

## About This Letter

Thessalonica was the capital of the Roman province of Macedonia, a busy port city on the great Roman road called the Via Egnatia. Paul, Silas, and Timothy brought the gospel there around A.D. 49–50, soon after leaving Philippi. Some Jews and many God-fearing Greeks and prominent women believed. But opposition quickly rose, a mob attacked the house of a believer named Jason, and Paul and Silas had to leave the city by night after only a short stay (Acts 17:1–10).

Paul worried about the young church he had been forced to leave behind. From Athens he sent Timothy back to strengthen them, and when Timothy returned with good news of their faith and love, Paul wrote this letter, probably from Corinth around A.D. 50–51 (1 Thessalonians 3:1–6; Acts 18:5). Most scholars agree that it is one of Paul's earliest letters, and perhaps the oldest book of the New Testament.

## The Shape of the Letter

In chapters 1–3, Paul gives thanks for the Thessalonians' faith, love, and hope, recalls how they turned to God from idols, defends the sincerity and gentleness of his ministry among them, and shares his joy at Timothy's report. In chapters 4–5, he gives instruction: to live in holiness and sexual purity, to love one another, to work quietly with their own hands, and to grieve with hope for believers who have died, because the Lord Himself will descend from heaven and the dead in Christ will rise. He reminds them that the day of the Lord will come like a thief in the night, so they should live as children of light, and he closes with a series of short commands about church life, prayer, joy, and thanksgiving.

## Themes to Watch For

Watch for the triad of faith, love, and hope, the joy of believers who received the word in the midst of suffering, Paul's model of gentle and sacrificial ministry, holiness as God's will, brotherly love, the dignity of honest work, the hope of Christ's return and the resurrection of the dead, readiness and watchfulness, encouragement of one another, and the call to rejoice always, pray without ceasing, and give thanks in everything. First Thessalonians shows the warm heart of a pastor for a young, persecuted church, and it offers lasting comfort to all who grieve.
