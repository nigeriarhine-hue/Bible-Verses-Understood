# Mark

## About This Gospel

Mark is the shortest of the four Gospels and probably the first to be written. Early Christian writers, beginning with Papias in the early second century, report that John Mark, a companion of Peter and Paul (Acts 12:12, 25; 1 Peter 5:13), wrote down Peter's preaching about Jesus. Many scholars date the book to the 60s A.D., likely written in Rome for Christians who faced suspicion and persecution under Nero. Mark often explains Jewish customs and translates Aramaic words, which suggests that many of his readers were Gentiles.

## The Shape of the Book

Mark tells the story with urgency. His favorite word, "immediately," moves the narrative quickly from scene to scene. The first half of the book (chapters 1–8) shows Jesus' authority in Galilee as He teaches, heals, casts out demons, and calms storms, while the disciples and the crowds keep asking, "Who is this?" At Caesarea Philippi, Peter confesses that Jesus is the Christ (8:29). The second half (chapters 8–16) follows Jesus "on the way" to Jerusalem as He teaches that the Son of Man must suffer, die, and rise, and as He calls His followers to take up their cross. Roughly a third of the book describes Jesus' final week.

## Themes to Watch For

Mark presents Jesus as the Son of God (1:1; 15:39) whose identity is most clearly revealed at the cross. He emphasizes that Jesus came "not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many" (10:45). Mark is honest about the disciples' fear, misunderstanding, and failure, and equally clear about Jesus' patient grace toward them. His vivid details, such as the cushion in the boat, the green grass at the feeding of the five thousand, and Jesus' Aramaic words "Talitha cumi" and "Ephphatha," give the book the feel of eyewitness memory.
