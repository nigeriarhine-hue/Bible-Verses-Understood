# 2 Timothy

## About This Letter

Second Timothy is Paul's last known letter, written from prison in Rome shortly before his death. His circumstances were very different from his earlier, gentler house arrest (Acts 28:30–31). Now he was chained like a criminal (2 Timothy 1:16; 2:9), many of his friends had deserted him (1:15; 4:10, 16), and he expected to be executed soon: "the time of my departure has come" (4:6). Early Christian tradition holds that Paul was beheaded in Rome during the persecution under the emperor Nero, around A.D. 64–67.

As with 1 Timothy and Titus, most Christians through history have accepted the letter as Paul's own, while some modern scholars think a follower wrote it in his name; others note that its intensely personal details, names, and requests are hard to explain unless Paul wrote it. Timothy was still serving in Ephesus, where false teaching continued to threaten the church. Paul writes to encourage his beloved son in the faith to be strong, to suffer for the gospel without shame, to guard and pass on the truth, and to come to him quickly before winter.

## The Shape of the Letter

In chapter 1, Paul thanks God for Timothy's sincere faith, urges him to fan into flame the gift of God, reminds him that God gave a spirit of power, love, and self-control rather than fear, and calls him not to be ashamed of the gospel or of Paul's chains. In chapter 2, he calls Timothy to be strong in grace and to entrust the teaching to faithful people, using images of a soldier, an athlete, a farmer, a worker who correctly handles the word of truth, and a vessel for honorable use, and urging gentleness toward opponents. In chapter 3, he warns that difficult times will come, describes the character of people in the last days, and points Timothy to his own example and to the God-breathed Scriptures, which are able to make him wise for salvation. In chapter 4, Paul solemnly charges Timothy to preach the word in season and out of season, looks back on his own life as a finished race and forward to the crown of righteousness, and closes with personal requests and greetings.

## Themes to Watch For

Watch for courage instead of fear, suffering for the gospel, the power of God's grace, guarding the good deposit and passing it on to others, endurance, the trustworthiness of God even when people are faithless, the inspiration and usefulness of Scripture, the call to preach, faithfulness to the end, and the hope of the crown of righteousness. Second Timothy is a father's farewell to his son in the faith, and it speaks to every believer who wants to finish well.
