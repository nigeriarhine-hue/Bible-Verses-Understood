# Matthew

## About This Gospel

The Gospel of Matthew opens the New Testament and serves as a bridge between the Old Testament and the story of Jesus. Early Christian tradition consistently names Matthew, the tax collector whom Jesus called to follow Him (Matthew 9:9), as its author. It was probably written in the second half of the first century, for a community that included many Jewish believers who knew the Scriptures of Israel well.

Matthew's central concern is to show that Jesus is the Messiah promised to Israel, the Son of David and the Son of Abraham, and that in Him God's promises are fulfilled. More than any other Gospel, Matthew points out how events in Jesus' life fulfill the Old Testament, often with the phrase "that it might be fulfilled." He presents Jesus as a new and greater Moses who teaches God's will with authority, as the true Son of God who remains faithful where Israel failed, and as "God with us."

## How the Book Is Arranged

Matthew organizes Jesus' teaching into five major sections, each ending with a phrase like "When Jesus had finished these words": the Sermon on the Mount (chapters 5–7), the mission instructions to the Twelve (chapter 10), the parables of the kingdom (chapter 13), teaching on life in the community of disciples (chapter 18), and teaching about the end of the age (chapters 24–25). Between these teaching sections come narratives of Jesus' miracles, conflicts, and journeys. The book moves from His birth and early ministry in Galilee to His final week in Jerusalem, His death, and His resurrection.

## Themes to Watch For

The kingdom of heaven is Matthew's great theme: God's reign arriving in Jesus, calling for repentance and a righteousness that goes deeper than outward rule-keeping. Matthew also emphasizes that Jesus' mission, first directed to Israel, is meant for all nations, a theme that begins with the visit of the wise men and ends with the command to make disciples of all nations. The book is framed by the promise of Immanuel, "God with us" (1:23), and Jesus' final promise, "I am with you always" (28:20).
