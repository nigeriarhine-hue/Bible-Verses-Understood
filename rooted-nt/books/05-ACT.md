# Acts

## About This Book

The Acts of the Apostles is the second volume of Luke's two-part work. Like his Gospel, it is addressed to Theophilus, and it picks up exactly where the Gospel ends, with the risen Jesus and His ascension (Acts 1:1–11). Luke was a companion of Paul, and in several passages he writes "we," showing that he was present for parts of Paul's journeys (Acts 16:10–17; 20:5–21:18; 27:1–28:16). Acts covers about thirty years, from Jesus' ascension around A.D. 30 to Paul's house arrest in Rome in the early 60s. Many scholars date the book to the 60s–80s A.D.

## The Shape of the Book

Jesus' words in Acts 1:8 provide an outline: "you will be witnesses to me in Jerusalem, in all Judea and Samaria, and to the uttermost parts of the earth." Chapters 1–7 tell of the church in Jerusalem, beginning with the coming of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost. Chapters 8–12 describe the gospel spreading to Samaria, to an Ethiopian official, to the Roman centurion Cornelius, and to Antioch, along with the dramatic conversion of Saul of Tarsus. Chapters 13–28 follow Paul's missionary journeys through Asia Minor and Greece, his arrest in Jerusalem, his trials, and his voyage to Rome. In the first half, Peter is the leading figure; in the second, Paul.

## Themes to Watch For

Acts is often called the Acts of the Holy Spirit, because the Spirit empowers, guides, and unites the church at every turn. Luke shows the gospel crossing one barrier after another, from Jews to Samaritans to Gentiles, until it reaches the capital of the empire. He highlights the preaching of the apostles, centered on Jesus' death and resurrection; the prayer, generosity, and fellowship of the early believers; the suffering and courage of Christ's witnesses; and God's sovereign hand moving His word forward despite opposition. Again and again Luke reports that "the word of God increased." The book ends with Paul in Rome proclaiming the kingdom of God "with all boldness, without hindrance" (Acts 28:31), an open ending that invites every generation to continue the story.
