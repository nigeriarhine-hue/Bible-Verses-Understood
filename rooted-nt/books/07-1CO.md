# 1 Corinthians

## About This Letter

Paul founded the church in Corinth during his second missionary journey, around A.D. 50–52, staying for a year and a half (Acts 18:1–18). Corinth was a wealthy, cosmopolitan port city, the capital of the Roman province of Achaia, famous for commerce, athletic games, and moral looseness. Paul wrote this letter from Ephesus around A.D. 53–55 (16:8), after receiving troubling reports from members of Chloe's household (1:11) and a letter from the church with questions (7:1). Paul had already written them an earlier letter that has not survived (5:9).

## The Shape of the Letter

The first part of the letter (chapters 1–6) addresses problems Paul had heard about: divisions around favorite leaders, pride in human wisdom, a case of sexual immorality the church had tolerated, lawsuits between believers, and the misuse of Christian freedom. The second part (chapters 7–16) answers questions the Corinthians had asked, introduced by the phrase "now concerning": marriage and singleness, food offered to idols, order in worship, the Lord's Supper, spiritual gifts, and the resurrection of the dead. In the middle of his teaching on spiritual gifts, Paul writes his great chapter on love (chapter 13). He ends with instructions about a collection for the believers in Jerusalem and personal greetings.

## Themes to Watch For

Look for the message of the cross, which turns human wisdom and status upside down; the church as God's temple and Christ's body; the call to holiness in a permissive culture; the principle that freedom must be governed by love for others; the importance of order and edification in worship; the supremacy of love above every spiritual gift; and the certainty of the bodily resurrection, grounded in Christ's own resurrection. Though written to a troubled church nearly two thousand years ago, 1 Corinthians speaks with striking relevance to believers living in proud, divided, and pleasure-seeking cultures today.
